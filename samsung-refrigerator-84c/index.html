Samsung Refrigerator 84C Error Code: Compressor Lock (Meaning, Causes, What to Do)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

84C on a Samsung refrigerator means Compressor Lock: the inverter control tried to run the compressor and it didn't start turning. It usually shows up with a fridge that has stopped cooling. Samsung's advice is not to unplug the fridge or keep pressing buttons to clear the code, because that makes the problem harder for a technician to diagnose, and to request service. Move perishable food to a cooler or another fridge in the meantime.

What the code means

Samsung's US error code list names 84C "Compressor Lock." Samsung Canada's support answer for 84E or 84C calls it "Compressor Motor Locked Over RPM" and says it happens when the compressor runs into a restriction. In Samsung's Fast Track Troubleshooting sheet for RF28K9070, RF22K9381 and RF28K9380 French-door models, code 84 is "COMP Motor stall," and the checks listed are whether the compressor and the sealed refrigeration system (the cycle) are normal, the input voltage, and the inverter PCB (the board that drives the variable-speed compressor) for poor soldering or shorted parts. Samsung lists the neighboring compressor codes separately: 83E is abnormal compressor current, 85E is compressor under-voltage (a brown-out) and 86E is compressor over-voltage (a surge). The letter at the end only changes how the code is displayed, so 84C and 84E point to the same fault. Unlike those voltage codes, Samsung doesn't give owners a reset for 84C; it says not to unplug the fridge unless directed and to get service.

Likely causes

  • A compressor that is mechanically stuck or can't start (Samsung's "Compressor Lock")
  • A restriction in the sealed refrigeration system that the compressor can't push against (named in Samsung Canada's answer and the Fast Track sheet's "check the cycle" step)
  • A fault on the inverter PCB, such as poor soldering or a shorted part, which the Fast Track sheet tells technicians to check
  • Low or unstable supply voltage at the outlet, which Samsung's troubleshooting sheet also lists as a check
  • Loose or damaged compressor wiring between the inverter board and the compressor

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Don't unplug the fridge or tap through the panel trying to make 84C go away. Samsung says both make it harder for the technician to diagnose the problem.
  2. On a Family Hub model, you can see current and past error codes by running Self Check: Apps, then Fridge Manager, Fridge Settings, Self Check, and Start. Note every code it lists for the service call.
  3. Check that the fridge is plugged straight into a working wall outlet, not an extension cord, and that the breaker hasn't tripped. Supply voltage is one of the checks in Samsung's troubleshooting sheet.
  4. Move meat, dairy and other perishables to a cooler with ice or another fridge, and keep the doors shut as much as possible while you wait.
  5. Write down the model and serial number from the label inside the fridge, then request service through Samsung Support or the Samsung Members app.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • 84C is on the display. Samsung's guidance for this code is to request service rather than reset it.
  • Testing the inverter PCB and compressor wiring. The inverter circuits carry high DC voltage, so this is technician work.
  • Diagnosing a sealed-system restriction or a seized compressor, which requires an EPA-certified refrigeration technician to open the system and recharge refrigerant.
  • The fridge is still under warranty. Check your Samsung warranty terms, since some models carry separate, longer coverage on the compressor, before paying for a repair.
