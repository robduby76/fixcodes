Samsung Microwave SE (5E) Error Code: A Touch Key Is Stuck or Shorted

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

SE on a Samsung microwave (it can also read 5E, -SE- or C-d0 on some models) means the control thinks a touch key has been held down for more than 10 seconds. That's usually a dirty, wet or stuck button, often from a spill or steam from the cooktop below. Unplug the microwave, clean the control panel, let it dry completely, then plug it back in. If SE comes back, Samsung says the touch panel needs a service visit.

What the code means

Samsung's microwave information-code tables (Samsung UK and Samsung Gulf) list "C-d0 / -SE- / SE" together with the description "The touch key was pressed for more than 10 seconds," and the action "Unplug the power cord and clean the button (dust, water). If the same problem appears again, contact the Samsung service centre." Samsung Russia's table lists SE/5E as a control panel malfunction and, for the matching C-d0 code, says to unplug the microwave, clean the control panel, make sure no liquid got onto it, wipe off any liquid and let it dry for 1 to 2 hours before trying again. Because the display uses seven-segment characters, SE often looks like 5E. In practice the control sees a key as permanently pressed: grease or moisture behind the membrane, a key that is physically stuck, or a worn-out or shorted keypad. Samsung US gives the same general routine for any microwave error code: unplug it or turn it off at the breaker, let it dry if moisture got into the electronics (a spill or steam from a cooktop), otherwise wait 60 seconds and turn it back on, and request service if the code returns. A related code, C-F2 or -tE-, means abnormal communication with the touch keys; Samsung's fix for that one is simply pressing Stop and trying again.

Likely causes

  • A button is being held down: something leaning on the panel, or a key stuck in the pressed position
  • Grease, food residue or dust on or around the touch keys
  • Moisture on or behind the control panel, from a spill, cleaning spray, or steam rising from the cooktop under an over-the-range microwave
  • A worn, cracked or internally shorted touch keypad (membrane) that reads as a constant key press
  • A fault in the control electronics, if a clean, dry panel still shows SE

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before opening panels.

  1. Check that nothing is pressing on the control panel and that no key is visibly stuck down.
  2. Unplug the microwave, or switch it off at the circuit breaker for an over-the-range model.
  3. Clean the control panel with a soft cloth that is only slightly damp with mild soapy water. Don't spray cleaner directly on the panel, since liquid can seep behind the keys.
  4. If liquid or steam may have gotten onto the panel, wipe it dry and leave the microwave unplugged for 1 to 2 hours (longer is fine) so it dries out completely. When cooking on the range below, use the hood fan to keep steam off the microwave.
  5. Plug it back in and try the keys. If SE is gone, you're done.
  6. Don't open the microwave case to get at the keypad or control board. Microwaves contain a high-voltage capacitor that can hold a dangerous charge even when unplugged.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • SE comes back after cleaning and drying: Samsung says to contact its service center. The usual repair is replacing the touch keypad or control panel assembly.
  • Keys respond only some of the time, or the panel looks cracked or bubbled: the keypad membrane is likely damaged and needs replacement by a technician.
  • Any repair that involves opening the microwave cabinet should be done by a qualified technician because of the high-voltage parts inside.
