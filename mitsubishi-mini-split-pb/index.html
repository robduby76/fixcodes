Mitsubishi Mini Split Error Code PB: Indoor Fan Motor Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

PB (sometimes shown as Pb) on a Mitsubishi Electric Mr. Slim mini split means "indoor fan motor error": the indoor unit's blower fan isn't running the way the control expects. Mitsubishi lists only two causes, a defective indoor fan motor or a defective indoor controller board, and the indoor unit flashes its OPERATION light or beeps 11 times. A homeowner can check that the fan isn't blocked and try a power reset, but testing the high-voltage fan connector and replacing the motor or board is a job for an HVAC technician.

What the code means

Mitsubishi Electric's service manual for the PKA-A12HA7/PKA-A18HA7 wall-mounted indoor units (OCH637) lists PB (Pb) as "Indoor fan motor error" in its self-diagnosis table, shown on an IR wireless remote setup as 11 beeps or 11 blinks of the OPERATION INDICATOR lamp. Its troubleshooting table names this check code "Fan motor trouble" and gives two causes: a defective fan motor and a defective indoor controller board, with the instruction to follow the DC fan motor check (fan motor/indoor controller circuit board). Mitsubishi Electric Singapore's Mr. Slim error chart uses the same 11-blink pattern and calls it "Indoor unit fan motor error." The outdoor unit service manual for PUZ-A/PUY-A NKA7 and NHA7 units (OCH636E) also lists Pb as "Indoor fan motor trouble" with the outdoor board indicator blinking 5 times, and says to check the winding of the indoor unit fan motor, referring to the indoor unit service manual for details.

Likely causes

  • A defective indoor fan (blower) motor
  • A defective indoor controller circuit board, including a blown fuse on the board that cuts power to the fan motor
  • Poor contact at the fan motor connector (CNMF) on the indoor controller board, which is the first thing Mitsubishi's fan motor check looks at

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before opening panels.

  1. Write down the code and count the beeps or blinks; PB is 11 on Mitsubishi's wireless-remote table.
  2. Turn the system off and look into the air outlet. If something is jammed in the fan wheel (the long barrel-shaped fan in a wall unit), that's worth telling the technician; don't reach into the unit while it has power.
  3. Clean the indoor air filters and make sure the intake and outlet aren't blocked.
  4. Turn the system off at the breaker for a few minutes and restart it. If PB comes back, stop resetting it and call an HVAC technician.
  5. Don't unplug or test the fan motor connector yourself. Mitsubishi warns that high voltage (around 300 V DC) is on that connector and that pulling it with the power on can damage both the board and the motor.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Confirm the code with the remote's self-diagnosis or at the outdoor controller board (Pb shows as 5 blinks in the OCH636E outdoor table).
  • With the power off, check the contact at the fan motor connector (CNMF) on the indoor controller board and repair any wiring fault.
  • Power supply check with CNMF removed: on the indoor controller board, measure between pins 1 (+) and 3 (-) for about 294 to 325 V DC, and between pins 4 (+) and 3 (-) for 15 V DC. If either is wrong, check the board fuse; replace the fuse if it's blown, otherwise replace the indoor controller board.
  • If the power supply is normal, run the fan and measure the sensor signal between CNMF pins 6 and 3. If it doesn't repeat between 0 and 15 V DC, replace the fan motor; if it does, replace the indoor controller board.
  • After each part change, check the fan's operation before closing up. On OCH636E outdoor units Mitsubishi also says to check the winding of the indoor fan motor.
  • Use the exact motor and board part numbers from the indoor unit's service manual. Under warranty, go through the installing contractor or a Mitsubishi Electric Diamond Contractor.
