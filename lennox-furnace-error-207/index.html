Lennox Furnace Error 207 (E207): Hot Surface Ignitor Sensed Open (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-10 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error 207 on a Lennox furnace means the control sensed that the hot-surface ignitor circuit is open, so it will not try to light the burners. The ignitor itself is often failed, or its plug or wiring is broken. Lennox says to measure the ignitor's resistance and replace it if it's open or outside the range in the installation manual, and the alert clears once the fault is fixed.

What the code means

Lennox service literature for its communicating SureLight furnaces (SL280V-series, EL297 and similar) defines E 207 as "Hot surface ignitor sensed open". The control checks the ignitor circuit before it opens the gas valve, and if it sees an open circuit it skips ignition entirely. The code shows on the furnace's seven-segment LED one character at a time (E, 2, 0, 7). Lennox's action is to measure the resistance of the hot-surface ignitor and replace it if it's open or not within the specified range in the installation and operating manual; on the SL280UHNV the SureLight silicon-nitride ignitor is specified at 39 to 70 ohms. The alert resumes normal operation after the fault is cleared. This is a different problem from E201 (lost communication with the blower motor) and from E270, which is a soft lockout after the ignitor ran but no flame current was sensed. E290 is a related ignitor-circuit fault (failed ignitor or triggering circuitry) with the same resistance check.

Likely causes

  • A failed hot-surface ignitor that has opened (cracked, burned out, or end-of-life); this is the usual cause
  • A loose, unseated, or damaged ignitor plug, or a broken wire between the ignitor and the integrated control
  • An ignitor whose resistance is outside Lennox's specified range (39 to 70 ohms on the SL280UHNV SureLight silicon-nitride ignitor)
  • A problem on the control's ignitor-triggering circuit (Lennox lists that separately as E290, with the same resistance check)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Read the code: watch the furnace's seven-segment LED through the sight glass. You can recall recent error codes by holding the diagnostic button until 'E' shows, then releasing it.
  2. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
  3. Make sure the furnace has power (service switch on, breaker not tripped) and the front door is fully seated, since the door interlock will keep the furnace from running.
  4. Check that the manual gas shutoff next to the furnace is open (handle in line with the pipe). A closed valve can cause a no-heat call, but E207 specifically means the ignitor circuit looks open, not that gas is off.
  5. Turn off power at the furnace switch or breaker and wait a few minutes. With power off, look at the ignitor near the burners: a cracked or broken element, or a loose plug, is enough to set E207. Don't touch the ignitor element; it is brittle.
  6. If the plug is simply unseated, push it firmly into place, restore the door and power, and call for heat. If E207 comes back, stop resetting and call a technician. Replacing an ignitor and measuring its resistance is a job for someone comfortable working inside a gas furnace with the power off.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E207 comes back after the ignitor plug is seated and the door is on. Lennox's next step is to measure ignitor resistance and replace it if it's open or out of range.
  • The ignitor is visibly cracked, or a technician measures it open or outside the model's specified ohms (39 to 70 on the SL280UHNV).
  • The ignitor tests good but the control still reports E207 or E290, which can point to the ignitor wiring or the integrated control.
  • You are not comfortable working around the burners and gas valve with the furnace open. A licensed technician should replace the ignitor and confirm the specified range in your model's manual.
  • If you smell gas or a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
