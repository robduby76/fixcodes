Code: H3🔧 Gree Mini Split

Gree Mini Split Error Code H3: Compressor Overload Protection (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

H3 on a Gree mini split means compressor overload protection: the overload protector on the compressor opened, usually because the compressor got too hot, or the control lost the signal from that protector. Gree's service manuals check the overload protector and its wiring first, then the electronic expansion valve, then the refrigerant charge, and only then the outdoor control board. Clearing dirt from the outdoor coil, giving the unit room to breathe and a clean power reset are fair homeowner steps; the electrical and refrigerant checks are for a licensed HVAC technician.

What the code means

Gree's error tables list H3 as "Overload protection for compressor" (27 SEER Jasmine), "Compressor over load protection" (Free Match) or "Compressor overload protection" (Multi21+ and Gree's after-sales error code list). The compressor carries an overload protector (SAT) wired back to the outdoor board; the Jasmine manual's overload sensor stops the compressor above 115 °C and releases the protection once it cools below 95 °C. When the protector trips, the compressor stops. In cooling and drying the indoor fan keeps running; in heating the whole unit stops. On Multi21+ units the compressor restarts on its own after at least 3 minutes once the overload switch resets, but if the protection happens more than 6 times the unit locks out until power is switched off and back on (the count clears after the compressor runs longer than 30 minutes). On Free Match and Multi21+ models the code can be viewed through the remote within the first 200 seconds and is shown directly after that; units with indicator lamps instead of a display blink 3 times. Gree troubleshoots H3 with the same chart it uses for high discharge temperature (E4), because both point to a compressor running too hot.

Likely causes

  • A loose or disconnected overload protector wire or terminal (OVC-COMP on the Jasmine board); Gree says the resistance across this terminal should normally be under 1 ohm
  • A failed overload protector (SAT) on the compressor
  • A loose or failed electronic expansion valve: its FA wiring terminal or the EKV coil
  • Refrigerant leak or low charge, which makes the compressor run hot
  • Poor heat exchange: a dirty outdoor coil, blocked air inlet or outlet, or a bad spot for the outdoor unit to shed heat
  • Too much load on the system for long periods, or a faulty discharge temperature sensor (Jasmine's main check points)
  • A faulty outdoor control board, the last step on Gree's chart

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before opening panels.

  1. Write down the code and when it appears (right after startup, or after a long run on a hot day). That helps a technician tell a wiring fault from an overheating problem.
  2. Look at the outdoor unit: clear leaves, grass, snow or stored items from around it, and make sure nothing blocks the air coming out of the fan.
  3. If the outdoor coil is visibly dirty, turn the power off at the disconnect and gently rinse it with a garden hose from the inside out where you can reach. Don't use a pressure washer.
  4. Clean the indoor filters too, since an overloaded system runs the compressor harder.
  5. Turn the unit off at the breaker and leave it off for 20 to 30 minutes so the compressor and its protector can cool, then restore power. Gree's charts start with the power off for that long.
  6. If H3 keeps coming back, stop resetting it and call a licensed HVAC technician. The checks inside the outdoor unit involve live electrical parts and refrigerant.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • With the unit powered off for 20 to 30 minutes, confirm the overload protector SAT is connected to the outdoor board as shown on the wiring diagram.
  • At ambient temperature, measure the overload protector's resistance with an ohmmeter. Gree's chart passes it under 1000 ohms (the error list notes it is normally under 1 ohm); otherwise replace the overload protector.
  • Check that the electronic expansion valve's FA wiring terminal is connected well, then check the EKV coil: the resistance between each of the first four pins near the terminal hole and the fifth pin should be about the same and under 100 ohms. Replace the coil if it isn't.
  • Check for a refrigerant leak and, if needed, repair it and charge refrigerant to the nameplate specification.
  • Check the discharge temperature sensor, heat exchange at both coils and whether the system is badly overloaded, per Gree's main detection points.
  • If all of that checks out and H3 remains, Gree's chart ends with replacing the outdoor control board.
