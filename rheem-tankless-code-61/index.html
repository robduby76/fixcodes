Rheem Tankless Water Heater Code 61: Blower Fan Speed Problem (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 6 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Code 61 on a Rheem tankless water heater means the combustion blower (the fan that pushes air through the burner) isn't running at the right speed, or the control board isn't getting a correct speed signal from it. Common causes are a loose fan connector, a dirty or dragging blower, a restricted vent, or a failed blower motor or control board. Rheem's owner manual lists it as a faulty blower motor and says to contact a dealer or qualified technician.

What the code means

Rheem's High Efficiency Tankless Water Heater with Recirculation Troubleshooting Manual (AP24838) calls 61 "Blower motor speed incorrect for combustion": the heater checks the fan's RPM signal after the fan starts, and if that signal is wrong it shows 61. Rheem's SVC 820 pocket troubleshooting manual words it as "The Blower motor speed was not appropriate to allow proper combustion." The older SVC 810 training manual calls it "Combustion Fan Failure" (fan motor does not work properly), and Rheem's tankless Use and Care Manual lists "61 or 99" as "Faulty blower motor. Contact a dealer or qualified service technician." Rheem Australia's continuous-flow list gives the same idea: abnormal fan speed or fan failure.

Likely causes

  • A loose or poorly seated connector between the blower motor and the control board (Rheem's first check, and sometimes the whole fix)
  • Dust or debris in the blower motor or blower housing, or a fan that doesn't turn freely
  • Venting that is too long, has too many bends, or is blocked at the intake or exhaust, so the fan can't move enough air
  • Damaged or loose wiring in the harness to the fan motor
  • A failed blower motor
  • A failed control board that isn't powering or reading the fan correctly

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Do Rheem's reset: turn off all hot water faucets, turn the heater off with the POWER ON/OFF button, wait about 5 minutes, turn it back on, then open a hot faucet and check the display. If the code is still there, turn the faucet and heater off, unplug the heater for 30 seconds, plug it back in and repeat.
  2. Go outside (or to the vent terminations) and check that the air intake and exhaust aren't blocked by snow, leaves, nests, lint or anything stacked against them. Clear anything you find.
  3. If the heater was recently installed or the vent was changed, ask the installer to confirm the vent run is within the maximum length and number of elbows in your model's manual; Rheem's older service manual lists over-length venting as a code 61 check.
  4. While hot water is running, you can feel for airflow at the exhaust termination; Rheem's service manual asks technicians to confirm some air is coming out of the flue.
  5. Don't open the heater's cover or probe the fan connector. Rheem's own procedure involves live DC voltages up to about 195 volts at the blower connector and warns that touching the wrong two wires can damage the blower motor and control board, so leave it to a technician if the reset doesn't clear 61.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Turning power off, removing and reinserting the blower connector at the control board and at the motor, then running the unit again (Rheem says if 61 doesn't return, it was a loose connection).
  • Removing the blower motor, cleaning the motor and its housing, checking it turns freely, and reassembling.
  • Measuring the blower motor's supply, control and speed-signal voltages at the control board connector per the model's troubleshooting manual (for example, about 145 to 195 DC volts supply, 12 to 18 DC volts control and a 4 to 10 DC volt pulsing speed signal on the AP24838 models), using Rheem's warning about which wire pairs never to bridge.
  • Replacing the control board or blower motor according to which reading fails, then checking gas supply and venting if both test good.
  • Inspecting the fan motor wiring harness for damaged or loose terminals and checking venting length and bends against the installation manual.
