Code: 10🚿 Rinnai Tankless Water Heater

Rinnai Tankless Error Code 10: Air Supply or Exhaust Blockage (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Code 10 on a Rinnai tankless water heater means Air Supply or Exhaust Blockage: the heater can't move enough combustion air in or exhaust out, so it shuts down. The quick homeowner checks are to clear anything blocking the vent termination outside and, on condensing models, make sure the condensate drain line isn't clogged or frozen and clean the inlet air filter. Rinnai marks most other code 10 remedies (vent joints, vent length, DIP switches, fan) as licensed professional only.

What the code means

Rinnai's V53De and VA Indoor Installation and Operation Manuals both define code 10 as Air Supply or Exhaust Blockage. Rinnai's Troubleshooting Tankless Water Heater Diagnostic Codes manual, which applies the code to SENSEI and non-SENSEI models, explains the most common triggers. On condensing models, a clogged or frozen condensate drain line backs up into the condensate trap inside the heater, and the heater shuts off to prevent spillage and sets code 10. A dirty inlet air filter (under the front cover, top right of the cabinet) restricts air. In the vent system, Rinnai lists vent sections that aren't fully seated or weren't joined with the vent supplier's lubricant, too long an equivalent vent length, a vent pipe crushed by spray foam where it passes through the wall, water, condensation or insects inside the pipe, and vent terminals too close to other terminals, overhangs, grade, walls or porches. Inside the heater, Rinnai names a blocked fan wheel or air duct, a stuck check valve behind the fan housing, debris in the burner chamber, build-up between the heat exchanger fins, a wrong high-altitude setting, wrong DIP switch settings, and on some models incorrect manifold gas pressures. A defective fan motor or fan bearing, fan wiring harness or PC board can also cause it. Like other Rinnai codes, it stops flashing once the problem is corrected and the heater supplies hot water.

Likely causes

  • Snow, leaves, a nest or other debris blocking the vent termination or air intake outside
  • On condensing models, a clogged or frozen condensate drain line backing up into the heater's condensate trap
  • A dirty inlet air filter under the front cover (condensing models)
  • Vent pipe sections not fully seated, joints assembled without the vent supplier's lubricant, or venting materials Rinnai doesn't approve for the model
  • A vent run longer than the allowed equivalent length (Rinnai counts a concentric 90-degree elbow as six feet and a 45-degree elbow as three feet)
  • Spray foam around the wall penetration that collapsed the vent pipe
  • Water, condensation or insects inside the vent pipe
  • Vent terminals too close to another termination, an overhang, grade, a wall or a porch, so exhaust gets pulled back in
  • A blocked fan wheel, housing or air duct, or a stuck check valve behind the fan housing
  • Debris in the burner chamber or build-up between the heat exchanger fins
  • Wrong DIP switch, vent-length or high-altitude settings, or incorrect manifold gas pressures on models with a burner sensor
  • A defective fan motor or fan bearing, fan wiring harness, or PC board

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Go outside and look at the vent termination and air intake. Clear snow, ice, leaves, nests or anything else blocking them, without taking the vent apart.
  2. On a condensing model, find the condensate drain line and check that it isn't kinked, clogged or frozen. Rinnai says a backed-up condensate line is enough to set code 10.
  3. On a condensing model, turn off power, open the front cover and clean the inlet air filter in the top right of the cabinet. Your manual shows how to remove it.
  4. If the heater was just installed, moved, or had vent or wall work done (including spray foam), ask the installer to recheck the vent joints, vent length and terminal clearances.
  5. Close the hot water tap, then open it again so the heater tries another cycle. Rinnai says the code stops flashing once the problem is fixed and the heater supplies hot water.
  6. Have the vent system inspected at least once a year, and keep the fan and motor free of dust, which Rinnai's VA manual lists as routine maintenance.
  7. If 10 keeps coming back, stop retrying and call a licensed professional. Rinnai marks the remaining code 10 remedies as licensed professional only. If you smell gas or exhaust at any point, leave the house and call your gas company from outside.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Code 10 returns after the vent termination, condensate line and air filter check out.
  • Running Rinnai's test of removing the exhaust section from the top of the heater (only while it's coding out, and reconnecting immediately) to tell a vent restriction from a fault inside the heater.
  • Marking and pulling apart each vent joint to check insertion depth, reassembling with the vent supplier's lubricant, and confirming the equivalent vent length and approved materials for the model.
  • Looking down the wall penetration for a crushed pipe and checking terminal clearances from other vents, overhangs, grade and porches.
  • With power off, removing the fan motor to inspect the fan wheel, housing, air duct and the check valve behind the housing.
  • Checking DIP switch, vent-length and high-altitude settings and, on models with a burner sensor, low and high fire manifold gas pressures.
  • Removing the heat exchanger and burner to clear debris and blowing out the heat exchanger fins with 120 psi compressed air, or replacing the heat exchanger if it can't be cleaned. Rinnai's steps differ by model on the burner: one procedure says never to blow out the burner with compressed air because it can damage it, so follow the steps for your exact model.
  • Replacing a defective fan motor, fan harness or PC board. If the heater is under warranty, contact Rinnai or a Rinnai-authorized professional instead of opening it yourself.
