Code: 11🚿 Takagi Tankless Water Heater

Takagi Error Code 11: Ignition Failure on the 110 (T-KJr2) and 310 (T-K4)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error code 11 on a Takagi tankless water heater means ignition failure: the heater tried to light three times and never fired. It's the code the 110 (T-KJr2) and 310 (T-K4) show on the remote controller, and the red light on their computer board flashes three times for it. Start by resetting the hi-limit switch, then make sure the gas is on, the gas line isn't full of air, and the vent isn't blocked. If those check out, the flame sensor, igniter or gas valves are next.

What the code means

Takagi's troubleshooting guide for the 110 (T-KJr2), 310 (T-K4) and 510 (T-D2) lists code 11 as Ignition Failure: the heater does not fire after three failed attempts at ignition. On the 110 and 310 the remote controller shows 11 and the red LED on the computer board flashes three times. The 510 and the newer models show the same problem as code 111. Code 12 is the related flame loss code, where the flame went out while the heater was still supposed to be running; it also shows as three flashes on the 110 and 310, so if you only have the red light to go by, a remote controller tells you which of the two you have.

Likely causes

  • A tripped hi-limit switch
  • Not enough gas reaching the heater, or debris or air in the gas line
  • A faulty pressure regulator at the gas meter
  • On propane units, a cold regulator during colder parts of the day
  • Gas supply pressure out of spec in standby or while the heater runs
  • A disconnected clear plastic tube between the proportional gas valve and the combustion chamber
  • A blown overheat cutoff fuse (OHCF)
  • A dirty flame sensor (the flame comes on for only 1 to 2 seconds, then goes out)
  • A weak or missing igniter spark from loose igniter wires or a failed igniter
  • A blocked vent (bird nests, animals, trash), or a computer board or gas solenoid fault

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Reset the hi-limit switch: turn the power to the heater off, push the white button in until you hear and feel it click, then turn the power back on and test. If the heater fires, Takagi says the cause of the trip still has to be found, so call its Technical Service Department or a technician.
  2. Make sure the gas valve to the heater is fully open and, on propane, that the tank isn't empty. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas company from outside.
  3. If the heater was just installed or the gas was recently off, air in the gas line is a common cause; Takagi's guide covers purging it, and a technician can do it quickly if you're not comfortable working on gas fittings.
  4. Check the outdoor vent termination for bird nests, animals, trash, snow or ice.
  5. Watch through the sight glass next to the igniter while the heater tries to light. Takagi says a strong blue spark stays steady and in place, while a weak spark jumps around. Note what you see for the technician.
  6. If the flame comes on for only 1 to 2 seconds before going out, the flame sensor likely needs cleaning, which Takagi's guide covers as part of cleaning the burner and rod assembly.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Have a technician check the inlet gas pressure with a manometer, both in standby and with the highest-flow fixtures open, against the specs for your model, and check the meter or propane regulator.
  • Have the clear plastic tube from the proportional gas valve to the combustion chamber checked and reattached if it's off.
  • Have the overheat cutoff fuse (OHCF) checked for continuity; Takagi asks that you contact its Technical Service Department if it has blown.
  • Have the igniter wire connections checked, and the igniter replaced if the spark is weak or missing and the wires are secure.
  • If none of that finds it, Takagi points to a computer board or gas solenoid fault and says to contact its Technical Service Department.
