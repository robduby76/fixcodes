Code: C2🔥 Lennox Gas Furnace

Lennox Furnace C2: Second-Stage Cooling, Not an Error (Meaning and What to Check)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

C2 on a Lennox furnace or air handler display means the system is running second-stage (high) cooling. It's a status readout, not a fault. Lennox shows the C, then the stage number (1 or 2), then the blower airflow setting, and repeats. Real Lennox faults show as an E followed by three digits, such as E201 or E228.

What the code means

Lennox's service literature for its SureLight communicating-enabled furnaces (for example the SL280UHNV) lists the display codes in its Integrated Diagnostic Codes/Status of Equipment table. There, C means cooling stage: the C shows (1 second on, 0.5 second off), then 1 or 2 for the stage, a pause, then the cfm (airflow) setting for the blower, and the sequence repeats. So C2 means the thermostat is calling for second-stage cooling and the furnace blower is moving air for it. Lennox air handlers use the same idea: their installation instructions say C1 or C2 is shown, followed by the cfm setting, when the air handler is paired with a non-communicating outdoor unit, while a communicating iComfort outdoor unit shows a percentage instead, such as C70 or C100. Other letters are status too: a blinking decimal point is idle, A is the airflow setting, H is gas heat (blinking during ignition), h is heat pump, d is dehumidification, dF is defrost and U is discharge air temperature. A number after A, like A1375, is the airflow in cfm, not a fault code.

Likely causes

  • Nothing is wrong: the thermostat is calling for second-stage (high-capacity) cooling, which is when C2 is supposed to show
  • A two-stage thermostat that moves to stage 2 when the house is well above the setpoint or after stage 1 has run a while
  • If C2 shows but the house isn't cooling, the indoor side is getting the call; the problem is usually the outdoor unit, its power, or airflow, not the furnace display itself
  • A dirty air filter or blocked return grilles, which cut airflow and cooling even when the display looks normal
  • An outdoor unit with its breaker or service disconnect off, so the blower runs indoors but nothing is cooling the air

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Read the full sequence on the display. C then 1 or 2 then a cfm number is normal cooling. An E with three digits (for example E201, E228, E312) is a real fault; look that code up instead.
  2. If the house is cooling, leave it alone. C2 will drop back to C1 or the idle decimal point when the thermostat is satisfied.
  3. If air is blowing but it isn't cold, go outside and check whether the outdoor unit's fan and compressor are running. If not, check its breaker in the main panel and the disconnect box next to the unit.
  4. Replace the furnace air filter and make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked or closed.
  5. Check the outdoor coil for leaves, grass clippings or debris and clear a couple of feet around it. Turn power off at the disconnect before touching anything near the unit.
  6. Check the thermostat is set to Cool with a setpoint below room temperature, and replace its batteries if it uses them.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The display shows C2 and the blower runs, but the outdoor unit won't start even with its breaker and disconnect on. That points to parts like a capacitor or contactor, which are high-voltage work.
  • The outdoor unit runs but the air isn't cool, or you see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil. That can mean low refrigerant or a coil problem, which needs a licensed technician.
  • The display shows an E-code fault, or it's blank with power on.
  • Changing the furnace's airflow or staging configuration with the control's push button. Lennox writes those procedures for the installer.
