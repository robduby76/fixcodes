Code: 022🚿 AO Smith Electric Water Heater

AO Smith Water Heater Error Code 022 (22): Lower Element Circuit Failure (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error 022 (often searched as code 22) on an A. O. Smith electric or hybrid heat pump water heater with the electronic control display means the lower heating element circuit failed. The upper element still works, so the heater keeps running in limp mode with less hot water. A. O. Smith's steps are to turn off the breaker, check the element circuit for 5 to 25 ohms and replace the element if it's out of range, check the wires at the elements, thermostat and control board for damage, then turn the power back on.

What the code means

In A. O. Smith's Residential Electronic Thermostat Electric Water Heater Use and Care Guide, diagnostic code 022 with a flashing alert icon, flashing red LED and beeper is listed as Lower element circuit failure, with the notice that the upper element is still operable. The Residential Hybrid Electric Heat Pump Water Heater Service Handbook (HPTS, HPTA and HPTV models and their HPS10, HPA10 and HPV10 retail versions) lists the same code and the same steps. When the control decides the lower element (or, on the electric model, the lower thermistor) has failed, it cancels the lower element's call for heat and goes into limp mode: on the electric model the upper element keeps heating the water, and on the hybrid the upper element covers the lower element's call for heat (A. O. Smith calls this Limp Mode 2). The heater stays in limp mode until the failure is fixed, which is why you'll usually notice hot water running out sooner. Its twin code is 021, upper element circuit failure, where the lower element keeps working instead.

Likely causes

  • A burned-out lower heating element. A. O. Smith's guide notes that the lower element does most of the work, so it usually wears out before the upper one
  • A grounded or shorted lower element, which A. O. Smith's element test checks for by measuring from each terminal to the element flange or tank shell
  • Damaged, burned or loose wires at the lower element, the thermostat or the control assembly board, which A. O. Smith's 022 steps say to inspect
  • On the electric model, a failed lower thermistor (temperature sensor), which A. O. Smith's limp mode description lists alongside a failed lower element

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Turn off the water heater's breaker and confirm the power is off with a non-contact circuit tester. A. O. Smith warns that working near an energized circuit can cause severe injury or death from electrical shock.
  2. Remove the lower access panel and insulation so you can reach the lower element's terminals. Look for burned, melted or loose wires there and at the thermostat and control board connections, as A. O. Smith's 022 steps say.
  3. With the power off, disconnect one wire from the lower element and measure across the two element terminals with a multimeter. A. O. Smith's code chart says the element circuit should read 5 to 25 ohms; an open reading or one far outside that range means the element needs replacing.
  4. Also check for a grounded element: put one probe on an element terminal and the other on the element flange or tank shell. A. O. Smith's handbook says any continuity means the element is grounded and should be replaced.
  5. To replace the element, A. O. Smith's procedure is to turn off the power and the cold water supply, run a hot faucet until the water is cool, drain the tank with a garden hose, remove the power wires and unscrew the old element with an element wrench. Fit a new element that matches the voltage and wattage on the heater's data plate, with a new rubber gasket lightly lubricated with a drop of dish soap.
  6. Refill the tank completely and open a hot water faucet until all air is out before turning the power back on. A. O. Smith says the tank must be completely full of water first, since powering an empty tank can burn out the upper element (dry firing).
  7. Turn the breaker back on. If the wiring and element check out and 022 still shows, A. O. Smith's next step is to contact a qualified person. On the hybrid models, the service handbook says you can clear a code from the display temporarily by pressing the Up and Down temperature buttons together until it shows CLR, but it will come back until the fault is fixed.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • You're not comfortable testing 240 V wiring or draining and refilling the tank. A. O. Smith's chart notes these steps are intended for a qualified person and points you to the Technical Assistance Hotline on the warranty sheet.
  • The new element tests fine and the wiring looks good but 022 comes back. The lower thermistor or the control assembly may need checking.
  • You see burned or melted wiring, scorched terminals or water leaking at the element gasket after a replacement.
  • The heater is under warranty. Check with A. O. Smith or the installer before replacing parts yourself.
