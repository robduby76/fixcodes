Code: E3🧺 Hisense Dishwasher

Hisense Dishwasher E3 Error Code: Water Not Heating, or Not Draining (Depends on Model)

Reviewed 2026-10-30 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

E3 on a Hisense dishwasher has two different meanings depending on the model. On many Hisense dishwashers (for example HSCE14FS, HV543D10 and HV523E15UK) E3 means the water didn't reach the required temperature, and Hisense names a malfunctioning heating element as the cause. On other models (for example HS520E40WUK) E3 means a water drainage malfunction: the dishwasher couldn't pump the water out. Check the error code table in your own manual first, then follow the heating or the drain checks below.

What the code means

Hisense uses two different dishwasher error code tables, so the table in your own manual is the final word. In the Hisense HSCE14FS freestanding dishwasher manual, E3 is "Not reaching required temperature" with the possible cause "Malfunction of heating element." On that table E1 is longer inlet time, E4 is overflow, E9 is a button held for more than 30 seconds and Ed is a communication failure between the main board and the display board. The HV523E15UK manual uses the same E3 meaning, and the HV543D10 manual describes E3 as a heating element malfunction. The Hisense HS520E40WUK manual uses a different table: E1 door open, E2 water inlet, E3 water drainage malfunction, E4 temperature sensor, E5 overflow or leakage, E6 water leakage and E7 heating element. A quick way to tell which table your machine uses: if your manual lists E2 as a water inlet fault and E7 as the heating element, your E3 is the drain code.

Likely causes

  • On "not reaching temperature" models: the heating element has failed (the cause Hisense names in the manual)
  • On "not reaching temperature" models: a faulty temperature sensor, heater wiring or control board stopping the element from heating (likely causes for a technician to check, not named in the user manual)
  • On "drainage" models: a clogged filter in the base of the tub
  • On "drainage" models: a kinked, squashed or blocked drain hose, or a blocked sink drain or waste connection
  • On "drainage" models: a blocked or failed drain pump (a likely cause once the filter and hose check out, not named in the user manual)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Look up E3 in the error code table of your dishwasher's manual (search your model number on the Hisense site or a manual site) so you know which meaning applies.
  2. For any E3: switch the dishwasher off at the power button, unplug it or switch it off at the socket for a minute, then run a short programme to see if the code comes back.
  3. For a drainage E3: with the dishwasher off, lift out the filters from the base of the tub (rotate the coarse filter anticlockwise to unlock it), rinse them under running water with a soft brush, and refit them firmly. Hisense says never to run the dishwasher without the filters in place.
  4. For a drainage E3: check the drain hose behind the machine isn't kinked or squashed, and that the sink or waste pipe it drains into isn't blocked. If your kitchen sink drains slowly, that needs clearing first.
  5. For a drainage E3: check the drain hose height. Hisense manuals say the waste connection must be no more than 100 cm (1000 mm) above the bottom of the dishwasher, and an extension hose must be no longer than 4 metres.
  6. For a heating E3: make sure the dishwasher is on its own properly rated circuit, as the manual asks, and try a different programme. If the code keeps coming back on every wash, the heating element or its circuit needs a technician.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E3 on a "not reaching temperature" model keeps coming back: the heating element, temperature sensor or control board needs testing with a meter, which means working on live mains parts.
  • E3 on a "drainage" model keeps coming back with clean filters and a clear hose: the drain pump likely needs checking or replacing.
  • There's water on the floor or in the base of the machine. Hisense says to turn off the main water supply before calling for service and remove any water in the base pan before restarting.
  • The dishwasher is under warranty: contact Hisense customer service rather than taking it apart.
