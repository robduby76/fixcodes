Carrier Furnace Code 24: Secondary Voltage (24V) Fuse Is Open (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-21 · Based on 5 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Status code 24 on a Carrier furnace (2 short flashes, then 4 long flashes) means the 3-amp fuse protecting the 24-volt control circuit has blown, almost always because something in the low-voltage wiring shorted. Replacing the fuse alone won't hold if the short is still there.

What the code means

Carrier's troubleshooting guides define status code 24 as 'Secondary voltage fuse is open', indicating the fuse is open and there is a short in the low-voltage (24 VAC) wiring. Carrier status codes are two-digit numbers: the first digit is the number of short flashes and the second is the number of long flashes, so code 24 is 2 short flashes followed by 4 long flashes on the control board LED. The 24-volt circuit is protected by a 3-amp automotive blade-type fuse on the furnace control; any short in the 24-volt wiring during installation, service, or maintenance can blow it. With the fuse open, the thermostat circuit is dead and the furnace will not heat or cool until the short is found and the fuse replaced.

Likely causes

  • Thermostat wire shorted: staples, sharp sheet-metal edges, rodent damage, or bare wire ends touching at the thermostat or furnace terminal strip
  • Thermostat being replaced or rewired with power on, letting R touch C or another terminal
  • Outdoor AC/heat pump low-voltage wire (Y and C) chafed or shorted outside or at the condensing unit
  • Accessories on the 24V circuit shorting: humidifier (HUM terminal), zone controls, UV lights, or condensate pumps
  • A shorted wire to ground in the furnace's own safety circuits: the limit / flame rollout switch circuit or a pressure switch lead (per Carrier's checkout)
  • A gas valve or gas valve wire shorted to ground
  • Less commonly, a failed furnace control board (Carrier's chart replaces the control only when the fuse tests good but code 24 still shows)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Confirm the code: look through the blower door sight glass and count 2 short flashes then 4 long flashes. Write it down before cutting power.
  2. Power OFF at the furnace service switch and breaker, then remove the blower access panel.
  3. Find the 3-amp blade fuse on the control board and check it: a visibly broken element, or no continuity with a multimeter, means it's blown.
  4. Look for the obvious short first: thermostat wires at the furnace terminal strip with stray strands touching, wires pinched by the panel, chewed or stapled cable, and the low-voltage wire at the outdoor unit.
  5. Isolate the short the way Carrier's guide does: disconnect all thermostat leads (and any wire on the HUM terminal) from the control, then install a new fuse of the identical 3-amp rating. Never use a larger fuse.
  6. Restore power with the blower door switch held closed. If code 24 comes back with the thermostat wires disconnected, the short is inside the furnace (limit, pressure switch, or gas valve circuit) — stop here and call a pro.
  7. If the furnace stays normal with the leads off, the short is in the thermostat, outdoor-unit, or accessory wiring. Reconnect one circuit at a time; the one that blows the fuse (or brings back code 24) is the one to repair or replace.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Code 24 returns with all thermostat wiring disconnected, meaning the short is in the furnace's limit, pressure switch, or gas valve circuits.
  • The fuse keeps blowing and you can't find damaged wiring you can safely reach.
  • The short is in buried or in-wall thermostat cable, or the outdoor unit's low-voltage wiring needs replacing.
  • Testing requires continuity checks to chassis ground on control harness wires or jumpering thermostat terminals (Carrier's service checkout).
  • You smell gas — leave the house and call the gas utility. Never jumper or bypass limit, rollout, or pressure switches, and never install a fuse larger than 3 amps.
