Code: P5🔧 Midea Mini Split

Midea Mini Split Error Code P5: Condenser High Temperature Protection or Mode Conflict (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

P5 on a Midea mini split usually means the outdoor coil (the condenser) got too hot, so the system stopped to protect itself: Midea's service manuals trigger it when the outdoor pipe temperature goes above 65 °C (149 °F) and let the unit run again once it drops below 52 °C (about 126 °F). On older Midea multi-zone systems, P5 on an indoor head can instead mean a mode conflict, where one head asks for cooling while another asks for heating. Cleaning around the outdoor unit and matching the modes on every head are fair homeowner steps; sensor, board and refrigerant checks are for a licensed HVAC technician.

What the code means

Midea uses P5 for two different things, so check which unit is showing it. On the outdoor unit's display of Midea multi-zone systems (the Advantage series service manual, section 7.4.2.12) and on Midea's Mini VRF outdoor units, P5 is "High temperature protection of condenser": when the outdoor pipe (condenser coil) temperature is more than 65 °C, the unit stops, and it runs again when that temperature falls below 52 °C. Midea lists a faulty condenser temperature sensor, a dirty heat exchanger and a blocked refrigerant system as the potential causes; the Mini VRF manual also names a blocked refrigerant pipe. On the indoor heads of the same Advantage multi-zone systems, P5 on old models (shown as "- -" on newer ones) means "Indoor units mode conflict": the indoor units can't run cooling and heating at the same time, and heating has priority. If head A is cooling or in fan mode and head B is switched to heating, A turns off and B heats; if A is heating and B is set to cooling or fan, B goes to standby and A keeps going. Midea's single-zone wall-unit tables we checked (Oasis Plus 3D, older wall-mount inverter) don't list a P5, so on a single-zone unit look up your exact model's manual before acting on either meaning.

Likely causes

  • Outdoor coil running too hot because air can't move through it: plants, fences, leaves or items stacked against the outdoor unit, or hot exhaust air being drawn back in (Midea's chart checks ventilation)
  • A dirty or clogged condenser coil (heat exchanger)
  • The outdoor fan not running properly
  • Very high outdoor temperatures: Midea's charts include an outdoor-temperature check (50 °C / 122 °F on the Advantage chart, 43 °C / about 109 °F on the Mini VRF chart), and above that the protection is expected
  • A faulty condenser (T3) temperature sensor or a bad sensor-to-board connection
  • A blocked refrigerant system or pipe, or an incorrect refrigerant charge
  • A faulty outdoor main board
  • On multi-zone indoor heads only: one head set to cooling or fan while another is set to heating (mode conflict)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before opening panels.

  1. Check where the code shows. If it's on one indoor head of a multi-zone system and another head is heating, set every head to the same mode. Midea's manual says heating wins, so the head in cooling or fan mode turns off or goes to standby until the modes match.
  2. For P5 on the outdoor unit, turn the system off and look at the outdoor unit from outside. Clear leaves, grass clippings, plants and anything stacked around it so air can flow freely through the coil.
  3. With power off at the breaker, gently rinse the outdoor coil from the inside out with a garden hose on low pressure, or use a no-rinse coil cleaner made for condensers. Don't use a pressure washer, which bends the fins.
  4. Restore power and run the unit in cooling. Check that the outdoor fan is spinning; if the compressor runs but the fan doesn't, tell the technician.
  5. If P5 shows up only during a heat wave and clears once things cool off, note the times and temperatures. If it keeps coming back after the coil is clean and clear, call a licensed HVAC technician. Don't open the outdoor unit's electrical box.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Check the connection between the condenser temperature sensor (T3) and the outdoor PCB and correct it if loose or damaged, as Midea's chart does first.
  • Compare the actual condenser temperature with 65 °C. If it isn't that hot, measure the sensor's resistance against the temperature/resistance table in the service manual appendix; replace the sensor if it's out of range, otherwise replace the outdoor main board.
  • If the condenser really is above 65 °C: check whether the outdoor ambient temperature is extreme (Midea's Advantage chart checks for above 50 °C, the Mini VRF chart for above 43 °C), then check outdoor ventilation, outdoor fan operation (follow Midea's fan speed out-of-control procedure if needed) and coil cleanliness.
  • Check the refrigerant system. Midea's Advantage chart adds refrigerant if the charge is low; the Mini VRF chart, for a blocked pipe, says to recover the refrigerant, blow the pipe through with high-pressure nitrogen, evacuate and recharge.
  • If everything checks good, replace the outdoor main board.
