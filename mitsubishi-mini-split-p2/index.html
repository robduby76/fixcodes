Mitsubishi Mini Split Error Code P2: Pipe (TH2) Liquid Sensor Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

P2 on a Mitsubishi mini split means the indoor unit's liquid pipe thermistor, called TH2, is reading as shorted or open. The unit pauses for 3 minutes and tries again; if the reading is still bad it stops and shows P2. The usual causes are a loose connector on the indoor controller board, a broken sensor wire, a failed thermistor, a refrigerant circuit problem that pushes the liquid pipe temperature to an extreme, or less often a faulty indoor controller board.

What the code means

Mitsubishi's Mr. Slim (P-series) service manuals list P2 as "Pipe (TH2) sensor error" in the indoor unit check-code table, and the self-diagnosis table calls it an abnormality of the pipe temperature thermistor, liquid (TH2). The board checks this sensor all the time in cooling, drying and heating, except during defrost. A reading of 90 °C (194 °F) or more counts as a short, and -40 °C (-40 °F) or less counts as an open circuit. When either is detected the unit goes into 3-minute resume prevention mode, and P2 is set only if it doesn't return to normal within those 3 minutes; if it resets normally the unit goes back to normal operation. On the IR wireless remote self-check, P2 is 2 blinks or beeps, the same count as P9. On some outdoor boards (for example the PU(Y)(Z)-A NKA7 series) it shows as LED1 4 blinks and LED2 1 blink, shared with P1 and P9. P2 is the liquid pipe sensor; P9 is the condenser/evaporator pipe sensor (TH5), P1 is the room air sensor (TH1), and P8 is a pipe temperature error where the sensors read fine but the coil isn't changing temperature as expected.

Likely causes

  • The liquid pipe thermistor (TH2) has failed, so its resistance no longer matches Mitsubishi's characteristic table.
  • The thermistor connector is loose or not fully seated on the indoor controller board (CN44, the 4-pin white connector shared by TH2 and TH5 on the PLA-A BA4 cassette manual).
  • A broken thermistor wire or poor contact in the wiring.
  • The liquid pipe really is at 90 °C (194 °F) or more, or -40 °C (-40 °F) or less, because of a defective refrigerant circuit.
  • A defective indoor controller board.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

The indoor controller board is reached with the unit's covers off and can carry line voltage. Switch off power at the breaker or disconnect before touching connectors or measuring thermistor resistance. Refrigerant circuit work needs an EPA-certified technician.

  1. Turn the system off at the breaker or disconnect, wait a minute, then turn it back on. Mitsubishi's countermeasure ends with turning the power off and on again, and a one-off glitch may clear.
  2. If P2 comes straight back, note the exact indoor model number from the unit's rating label. The thermistor sits on the indoor unit's liquid refrigerant pipe and its connector is on the indoor controller board, so the checks need covers off and expose live parts.
  3. Clean or replace the air filter if it's dirty. It won't fix a broken sensor, but it rules out an airflow problem making the coil run unusually cold or hot.
  4. Call a Mitsubishi dealer or HVAC technician for the connector, resistance, refrigerant and board checks below.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • With power off, check the TH2 resistance against Mitsubishi's table (TH1, TH2 and TH5 share it): about 15.0 kΩ at 0 °C (32 °F), 9.6 kΩ at 10 °C (50 °F), 6.3 kΩ at 20 °C (68 °F), 4.3 kΩ at 30 °C (86 °F) and 3.0 kΩ at 40 °C (104 °F). Pull or bend the lead wire gently while measuring; Mitsubishi notes this can reveal a broken wire or contact failure.
  • Check for contact failure at CN44 on the indoor controller board. Reseat it, turn the power on and check whether the unit restarts.
  • Operate in test run mode and check the liquid pipe temperature on the remote controller. If it's extremely low in cooling or extremely high in heating, the refrigerant circuit may have a defect.
  • If the displayed liquid pipe temperature is extremely different from the actual pipe temperature and the thermistor and wiring check out, replace the indoor controller board.
  • Turn the power off and on again to operate after the checks.
