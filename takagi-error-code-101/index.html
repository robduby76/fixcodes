Code: 101🚿 Takagi Tankless Water Heater

Takagi Error Code 101: Abnormal Combustion Warning (Before a 991 Shutdown)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error code 101 on a Takagi tankless water heater is a warning that combustion isn't right: the air-fuel mix is running gas rich, usually because the heater isn't getting enough air. The heater keeps making hot water for now, but Takagi says it cuts back the gas input and a shutdown (code 991) is coming if the cause isn't fixed. Common causes are a blocked vent or intake, lint, dust or grease in the heater, bad weather at the vent, undersized gas lines, or DIP switches set wrong for the altitude or installation.

What the code means

Takagi's condensing service handbook describes code 101 as a warning that the air-fuel ratio for combustion is gas rich. The heater continues to operate, but shutdown is imminent: the next step is code 991, which forces the heater off. On the 510 (T-D2) the code shows as 101; on the 110 (T-KJr2) and 310 (T-K4) the remote shows 10 and the red LED on the computer board flashes five times (the same flash count as 991/99). Takagi's troubleshooting guide also notes that a 101 reduces the amount of gas going into the heater, so lukewarm water can be a symptom. Once the cause is found and fixed, the computer board has to be reset to clear the code.

Likely causes

  • A blocked vent or intake: birds' nests, trash, or construction debris in the vent pipe
  • A backflow preventer installed backwards
  • Dirt or a partial blockage in the heat exchanger or fan, so the fan can't push enough air
  • Intake air contaminated with dust, lint (a laundry machine nearby), grease (a kitchen) or other contaminants, fouling the burner
  • Weather at the vent, such as high winds or a frozen vent termination flap
  • An installation around or above 3,000 feet without the high-altitude DIP switch setting
  • DIP switches set wrong for the gas type or installation
  • Undersized gas lines or manifold gas pressure that's out of adjustment
  • Vent terminations too close to each other or to another appliance's exhaust, so flue gas gets pulled back into the intake

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Treat 101 as an early warning: the heater still works, but Takagi says a 991 shutdown follows if nothing is done, so deal with it now rather than waiting.
  2. Go outside and check the vent and intake terminations. Clear nests, leaves, trash, snow or ice, and make sure the termination flap isn't frozen shut. If the code shows up on windy days, note that for your installer.
  3. Check that no other appliance's exhaust (a dryer vent, another water heater or furnace) sits close enough to the heater's intake to be pulled in.
  4. Look at where the heater is installed. If it's near a washer or dryer, in a kitchen, or somewhere dusty (including after drywall work), lint, grease or dust has probably gotten into the fan and burner; Takagi calls for cleaning the burner and fan, which is a job for a technician if you're not comfortable opening the heater.
  5. If you live around or above 3,000 feet, or the heater was just installed, ask the installer whether the altitude and gas type DIP switches were set for your location.
  6. After the cause is fixed, the code has to be cleared by resetting the computer board inside the heater (on condensing models: standby light off, hold INCREASE and DECREASE on the board for five seconds, then power the heater off and back on). This means opening the front cover, so leave it to a technician if you'd rather not.
  7. Make sure you have a working carbon monoxide alarm. A gas-rich flame and blocked venting can push exhaust indoors.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Have a technician check the vent length and layout against the installation manual (Takagi counts each 90-degree elbow as 5 feet of straight pipe) and check for a backwards backflow preventer.
  • Have the fan, burner and heat exchanger air passages inspected and cleaned if there's dust, lint or debris inside the cabinet.
  • Have the inlet gas pressure checked under maximum demand; Takagi says a drop of more than about 1 inch of water column points to undersized gas lines or a supply problem.
  • Manifold gas pressure should only be checked and adjusted by a qualified technician, and Takagi's handbook says to do it only with approval from its Technical Support.
