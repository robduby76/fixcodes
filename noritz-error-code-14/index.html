Code: 14🚿 Noritz Tankless Water Heater

Noritz Error Code 14: Thermal Fuse Tripped by Overheating (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Code 14 on a Noritz tankless water heater means the heat exchanger overheated badly enough to blow the thermal fuse, a one-time safety device that shuts the burner off. Noritz says the most common cause is scale build-up from hard water. The fuse is physically broken once it trips, so the code can't be cleared with a reset: a service technician has to replace the fuse, and Noritz recommends replacing the heat exchanger at the same time.

What the code means

Noritz's Knowledge Base says error code 14 (EC14) indicates a significant overheat of the heat exchanger, and the thermal fuse had to step in and shut the unit down. The thermal fuse is wrapped around the heat exchanger and works like a fuse in a car: it is a one-time-use part that is physically broken once an overheat trips it. Noritz says the most common cause is scale build-up from hard water without water treatment or regular descaling flushes. Scale acts as an insulator, so heat from the burner can't pass into the water, the heat exchanger surface gets hot enough to trip the fuse, and combustion stops immediately. Once EC14 appears it cannot be reset; the fuse has to be replaced, which means removing the heat exchanger and takes about 2 to 4 hours depending on access. Because that is nearly the same work, Noritz highly recommends replacing the heat exchanger at the same time, and warns that putting an old scaled-up heat exchanger back in can bring code 14 back even if you descale right afterward. The Noritz EZ service manual lists 14 as Overheat Prevention Device (Thermal Fuse) triggered, with checkpoints for melting or damage to the thermal fuse and for an improper thermal fuse connection, and says that when the thermal fuse is triggered, resetting the unit won't clear the error and the unit must be serviced before it will work again.

Likely causes

  • Scale build-up inside the heat exchanger from hard water, which Noritz names as the most common cause
  • No water treatment (such as a water softener or Noritz Scale Shield) in a hard water area
  • Skipped or overdue descaling flushes
  • A loose or improper thermal fuse connection, which the Noritz service manual lists as a checkpoint

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Don't try to bypass the thermal fuse. Noritz calls it a critical safety device that protects the unit and your property from an extreme overheat.
  2. Know that a power reset won't clear 14. Noritz says the fuse is physically broken once it trips, so the heater stays off until it's repaired.
  3. Write down your model number from the rating label so the service technician can order the right thermal fuse and heat exchanger.
  4. Plan to prevent a repeat. Noritz says EC14 is a good sign you're in a hard water area; check the water quality and treatment guidelines in your installation and owner's manual, and consider a water softener or a Noritz Scale Shield.
  5. Once the heater is repaired, keep up with regular descaling flushes as your manual describes.
  6. For help, call Noritz technical assistance at 866-766-7489 or use its Contractor Locator to find a local service representative.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Replacing the thermal fuse, which means removing the heat exchanger (Noritz estimates 2 to 4 hours).
  • Replacing the heat exchanger at the same time, as Noritz highly recommends, since a scaled-up exchanger can trip a new fuse again.
  • Checking the thermal fuse for melting or damage and checking its connection, per the Noritz service manual.
  • Checking the water quality and setting up water treatment so scale doesn't build up again.
