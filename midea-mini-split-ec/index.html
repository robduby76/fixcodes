Midea Mini Split Error Code EC: Refrigerant Leakage Detected (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

EC on a Midea mini split means the unit's refrigerant leakage detection has tripped: during cooling, the indoor coil didn't get as cold as it should after the compressor started, so the unit shut itself off. Midea's service manuals list a refrigerant leak or a blockage, a loose or faulty coil sensor (T2), or a faulty indoor board as the causes. A restart is the only safe homeowner step; finding and fixing a leak is a job for a licensed HVAC technician.

What the code means

Midea's error tables list EC as "Refrigerant leakage detection." In the MTIU cassette and duct service manual (section 17.4.6), Midea takes the evaporator coil temperature (T2) at the moment the compressor starts as a baseline. If, within the first 8 minutes after compressor start-up, the coil does not hold at least 2 °C (about 3.6 °F) below that baseline for 4 seconds, and the compressor has not run above 50 Hz for 3 minutes, the unit counts a failure; after 3 of these, it shows EC and switches off. In plain terms, the indoor coil isn't cooling down the way a properly charged system would. On Midea units with indicator lamps instead of a display, the same fault shows as the timer lamp off with the operation lamp flashing 7 times. Newer Midea light commercial manuals label refrigerant leakage detection EL 0C instead (lamp flashing 8 times there, for some models). Don't confuse plain EC with the newer four-character codes that start with EC followed by numbers, such as EC 07 or EC 51; in those manuals the EC prefix marks outdoor-unit faults like fan speed or EEPROM errors, not a refrigerant leak.

Likely causes

  • A refrigerant leak. Low charge means the indoor coil can't get cold enough, which is exactly what EC watches for. Midea's flowchart checks for leaks when no cool air is coming out.
  • A blockage in the refrigerant system. Midea lists blockages alongside leaks and says to check for and clear them if no leak is found.
  • A loose or poorly attached evaporator coil sensor (T2). If the sensor isn't clamped to the coil, it reads warm and makes the unit think the coil isn't cooling.
  • A faulty T2 sensor.
  • A faulty indoor control board (PCB). Midea replaces it when cool air is blowing, the sensor is secure and the error still comes back after a restart.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Refrigerant work requires an EPA-certified technician in the US, and many newer Midea mini splits use R32, which is mildly flammable, so keep flames and sparks away from any suspected leak and ventilate the room. Inverter outdoor units store a dangerous charge in their capacitors even after power is off, so leave anything behind the covers to a licensed HVAC technician.

  1. Do Midea's restart step: turn the unit off at the breaker or disconnect, wait at least 2 minutes (the newer light commercial manual says 15), then restart it in cooling mode.
  2. Hold your hand in front of the indoor unit's air outlet. Whether cool air is coming out is the first question in Midea's flowchart, so tell your technician what you felt.
  3. Clean or replace the indoor unit's air filters and make sure nothing is blocking the indoor or outdoor unit, so you can rule out airflow problems before the service visit.
  4. If EC appears soon after installation, call the installer first, since flare connections and the refrigerant charge are part of their work.
  5. Don't add refrigerant yourself or run the unit repeatedly to clear the code. Refrigerant work requires a certified technician, and many newer Midea units use R32, which is mildly flammable.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Cool air is blowing out of the indoor unit. Midea's technician checks that the T2 coil sensor is securely attached (and, in the newer manual, that the T1 room sensor isn't installed too close to the evaporator), then replaces the indoor board if the error remains after a restart.
  • No cool air is coming out. The technician leak-tests the system; if a leak is found, Midea says to repair it and recharge the refrigerant.
  • No leak is found. The MTIU manual says to check the system for blockages and clear them. The newer light commercial manual instead says to charge the refrigerant appropriately, about 10% of the nameplate charge.
  • The T2 sensor itself tests out of range. A technician replaces it with the part matched to the indoor unit's model number.
