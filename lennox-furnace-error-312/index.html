Code: E312🔥 Lennox Gas Furnace

Lennox Furnace Error 312 (E312): Restricted Airflow, Blower in Cutback Mode (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error 312 on a Lennox furnace is a warning, not a shutdown. It means the variable-speed blower can't move the airflow it was set for during cooling or continuous fan, so it has dropped into a protective cutback mode. Lennox's fix is to check the air filter and the duct system: replace a dirty filter, or have restricted ductwork repaired or added. The code clears on its own after the current call ends.

What the code means

Lennox service literature for its communicating SureLight furnaces (SL280UHV, SL280DFNV and similar) lists E 312 as 'Restricted air flow in cooling or continuous fan mode is lower than cfm setting.' The action column says 'Warning Only': the indoor blower is running at a reduced CFM in cutback mode, because the variable-speed motor has preset speed and torque limiters that protect it from damage when it operates outside its design range of 0 to 0.8 inches of water column total external static pressure. In plain terms, something is choking the airflow, so the motor backs off instead of straining. Lennox's instruction is to check the filter and duct system, replace the filter if needed or repair or add duct, and it notes the code is cleared after the current service demand is satisfied. On a Lennox smart thermostat the same condition appears as alert 312, 'Reduced/Airflow-Indoor Blower Cutback', an information-only dealer alert that says static pressure has exceeded the capability of the blower motor. Its heating-mode sibling is E 311, where the furnace also reduces its firing rate to match the reduced airflow.

Likely causes

  • A dirty or clogged air filter (the first thing Lennox says to check)
  • A filter that's too restrictive for the system, such as a thick high-efficiency filter in a rack or return that wasn't designed for it
  • Closed or blocked supply registers or return grilles, for example furniture or rugs over vents or many rooms shut off
  • Undersized, crushed, kinked or disconnected ductwork that pushes static pressure above the blower's 0 to 0.8 in. w.c. design range (Lennox's fix is to repair or add duct)
  • A dirty or iced-up indoor (evaporator) coil restricting airflow in cooling, a common cause of high static pressure
  • On zoned systems, too little open duct for the zone that's calling; Lennox's zoning guide ties blower cutback alerts such as E312 to excessive blower power and zone airflow settings

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Read the code: look at the seven-segment LED on the furnace control, or the alert on your Lennox thermostat. On SureLight controls you can view the last 10 error codes by holding the diagnostic button until 'E' shows, then releasing it.
  2. Know that E312 is a warning only. Lennox says the furnace keeps running, with the blower at reduced airflow, and the code clears after the current cooling or fan call ends.
  3. Turn the system off at the thermostat and check the air filter. Replace it if it's dirty, and make sure it's the right size and seated correctly with the airflow arrow pointing toward the furnace.
  4. If you recently switched to a thicker or higher-MERV filter and E312 started afterward, try the type the system was installed with.
  5. Walk the house and open supply registers and return grilles, and move anything covering them.
  6. In cooling mode, check the indoor coil area and the refrigerant line for frost or ice. If you see ice, switch cooling off and run the fan only so it thaws, then call a technician, since icing has its own cause.
  7. Look at any duct you can reach in the basement or attic for crushed, kinked or disconnected sections.
  8. Run cooling or the fan again. If E312 keeps coming back on every call with a clean filter and open vents, the duct system needs a technician.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E312 keeps returning with a clean filter and open vents. A technician should measure total external static pressure, since Lennox designs these blowers for 0 to 0.8 in. w.c.
  • The fix needs duct work: Lennox's instruction is to repair or add duct, which usually means resizing returns or supplies.
  • The indoor coil is dirty or icing up, which needs cleaning or a refrigerant-side diagnosis.
  • You have a zoned Lennox system. Zone airflow and blower settings are configured by a dealer.
  • You also see E 311 (heat rate reduced to match airflow) or the furnace overheats or trips limits in heating, which points to the same airflow restriction affecting the furnace.
  • You smell gas: leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
