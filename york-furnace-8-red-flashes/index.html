Code: 8 red flashes🔥 York Gas Furnace

York Furnace 8 Red Flashes: Flame Lost Too Many Times, 1-Hour Lockout (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-21 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Eight red flashes on a York furnace control board mean the burner flame lit but was lost five times (four recycles) during one heating cycle, so the control locked the furnace out for one hour. York's manuals point to low gas pressure, a dirty or faulty flame sensor, or a faulty gas valve. After the hour the furnace tries again on its own; a dirty flame sensor is the most common thing to check first.

What the code means

York (Johnson Controls) furnace boards show faults with an LED behind the view port in the blower door. Fault codes flash on and off the number of times equal to the code, with a 2-second pause between codes, so 8 red flashes is code 8. York's Y91E condensing single-stage installation manual defines 8 red flashes as: the flame has been lost five times (four recycles) during the heating cycle; check for low gas pressure, a dirty or faulty flame sensor, or a faulty gas valve; the furnace locks out for 1 hour and then restarts. The TM9E and TM9V single-stage manuals use the same wording, and the modulating YP9C/CP9C/LP9C/TP9C manual calls it a lockout due to too many flame recycles (flame lost five times during a single heating cycle) with the same three causes. In plain terms, the furnace did light, but the control kept losing proof of flame partway through the cycle. Each time it loses the flame signal it shuts the gas valve and starts over (a recycle); after the fifth loss in one heat call it stops for an hour. That's different from 7 red flashes, where the flame never established in three ignition trials. York lists normal flame sense current at about 3.7 µA DC, a low flame signal warning (rapid amber flash) at 1.5 µA, and a continuous amber flash below 0.35 µA, so a weak flame signal often shows up as amber warnings before it turns into 8 red flashes. Always match the code to the chart on your own furnace door, since York boards differ.

Likely causes

  • A dirty or oxidized flame sensor rod, which weakens the flame signal so the control thinks the flame went out (York lists a dirty or faulty flame sensor)
  • Low gas pressure, for example a partly closed gas shutoff, a low propane tank, or a supply problem, so the flame becomes weak or unstable
  • A faulty gas valve that doesn't hold the flame steady through the cycle
  • A poor ground or loose wiring on the flame sensor circuit, which also weakens the flame signal
  • A burner or flame problem that keeps the flame from contacting the sensor rod reliably

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Confirm the code: count the red flashes through the view port before turning off power, since a power reset clears the current flash. Eight flashes, a 2-second pause, then repeating is code 8. York boards also store the last five codes; with no thermostat call active, press and release the LAST ERROR button to replay them.
  2. Look for clues in the stored codes: rapid or continuous amber flashes alongside code 8 point to a weak flame signal, which usually means the flame sensor.
  3. Make sure gas is reaching the furnace: check that other gas appliances work, that the manual shutoff valve on the furnace gas line is fully open, and on propane, that the tank isn't running low.
  4. If you smell gas, don't touch switches or try to light anything. Leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
  5. If you're comfortable with basic furnace maintenance: turn off power and gas, remove the flame sensor (a single screw on most models), gently clean the metal rod with a fine abrasive pad, not sandpaper that leaves residue, and reinstall it securely. If not, this is a quick job for a technician.
  6. After cleaning the sensor or restoring gas, you can wait for the furnace to retry on its own after the one-hour lockout, or turn the power off and back on to restart it right away.
  7. Don't try to adjust gas pressure or repair the gas valve or control board yourself, and never bypass any safety control. York's manuals say the control board is not field repairable.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Call a technician if 8 red flashes comes back after the flame sensor and gas supply checks.
  • A technician measures flame sense current (York gives about 3.7 µA DC as normal and 0.1 µA as the lockout point), checks the sensor ground and wiring, and measures inlet and manifold gas pressure.
  • If gas pressure is low, they trace it to the supply, regulator or gas valve, and replace the gas valve with the correct part for your model if it's at fault.
  • They also inspect the burners and flame pattern to make sure the flame reaches the sensor properly. If a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.
