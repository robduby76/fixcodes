Code: 991🚿 Takagi Tankless Water Heater

Takagi Error Code 991: Abnormal Combustion (Heater Shut Down)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error code 991 on a Takagi tankless water heater means abnormal (imperfect) combustion, and the heater has shut itself down. Takagi says it's usually caused by not enough intake air or a blocked exhaust, which makes the burner run gas rich. Check the vent and intake for blockages, look for lint, dust or grease in the heater, and make sure the gas line is big enough; then the error has to be cleared with a reset on the computer board.

What the code means

Takagi's service handbook for its condensing models (240, 340 and 540) says code 991 indicates imperfect combustion, which can be caused by insufficient intake air or exhaust obstructions. It's the second stage of code 101: 101 is a warning that the air-fuel ratio is gas rich and the heater keeps running, but if the problem isn't fixed it moves on to 991, which forces the water heater to shut down. Takagi notes undersized gas lines can cause either code. On the 510 (T-D2) and 910 (T-M50) the display shows 991; the 110 (T-KJr2) and 310 (T-K4) show five flashes on the board LED or code 99 on the remote. The condensing models also show five flashes on the board's green LED. Don't confuse it with 391 (air-fuel ratio rod failure) or 111 (ignition failure).

Likely causes

  • A blocked vent or air intake, such as a bird's nest, trash, or a frozen vent termination flap
  • Venting that's too long or the wrong size for the DIP switch settings (Takagi counts each 90-degree elbow as 5 feet of straight pipe)
  • Dust, lint (a dryer nearby), grease (a kitchen) or other contaminants in the intake air, clogging the fan, burner or heat exchanger fins
  • A gas line, gas meter or regulator that's too small for the heater
  • Exhaust from the heater or another appliance being pulled back into the air intake
  • High winds, or an installation above 2,000 feet (condensing models) or around 3,000 feet (older models) without the high-altitude DIP switch setting
  • Manifold gas pressure that's out of adjustment

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Go outside and look at the vent and air intake terminations. Clear any nests, leaves, trash, snow or ice, and make sure the termination flap isn't frozen.
  2. Check whether another appliance's exhaust (a furnace, dryer or another water heater) vents close enough to be pulled into the Takagi's intake, and whether the terminations meet the clearances in the installation manual.
  3. Look at where the heater sits. If it's near a dryer, a kitchen, a workshop or anywhere dusty, lint or grease in the cabinet or fan is a likely cause, and Takagi says the fan and burner should be cleaned.
  4. If the heater was installed recently or a gas appliance was added, ask whether the gas line was sized for it. Takagi says a pressure drop of more than 1 inch W.C. between standby and full firing points to an undersized line, meter or regulator.
  5. After the cause is fixed, clear the code. On condensing models, with the heater powered on, press the on/off button on the remote so the amber standby light is off, hold the INCREASE and DECREASE buttons on the computer board together for five seconds until the green LED goes off, then turn the power off at the switch above the board and back on after 5 seconds. Older models have their own board reset procedure in the troubleshooting guide. If you're not comfortable opening the front cover, leave this to a technician.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Have a technician confirm the venting diameter, length and DIP switch settings (vent length, gas type and altitude) match the installation manual.
  • Have a technician measure gas supply pressure at standby and at maximum firing and confirm the gas line is sized correctly.
  • Have a technician clean the fan, burner and heat exchanger air passages if contaminated air is found inside the cabinet.
  • Manifold gas pressure should only be adjusted by a qualified technician; Takagi says to contact its Technical Support about it, and to adjust only with their approval once venting, DIP switches and blockages have been ruled out.
