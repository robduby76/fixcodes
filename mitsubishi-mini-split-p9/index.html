Mitsubishi Mini Split Error Code P9: Pipe (TH5) Sensor Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

P9 on a Mitsubishi mini split means the indoor unit's condenser/evaporator pipe thermistor, called TH5, is reading as shorted or open. The unit pauses for 3 minutes and tries again; if the reading is still bad it stops and shows P9. The usual causes are a loose connector on the indoor controller board, a broken sensor wire, a failed thermistor, a refrigerant circuit problem that pushes the pipe temperature to an extreme, or less often a faulty indoor controller board.

What the code means

Mitsubishi's Mr. Slim (P-series) service manuals list P9 as "Pipe (TH5) sensor error" in the indoor unit check-code table, and the troubleshooting table calls it an abnormality of the pipe temperature thermistor, condenser-evaporator (TH5). The board checks this sensor all the time in cooling, drying and heating, except during defrost. A reading of 90 °C (194 °F) or more counts as a short, and -40 °C (-40 °F) or less counts as an open circuit. When either is detected the unit goes into 3-minute resume protection mode, and P9 is set only if it doesn't return to normal within those 3 minutes. On the IR wireless remote self-check, P9 is 2 blinks or beeps, the same count as P2. On some outdoor boards (for example the PUY-A NHA7/NKA7 series) it shows as LED1 4 blinks and LED2 1 blink, shared with P1 and P2. P9 is the condenser/evaporator pipe sensor; P2 is the liquid pipe sensor (TH2), P1 is the room air sensor (TH1), and P8 is a pipe temperature error where the sensors read fine but the coil isn't changing temperature as expected.

Likely causes

  • The condenser/evaporator thermistor (TH5) has failed, so its resistance no longer matches Mitsubishi's characteristic table.
  • The thermistor connector is loose or not fully seated on the indoor controller board (CN44, the 4-pin white connector shared by TH2 and TH5 on the PLA-A BA4 cassette manual).
  • A broken thermistor wire or poor contact in the wiring.
  • The pipe really is at 90 °C (194 °F) or more, or -40 °C (-40 °F) or less, because of a defective refrigerant circuit.
  • A defective indoor controller board.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

The indoor controller board is reached with the unit's covers off and can carry line voltage. Switch off power at the breaker or disconnect before touching connectors or measuring thermistor resistance. Refrigerant circuit work needs an EPA-certified technician.

  1. Turn the system off at the breaker or disconnect, wait a minute, then turn it back on. Mitsubishi's countermeasure ends with turning the power off and on again, and a one-off glitch may clear.
  2. If P9 comes straight back, note the exact indoor model number from the unit's rating label. The thermistor sits in a holder on the indoor coil piping and its connector is on the indoor controller board, so the checks need covers off and expose live parts.
  3. Clean or replace the air filter if it's dirty. It won't fix a broken sensor, but it rules out an airflow problem making the coil run unusually cold or hot.
  4. Call a Mitsubishi dealer or HVAC technician for the connector, resistance, refrigerant and board checks below.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • With power off, check the TH5 resistance against Mitsubishi's table (TH1, TH2 and TH5 share it): about 15 kΩ at 0 °C (32 °F), 9.6 kΩ at 10 °C (50 °F), 6.3 kΩ at 20 °C (68 °F), 5.4 kΩ at 25 °C (77 °F), 4.3 kΩ at 30 °C (86 °F) and 3.0 kΩ at 40 °C (104 °F). R0 = 15 kΩ ± 3%, B = 3480 ± 2%.
  • Check for contact failure at CN44 on the indoor controller board. Reseat it, turn the power on and check whether the unit restarts.
  • Operate in test run mode and read the condenser/evaporator pipe temperature on the outdoor controller board (Mitsubishi says to connect the A-Control Service Tool PAC-SK52ST for this). If it's extremely low in cooling or extremely high in heating, the refrigerant circuit may have a defect.
  • If the displayed pipe temperature is very different from the actual pipe temperature and the thermistor and wiring check out, replace the indoor controller board.
  • If none of the above is found, Mitsubishi says there is no abnormality in the unit; turn the power off and on again to operate.
