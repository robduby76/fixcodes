Lennox Furnace Error 250 (E250): Limit Switch Circuit Open (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-10 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error 250 on a Lennox SureLight furnace means the control sees the primary limit switch circuit open. The limit opens when the heat exchanger gets too hot, usually from restricted airflow. If it doesn't close again within 3 minutes, the furnace goes into a 1-hour soft lockout.

What the code means

Lennox service literature for the SL280UHNV defines E 250 as 'Limit switch circuit open'. The seven-segment LED flashes the code one character at a time (E, 2, 5, 0). The primary limit (S10) sits in the heating vestibule panel and opens when excess heat is sensed in the heat exchanger. While it is open, the control runs the supply air blower and closes the gas valve. The limit resets automatically when temperature returns to normal, but if it doesn't close within three minutes the control goes into Watchguard (a 1-hour soft lockout). Lennox says normal operation resumes after the fault is cleared. Its related code E 274 means the furnace has hit the maximum number of recycles because the limit opened or stayed open longer than 3 minutes. The factory-set limit cannot be adjusted.

Likely causes

  • Restricted airflow: a dirty or clogged filter, closed or blocked supply or return registers, or a dirty blower wheel or coil (Lennox says to check for proper air flow)
  • A blockage in the heater, which Lennox lists as a check
  • An incorrect firing rate (gas input too high), which Lennox lists as a check
  • A very restrictive high-efficiency filter; Lennox notes the added pressure drop can make the limit trip more often in winter
  • A weak or failing blower, so heat is not carried away from the heat exchanger
  • A failed or open primary limit switch or loose limit wiring (inferred; Lennox says refer to the parts handbook if the limit must be replaced)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Read the code on the furnace's seven-segment LED through the sight glass, or on a Lennox communicating thermostat.
  2. Turn the thermostat off and let the furnace cool for 30 to 60 minutes. A 1-hour soft lockout clears on its own, and normal operation resumes after the fault is cleared.
  3. Check the air filter and replace it if dirty. If you use a high-efficiency filter, make sure it is properly sized for your system.
  4. Make sure supply registers and return grilles are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
  5. Confirm the blower runs when the furnace calls for heat. If the blower doesn't run at all, turn the power off and call a technician.
  6. Restore power and run a heat cycle. If E250 returns, don't keep resetting the furnace; call a technician.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E250 or E274 returns after the filter is replaced and airflow is clear.
  • The blower doesn't run or runs slowly, or you suspect a failing blower motor.
  • Lennox says to check the furnace firing rate and the heater for blockage; that means gas pressure and combustion work for a licensed technician.
  • The limit switch needs testing or replacing. Never bypass or jumper a limit switch; it is a safety device.
  • You smell gas or suspect a cracked heat exchanger: leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
