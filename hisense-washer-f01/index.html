Code: F01🧺 Hisense Washer

Hisense Washer F01 Error Code: Water Inlet Fault, Washer Not Filling (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-09 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

On Hisense washing machines, F01 is a water inlet fault: the washer isn't getting water, so it stopped the programme. Hisense's manuals ask you to check that the tap is open and the pressure isn't low, then look for a pinched or frozen inlet hose and a clogged inlet filter, and restart. If F01 comes back after that, the inlet valve or its wiring needs a technician.

What the code means

Hisense's user manual for the HWF5I9014 front-loader lists F01 as "Water inlet fault" and tells you to check whether the water tap is open and the water pressure isn't too low, whether the water inlet hose is pinched, whether the water inlet hose filter is clogged, and whether the water inlet hose is frozen. After troubleshooting, press Start/Pause and then press Confirm Start within 60 seconds to continue. Hisense's WF1I7022BWJ2 manual words the same code as "Water inflow fault" with the same four checks, and after troubleshooting you press Start/Pause to continue. A Hisense WF1Q9041BW manual also lists F01 as a water inflow fault with the same checks and has you press the start button again once the cause is removed. Hisense manuals group the codes this way: F01 is a water inlet fault, F03 drainage, F13 door lock, F14 door unlock and F24 overflow, and the HWF5I9014 manual treats other codes as an electronic module fault. If your washer fills but the water won't leave, that's F03, not F01. If the drum won't take water at all, F01 is the code to expect. Hisense's manuals also say the inlet water pressure must be between 0.1 and 1 MPa (1 to 10 bar), and that the inlet filter should be cleaned regularly, and right away if the water supply is slow.

Likely causes

  • A water tap that's closed or only partly open, or low water pressure at the supply (Hisense HWF5I9014 manual: check the tap is open and the pressure isn't too low)
  • A pinched, kinked or squashed water inlet hose (listed in all the Hisense manuals)
  • A clogged inlet filter or inlet hose filter, often from sediment or limescale (Hisense manuals: clean the inlet filter regularly, immediately if the water supply is slow)
  • A frozen inlet hose in a cold garage, shed or unheated room (listed in all the Hisense manuals)
  • A failed water inlet valve or loose valve wiring (general cause: the manuals send you to service if the checks above don't clear F01)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Check that the water tap behind the washer is fully open. Hisense lists the tap and low water pressure as the first things to check.
  2. Look along the inlet hose from the tap to the back of the machine. Straighten any kink or pinch, and make sure it isn't squashed behind the washer.
  3. Clean the inlet filter. Turn off the tap, unplug the washer, put a towel and shallow container under the connection, unscrew the inlet hose from the back of the machine, pull out the mesh filter and rinse off sediment and limescale. Refit the filter, screw the hose back on and turn the tap on to check the connections for leaks. Hisense advises doing this regularly, and right away if the water supply is slow.
  4. If the washer sits somewhere that can freeze, warm the room and let the hose thaw before you try again.
  5. Plug the machine in and, on the HWF5I9014, press Start/Pause and then Confirm Start within 60 seconds to continue. On other Hisense models, press Start/Pause to continue.
  6. Check the tap runs normally by running it into a bucket. If other taps in the home are weak, the problem is the supply, not the washer.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • F01 comes back after the tap, hose and inlet filter are clear and the machine is restarted. Hisense's manuals send you to a service technician when the checks don't clear it.
  • The tap and hose are fine and water still doesn't enter the drum, or the machine makes no filling sound at all, which can point to the inlet valve or its wiring.
  • Water leaks from the inlet connection or valve, or the inlet hose is damaged.
  • The machine is under warranty. Let Hisense service open it so you don't void the cover.
