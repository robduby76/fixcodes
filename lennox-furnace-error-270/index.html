Code: E270🔥 Lennox Gas Furnace

Lennox Furnace Error 270 (E270): Soft Lockout, No Flame Current Sensed (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-11 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error 270 on a Lennox SureLight furnace is a soft lockout. The control tried to light the burner the maximum number of times and never sensed flame current. Lennox says to check gas flow, confirm the ignitor lights the burner, and check flame sensor current. It clears when a heat call finishes successfully.

What the code means

Lennox service literature for the SL280UHNV and unit information for the EL297UHE define E270 as 'Soft lockout - Exceeded maximum number of retries. No flame current sensed.' In plain terms, the furnace went through its ignition attempts and the control never detected a flame signal, so it shut the gas off and locked out. A soft lockout is not permanent: Lennox says the code clears when a heat call finishes successfully. The seven-segment LED flashes the code one character at a time (E, 2, 7, 0). Lennox's flame sensor guidance says flame is sensed by a small current passed through the flame and the sensing electrode, and the SL280UHNV table lists a normal flame signal of 2.6 microamps or greater, low at 2.5 or less, and drop-out at 1.1. Do not confuse E270 with E240 (low flame current during run mode) or E271 (retries exceeded because the low pressure switch opened); those are separate codes with different causes.

Likely causes

  • No gas reaching the burner, such as a closed gas valve, an empty propane tank or an interrupted supply (Lennox says to check for proper gas flow)
  • A hot surface ignitor that is not lighting the burner (Lennox says to ensure the ignitor is lighting the burner)
  • A dirty, coated or failing flame sensor that can't prove flame even when the burner lights (Lennox says to check flame sensor current)
  • Poor furnace grounding or a weak flame signal path (Lennox lists a neutral-to-ground voltage check under the related low flame current code E240)
  • A gas valve or control fault that keeps gas from flowing during the trial for ignition (inferred; needs a technician to confirm)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Read the code on the furnace's seven-segment LED through the sight glass, or on a Lennox communicating thermostat.
  2. Turn the thermostat off for a minute or two, then call for heat again. A soft lockout clears when a heat call finishes successfully, so one good cycle resets it.
  3. Check that the gas valve on the supply line is open (handle in line with the pipe) and, if you use propane, that the tank isn't empty.
  4. Look through the sight glass during a heat call. If the ignitor glows and the burner lights briefly and then shuts off, the flame sensor is the likely culprit. If the ignitor never glows or the burner never lights, note that for the technician.
  5. Check the air filter and make sure supply and return registers are open. This won't fix E270 directly but avoids stacking other faults on top of it.
  6. If E270 comes back after one or two more heat calls, stop resetting the furnace and call a technician.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E270 returns after you confirm the gas valve is open and gas is available.
  • The ignitor doesn't glow or doesn't light the burner; testing ignitor resistance and wiring is work for a technician.
  • The flame sensor needs cleaning or replacement and its microamp reading needs checking (Lennox's table shows low at 2.5 microamps or less).
  • You suspect a gas valve, gas pressure or grounding problem. Lennox lists gas flow and ground checks, which require gauges and a multimeter.
  • You smell gas: leave the house right away and call your gas utility from outside. Don't touch switches or use a phone indoors.
