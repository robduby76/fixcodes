Code: 2 red flashes🔥 York Gas Furnace

York Furnace 2 Red Flashes: Pressure Switch Stuck Closed (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-25 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Two red flashes on a York furnace control board mean the pressure switch is closed when it should be open. At the start of every heat cycle, before the inducer motor runs, the board checks that the pressure switch contacts are open. If they're already closed, the board won't let ignition continue. York's manuals say to check for a faulty pressure switch or miswiring, and the door label on TM9E furnaces sums it up as "pressure switch stuck closed." Unlike 3 red flashes, this usually isn't a vent blockage; it's the switch or its wiring.

What the code means

York (Johnson Controls) furnace boards show faults with an LED behind the view port in the blower door. Fault codes flash on and off the number of times equal to the code (1/3 second on, 1/3 second off), with a 2-second pause between codes, so 2 red flashes is code 2. York's Y91E and TM9E installation manuals define it as: the pressure switch is closed when it should be open. The control confirms that the pressure switch contacts are open at the beginning of each heat cycle and doesn't let the ignition sequence continue if they're closed. The fix York lists is to check for a faulty pressure switch or miswiring. The reason for this check is safety: the pressure switch is supposed to close only after the inducer proves draft through the vent. If it's already closed with the inducer off, the board can't trust it to prove draft, so it stops before the ignitor and gas valve are ever energized. On the modulating YP9C/CP9C/LP9C/TP9C series, the same code is worded as the pressure switch being closed with inducer pressure below the switch setpoint, and York says to check the pressure switch and the pressure transducer. It's the opposite of 3 red flashes, where the switch stays open after the inducer starts.

Likely causes

  • A faulty pressure switch with contacts stuck (welded) closed
  • Miswired pressure switch wiring, for example wires on the wrong terminals or not matching the furnace wiring diagram
  • A jumper wire or bypass left across the pressure switch after previous troubleshooting (which also defeats a safety control)
  • On YP9C/CP9C/LP9C/TP9C modulating furnaces, a pressure transducer problem (York says to check the pressure switch and pressure transducer)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Confirm the code: count the red flashes through the view port before turning off power, since a power reset clears the current flash. Two quick flashes, a 2-second pause, then repeating is code 2. Don't confuse it with 3 red flashes (switch open when it should be closed).
  2. Note whether the inducer motor runs. With code 2, the board normally stops before starting the ignition sequence, so you may hear nothing at all after the thermostat calls for heat.
  3. Turn the thermostat down and switch off power to the furnace. Look at the pressure switch wires and check that nothing has been added across the switch terminals, like a jumper wire or clip. If someone left a jumper in place, don't run the furnace that way; have it removed and the switch tested.
  4. Turn the power back on and try one heat call. If 2 red flashes comes straight back, stop there. Testing the switch means checking continuity or live 24-volt readings across its terminals, which is technician work.
  5. Never bypass or adjust the pressure switch to make the furnace run. York's manuals warn that doing so lets the furnace operate under potentially hazardous conditions.
  6. Don't try to repair the control board. York says controls aren't field repairable and should be replaced with UPG Source 1 parts.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Call a technician if the code returns after a power cycle. A tech checks the pressure switch wiring against the furnace wiring diagram and tests whether the switch contacts are closed with the inducer off.
  • If the switch reads closed with no draft on it, the tech replaces it with the exact OEM pressure switch for your model (high-altitude installs may use a different switch, per York's high-altitude pressure switch table).
  • York's boards store recent fault codes. With no thermostat call active, a technician can press the LAST ERROR button to replay the last five codes, which shows whether 2 red flashes has been coming and going.
  • On a modulating YP9C-family furnace, the technician also checks the pressure transducer, as York's troubleshooting section directs.
