Code: E1🔧 York Mini Split

York Mini Split Error Code E1: High Pressure Protection (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

E1 on a York ductless mini split means high pressure protection: the outdoor unit's high-pressure switch opened because system pressure went above its limit, so the compressor stopped. York's ductless manuals point first at airflow problems, such as a dirty filter, a blocked or dirty outdoor coil, tight clearances around the outdoor unit, a fan that isn't running or service valves that aren't fully open, and then at the pressure switch, its wiring or the outdoor board. On York M series multi-zone units, if it trips twice within an hour the compressor stays off until the outdoor unit's power is reset.

What the code means

York (Johnson Controls) uses E1 for high pressure protection on its ductless mini splits. The M series multi-zone service manual (outdoor models DHM18CMB21S through DHM42CMB21S, on yorknow.com) lists E1 as High Pressure Protection, an outdoor-unit error shown on the indoor display, the wired zone controller and the outdoor unit's display, with the running LED flashing once. Its control description says that when the high-pressure switch senses system pressure above the limit, the switch cuts off and the system stops; the switch has to read open for 3 seconds before the compressor shuts down, and if high pressure protection happens twice within one hour, the compressor can only be restarted by resetting main power to the outdoor unit. The LX multi-zone heat pump installation manual (898150-UIM-B-0113, DHPM/DHMF models) also lists E1 as System high pressure, with all system functions stopped except the indoor fan. Older York single-zone P series 115 V units don't use E1 at all in their code list (it starts at E2 anti-freezing), so check which series you have. Nearby codes: E2 is indoor coil freeze protection and E3 is low pressure protection.

Likely causes

  • Poor airflow across the indoor coil, most often a dirty indoor filter. York's LX manual lists restricted indoor airflow first among its checks.
  • A dirty or blocked outdoor coil, or not enough clearance around the outdoor unit. Both trap heat and push the high-side pressure up.
  • An indoor or outdoor fan that isn't running, or an indoor swing louver that's stuck closed. The M series flowchart checks each of these.
  • Gas or liquid service valves that weren't fully opened after installation.
  • An outdoor unit service panel left off or loose, which the M series flowchart also checks.
  • A restriction in the refrigerant piping, such as a blocked filter or capillary at the main pipe or indoor unit connections.
  • A disconnected or failed high-pressure switch (sensor), or a failed outdoor control board if the pressure is actually normal.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

The outdoor unit has line voltage and an inverter board that can stay charged after power is removed, and the refrigerant side runs at high pressure, especially during an E1 trip. Shut off power at the disconnect before cleaning the outdoor coil, keep the electrical covers on and leave gauge work and any refrigerant repair to a licensed technician.

  1. Turn the system off with the remote, wait a few minutes and turn it back on. On a York M series multi-zone, if E1 tripped twice within an hour you'll need to switch the outdoor unit's breaker or disconnect off and back on before the compressor restarts.
  2. Clean or replace the indoor unit's filters, and make sure nothing is blocking the indoor unit's air intake or outlet.
  3. Check that the indoor unit's mode is set correctly and that the swing louver opens fully when it runs.
  4. With power off at the outdoor disconnect, clear leaves, grass, snow or stored items away from the outdoor unit so it has proper clearance, and gently rinse a dirty outdoor coil with a garden hose (no pressure washer).
  5. Make sure the outdoor unit's service panel is on and fastened.
  6. If E1 comes back after those checks, stop resetting it and call a technician. Repeated high pressure trips stress the compressor.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • A technician should confirm with gauges whether the pressure really is high, which is the first step in York's E1 flowchart.
  • If the pressure is normal, the high-pressure switch and its connection to the outdoor control are checked; York's LX manual says to replace the high pressure sensor if the connection is good and E1 remains, and the M series flowchart ends with replacing the outdoor mainboard if the switch tests normal.
  • Fan motors and swing motors that don't run need their signals and motors measured.
  • Service valves not fully open, a refrigerant restriction or an overcharge all need refrigerant work by an EPA-certified technician.
  • If you have a new installation that shows E1 right away, call the installer, since valves and charge are set at install.
