Code: E1🧺 Fisher & Paykel Dishwasher

Fisher & Paykel Dishwasher Error Code E1: Flood Detected (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

E1 on a Fisher & Paykel DishDrawer means "Flood detected": the flood sensor in the base of the cabinet has picked up water, so the dishwasher stops washing and runs the drain pump. Usually something has made a drawer leak or overflow, such as a lid that couldn't close, too much foam, or a leaking hose or seal. Press the power button to stop the beeping, try one reset, and if E1 comes back, switch off the power and water and call Fisher & Paykel Customer Care.

What the code means

Fisher & Paykel's DishDrawer user guide 590199D (DD24, DD60, DD36 and DD90 Classic, Designer and Integrated models) lists E1 as "Flood detected." The DishDrawer service manual 590409A (DD607 and DD247 series) explains how it works: a flood sensor sits in the mains filter housing in the base of the chassis, and when it has been activated for more than 20 seconds the controller shows E1, sounds a continuous pulsing beep and runs the drain pump continuously to empty the drawers. On integrated and flat-door models without a code display, the manual says E1 shows as the Rinse light on the secondary display. The service manual splits E1 into two kinds: a wet E1, where water really is in the chassis base and is usually from a leak, and a dry E1, where the base is dry and the fault is in the sensor, wiring, mains filter board or controller. Newer DishDrawer models report a flood as F1 instead; see the separate F1 page if that's what your display shows.

Likely causes

  • A drawer lid that couldn't seal because something tall (a large utensil, cup or glass on the top rack) sticks up above the tub rim
  • A jammed or broken lid yoke, or a slow or failed lid actuator, so the lid doesn't close fully
  • Overfilling, for example a water inlet valve that doesn't shut off fully
  • Excess foam from the wrong detergent or from spilled rinse aid, which escapes the tub
  • A drawer that isn't draining properly
  • A leak from the lid seal, heater plate or wash pump seal, drain hose or its O-ring seals, dispenser, internal fill hose, or the water softener on models that have one
  • A loose connection between the inlet hose and inlet valve, or a leaking inlet valve body
  • A damaged heater plate (chipped enamel)
  • A dry E1 with no water in the base: corrosion at the flood sensor, a sensor touching the chassis, corroded harness connections, a failed mains filter board or controller

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Switch the power off at the outlet or breaker before touching water around or under a dishwasher; the flood sensor sits in the mains filter housing beside the power board. Wipe up water on the floor straight away so nobody slips. Fisher & Paykel's service manual says power has to be disconnected and reconnected after a fault is cleared, so don't keep restarting the dishwasher while the base is still wet.

  1. Stop the beeping. Fisher & Paykel's service manual says pressing the KEYLOCK button silences it and also stops the drain pump that runs during an E1.
  2. Turn off the water supply to the dishwasher before you go looking for the leak.
  3. Open each drawer and look for anything sticking up above the tub rim, such as a tall glass, cup or utensil, that could keep the lid from sealing. Move it and reload the drawer.
  4. Look for suds in the tubs. If you see foam, think about whether rinse aid was spilled or hand-dishwashing liquid went in by mistake, and use only automatic dishwasher detergent from now on.
  5. Check behind and under the cabinet for water, and check the inlet and drain hoses for splits, kinks or a loose connection.
  6. Reset once, the way Fisher & Paykel's user guide says for E codes: press the power button, wait a few seconds, then press it again. If the code clears, you can keep using the dishwasher.
  7. If E1 comes back, press the power button again and note the extra number that briefly appears (Classic models); Customer Care can use it to pin down the fault. Then unplug the dishwasher or switch it off at the breaker, turn off its water supply, and contact Fisher & Paykel Customer Care.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E1 comes straight back after a reset. The base needs to be checked and dried, and the flood sensor sits inside the chassis behind the drawers.
  • There's water in the cabinet base and you can't see where it's coming from. The service manual has a technician test the lid actuators and fill valve in diagnostic mode and check the lid, heater plate and wash pump seals, the internal fill hose, the valve body and the drain hose.
  • E1 shows but there's no water anywhere (a dry E1). Fisher & Paykel's manual points to corrosion at the flood sensor or harness, then the mains filter board, then the controller.
  • The heater plate enamel is chipped or a lid actuator is slow or not working; those are parts swaps behind the drawer.
  • The dishwasher is under warranty. Contact Fisher & Paykel Customer Care with the fault code rather than opening it up yourself.
