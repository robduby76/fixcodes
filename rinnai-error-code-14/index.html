Code: 14🚿 Rinnai Tankless Water Heater

Rinnai Tankless Error Code 14: Thermal Fuse or Overheat Switch Tripped (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Code 14 on a Rinnai tankless water heater means the overheat safety circuit opened: either the overheat (bi-metal) switch on the heat exchanger tripped or a thermal fuse wrapped around the heat exchanger blew. Rinnai says code 14 is a sign of overheating and should be taken seriously. Common causes are scale build-up from hard water, blocked air flow or venting, debris in the burner or heat exchanger, wrong gas type or settings, and short cycling. Rinnai marks the repairs as licensed professional only.

What the code means

Rinnai's V53De Installation and Operation Manual defines code 14 as Thermal Fuse has activated. Rinnai's Troubleshooting Tankless Water Heater Diagnostic Codes manual, which covers both SENSEI and non-SENSEI models, explains that two kinds of parts can cause it. The overheat bi-metal switch is mounted on the heat exchanger and normally resets itself once the heat exchanger cools down, so the heater may run again, but it will keep tripping if the cause isn't fixed. The thermal fuses are wrapped around the heat exchanger and are one-shot devices: once blown they have to be replaced, which means removing the heat exchanger and inspecting it. Rinnai's checklist covers the gas type and any gas conversion, clearances around the heater and vent terminals, the safety circuit wiring, DIP switch and parameter settings (one DIP switch, if set to ON, shuts off the gas valve and shows code 14), gas pressures, short cycling, debris or scale in the burner and heat exchanger, and cracks, separations or black discoloration on the heat exchanger. If the code persists after all of that, Rinnai's last step is replacing the PC board.

Likely causes

  • Scale build-up inside the heat exchanger from hard water, which causes hot spots (Rinnai says hard water must be treated and the heat exchanger flushed)
  • Restricted air flow around the heater or a blocked exhaust outlet, or vent clearances and lengths outside Rinnai's specifications
  • Debris, insects or foreign material in the burner, combustion chamber, exhaust piping or between the heat exchanger fins
  • Wrong gas type, an improper gas conversion, or incorrect gas pressure, DIP switch or parameter settings
  • Short cycling, for example low water flow in a recirculating system or an aquastat set too close to the heater's set point
  • A cracked or damaged heat exchanger, a wiring fault in the safety circuit, or (last) a failed PC board

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Write down the code, then check the outside of the heater: make sure nothing is stacked against it or blocking the air intake or exhaust vent outside.
  2. If the heater has a recirculation system or pump, note whether it turns on and off very often; Rinnai lists short cycling as a cause that a technician should look at.
  3. If you have hard water and the heater hasn't been flushed recently, mention it to the technician. Rinnai's manual says the heat exchanger must be flushed regularly as part of normal maintenance and gives a flushing procedure.
  4. The overheat switch can reset itself after cooling, so the heater may start working again. Don't treat that as fixed; Rinnai says code 14 should be taken seriously and the switch will keep tripping until the cause is corrected.
  5. Don't open the unit, bypass the safety circuit or change DIP switches yourself. Rinnai lists code 14 remedies as licensed professional only. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Call a licensed technician for code 14, especially if it returns. If a thermal fuse blew, it has to be replaced, which means removing the heat exchanger.
  • The technician checks the gas type and any past gas conversion, the DIP switch and parameter settings, clearances and venting, and measures the safety circuit resistance to tell an open overheat switch from a blown thermal fuse.
  • They use a manometer to confirm low-fire and high-fire manifold gas pressures, and look for short cycling.
  • They inspect the burner, manifold and heat exchanger fins for debris or scale and the heat exchanger for cracks, separations or black discoloration where the copper meets the combustion box. Rinnai says to replace the heat exchanger if damage or blockage like that is found, and to flush it per the manual.
  • If code 14 still appears after all checks, Rinnai's guide calls for replacing the PC board.
