Code: 23🧺 Smeg Dishwasher

Smeg Dishwasher Error 23 (Er 23): Boiler Not Heating (Commercial Models)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error 23 (shown as Er 23 or 23) on a Smeg dishwasher means the boiler is not heating up as expected. This code belongs to Smeg's commercial (Smeg Foodservice) undercounter, glass washer and hood-type machines, which have a separate rinse boiler; Smeg's home dishwashers use E-codes instead. Smeg tells users to switch off and on, and if 23 returns, to contact After-sales Service. The likely causes are a burnt-out boiler heating element, a boiler pressure switch or temperature probe fault, or the relay or contactor that powers the element.

What the code means

In Smeg's commercial dishwasher manuals the display fault table lists Er 23 as "Boiler does not heat up as expected" (for example the undercounter and hood-type manual on doc.smeg.it) and the newer SP5/SP6 manual lists 23 as "Boiler heating failure". The SP5/SP6 technician table explains the logic: the alarm is triggered if the boiler temperature has not risen by at least 2 degrees above the lowest temperature recorded during the time-out, which is 150 seconds on three-phase machines and 300 seconds on single-phase machines. It is only active while the boiler is in its heating phase. Smeg lists the possible causes as a boiler pressure switch fault, temperature probe fault, heating element fault, or a fault in the control unit or its control relay. The older manual adds that the problem can happen because water is continually entering the tank (the same causes as Er 05, the tank fill fault), and warns that a burnt-out boiler element is often the result of the element running with no water in the boiler because the boiler pressure switch is reporting full when the boiler is actually empty. In that case the boiler air trap and pressure switch should be checked and replaced if needed, or the new element will fail too. Er 24 (boiler probe), Er 25 (boiler fill) and Er 26 (boiler drain) are separate codes.

Likely causes

  • Boiler heating element burnt out or open
  • Boiler pressure switch or air trap fault, reporting full when the boiler is empty (which also burns out elements)
  • Boiler temperature probe faulty or out of position
  • Contactor, boiler safety thermostat or main board relay not powering the element
  • Water continually entering the tank (fill valve leaking or tank level fault), so the boiler can't catch up

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Switch the machine off at the main switch, wait a few minutes and switch it back on. Smeg's manuals say to restart first and follow the table only if the fault persists.
  2. Check the water supply tap is fully open and the inlet water filter is clean, since a fill problem can feed into a boiler heating fault.
  3. Look for clogged rinse nozzles on the rinse arms and clean them.
  4. If 23 comes back after a restart, stop there: Smeg's user table says to contact After-sales Service for 23. Element, thermostat, contactor and pressure-switch checks involve mains wiring and should be done by a qualified technician.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Error 23 returns after a restart: Smeg's user table says to contact After-sales Service.
  • The boiler element has failed: the technician should also check the boiler pressure switch and air trap, or the new element can burn out too.
  • Rinse water is cold or the machine is stuck waiting for temperature during service.
  • The machine is three-phase or hard-wired, which needs an electrician or commercial dishwasher technician.
