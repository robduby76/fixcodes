York Furnace 7 Red Flashes: No Ignition, Flame Not Established (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Seven red flashes on a York furnace control board mean the furnace tried to light three times and never established a flame, so it locked out. York's manuals say to check that the gas valve switch is ON, then look for low or no gas pressure, a faulty gas valve, a dirty or faulty flame sensor, a faulty hot surface ignitor, loose wires or a burner problem. The lockout is a one-hour soft lockout: the furnace waits an hour and then tries to light again on its own.

What the code means

York (Johnson Controls) furnace boards show faults with an LED behind the view port in the blower door. Fault codes flash on and off the number of times equal to the code, with a 2-second pause between codes, so 7 red flashes is code 7. York's Y91E, TM9E and TM9V installation manuals define 7 red flashes as: the flame could not be established during three trials for ignition. The YP9C manual words it as lockout due to no ignition. Here's what happens during each try, per the Y91E manual: once the pressure switch proves draft, the hot surface ignitor warms up for 17 seconds, then the gas valve opens. If the flame sensor doesn't detect flame within 7 seconds, the gas valve shuts off, the inducer runs a 15-second purge, and the control retries with a longer 27-second ignitor warm-up. After three failed retries in one call for heat, the furnace shuts down for one hour, then starts a normal cycle if there's still a call for heat, and locks out again after three more failures if the problem isn't fixed. York designed this soft lockout so a temporary gas interruption doesn't leave an empty house without heat: the furnace keeps trying each hour and resumes once gas returns. Seven red flashes is different from 8 red flashes, which means the burners did light but the flame was lost five times during one heating cycle (usually low gas pressure, a dirty flame sensor or a faulty gas valve).

Likely causes

  • The gas valve switch or knob on the furnace is turned OFF
  • The gas supply is off or gas pressure is low, for example a closed shutoff valve, an interrupted utility supply or an empty propane tank
  • A dirty or faulty flame sensor that can't prove the flame even though the burners light briefly
  • A cracked or failed hot surface ignitor that doesn't glow
  • A faulty gas valve that doesn't open
  • Loose wires at the ignitor, flame sensor or gas valve
  • A burner problem, such as dirty or misaligned burners

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Confirm the code: count the red flashes through the view port before turning off power, since a power reset clears the current flash. Seven flashes, a 2-second pause, then repeating is code 7. York boards also store the last five codes; with no thermostat call active, press and release the LAST ERROR (or SW) button to replay them.
  2. Check the gas valve switch on the furnace and make sure it's in the ON position, the first thing York's manuals list.
  3. Make sure gas is reaching the house: check that other gas appliances (stove, water heater) work, that the furnace's manual shutoff valve on the gas line is open, and on propane, that the tank isn't empty.
  4. If you smell gas, don't touch switches or try to light anything. Leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
  5. Watch a heating cycle through the view port: if the ignitor doesn't glow, the ignitor or its wiring is the likely problem; if the burners light and then go out after a few seconds, the flame sensor is the likely problem.
  6. After fixing the gas supply, you can wait for the furnace to retry on its own after the one-hour lockout, or turn the power off and back on to restart it right away.
  7. Don't try to repair the control board or gas valve, and never bypass any safety control. York's manuals say controls aren't field repairable and that bypassing a safety control lets the furnace run under potentially hazardous conditions.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Call a technician if the gas supply is fine and 7 red flashes keeps coming back.
  • A technician checks inlet and manifold gas pressure, whether the ignitor heats up, whether the gas valve opens when it gets 24 volts, the flame sensor and its wiring, and the burners, then cleans or replaces the flame sensor, ignitor or gas valve as needed.
  • York's manuals list normal flame sense current at about 3.7 microamps DC, with a low-signal warning (rapid amber flash) at 1.5 and lockout at 0.1; a technician can measure this to tell a weak flame sensor from a gas or ignitor problem.
  • Any gas valve work or gas pressure adjustment should be done by a qualified technician.
