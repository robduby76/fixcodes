Code: CH E🧊 LG Refrigerator

LG Refrigerator CH E Error Code (Er CH, E CH): Sealed System Problem (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-16 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

CH E on an LG refrigerator (also Er CH or E CH) points to the high-pressure side of the sealed refrigerant system: LG calls it high side cycle leakage and names the condenser as a possible leak source. That's the difference from its twin CL, which is the low-pressure side and the evaporator. A faulty valve, a restriction, a bad compressor or doors left open a long time can also set it. Check the doors and unplug the fridge for five minutes, but LG says a real CH needs a service technician, and clearing the code from the display doesn't fix a leak.

What the code means

LG calls CH "High side cycle leakage". LG Canada says CH E or E CH indicates a high side refrigerant leak, with the condenser (the high-pressure side of the cooling system) a possible source. LG's digital and analog error code guide adds that the fridge sets CH when its temperature and compressor sensors report abnormal values. LG USA's Er CH and Er CL article says the code appears when the fridge detects insufficient cooling or an abnormal internal temperature, and lists low refrigerant or a leak, a compressor, valve or other sealed-system problem, a blockage in the sealed system, or a door left open for a long time. Its twin code, CL (Er CL, E CL), means the same kind of problem on the low-pressure side, usually the evaporator. LG also notes CH can show up right after a repair if the freezer hasn't yet cooled below 14°F (-10°C); in that case let it run about 24 hours.

Likely causes

  • A refrigerator or freezer door left open or ajar for a long time, so the fridge can't pull the temperature down
  • A refrigerant leak on the high-pressure side of the sealed system, possibly at the condenser (the possible source LG names for CH)
  • A restriction or blockage in the refrigerant flow inside the sealed system
  • A failing compressor
  • A defective valve in the sealed system
  • A recent repair: the freezer hasn't cooled below 14°F (-10°C) yet, so the code shows until it catches up

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Check every door. Close the refrigerator and freezer doors completely if any were left open, give the compartments time to cool, and see if the code clears.
  2. Restart the refrigerator. Unplug it from the wall outlet, wait five minutes, and plug it back in.
  3. Don't rely on clearing the code. LG's current CL E / CH E article (revised 10/29/2026) says clearing a code from the display does not repair a refrigerant leak and that LG does not provide user troubleshooting steps for these codes.
  4. If the fridge was just repaired, let it run for about 24 hours so the freezer can get below 14°F (-10°C). LG says to contact support if the code is still there after 24 hours or the freezer is still too warm.
  5. Keep an eye on food temperatures with a thermometer. LG warns the fridge may not be cooling properly while CH is showing, so move perishables to a cooler or another fridge if temperatures climb.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • CH comes back after the door check and restart. LG says CH E and E CH need professional diagnosis and repair, so contact LG to request service with the code and your model number.
  • Anything inside the sealed system: refrigerant leaks, the condenser, compressor, valves or tubing. LG warns not to take the unit apart or replace internal parts yourself because of the risk of fire, electric shock or serious injury, and refrigerant work requires a certified technician.
  • The freezer is still too warm 24 hours after a repair.
  • The refrigerator is under warranty. LG notes a service fee may apply if it's out of warranty.
