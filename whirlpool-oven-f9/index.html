Whirlpool Oven F9 or F9 E0 Error Code: Miswired Power Supply (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

F9 or F9 E0 (F9E0) on a Whirlpool range or wall oven means the control thinks the power coming in is wrong: most often the house outlet or the hard-wired connection to the oven is miswired. It can also show up after a power outage or surge, or if a key was pressed during the 10-second power-up delay. Turn the breaker off for one minute and back on; if F9 returns, keep the power off and have an electrician check the supply and the terminal block wiring.

What the code means

Whirlpool's error code chart lists F9 and F9 E0 together and says the electrical outlet in the home or the electrical connection to the appliance may be miswired. On wall ovens, Whirlpool says it most often means the oven is wired incorrectly at the home supply or at the hard-wire connection, and specifically to make sure L2 and neutral (N) aren't swapped. Whirlpool's range tech sheet W11392685 labels the same code (F9 on LCC 90/95 controls, F9E0 on LCC 180/190 controls) as "Miswired house / Miswired range". The code isn't always a real wiring fault: Whirlpool explains that unexpected "noise" on the power line during an outage can be read by the control as incorrect voltage, and pressing any keys before the 10-second delay at power-up can also trigger it. If the range or oven was installed recently (Whirlpool says within about 3 months), suspect the installation wiring first.

Likely causes

  • The house outlet or circuit feeding the oven is miswired
  • Wires connected to the wrong terminals at the range terminal block or the wall oven's hard-wire connection, such as L2 and neutral swapped
  • A recent installation (within about 3 months) where the electrical hookup wasn't done correctly
  • Power-line noise during a power outage or surge that the control read as incorrect voltage
  • Keys pressed during the 10-second delay right after power was restored
  • Loose, burnt or damaged supply wires at the terminal block

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before opening panels.

  1. Do Whirlpool's power reset: turn off the oven's circuit breaker(s) or fuse(s) for one minute, turn them back on, then don't touch the controls while you watch the display for one minute to see if the code returns.
  2. If there was a recent outage or storm and the code clears after the reset, try a normal bake to confirm the oven works.
  3. If the range or wall oven was installed or moved recently, or someone worked on the outlet or circuit, treat that as the likely cause.
  4. If F9 or F9 E0 keeps coming back, Whirlpool's advice is to disconnect power and have a qualified electrician verify the electrical supply. Don't keep resetting it or using the oven while it's showing.
  5. Before the visit, note your model number (on the label inside the oven door frame or drawer area) and whether the code is F9 or F9 E0.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Entering the control's diagnostics mode (CANCEL, CANCEL, START within 5 seconds on many Whirlpool electric ranges) to confirm the stored code, then clearing it after the repair.
  • With the power off and test equipment connected, verifying the house supply provides 240 VAC between L1 and L2 and 120 VAC from each of L1 and L2 to neutral. If not, the house wiring needs an electrician.
  • If the supply measures correctly, checking that the supply wires are connected to the correct terminals at the range terminal block (or the wall oven's junction box) and that the connections are tight and not burnt.
  • On wall ovens, confirming L2 and neutral aren't swapped at the hard-wire connection.
  • Restoring power, verifying normal operation, and checking diagnostics again for any remaining codes.
