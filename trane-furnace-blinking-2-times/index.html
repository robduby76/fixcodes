Code: 2 flashes🔥 Trane Gas Furnace

Trane Furnace Blinking 2 Times: System Lockout, Ignition Retries Exceeded (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-10 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

A red LED blinking 2 times then pausing on a Trane (or American Standard) furnace control board means the furnace locked out after too many failed ignition attempts. Either the burners never lit, or the flame lit and then went out, so the board stopped trying for a while. A gas valve that's closed, a blocked vent, or a weak ignitor or flame sensor is the usual cause.

What the code means

Trane's integrated furnace control (IFC) flash-code chart lists 2 flashes as "System Lockout (Retries or Recycles exceeded)". In plain terms, the board tried to light the burners several times and didn't get a reliable flame, so it shut the gas off and stopped retrying. On newer Trane furnaces with a two-digit display, the same lockouts show as E2.1 or E2.2. Trane's A801X installer's guide defines E2.1 as Retry Exceeded: flame was never sensed, with a one-hour lockout after 3 tries, and E2.2 as Recycles Exceeded: flame was sensed and then lost, with a one-hour lockout after 10 times. The S8V2 (FNR) manual uses the same E2.1 and E2.2 wording (retries exceeded, flame never sensed; recycles exceeded, loss of flame after being sensed). This is different from 3 flashes, which is a draft pressure or venting problem, and from 4 flashes, which is an open temperature limit. Slow flashing is normal with no call for heat, and fast flashing is normal during a call for heat.

Likely causes

  • The manual gas shutoff next to the furnace is closed, or the home's gas supply is off or very low, so the burners never light (Trane tells technicians to confirm flame was never sensed on E2.1)
  • A blocked, frozen, or disconnected intake or exhaust pipe, or a venting problem that keeps the burners from staying lit
  • A weak, cracked, or failed hot-surface ignitor that doesn't get hot enough to light the gas
  • A dirty or failed flame sensor, so the flame lights and then the board shuts the gas off (Trane's E2.2: flame sensed, then lost)
  • A failed gas valve or a wiring problem on the gas-valve circuit (Trane also lists a separate 7-flash gas valve circuit error)
  • A dirty air filter or other airflow problem that leads to flame loss after the burners have lit

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Count the red LED through the sight glass on the lower door before you touch anything: 2 flashes and a pause is this lockout. Trane's chart lists slow flashing as normal with no call for heat and fast flashing as normal during a call for heat. 3 flashes is a pressure-switch or vent problem, and 4 flashes is an open limit.
  2. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside. Don't keep resetting the furnace.
  3. Check that the manual gas shutoff next to the furnace is open (handle in line with the pipe) and that other gas appliances in the house still work.
  4. Look at the outdoor intake and exhaust pipes or the chimney/vent cap. Clear snow, ice, leaves, or a nest, and make sure the pipes aren't disconnected or crushed.
  5. Check the furnace air filter and replace it if it's dirty. Open supply registers and make sure return grilles aren't blocked.
  6. Trane's lockout lasts about an hour on the E2.1 / E2.2 boards. You can wait it out, or cycle power at the furnace switch or breaker for a minute to clear a one-time lockout, then call for heat again and watch whether the ignitor glows and the burners stay lit. If 2 flashes comes back, stop resetting and call a technician.
  7. Note your model number from the rating label inside the door before you call, since XR, XB and XV names cover many boards.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • 2 flashes comes back after the gas is on, the vent is clear, and a power cycle or the one-hour wait. Repeated lockouts usually mean the ignitor, flame sensor, gas valve or venting needs a technician.
  • The ignitor never glows, the gas valve never clicks, or the burners light and go out right away. That's live-voltage and gas-valve work.
  • You see a cracked ignitor, a heavily oxidized flame sensor, soot, or signs of flame rollout.
  • The display shows E2.3 (gas valve not energized when it should be) or 7 flashes (gas valve circuit error). Don't open or rewire the gas valve yourself.
  • Never jumper a pressure switch, limit, or flame sensor to 'get heat tonight.' If you smell gas or a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
