Carrier Error Code 178: Indoor Unit Communication Fault (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Carrier fault 178 on an Infinity or Evolution communicating thermostat (wall control) means the thermostat has lost communication with the indoor unit, the furnace or fan coil. Carrier's master fault code list calls it "Indoor Unit Communication Fault." It's a wiring or power problem on the four-wire ABCD bus far more often than a broken part: check that the furnace or air handler has power, the panel door is closed, and the thermostat wires are tight, then cycle power. If it keeps coming back, a technician needs to test the bus voltage and isolate the bad wire or board.

What the code means

Carrier's communicating systems (Infinity, Evolution and related Bryant models) connect the thermostat, indoor unit, outdoor unit and accessories over a four-wire connection called the ABCD bus. Per Carrier's Infinity System Control installation instructions, A (green) and B (yellow) carry data, while C (white, 24 VAC common) and D (red, 24 VAC hot) supply power, and every ABCD connector in the system must be wired the same way. Carrier's Residential Fault Code List (Technical Information TIC2025-0010) lists code 178 as a wall control fault: "Indoor Unit Communication Fault." In other words, the thermostat can't talk to the furnace or fan coil. Its neighbor, 179, is the same fault for the outdoor unit, and 180 to 182 cover the zoning and network interface modules. Because the indoor unit is also where the bus gets its 24 VAC power, anything that kills power to the furnace or air handler, or breaks or shorts one of the four wires, can bring up 178.

Likely causes

  • No power at the indoor unit (furnace or fan coil switch or breaker off, or the blower door open so its safety switch cuts power). General HVAC background: the indoor unit powers the bus, so if it is off the thermostat loses it.
  • A loose, swapped or broken wire on the ABCD terminals at the thermostat or the indoor unit. Carrier requires every ABCD connector to be wired consistently.
  • Damaged thermostat cable, such as a nicked, corroded, shorted or rodent-chewed wire, or high resistance in a long run. Carrier's distributor Mingledorff's lists failed thermostat wire as a common cause.
  • A failed circuit board (thermostat, indoor control, outdoor control or an accessory) dragging down the communication voltage on the whole bus, per Mingledorff's.
  • Electrical noise on long or poorly routed runs. Carrier suggests shielded twisted-pair cable near high-voltage or network wiring, and 100-ohm end resistors for runs over 100 feet.
  • General HVAC background, not from Carrier's 178 entry: a tripped condensate overflow (float) switch or a blown low-voltage fuse on the furnace board can also cut 24 VAC to the bus on some installations.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels.

  1. Write down every code the thermostat shows, including any 179 (outdoor) or 180-182 codes. Several communication codes at once point toward wiring or a board pulling down the whole bus.
  2. Make sure the furnace or air handler has power: check its service switch (often a light switch on or near the unit) and its breaker. Make sure the blower compartment door is fully closed, since it holds in a safety switch.
  3. If the air handler or furnace has a condensate overflow switch and the drain pan is full of water, the switch may have cut power. Clear the drain line or call a pro.
  4. Cycle power: turn off the breakers for the indoor and outdoor units for about a minute, then turn the indoor unit back on first and the outdoor unit after.
  5. With power off, pull the thermostat off its base and check that the four ABCD wires are seated and tight, with no bare copper touching another terminal. Don't change which wire goes where.
  6. If 178 returns, or the thermostat shows "Indoor unit not found," stop there and call a Carrier dealer. Testing the communication bus needs a meter and the system's wiring diagrams.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • 178 keeps returning after the indoor unit's power, door and wiring are checked.
  • The thermostat shows "Indoor unit not found" or has dropped into demo mode.
  • Both 178 and 179 (or zoning codes 180-182) appear, which suggests a board or wire is pulling down the whole bus.
  • A technician should check the A-B data voltage (Mingledorff's says about 4 VDC is good and below 3.2 VDC causes communication failures) and isolate the bus as Carrier recommends: connect only the thermostat and indoor unit, then add each component back one at a time.
  • A wire run needs replacing, for example through an attic or wall where rodents may have damaged it, or a board needs replacing.
