Code: 6 flashes🔥 Trane Gas Furnace

Trane Furnace Blinking 6 Times: 115 VAC Power Reversed, Poor Grounding or Low Voltage (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-18 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

On older Trane and American Standard furnaces with an integrated furnace control (IFC), a red LED blinking 6 times then pausing means the control sees 115 volt AC power reversed, poor grounding, or system voltage too low. The control is polarity sensitive, so this is mostly a wiring or power-supply problem. Electrical checks at the furnace belong to a technician, but a few safe observations can help you describe it.

What the code means

Trane's installer's guide 18-CH49D1-1 lists integrated furnace control error flash codes. Under that chart, 6 flashes means "115 volt AC power reversed, poor grounding or system voltage too low". The same guide cautions that the integrated furnace control is polarity sensitive: the hot leg of the 115 VAC supply must be connected to the BLACK field lead. So the control is reporting a problem with the incoming line power (hot and neutral swapped, a missing or poor ground, or low supply voltage) rather than a failed burner or sensor. Numbering is not the same on every Trane board. On the Symbio 700 ignition control described in Trane's help center (a commercial rooftop unit), 6 flashes instead means the flame rollout switch is open. On a residential furnace, check the chart on your own furnace door and match it to your board before acting on this page. Related codes on the same IFC chart include 5 flashes (flame sensed when no flame should be present), 7 flashes (gas valve circuit error), 8 flashes (low flame sense signal) and 9 flashes (check ignitor circuit and Line N to 24VAC common voltage, possible grounding problem).

Likely causes

  • 115 VAC hot and neutral reversed at the furnace (for example after a new installation, a replaced control, or a rewired disconnect or outlet)
  • Poor or missing equipment ground at the furnace or its junction box
  • System (line) voltage too low, such as a loose connection, undersized wiring or a weak supply circuit
  • A loose, corroded or damaged power connection at the furnace switch, junction box or control board
  • A fault in the control board itself (confirmed only by a technician after power and ground are verified)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Count the red LED through the sight glass in the lower door: 6 flashes and a pause is this code, on an IFC that uses the chart above. Slow flashing is normal with no call for heat; fast flashing is normal during a call for heat.
  2. Turn the thermostat to off, then turn the furnace power off at the switch for about 30 seconds and back on to see whether the code clears. A brief voltage dip may not return.
  3. Check that the furnace power switch is fully on, and that the breaker has not tripped or is not loose. Reset a tripped breaker once at most; if it trips again, stop.
  4. If the code appeared right after installation, a repair, or electrical work, tell the installer. Reversed power or a missing ground is a wiring error that must be corrected by a qualified person.
  5. Do not open the junction box, swap wires, or test line voltage yourself. Write down your model number from the rating label inside the door and the flash pattern before you call.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • 6 flashes returns after a power reset.
  • The furnace was recently installed, moved, or had its control board or wiring replaced.
  • Verifying hot-to-black-lead polarity, a good equipment ground, and line voltage at the furnace with a meter. That is live-voltage work.
  • Checking Line N to 24VAC common voltage and the rest of the grounding path if the IFC also shows 9 flashes.
  • Never bypass or jumper a safety control, and never defeat the ground. If a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.
