Mitsubishi Mini Split Error Code P6: Freezing or Overheating Protection (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

P6 on a Mitsubishi Electric indoor unit means freezing or overheating protection has tripped. In cooling or dry mode the indoor coil got too cold (it was starting to freeze); in heating mode it got too hot. The unit pauses for 6 minutes, and if the same thing happens again soon after, it stops and shows P6. Mitsubishi's service manual puts a clogged air filter and blocked or short-cycling airflow first, then running outside the unit's operating range, then the indoor fan motor, outdoor fan, refrigerant overcharge or a clogged refrigerant circuit.

What the code means

Mitsubishi Electric's PLA-A BA4 ceiling cassette service manual (OCH482) lists P6 as "Freezing/Overheating protection operation", reported as 6 blinks or beeps on the wireless remote's self-check. In cooling or drying mode (freezing protection), the unit enters a 6-minute restart-prevention mode if the indoor pipe (liquid or condenser/evaporator) temperature stays under -15 C (5 F) for 3 minutes, starting 3 minutes after the compressor starts. It is judged abnormal if the pipe stays under -15 C (5 F) for 3 minutes again within 16 minutes after that 6-minute pause. In heating mode (overheating protection), the unit enters the 6-minute pause if the condenser/evaporator pipe temperature goes over 70 C (158 F) after the compressor starts, and it is abnormal if over 70 C (158 F) is detected again within 10 minutes after the pause. On City Multi systems, Mitsubishi's error code list shows P6 as four-digit code 1503, "Freeze protection of Branch box or Indoor unit". Neighboring codes: P5 is drain overflow protection and P8 is a pipe temperature error.

Likely causes

  • A clogged air filter, which cuts airflow over the indoor coil. Mitsubishi lists this first for both cooling and heating.
  • A short cycle of the air path, meaning something is blocking the indoor unit's air inlet or outlet so it re-breathes its own air.
  • Running outside the unit's operating range: very low load (low temperature) in cooling, or overload (high temperature) in heating.
  • A defective indoor fan motor, or an indoor controller board that isn't driving the fan correctly.
  • Defective outdoor fan control.
  • Refrigerant overcharge, or a clogged refrigerant circuit.
  • In heating mode, a defective bypass circuit in the outdoor unit.
  • On City Multi systems with a branch box (code 1503), wrong piping or wiring between the indoor unit and branch box, or a miswired or failing expansion valve (LEV) in the branch box or indoor unit.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off the breaker for the indoor and outdoor units before cleaning filters or touching anything beyond what your owner's manual covers. Don't chip or pry ice off the indoor coil, which can puncture it; let it melt. Fan motors, control boards and refrigerant work involve line voltage and pressurized refrigerant, so leave those to a licensed HVAC technician.

  1. Note whether P6 appeared while cooling, drying or heating. It points to freezing protection in cooling or dry mode and overheating protection in heating, and a technician will want to know which.
  2. Turn the system off and check the indoor unit's air filters. Clean or replace them if they are dusty; this is Mitsubishi's first check for P6 in both modes.
  3. Clear anything blocking the indoor unit's air intake or outlet, such as furniture, curtains or stored items, so air isn't short-cycling back into the unit.
  4. If the indoor coil is visibly iced up after cooling, leave the system off (or run fan only if your remote allows it) until the ice melts before restarting.
  5. Check that the outdoor unit isn't blocked by leaves, snow or debris and that its fan has clear space around it.
  6. Think about the conditions. Cooling a room on a cold day, or heating on a very hot one, can push the unit outside its operating range.
  7. Turn the power off and on again, as Mitsubishi's manual says to do after checking, then run the unit. If P6 comes back, call a technician.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • P6 returns with clean filters and clear airflow. A technician checks the indoor fan motor and indoor controller board, which Mitsubishi lists next.
  • The outdoor fan isn't running or is running erratically. Mitsubishi's countermeasure is to check the outdoor fan motor and its control.
  • The system was recently installed or serviced. A refrigerant overcharge or a clogged refrigerant circuit needs gauges and the operating conditions checked.
  • P6 shows in heating mode only. The technician also checks the outdoor unit's bypass circuit, which Mitsubishi lists as a heating-mode cause.
  • On a City Multi system (code 1503), the piping and wiring between the indoor unit and branch box, and the expansion valves, need to be checked.
  • The system is under warranty. Contact the installing contractor or a Mitsubishi Electric Diamond Contractor.
