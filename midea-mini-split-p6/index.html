Code: P6🔧 Midea Mini Split

Midea Mini Split Error Code P6: Low Pressure Protection or IPM Fault (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

P6 on a Midea mini split means one of two things depending on the system. On single-zone Mission 3D and Aurora inverter units it's low pressure protection: the low-pressure switch on the outdoor unit opened, and Midea's manuals say this applies only to the 36K (3-ton) models. On Advantage series multi-zone outdoor units, P6 is an inverter module (IPM) malfunction instead. Either way the unit stops to protect the compressor. Clearing airflow and power-cycling is safe for you to do; refrigerant, pressure switch and board work belong to a licensed HVAC technician.

What the code means

Midea's Mission 3D Inverter service manual (MBSUL-A-1604) lists P6 as "Low pressure protection (Only for 36K)". It says that when system pressure reaches a certain value the low pressure protector switches off, and once pressure returns to normal the protection code disappears. The Aurora Inverter service manual uses the same definition, with a flowchart that also applies only to 36K models: check the wiring from the low pressure protector to the outdoor main board, check the protector itself (it should read about 0 ohms when closed), check whether the outdoor temperature is too low, make sure the high-pressure (liquid) service valve core is fully open, check the indoor fan in cooling mode, check the refrigerant charge, and replace the outdoor main board last. Midea's Advantage series multi-zone service manual (M2OF/M3OF outdoor units) is different: there P6 is "Inverter module (IPM) malfunction", shown when the voltage signal the IPM sends to the compressor drive chip is abnormal, and the unit turns off. That manual puts low pressure protection under P2 instead, so check which series you have before you troubleshoot.

Likely causes

  • Low refrigerant charge from a leak or an undercharged install (low pressure protection P6 on 36K single-zone units).
  • A service valve on the outdoor unit that wasn't fully opened after installation.
  • Very low outdoor temperature, which Midea's flowchart lists as a check before blaming parts.
  • Weak indoor airflow in cooling (an indoor fan that isn't running right, or a badly clogged filter or coil).
  • A loose or broken wire between the low pressure switch and the outdoor main board, or a failed low pressure switch.
  • A failed outdoor main board, which Midea lists last.
  • On Advantage multi-zone outdoor units: an IPM (inverter module) fault, compressor wiring mistake, outdoor fan problem, compressor failure or outdoor board failure.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the breaker before opening any panel. Midea's service manuals warn that the outdoor unit's electrolytic capacitors hold a dangerous high-voltage charge even after power is shut off, so board, IPM and pressure switch checks belong to a licensed HVAC technician. Refrigerant work also requires an EPA-certified technician.

  1. Check the model number on the outdoor unit's label. Midea only defines low pressure P6 on 36K single-zone units (Mission 3D, Aurora); a multi-zone Advantage outdoor unit (model starting M2OF or M3OF) means the inverter-module version of P6.
  2. Clean or replace the indoor unit's air filters and make sure nothing is blocking the indoor unit's air intake or outlet.
  3. Clear leaves, snow, ice and debris from around the outdoor unit so it has open airflow.
  4. Turn the system off at the breaker, wait about 2 minutes, and turn it back on. Midea's manuals say the low pressure code clears once pressure returns to normal.
  5. If P6 comes back, stop resetting it and call a licensed HVAC technician. Repeated restarts on a low-charge system can damage the compressor.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The technician connects gauges to check suction pressure and charge. If it's low, Midea's flowchart says to find and repair the cause and add refrigerant.
  • If the system was just installed, confirm the service valve cores on the outdoor unit are fully open.
  • Check the indoor fan in cooling mode and follow Midea's fan-speed troubleshooting if it isn't running right.
  • With the power off and the plug disconnected, check the low pressure switch wiring to the outdoor main board and measure the switch. Replace the switch if it's open when it should be closed.
  • If wiring, switch, charge and airflow all check out, Midea's next step is replacing the outdoor main board.
  • On Advantage multi-zone outdoor units, follow the IPM path instead: check compressor wiring, IPM installation and continuity, outdoor fan, compressor, then the outdoor board.
