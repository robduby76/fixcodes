Lennox Furnace Error 225 (E225): High Pressure Switch Failed Open (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-17 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error 225 on a Lennox furnace means the high pressure switch failed open: it did not close when the furnace called for heat. The switch proves the combustion air inducer is moving enough air through the vent on high fire, so the control won't proceed until it closes. Lennox says to check the pressure at which the switch closes on a heat call, measure operating pressure, and inspect the vent and inducer for correct operation and restriction. The code resumes normal operation once the fault is cleared.

What the code means

Lennox unit information and service literature for its SureLight two-stage furnaces (SL280, EL296, EL297 families) list E 225 as "High pressure switch failed open." The code appears on the furnace's seven-segment LED one character at a time (E, 2, 2, 5). A two-stage furnace uses a low pressure switch for low fire and a high pressure switch for high fire; each closes only when the combustion air inducer pulls enough draft. E225 means the high switch did not close on a heat call. Lennox's action is to check the pressure (inches w.c.) at which the high pressure switch closes on a heat call, measure operating pressure (inches w.c.), and inspect the vent and combustion air inducer for correct operation and restriction. The furnace resumes normal operation after the fault is cleared. Related codes: E223 is the low pressure switch failed open, E224 the low switch failed closed, E226 the high pressure switch failed closed, and E227 the low pressure switch opening during ignition or run mode.

Likely causes

  • A restricted or blocked vent or combustion air intake (debris, nests, snow or ice at the termination, crushed or sagging pipe)
  • A combustion air inducer that is weak, dirty, or not running correctly, so it can't pull the pressure the high switch needs
  • A cracked, disconnected, or water-filled pressure switch hose, or a loose switch wire
  • On condensing models, a clogged condensate trap or drain backing water into the inducer or hose (a common field cause, not a Lennox-listed one)
  • A high pressure switch that has failed and no longer closes at its specified pressure

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Read the code: watch the seven-segment LED through the sight glass. Recent codes can be recalled with the diagnostic button as described in your furnace's manual.
  2. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
  3. Go outside and check the furnace's vent and combustion air intake terminations. Clear snow, ice, leaves, or nests from the openings. Do not stand on a slick roof, and do not take the vent apart.
  4. If your furnace is condensing (white PVC pipes), look at the condensate trap and drain. If water is backed up or the line is clogged, turn power off first; a technician can help if you aren't sure.
  5. Turn power off at the furnace switch or breaker and look for a pressure switch hose that is cracked, kinked, or popped off its nipple. Reseat it only if it is plainly loose.
  6. Restore power and call for heat. If E225 returns, stop resetting. Measuring closing and operating pressure with a manometer and testing the switch is technician work. Never jumper or bypass a pressure switch.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E225 returns after the vent terminations and condensate drain are clear.
  • Lennox's next step needs a manometer to check the closing pressure and operating pressure in inches w.c.
  • The inducer is noisy, slow, or not running, or the switch or hose is damaged.
  • You see water in the inducer housing, or signs of a blocked flue.
  • If you smell gas or a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
