Carrier Furnace Code 21: Gas Heating Lockout, Control Will Not Auto-Reset (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-10 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Carrier furnace code 21 is a gas heating lockout: the control thinks the gas-valve relay is stuck closed, or the gas-valve wiring is miswired or shorted. Unlike most Carrier lockouts, this one will not reset itself. Don't keep cycling the furnace; this is a job for a licensed technician, and if you smell gas, leave and call your gas utility.

What the code means

Carrier's troubleshooting guides for its variable-speed, two-stage and modulating gas furnaces all define status code 21 as "GAS HEATING LOCKOUT". On the 58MVP / 58MVB / 58UVB guide (58-06T), it means the main gas-valve relay (MGVR) on the variable-speed furnace control is stuck closed, or there is a miswire or short to the gas-valve wiring. The 2-stage guide (58-3T) uses the same definition on the 2-stage control, and the 59MN7A modulating guide says the gas-valve relay (GVR) is stuck closed or there is a miswire or short to the gas-valve BLUE wire. In all three, the control will NOT auto-reset, which is different from code 14 (ignition lockout) and code 13 (limit circuit lockout), which reset after about 3 hours. Carrier's 58-06T service label tells you to check for a mis-wired gas valve or a defective control (valve relay), and the detailed procedure has the technician confirm the BLUE wire goes to the gas valve M terminal and the GREEN/YELLOW wire to the C terminal (on the 59MN7A, a short or miswire on the BLUE wire). The LED shows 21 as 2 short flashes and 1 long flash. Code 22 is a different, also serious fault: flame is proved while the gas valve is off.

Likely causes

  • A miswired or shorted gas-valve harness (Carrier lists this first; on many boards the BLUE and GREEN/YELLOW leads must go to the valve's M and C terminals)
  • A stuck-closed gas-valve relay on the furnace control board (Carrier: defective control, valve relay)
  • Reversed BLUE and GREEN wires at the gas valve, which the 58-06T procedure specifically checks for
  • A short to the gas-valve BLUE wire (59MN7A modulating furnaces)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Read the code before you do anything else. On older boards, 21 is 2 short flashes and 1 long flash. On a 3-digit display, a fault looks like 21.1 or similar, not a status word like CL, Ht or iDL. If you see CL, that's cooling mode, not this lockout.
  2. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside. Don't try to rewire the gas valve or keep resetting the furnace.
  3. Turn the thermostat off, then switch the furnace off at its service switch or breaker. Because Carrier says code 21 will not auto-reset, a short power cycle is only to make the house safe, not a fix.
  4. Don't open the gas-valve wiring or jumper anything on the control. Carrier's checkout for 21 involves live 24-volt tests and, if the code is still there with the thermostat disconnected, replacing the control or correcting a miswire.
  5. Note the model and serial numbers from the rating label inside the door, and write down the exact code, so the technician can bring the right board.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Any confirmed code 21. Carrier's guides treat this as a gas-valve wiring or control-relay fault that will not auto-reset, and the checkout is for a licensed technician.
  • You smell gas, hear the gas valve humming when there is no call for heat, or the burners stay on when the thermostat is off. Leave and call your gas utility, then a technician.
  • The BLUE and GREEN/YELLOW gas-valve leads need tracing or the control board needs replacement. That's gas and 24-volt work, not a homeowner repair.
  • A different code appears after the thermostat is disconnected (for example 22, 14 or 34). Diagnose that code rather than guessing at the gas valve.
  • Never jumper the gas valve or bypass the control relay to 'get heat tonight.'
