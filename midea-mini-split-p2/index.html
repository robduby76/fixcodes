Code: P2🔧 Midea Mini Split

Midea Mini Split Error Code P2: Compressor Top or IPM Module High Temperature Protection (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

P2 on a Midea mini split is a high temperature protection in the outdoor unit. Depending on the series, Midea's manuals define it as the compressor top overheating (the overload protector opened) or the IPM (inverter power module) on the outdoor board getting too hot. Either way the unit stops to protect itself. Clearing blocked airflow around the indoor and outdoor coils and a 10-minute power-off restart are Midea's first steps and are safe for a homeowner; checking the overload protector, the refrigerant charge or the outdoor board is a job for a licensed HVAC technician.

What the code means

Midea uses P2 for two closely related overheat protections, so the exact wording depends on your model's service manual. Older Midea wall-mount inverter manuals list P2 as "High temperature protection of compressor top": the outdoor board reads a signal through the compressor's overload protector (OLP), and if that sampling voltage is not 5 V the board shows P2. On units with indicator lamps instead of a display, that P2 shows as the operation lamp flashing 3 times with the timer lamp flashing. The Midea Oasis Plus 3D Inverter (R32) manual instead lists P2 as "High temperature protection of IPM module": the temperature of the inverter power module went above its limit. Newer Midea manuals, such as the Light Commercial Inverter (R32) series, combine both under the code PC 02 ("Top temperature protection of compressor or High temperature protection of IPM module", 3 flashes), with the compressor top check at more than 90 °C. In plain terms, something in the outdoor unit is running too hot, usually because the coils can't shed heat, the refrigerant system has a problem, or a sensor, connection or board is faulty.

Likely causes

  • Blocked airflow at the indoor or outdoor unit: dirty filters, a clogged coil (heat exchanger), or something blocking the outdoor unit's air inlet or outlet (Midea's first check)
  • A refrigerant system problem such as a leak (low charge) or a blockage, which makes the compressor run hot; Midea's wall-mount manual lists "system leakage or block"
  • Power supply problems, listed among the supposed causes for the compressor top P2
  • A loose or bad connection at the compressor overload protector (OLP), or a failed OLP that no longer reads near zero ohms
  • On models where P2 means IPM high temperature: loose fastening screws or dried-out thermal grease between the IPM and its radiator (heat sink)
  • A faulty outdoor control board, the last item on Midea's charts

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before opening panels.

  1. Write down the code and when it shows up, especially if it happens on hot days or after the unit has run a long time. That helps a technician tell an airflow or refrigerant problem from a board problem.
  2. Clean or replace the indoor unit's filters and make sure nothing blocks the indoor unit's air intake or outlet.
  3. Clear leaves, grass, snow, furniture or covers away from the outdoor unit so air can move freely through the coil. If the outdoor coil is visibly matted with dirt, have it cleaned.
  4. Turn the system off at the breaker, wait at least 10 minutes so the compressor and electronics cool down, then restore power and restart. Midea's chart starts with exactly this restart.
  5. If P2 comes back, stop resetting it and call a licensed HVAC technician. Don't open the outdoor unit's electrical box or handle the refrigerant system yourself; Midea warns that electricity remains in the capacitors even after power is off.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Confirm the airflow through the indoor and outdoor units is unobstructed and clean the heat exchangers if needed.
  • After a 10-minute power-off, check whether the compressor top temperature is really high (Midea's PC 02 chart uses more than 90 °C). If it is, check the refrigerant system for leaks or blockages and repair it.
  • If the compressor is not actually hot, check the overload protector (OLP) wiring and connection, then measure the resistance between the OLP's two terminals: it should be zero. Replace the OLP if it is open; if it reads zero and the connection is good, replace the outdoor control PCB.
  • On models where P2 or PC 02 means IPM high temperature, check that the screws holding the PCB and IPM to the radiator are tight. If they are loose, tighten them and apply silicone thermal grease; if they are tight, Midea's chart says to replace the outdoor control PCB.
  • On models with a sealed outdoor electric control box, Midea notes the box is replaced as a whole rather than the PCB alone.
