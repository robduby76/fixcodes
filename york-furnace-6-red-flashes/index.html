Code: 6 red flashes🔥 York Gas Furnace

York Furnace 6 Red Flashes: Pressure Switch Kept Opening, 1-Hour Lockout (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-19 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Six red flashes on a York single-stage furnace control board mean the pressure switch opened repeatedly while the furnace was already running a call for heat, so the control locked the furnace out for one hour. York's manuals point to a faulty inducer, a blocked vent pipe or a faulty pressure switch. After the hour the furnace tries again on its own, but if the draft problem is still there the code comes back.

What the code means

York (Johnson Controls) furnace boards show faults with an LED behind the view port in the blower door. Fault codes flash on and off the number of times equal to the code, with a 2-second pause between codes, so 6 red flashes is code 6. York's Y91E condensing single-stage installation manual defines 6 red flashes as: while the unit was operating, the pressure switch opened five times during the call for heat; check for a faulty inducer, blocked vent pipe or faulty pressure switch; the furnace locks out for 1 hour and then restarts. The TM9E and TM9V manuals use the same wording but say four times instead of five, so the exact count depends on your board. In plain terms, the inducer proved draft and the burners lit, but the draft kept dropping out partway through the cycle. Each time the pressure switch opens, the board shuts the gas valve; after too many drops in one heat call it stops trying for an hour. That's different from 3 red flashes (the pressure switch never closed at the start of the cycle) and 2 red flashes (the switch was already closed when it should have been open). Numbering isn't the same on every York board: on the modulating YP9C/CP9C/LP9C/TP9C series, six red flashes instead means a gas valve communication error, so match the code to the chart on your own furnace door before acting on this page.

Likely causes

  • A partly blocked vent pipe or vent terminal, for example from snow, ice, leaves, or a bird or animal nest, so draft is marginal and drops out
  • On condensing (90%+) furnaces, a partly blocked combustion air intake pipe or terminal
  • On condensing furnaces, a slow or blocked condensate drain or trap, letting water back up into the inducer or pressure switch hose (York's pressure switches also monitor the condensate drain system)
  • An inducer motor that's weak, worn or failing, so it can't hold enough draft for the whole cycle
  • A cracked, loose or water-filled pressure switch hose
  • A faulty pressure switch that opens intermittently
  • Loose wiring at the pressure switch or inducer

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Confirm the code: count the red flashes through the view port before turning off power, since a power reset clears the current flash. Six flashes, a 2-second pause, then repeating is code 6. York boards also store the last five codes; with no thermostat call active, press and release the LAST ERROR (or SW) button to replay them.
  2. Check the chart on your furnace door. If you have a modulating York (YP9C, CP9C, LP9C or TP9C), 6 red flashes means a gas valve communication error instead, which is a job for a technician.
  3. Go outside and check the vent and, on condensing furnaces, the intake pipe terminals. Clear snow, ice, leaves or nests from the openings. In winter, a terminal that frosts over during long runs can cause exactly this intermittent pattern.
  4. On a condensing furnace, check that the condensate drain and trap aren't clogged and that water drains freely. Gurgling from the inducer area can mean water is backing up.
  5. With power off, look at the rubber hose running to the pressure switch. If it's loose, push it back on; if it's cracked, split or has water in it, it needs replacing. York notes the hoses should be routed so condensate can't get into the switch.
  6. After clearing a blockage, you can wait for the furnace to retry on its own after the one-hour lockout, or turn the power off and back on to restart it right away.
  7. Never jumper, bypass or adjust the pressure switch to make the furnace run. York's manuals warn that doing so lets the furnace operate under potentially hazardous conditions.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Call a technician if 6 red flashes comes back after the vent, intake, drain and hose checks.
  • A technician checks inducer operation and draft, the vent and intake piping, the condensate trap and drain, and the pressure switch hose and wiring, then tests the pressure switch and replaces the inducer or switch with the correct parts for your model.
  • On a modulating York where 6 red flashes means a gas valve communication error, a technician checks the gas valve wiring and communication to the control.
  • Because a draft problem can let combustion gases back up, any repeated pressure switch lockout is worth a professional venting check. If a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.
