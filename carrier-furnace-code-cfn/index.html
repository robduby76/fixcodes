Carrier Furnace Code CFn: Continuous Fan Mode, Not an Error (Meaning and What to Check)

Reviewed 2026-11-13 · Based on 1 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

CFn on a Carrier furnace's 3-digit display means Continuous Fan Mode: the blower is running because the thermostat's fan setting is on (R-to-G closed). It's a status readout, not a fault. Real Carrier faults show as a number with a decimal, such as 33.1.

What the code means

Newer Carrier gas furnaces with an on-board 3-digit display, such as the 59SC6A condensing furnace, show the system status after startup or after no buttons have been pressed for 60 seconds. Carrier's System Status Display Codes table (Table 20) lists Cfn as Continuous Fan Mode, with the note Continuous Fan active. The other status displays are iDl (idle, no demands), Ht (gas heating), CL (cooling or heat pump active), Hpd (gas heat during a heat pump defrost) and bLr (secondary furnace of a twinned system running its blower). Carrier's sequence of operation says that when the thermostat closes the R-to-G circuit, the blower runs at the selected continuous-blower speed. During a call for heat the blower pauses during igniter warm-up, ignition and the blower-on delay, then runs at heat speed and returns to continuous-fan speed after the selected blower-off delay. Cfn also appears in the furnace's Main Menu (Table 21), where it is the Constant Fan blower speed (airflow) setting rather than a status. A fault shows as ##.#, and the status LED also flashes the major code.

Likely causes

  • Nothing is wrong: the thermostat fan switch is set to On (not Auto), so the blower runs continuously and the display shows Cfn
  • A smart thermostat has a circulate or fan-on schedule that closes R-to-G even with no heating or cooling call
  • Someone scrolled into the Main Menu, where Cfn is the constant-fan blower-speed setting rather than a status
  • The blower is running at the continuous speed between heating or cooling cycles, as designed
  • If the fan runs but airflow feels weak, a dirty air filter or blocked return grilles are the usual cause

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Read what the display shows once it returns to status. Cfn, iDl, Ht, CL, Hpd and bLr are normal operating modes. A number with a decimal, like 33.1, is an active fault; look up that code instead.
  2. If you want the blower to run only when heating or cooling, set the thermostat fan to Auto. The furnace should then go to iDl when there is no demand.
  3. On a smart thermostat, check for a circulate, fan-on or schedule setting that keeps the fan running.
  4. Replace the furnace air filter and make sure supply and return vents aren't blocked.
  5. If you landed in the menu by pressing buttons, wait 60 seconds without touching anything and the display returns to status. Don't change the Cfn airflow setting unless an installer advises it.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The display shows a ##.# fault code, or the status LED is flashing a code, and it keeps coming back.
  • The blower runs continuously with the thermostat fan set to Auto, which can point to wiring or a thermostat problem.
  • The continuous-fan airflow needs to be set for your ductwork; Carrier points installers to its air delivery table.
  • Working with the blower door off. The door switch cuts 115-volt power to the control, and Carrier warns not to tape or bypass it.
