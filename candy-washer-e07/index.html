Code: E07🧺 Candy Washer

Candy Washing Machine E07 (E7) Error Code: Door Lock Problem, Door Stuck Locked

Reviewed 2026-11-04 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

E07 (shown as E7 or E:07, or as 7 LED flashes on models without a display) on a Candy washing machine points to a problem with the door or the door lock. Candy's user manual calls E7 a door problem: check the door is fully shut and that no clothing is caught in it, and if the door is stuck locked, switch off, unplug, wait 2 to 3 minutes and try the door again. Candy's service note for its Smart Touch range describes E:07 as a door safety lock that is faulty and stuck in the closed position, or its wiring. If E07 keeps coming back after the basic checks, the door lock (interlock) or its wiring usually needs replacing.

What the code means

Candy's UM02 washing machine user manual lists E7 (7 flashes of the LEDs on models without a display) as "Door problem", with the owner checks: make sure the door is correctly closed, make sure the clothes inside the drum are not stopping the door from closing fully, and if the door is locked, turn off and unplug the machine, wait 2 to 3 minutes and reopen the door. Candy Hoover Service's technical note 00188/LB, which covers Candy Smart Touch and Hoover One Touch machines with the Invensys power board, is more specific: E:07 means "Defective Door Safety Lock Device (blocked closed) and/or Wiring". That is different from E:01 in the same note, which covers a door lock that won't lock (or a drum braking device fault). In other words, on these models E07 usually means the lock is stuck shut and the board can't confirm it has released. On LED-only models, the note says four LEDs flash together once a second for the number of the error (7 times for E07), then a long pause, repeating until the machine is switched off. Whitegoods Help's Candy code guide warns that E07 is a door lock code on some Candy model generations but relates to the motor circuit on others, so check the code table in your own manual if it has one before buying parts.

Likely causes

  • Door not fully closed, or clothing, a seal fold or a foreign object stopping it closing properly
  • Door lock (interlock) stuck in the locked position after a cycle or a power cut
  • Faulty door safety lock device (interlock) that no longer releases or reports its position correctly
  • Loose, damaged or corroded wiring between the door lock and the main power board
  • On some older model generations, a motor circuit fault rather than a door fault (check your manual)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Open the door if you can, push any clothing back into the drum so nothing is caught between the door and the rubber seal, then close the door firmly until it clicks and restart the programme.
  2. If the door is locked and won't open, switch the machine off, unplug it (or switch it off at the wall), wait 2 to 3 minutes, then try the door again. This is the step Candy's manual gives for E7.
  3. If there's still water in the drum, don't force the door. Drain it first through the pump filter at the bottom front of the machine, with towels and a shallow tray down, then try the door again.
  4. Check the door hinge and the hook (catch) on the door for damage or misalignment, and wipe any detergent build-up off the catch and the lock opening.
  5. If E07 keeps returning after a power reset, stop there. Testing or replacing the door interlock means removing panels near mains-voltage parts, so only do it if you're competent with appliance electrics, and always unplug the machine first.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E07 comes back every cycle after the door and power-reset checks.
  • The door stays locked after unplugging and waiting, and the drum is empty.
  • The door lock clicks but the machine still shows E07, which points to the interlock or its wiring.
  • Your manual lists E7 as a motor fault rather than a door fault, or you see a burning smell or scorch marks.
  • The machine is under warranty: contact Candy's customer service rather than opening it.
