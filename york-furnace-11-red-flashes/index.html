Code: 11 red flashes🔥 York Gas Furnace

York Furnace 11 Red Flashes: Main Limit Open Over 5 Minutes, Likely Blower Failure (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-23 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Eleven red flashes on a York furnace control board mean the main limit switch opened and stayed open for more than 5 minutes. York says this is usually caused by a failed blower motor or blower wheel, so the control assumes the blower isn't moving air and goes into a hard lockout. It won't restart on its own: power has to be turned off and back on after the problem is fixed. Check the filter and listen for the blower, but a dead blower motor, capacitor or wheel is normally a job for an HVAC technician.

What the code means

York (Johnson Controls) furnace boards show faults with an LED behind the view port in the blower door. Fault codes flash the number of times equal to the code, with a 2-second pause between codes, so 11 red flashes is code 11. York's Y91E condensing single-stage installation manual defines 11 red flashes as the main limit switch having opened its normally-closed contacts and remained open for more than 5 minutes, usually caused by a failed blower motor or blower wheel. The control enters a hard lockout and power must be cycled off and on to reset it after the problem is corrected. The sequence starts as code 4: when the main limit opens (4 red flashes), the control runs the supply air blower and inducer to cool the heat exchanger. If the limit hasn't closed within 5 minutes, the control operates as if the blower is not functioning, starts a hard lockout and switches to 11 red flashes. The TM9E manual uses the same wording. The TM9V manual says code 11 covers a limit circuit open for more than 5 minutes and less than 15 minutes; past 15 minutes it assumes a manual-reset rollout switch has opened and shows 5 red flashes instead. The modulating YP9C/CP9C/LP9C/TP9C manual calls Eleven Red Flashes 'main blower failure': the main limit opens and fails to reclose within 5 minutes, indicating the blower motor or blower wheel has failed. The wiring-diagram fault charts on these furnaces sum it up as 'Check blower motor/wiring'. Why it matters: the limit switch is a safety that opens when the heat exchanger gets too hot. If the blower is running, that heat is carried away and the limit recloses quickly; if it stays open for 5 minutes, the most likely reason is that air isn't moving. Always match the code to the chart on your own furnace door, since York boards differ.

Likely causes

  • Failed blower motor (York says code 11 is usually caused by a failed blower motor or blower wheel)
  • Blower wheel loose on the motor shaft, broken, or packed with dirt so it moves little air
  • Failed blower run capacitor on PSC-motor furnaces, or a failed ECM/X13 motor or motor module on variable-speed models
  • Loose, burned or disconnected blower motor wiring or connector (the door chart says check blower motor/wiring)
  • Severe airflow restriction that keeps the limit open: a badly clogged filter, closed or blocked supply/return registers, or a blocked return (York lists dirty filter, duct sizing and blower speed under the related 4 red flash limit code)
  • Less often, a limit switch that has failed open or its wiring, which a technician confirms with a meter

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Confirm the code: count the red flashes through the view port before turning off power, since a power reset clears the current flash. Eleven flashes, a 2-second pause, then repeating is code 11. York boards also store the last five codes; with no thermostat call active, press and release the LAST ERROR button to replay them. A 4 red flash code just before it in the history fits the York sequence.
  2. Check the air filter and replace it if it's dirty, and make sure supply and return registers are open and nothing is blocking the return grille.
  3. Turn the thermostat to Fan ON (or call for cooling) and listen and feel for airflow at the registers. If the blower doesn't run, hums without spinning, or barely moves air, the blower motor, capacitor or wheel is the likely problem.
  4. Reset: after checking the filter, turn the furnace power off at the switch or breaker, wait about 30 seconds, and turn it back on. Code 11 is a hard lockout and won't clear by itself. If it comes back, stop resetting and call a technician, since running the burner without proper airflow overheats the heat exchanger.
  5. With power off, you can open the blower door and look for an obviously loose wheel, heavy dirt buildup or a disconnected plug, but leave motor, capacitor and electrical testing to a pro. Capacitors can hold a charge even with power off.
  6. Never jumper or bypass the limit switch or any other safety control.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Call an HVAC technician if code 11 returns after a filter change and reset, or if the blower won't run.
  • They check line and control voltage to the blower motor, test the run capacitor on PSC motors, or test the ECM motor and module, and check the motor wiring and board connections.
  • They inspect and clean the blower wheel, confirm it's tight on the shaft, and replace the motor, capacitor or wheel with the correct part for your model.
  • They verify airflow, blower speed setting and temperature rise against the furnace rating plate, check the main limit switch and wiring, and look at the duct system if the limit keeps opening with a working blower.
