York Furnace 5 Red Flashes: Rollout Switch Open or Limit Open Over 15 Minutes, Hard Lockout (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-24 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Five red flashes on a York furnace control board mean the normally closed flame rollout switch has opened, or (on Y91E and TM9V boards) the limit circuit has been open for more than 15 minutes, which the control treats as a tripped manual-reset rollout switch. The furnace goes into a hard lockout. York says to check combustion air, inducer operation, and for a primary heat exchanger failure or burner problem. A rollout trip means flame or heat escaped the burner area, so if the switch trips again after one reset, shut the furnace down and call an HVAC technician.

What the code means

York (Johnson Controls) furnace boards show faults with an LED behind the view port in the blower door. Fault codes flash the number of times equal to the code, with a 2-second pause between codes, so 5 red flashes is code 5. York's Y91E condensing single-stage installation manual defines 5 red flashes as the normally closed rollout switch having opened, or the limit switch having been open longer than 15 minutes. The rollout control is manually reset. York says to check for proper combustion air, proper inducer operation, and primary heat exchanger failure or burner problem; the control enters a hard lockout and power must be cycled off and on after the problem is corrected. The TM9E manual uses the rollout-switch wording only. The TM9V manual explains the timing: if the main limit hasn't closed within 5 minutes the control shows 11 red flashes (blower assumed failed), and if it still hasn't closed after 15 minutes the control assumes a manual-reset rollout switch has opened and switches to 5 red flashes. On the modulating YP9C/CP9C/LP9C/TP9C series, Five Red Flashes means the rollout switch or the condensate pressure switch is open; that manual says to check the rollout switch(es) on the burner assembly, reset it by pushing the small button in the center, and contact a qualified serviceman if it can't be reset or trips again. Why it matters: York's manuals describe the rollout switches as manual-reset controls mounted on the burner assembly that shut off the gas valve when the area around the burner gets too hot, and say their operation indicates a malfunction in the combustion air blower (inducer), heat exchanger, or a blocked vent pipe connection. Always match the code to the chart on your own furnace door, since York boards differ.

Likely causes

  • Blocked or disconnected vent pipe or combustion air intake (York lists a blocked vent pipe connection among the causes of a rollout trip)
  • Inducer (combustion air blower) not running correctly, so combustion gases aren't pulled through the heat exchanger
  • Cracked or failed primary heat exchanger, or a burner problem such as misaligned or dirty burners (York: check for primary heat exchanger failure or burner problem)
  • Not enough combustion air reaching the furnace, for example in a closet or small room with blocked air openings
  • On Y91E and TM9V boards: the main limit stayed open for more than 15 minutes (usually after an 11 red flash blower failure), which the control reports as 5 red flashes
  • On YP9C/CP9C/LP9C/TP9C modulating furnaces: an open condensate pressure switch, for example from a blocked condensate drain or trap
  • Less often, a rollout switch that has failed open or loose wiring in the rollout circuit, which a technician confirms with a meter

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Confirm the code: count the red flashes through the view port before turning off power, since a power reset clears the current flash. Five flashes, a 2-second pause, then repeating is code 5. York boards also store the last five codes; with no thermostat call active, press and release the LAST ERROR button to replay them. An 11 red flash code just before it in the history points to a blower/limit problem rather than a flame rollout.
  2. Look for obvious problems you can fix safely: check that the vent and intake pipes outside are clear of snow, leaves, nests or ice, that the indoor vent connections aren't separated, and that the filter is clean and the registers are open.
  3. On YP9C/CP9C/LP9C/TP9C modulating furnaces, check that the condensate drain hose and trap aren't clogged or kinked, since that series also uses 5 red flashes for an open condensate pressure switch.
  4. York's YP9C manual says the rollout switch on the burner assembly is reset by pushing the small button in its center. If you're comfortable doing so, reset it once with power off, then turn power off and back on to clear the hard lockout. If it can't be reset or trips again, stop and call a qualified technician.
  5. Do not keep resetting a rollout switch. A repeat trip means flame or hot gases are escaping the burner area, which can damage the furnace and is a carbon monoxide risk. Make sure your CO alarms are working.
  6. Never jumper or bypass the rollout switch, limit switch or any other safety control.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Call an HVAC technician if the rollout switch trips again after one reset, can't be reset, or if you smell gas or see scorching around the burner area.
  • They check inducer operation and draft, inspect the vent and intake piping for blockage, and confirm the furnace has adequate combustion air.
  • They inspect the burners for alignment, dirt and flame pattern, and examine the primary heat exchanger for cracks or blockage, which can cause flame rollout.
  • They test the rollout switches and limit circuit with a meter, check the condensate pressure switch and drain on modulating models, and, if the code followed 11 red flashes, check the blower motor, capacitor and wheel.
