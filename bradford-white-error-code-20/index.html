Code: 20🚿 Bradford White Tankless Water Heater

Bradford White Error Code 20: High Limit Overheat Switch Open (Infiniti Tankless)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error code 20 (shown as Er.20) on a Bradford White Infiniti GS or GR condensing tankless water heater means the high limit overheat switch has opened. The switch is normally closed; when it opens, the heater shuts down and locks out. Bradford White lists 20 as a hard lock, so it needs a manual reset with the Power button. If it comes back, the switch connections and the switch itself need checking, and an overheating heat exchanger usually points to a flush.

What the code means

Bradford White's Infiniti GS (RTGS199) and GR (RTGR199) installation and operation manuals define code 20 as "High Limit Overheat Switch – Closed is Normal, Open is Fault." The high limit overheat switch is a safety switch that opens when it gets too hot, and the control treats an open switch as a fault. It is a hard lock, which Bradford White defines as a lockout that requires a manual reset (a soft lock resets itself when the issue subsides). The error flashes on the display as Er followed by the number. Two related codes are worth knowing: 16 means the operating temperature sensor or hot water (DHW) sensor read water hotter than 199°F (93°C), and 43 means the separate burner overheat switch is open. Code 20 on the Infiniti GS and GR is not the same as the codes on Bradford White tank water heaters, which use their own code lists, and older Infiniti K-series units use a different letter-number system.

Likely causes

  • The heat exchanger overheated and tripped the high limit overheat switch, for example from too little water flow or scale build-up (Bradford White lists flushing the heat exchanger for the related hot-water-temperature code 60)
  • A loose or poor connection at the high limit overheat switch
  • A failed high limit overheat switch that no longer closes, even when cool

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Press the Power button to clear the code, as Bradford White's table instructs, and see whether the heater runs normally.
  2. If the heater has been running hard, let it cool before resetting; the switch must close again before the heater can restart.
  3. Clean the cold water inlet filter. Bradford White's maintenance steps include removing it, cleaning out residue and making sure the cap is tightened securely.
  4. If you have hard water and the heater hasn't been flushed recently, plan a heat exchanger flush. Bradford White recommends a straight white vinegar solution (or a commercial descaler at the maker's dilution) circulated for at least 1 hour at about 4 gallons per minute through the isolation valves, but says flushing is a complicated job that should be done by an authorized technician or licensed professional, and that improper maintenance can void the warranty.
  5. Don't keep resetting the heater if code 20 returns; the switch is a safety device telling you something is overheating.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • A technician should inspect the high limit overheat switch connections and, with the switch at room temperature, measure its continuity; Bradford White's steps for the related burner overheat code say to replace a switch that is not closed.
  • Have the heat exchanger flushed and the water flow checked if the switch tests good but the code keeps returning.
  • Bradford White's installer menu can set a flushing alarm after 365, 700 or 1000 hours of hot water use, which a technician can turn on as a reminder.
  • Bradford White technical support is listed on its service literature at 800-334-3393.
