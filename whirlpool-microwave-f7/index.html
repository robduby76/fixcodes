Code: F7🔧 Whirlpool Microwave

Whirlpool Microwave F7 Error Code: Power to the Magnetron Interrupted (What to Do)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

F7 on a Whirlpool over-the-range microwave means the oven couldn't carry out the cooking function. Whirlpool's own article points to the control board or its wiring, and the code tables for Whirlpool's inverter models (such as the GH6177XP family) say it more specifically: power to the magnetron, the part that makes the microwave energy, was interrupted. Turn the breaker off for one full minute, turn it back on, and watch the display for a minute. If F7 comes back, stop using the microwave and book service, because the checks that follow (magnetron, its thermostat, door interlock switches, inverter board) are inside the high-voltage section.

What the code means

Whirlpool's Product Help article for F7 on over-the-range microwaves says the oven could not execute the function and that there may be a problem with the appliance control board or the associated wiring. Its owner step is a power reset: turn off the circuit breaker for one minute, turn it back on, monitor the microwave for one minute to make sure the code does not return, then verify operation. On Whirlpool inverter microwave models such as the GH6177XPS and GH6177XPB, the error code table from the model's tech sheet (as listed on MyPros+) defines F7 as power to magnetron interrupted, and MyPros+ also lists a Whirlpool service pointer titled MicroHood Combination Ovens F7 Error Code for those models. Appliantology, a technician community, explains that on these Panasonic-built inverter models F7 usually means the inverter board, the circuit that powers the magnetron, has failed, but technicians there warn that a shorted magnetron or an open thermostat on the magnetron or cavity can throw the same code, so those get checked before an inverter board is ordered. In short, anything that stops power reaching the magnetron mid-cycle can set F7, which is why it often shows up right after you press Start.

Likely causes

  • A temporary control glitch that a one-minute breaker reset clears, the first step in Whirlpool's F7 article
  • A loose connection or damaged wiring between the control board and the inverter or magnetron circuit
  • A failed inverter board on inverter models, the most common cause technicians on Appliantology report
  • A shorted magnetron, which Appliantology technicians say can throw F7 even with a good inverter
  • An open magnetron or cavity thermostat (thermal cutoff), often after the microwave overheated
  • A door interlock switch that isn't making contact, which cuts power to the cooking circuit
  • A faulty appliance control board, the part Whirlpool's article names

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Microwaves store lethal voltage in the high-voltage capacitor and inverter even when unplugged. Leave internal testing of the magnetron, inverter and interlocks to a trained technician.

  1. Turn off the microwave's circuit breaker (or unplug it if you can reach the plug) for one full minute, then turn it back on.
  2. Watch the display for about a minute with the microwave idle, then heat a cup of water for 30 seconds to confirm it actually heats.
  3. Close the door firmly and make sure nothing is stopping it from latching. A door that doesn't latch cleanly can keep the interlock switches from closing.
  4. If the microwave has been running long cycles, let it cool for an hour before trying again, and check that the vent grille on top isn't blocked, since overheating can open a thermostat.
  5. If F7 comes back, stop using the microwave and leave the breaker off until it is repaired. Note the model number from the label inside the door frame before you call.
  6. Don't open the cabinet to test the magnetron or inverter yourself. Microwaves hold a high-voltage capacitor and inverter circuit that can stay charged after unplugging.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • F7 comes back after a one-minute breaker reset: book service. Whirlpool's customer service number on its product help pages is 1-866-698-2538.
  • The microwave hums, the light and turntable run, but food doesn't heat: that matches power not reaching the magnetron, so have a technician test the magnetron, thermostats and inverter.
  • You smell burning, see sparks, or see a scorched spot through the vents: switch off the breaker right away and leave it off until a technician checks it.
