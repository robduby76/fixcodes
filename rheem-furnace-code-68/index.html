Rheem Furnace Code 68: No Communications with the Blower Motor

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Code 68 on a Rheem or Ruud communicating gas furnace means the furnace control board can't talk to the ECM blower motor. Rheem lists it as a critical fault: the furnace won't run in any mode, so you lose heat, cooling and fan until it's fixed. The usual causes are a loose motor connector, no line voltage reaching the motor, a damaged control cable between the board and the motor, or a failed motor.

What the code means

Rheem's fault code label for its serial communicating gas furnaces lists 68 under blower faults as "No Communications with Blower Motor." These furnaces use a communicating ECM blower motor that takes its speed commands from the integrated furnace control over a low-voltage control cable, while line voltage powers the motor through a separate 5-pin connector. The (-)96V installation manual says that if the motor isn't communicating properly with the furnace control, code 68 is displayed any time there is a call for cooling, heating or continuous fan. The 802V manual lists "No Blower Communications" (68) as a fault where the furnace will not operate in any mode. So 68 isn't a burner, flame or venting problem: it means the board sent a blower request and got no answer from the motor.

Likely causes

  • The 5-pin line-voltage connector at the blower motor is loose or its pins aren't fully seated, so the motor has no power
  • No 115-120 volt power reaching the motor because of a wiring fault between the furnace and the motor
  • The low-voltage motor control cable between the furnace control (connector P5) and the motor is loose, unplugged or damaged at either end
  • The blower door isn't closed, so the door switch keeps power off the motor while the board is asking for the blower
  • A failed ECM blower motor or its electronics, which Rheem's manual points to once power and wiring check out

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Write down the code and anything else the display shows. On these furnaces the display can alternate between 10 (one-hour lockout) and the fault behind it, and fault history shows at power-up, most recent first.
  2. Turn the furnace off at its power switch or breaker, wait about 30 seconds, and turn it back on with the blower door fully closed and latched. A door that's not pressing the door switch keeps the motor from getting power.
  3. With the power off, open the blower compartment and look at the motor. Check that the 5-pin power plug and the smaller control plug are pushed all the way onto the motor, and that the control cable is plugged in firmly at the furnace control board. Look for pinched, chafed or burned wires.
  4. Restore power, close the door, and run the fan from the thermostat. If 68 comes back right away on any call for heat, cooling or fan, the motor still isn't answering, and the next checks are live-voltage tests.
  5. Leave the furnace's memory card where it is. Rheem warns that the card holds model data the furnace needs and must stay with the furnace, even if the control board is replaced.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Code 68 returns after power-cycling and reseating the connectors. The furnace won't heat, cool or run the fan until it's cleared, so in cold weather it's an urgent call.
  • Checking line voltage at the motor: Rheem's manual has the tech verify 115-120 VAC between pins 4 and 5 of the 5-pin connector with the door switch closed, then fix the wiring if there's none.
  • Testing the motor directly: with the control plug pulled from P5 on the furnace control, applying 24 VAC to pins 3 and 4 should start the motor at about 75% of capacity. If it runs well the motor probably isn't the problem; if it doesn't and the wiring is good, the manual says the motor likely needs replacing.
  • Replacing the ECM motor or the furnace control board. Use the exact Rheem/Ruud replacement for your model, and never a control board taken from another furnace, which Rheem says can load the wrong model data.
  • You smell gas or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds. Leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
