Code: F3🔧 Daikin Mini Split

Daikin Error Code F3: Discharge Pipe Temperature Too High (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 6 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

F3 (shown as F3-00 on newer remotes) on a Daikin mini split means the outdoor unit's discharge pipe, the hot gas line leaving the compressor, got too hot, or the sensor on that pipe is reporting a bad reading. Daikin calls it "Discharge pipe temperature control" or "Malfunction of discharge pipe temperature". The compressor stops to protect itself and restarts once the pipe cools, but if it keeps happening the system shuts down. The most common real causes are low refrigerant, a closed or faulty service valve, a bad expansion valve, or a failed discharge pipe thermistor, so F3 that keeps coming back needs a technician.

What the code means

Daikin's ductless service manuals (for example SiBE041029_A, SiBE041102_A and the multi-zone manual SiBE121135_A) describe F3 like this: the outdoor board watches the discharge pipe thermistor, and if the temperature rises above a set limit the compressor stops. The error clears when the pipe cools below a lower limit, and if it repeats the system shuts down; it resets after about 60 minutes of continuous running with no other error. The limits depend on the unit size and compressor speed, for example 110 °C (230 °F) stop and 95 °C (203 °F) restart on smaller classes, and 120 °C (248 °F) and 107 °C (225 °F) on larger ones. Daikin's newer R-32 manuals (ESIE16-02B, ESIE17-08, Perfera FTXM-R) list it as F3-00 "Discharge pipe temperature abnormality": the unit keeps running at first, stops if the high temperature repeats 10 times within 200 minutes, and can then be reset from the remote. Daikin's Simple Self-Diagnosis code list gives the causes as a defective discharge pipe thermistor, an abnormal discharge pipe temperature, a defective outdoor control board, or a bad connector contact.

Likely causes

  • Refrigerant shortage from a leak or an undercharged install, which makes the compressor run hot (the cause Daikin's R-32 manuals list first).
  • A service (stop) valve on the outdoor unit left closed or partly closed after installation, or a faulty stop valve.
  • A faulty electronic expansion valve or its coil, or a clogged refrigerant circuit.
  • Moisture or non-condensable gas (air) in the refrigerant, for example from poor evacuation; Daikin notes moisture can freeze in the expansion valve.
  • A faulty four-way (reversing) valve.
  • A defective or loose discharge pipe thermistor, or a bad outdoor heat exchanger or outdoor temperature thermistor.
  • A defective outdoor unit control board.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off the breaker and outdoor disconnect before anyone opens the outdoor unit. Inverter boards can hold a dangerous charge after power is removed, the discharge line can be hot enough to burn, and refrigerant work (including mildly flammable R-32) must be done by a certified technician.

  1. Write down the full code (F3 or F3-00) and when it happens (cooling or heating, hot day or mild day). That helps the technician.
  2. Turn the system off with the remote, then switch off the breaker or outdoor disconnect, wait a few minutes and turn it back on. Daikin's newer models allow a manual reset from the remote once the pipe has cooled.
  3. Make sure the outdoor unit has clear airflow: remove leaves, snow or anything stacked against it, and keep the area around it clear.
  4. If the unit was installed or serviced recently, tell the installer about F3 right away. A stop valve left closed or a poor evacuation are installation problems Daikin lists for this code.
  5. If F3 returns, stop resetting it and call a licensed HVAC technician. Running a compressor repeatedly at high discharge temperature can damage it, and the remaining checks involve refrigerant and sealed-system parts.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Follow Daikin's F3 flowchart order: check the thermistors (discharge pipe, outdoor heat exchanger, outdoor temperature) and their connectors; check the outdoor electronic expansion valve and coil; then run the refrigerant line check (charge, four-way valve, moisture, stop valves); replace the outdoor PCB only if everything else checks out.
  • Confirm both stop valves are fully open and check the charge against the nameplate; Daikin's corrective action for shortage is to find the leak and charge the correct amount.
  • If moisture or non-condensables are suspected, recover the refrigerant, pull a vacuum and recharge with virgin refrigerant, as Daikin's R-32 manuals specify.
  • On R-32 models, check that the outdoor main PCB HAP LED is blinking at regular intervals and that the correct spare part is fitted before condemning the board.
  • Stop limits vary by model and capacity class (for example 110/95 °C on smaller classes and 120/107 °C on larger ones in the SiBE manuals); use the service manual for the exact outdoor model.
  • Daikin says to turn off the power switch before connecting or disconnecting any connector, or parts may be damaged.
