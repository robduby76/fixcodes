Code: LF🧺 Whirlpool Washer

Whirlpool Washer LF Error Code: Long Fill, Taking Too Long to Fill (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 5 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

LF (long fill) on a Whirlpool washer means the washer is taking too long to fill. Whirlpool groups it with F8 E1, which means the washer isn't detecting the correct amount of incoming water. Most of the time the cause is outside the washer: a water faucet that's off or only partly open, a kinked hose, clogged inlet screens, or a drain hose pushed too far into the standpipe so water siphons out as fast as it comes in. Check the hot and cold supply, the hoses and screens, and the drain hose setup before calling for service.

What the code means

Whirlpool's Product Help article for top-load washers says that if F8 E1 or LF (long fill) appears in the display, the washer is taking too long to fill. Its companion article explains that F8 E1 (shown as Lo FL on some models) means the washer is not detecting the correct amount of incoming water. These washers have sensors that detect the water supply and need water going to the valve through both the hot and cold hoses; if the control doesn't detect both, it treats it as a problem with the water pressure sensor, locks the lid, and runs an 8-minute pump-out. On models showing F8 E1 or Lo FL, pressing Power starts a drain routine of about 8 to 10 minutes during which the pump runs and the control won't accept input, so Whirlpool says to wait 10 minutes before trying anything. A very common hidden cause is siphoning: if more than 4.5 inches of drain hose sits in the standpipe, the hose is taped in, or the standpipe is under 39 inches tall, water drains out while the washer fills, so it never reaches the expected level. Whirlpool's technician sheets give the control logic: tech sheet W10405149A says LF flashes when the water level doesn't change for a period of time, or water is present but the control doesn't detect the level changing, and tech sheet W11448643 Rev B lists F8E1 as "No Fill, Long Fill" with low water pressure meaning fill times longer than six minutes. Both sheets also point to the pressure hose that runs from the tub to the control's water-level sensor, which can be pinched, kinked, plugged, or leaking air. Note that some cycles fill slowly on purpose: Whirlpool says the Bulky cycle can take 10 to 12 minutes to fill, and Deep Water Wash also lengthens fill time, without that being a fault.

Likely causes

  • The hot or cold water faucet is off or only partly open (these washers need both hoses supplying water)
  • A kinked inlet hose, or an old hose clogged with mineral build-up
  • Hot and cold hoses connected to the wrong valves
  • Clogged inlet screens on the back of the washer's water valve
  • An anti-flood or flood-safe device on the supply line reducing water pressure
  • Siphoning: more than 4.5 in (113 mm) of drain hose in the standpipe, the hose taped into the drain, or a standpipe shorter than 39 in (99 cm)
  • Low household water pressure (Whirlpool's tech sheet treats fill times over six minutes as low pressure)
  • A pinched, kinked, plugged, or leaking pressure hose between the tub and the water-level sensor
  • Less commonly, a failed water inlet valve or a main control that isn't switching the valve on

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. If the lid is locked and the washer is pumping out, let the drain routine finish (Whirlpool says about 8 to 10 minutes) before pressing anything else.
  2. Make sure both the hot and cold faucets behind the washer are fully open. Whirlpool says both hoses must be connected and supplying water.
  3. Check that the hoses aren't kinked behind the washer, and that the red hose goes to the hot inlet and the blue hose to the cold inlet.
  4. With the water off, remove the hoses (have a bucket ready) and run water through each one at a sink. Replace any hose that flows weakly.
  5. Look at the inlet screens on the back of the water valve. If they're clogged, clean them in place with an old toothbrush, mild soap and water, without pulling the screens out.
  6. If you have an anti-flood device on the supply line, try connecting the hoses directly, as Whirlpool recommends.
  7. Check the drain hose: no more than 4.5 in should go into the standpipe, it should be held by the U-shaped hose form, it must not be taped in (leave an air gap), and the standpipe should be at least 39 in tall.
  8. Run a Normal cycle and watch the fill. Remember that Bulky and Deep Water Wash cycles fill more slowly by design.
  9. If LF keeps coming back with good water flow and a correct drain setup, stop there. The next checks (pressure hose, valve and control tests) are done inside the cabinet.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • LF or F8 E1 returns even though both faucets are open, the hoses flow freely, the screens are clean and the drain hose isn't siphoning.
  • Water doesn't enter the washer at all, or only one temperature fills. A technician runs the valve test from the tech sheet and checks the valve coils and the control's valve outputs.
  • The pressure hose from the tub to the water-level sensor needs checking for kinks, blockage or an air leak, which means opening the washer.
  • The washer overfills or the code appears together with other water-level codes, which can point to the pressure sensor or main control.
  • The washer is still under warranty. Schedule Whirlpool service instead of opening the cabinet.
