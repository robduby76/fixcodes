Mitsubishi Mini Split Error Code E9: Outdoor Unit Communication (Transmitting) Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

E9 on a Mitsubishi Electric Mr. Slim or P-series system is an indoor/outdoor unit communication error detected by the outdoor unit (a transmitting error, also listed as code 6841). The outdoor control board keeps sending a signal over the connecting wire but can't get it through. Mitsubishi's service manuals point to a loose or poorly connected indoor/outdoor wire, electrical noise on that wire or the power supply, or a faulty communication circuit on the outdoor control board. A full power reset is the only owner step; if E9 comes back, the outdoor board usually needs a technician.

What the code means

Mitsubishi Electric's outdoor unit service manuals (for example the PUHZ-RP125VHA and PUZ-A18NHA2) list E9, with the four-digit equivalent 6841, as "Indoor/outdoor unit communication error (Transmitting error) (Outdoor unit)." The outdoor controller circuit board flags it in two situations: when it transmits a "1" on the communication line but reads back a "0" 30 times in a row, or when it can't find a free moment on the transmission line to send for 3 minutes. Mitsubishi's City Multi service handbook describes 6841 the same way and calls it a control communication synchronism that does not recover. The neighboring codes help narrow things down: E6 and E7 are the indoor unit's receiving and transmitting errors, and E8 (6840) is the outdoor unit failing to receive anything normally for 3 minutes. E9 specifically means the outdoor side is the one that can't transmit.

Likely causes

  • A contact failure on the indoor/outdoor connecting wire, such as a loose, corroded or partly broken connection at the indoor terminal block or the outdoor unit. This is the first cause Mitsubishi lists.
  • A defective communication circuit on the outdoor controller circuit board.
  • Electrical noise getting into the power supply feeding the system.
  • Electrical noise getting into the indoor/outdoor connecting wire, which garbles the signal between the boards.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off the breaker (or breakers) for both the indoor and outdoor units before any inspection. The cable between Mitsubishi indoor and outdoor units carries line voltage along with the communication signal, and the outdoor board sits next to high-voltage inverter parts, so leave terminal blocks and circuit boards to a licensed HVAC technician.

  1. Write down the exact code. E9 points at the outdoor unit's transmitting side, while E6, E7 and E8 point at other parts of the same communication line, and a technician will want to know which one you saw.
  2. Do a full power reset: switch off every breaker feeding the indoor and outdoor units, wait a few minutes, then switch them back on. Mitsubishi's remedy is to turn the power off and on again and see whether the error returns.
  3. After power comes back, give the system several minutes to run. E9 can take up to 3 minutes of failed transmitting before it shows again.
  4. Look outside for obvious damage to the cable or line set cover running between the units, such as chewed wiring, crushed covers or recent digging, trimming or construction nearby.
  5. Think about what changed recently. E9 right after an installation, a repair or new electrical equipment on the same circuit points toward a wiring connection or electrical noise, so call the installer or an electrician first.
  6. Don't open the outdoor unit or the indoor terminal cover yourself. The connecting cable between Mitsubishi units carries line voltage along with the signal.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E9 comes back after a full power reset. A technician checks the indoor/outdoor connecting wire at both units for disconnection or looseness, which is Mitsubishi's first check.
  • The wiring checks out and E9 keeps returning. Mitsubishi's service manuals say to replace the outdoor controller circuit board when the error is displayed again after a power reset.
  • The code showed up after a storm, a power surge or new equipment added nearby, since noise on the power supply or the connecting wire is a listed cause and may need an electrician to track down.
  • You have more than one indoor unit on the same outdoor unit, since every unit on the communication line needs to be checked.
  • The system is new or under warranty. Contact the installing contractor or a Mitsubishi Electric Diamond Contractor first.
