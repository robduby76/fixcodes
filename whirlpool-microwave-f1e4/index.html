Code: F1E4🔧 Whirlpool Microwave

Whirlpool Microwave F1E4 Error Code: Control Board or Microwave Relay Fault

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 5 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

F1E4 on a Whirlpool over-the-range microwave means the control could not carry out a function, and Whirlpool points to the appliance control board (ACU) or its wiring. Turn the microwave's breaker off for one full minute, turn it back on, and watch the display for a minute. If F1E4 comes back, stop using the microwave and book service: depending on the model, the fix is either a new control board or a check of the microwave relay on that board, both of which are technician jobs because of the high voltage inside.

What the code means

Whirlpool's Product Help article for F1E4 on over-the-range microwaves says the oven could not execute the function and that there may be a problem with the Appliance Control Board or the associated wiring. Its only owner step is a power reset: turn off the circuit breaker for one minute, turn it back on, monitor the microwave for one minute to make sure the code does not return, then verify operation. The technician sheets inside these microwaves are more specific, and they differ by model. Tech sheets W11279380 and W11463265 list F1E4 as an MW Relay failure: check the wiring to relay 4903 on the control board and check whether that relay's contacts have welded closed, then, if the problem persists, check the door (P2-2), neutral (P1-3) and line (P1-1) voltages against the board's pin voltage matrix. Tech sheet W11268412 lists F1E4 as a Microwave ACU PCBA failure with a single repair, replace the control board, and the newer touch-screen sheet W11381054 says the same (Microwave ACU failure, replace ACU). Either way, the code sits on the main control board side of the microwave, not on a part an owner can service. Every one of these sheets also notes that many failure codes clear with a one-minute power cycle, which is why that is the first step. F4E4, the humidity sensor error, is a different code with its own test.

Likely causes

  • A temporary control glitch, which Whirlpool's sheets say a one-minute power cycle often clears
  • A failed appliance control board (ACU), the repair named on tech sheets W11268412 and W11381054
  • A microwave power relay (relay 4903 on the control board) whose contacts have welded closed, on models covered by tech sheets W11279380 and W11463265
  • Loose or damaged wiring to the relay or control board
  • A door, line or neutral voltage problem at the control board, which the relay procedure checks if the fault persists

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before opening panels.

  1. Turn off the microwave's circuit breaker (or unplug it if you can reach the plug) for one full minute, then turn it back on.
  2. Watch the display for about a minute with the microwave idle. If F1E4 does not come back, try a short timed heat with a cup of water to confirm it works.
  3. If F1E4 returns, stop using the microwave and leave it off at the breaker until it is repaired. A relay stuck closed is not something to keep testing.
  4. Note your model number from the label (usually inside the door frame) before you call, since the repair depends on which tech sheet your model uses.
  5. Don't remove the cabinet or control panel to look at the board. Microwaves hold a high-voltage capacitor that can stay charged even when unplugged.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • F1E4 comes back after a one-minute breaker reset: book service. Whirlpool's number from the F1E4 article is 1-866-698-2538.
  • The microwave runs with the door open, won't stop, or heats when it shouldn't: switch off the breaker right away and call for repair.
  • You see sparks, smell burning, or the display shows other F codes too: leave the breaker off and have a technician check the control board and wiring.
