York Furnace 9 Red Flashes: Reversed Polarity, Grounding Problem or Reversed Transformer Wires (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-22 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Nine red flashes on a York furnace control board mean the control sees reversed line voltage polarity, a grounding problem, or reversed low-voltage transformer wires. York says both heating and cooling are affected and the furnace won't start its ignition sequence until the problem is fixed. York's checks are polarity at the furnace and the branch circuit, the furnace ground, and making sure the flame probe isn't shorted to the chassis. This is usually a wiring problem, often after new work on the furnace or its circuit, and in most cases it's a job for an electrician or HVAC technician.

What the code means

York (Johnson Controls) furnace boards show faults with an LED behind the view port in the blower door. Fault codes flash on and off the number of times equal to the code, with a 2-second pause between codes, so 9 red flashes is code 9. York's Y91E condensing single-stage installation manual defines 9 red flashes as reversed line voltage polarity, a grounding problem, or reversed low voltage transformer wires. Both heating and cooling operations are affected. York's checks are polarity at the furnace and branch, furnace grounding, and that the flame probe is not shorted to the chassis, and the furnace will not start the ignition sequence until the problem is corrected. The TM9E and TM9V single-stage manuals use the same wording. The modulating YP9C/CP9C/LP9C/TP9C manual calls it reversed line polarity or improper grounding and adds checking the transformer ground and the L1 and neutral connections. York's wiring instructions say the black furnace lead goes to the L1 (hot) supply wire, the white lead to neutral, and the supply ground to the green equipment-ground screw. Why it matters: the control proves flame by passing a tiny current through the flame and back to ground, so it needs hot, neutral and ground on the right terminals. If they're swapped or the ground is poor, the control can't read the flame safely, so it refuses to light. York's Y91E manual also notes that twinned furnaces must be on the same power source or line leg, or control voltage polarity problems can follow. Always match the code to the chart on your own furnace door, since York boards differ.

Likely causes

  • Line (hot) and neutral reversed at the furnace, its service switch, the outlet or plug if the furnace is cord-connected, or back at the panel (York says check polarity at the furnace and branch)
  • A missing, loose or corroded ground connection between the furnace and the electrical panel, or a poor transformer ground (York lists furnace grounding and, on YP9C, the transformer ground)
  • Reversed low-voltage wires on the 24 V transformer secondary, for example after a transformer or control board replacement
  • A flame probe (flame sensor) or its wire shorted to the furnace chassis
  • On twinned furnaces, the two furnaces fed from different line legs (phases) instead of the same power source, which York warns can cause control voltage polarity problems

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Confirm the code: count the red flashes through the view port before turning off power, since a power reset clears the current flash. Nine flashes, a 2-second pause, then repeating is code 9. York boards also store the last five codes; with no thermostat call active, press and release the LAST ERROR button to replay them.
  2. Think about what changed recently. Code 9 often shows up after new wiring, a new service switch, a replaced control board or transformer, or electrical work in the house. That work is the first place to look.
  3. If your furnace plugs into an outlet, a plug-in outlet tester (a few dollars at a hardware store) can show a reversed hot/neutral or open ground at that outlet without opening anything up.
  4. If the furnace is hard-wired, don't open the junction box or the board compartment unless you're qualified. Line voltage is present even with the thermostat off. Turn the furnace power off at the switch or breaker before removing any panels.
  5. With power off, you can look for an obviously loose or broken green ground wire or a flame sensor wire rubbing against the metal cabinet, but leave the repair to a pro if you find one.
  6. Never bypass any safety control or try to repair the control board. York's manuals say the control board is not field repairable.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Call an electrician or HVAC technician for code 9, since the fix is almost always in the line-voltage wiring.
  • They check polarity at the furnace and at the branch circuit (hot to the black furnace lead, neutral to white) and correct any reversal, including at a service switch or receptacle.
  • They verify a solid equipment ground from the furnace back to the panel, check the transformer ground and secondary wiring, and confirm the flame probe and its lead aren't shorted to the chassis.
  • On twinned systems they confirm both furnaces share the same power source or line leg. If polarity, ground and wiring all test correct and code 9 persists, they look at the control board and replace it with the correct part for your model.
