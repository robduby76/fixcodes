Lennox Furnace Error 227 (E227): Low Pressure Switch Open During Ignition or Run (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-16 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Error 227 on a Lennox furnace means the low pressure switch opened during the trial for ignition or while the furnace was running. Lennox says to check the pressure at which the switch closes on a heat call, measure operating pressure, and inspect the vent and combustion air inducer for correct operation and restriction. The fault clears and normal operation resumes once it is fixed.

What the code means

Lennox service literature for its SureLight furnaces (SL280UHV, SL280UHNV, EL297 and similar) defines E 227 as "Low pressure switch open during trial for ignition or run mode." The inducer must pull enough draft to close the low pressure switch before the burners light; E227 means the switch was open when it was needed closed, either while trying to light or after the burners were already firing. The code shows on the furnace's seven-segment LED one character at a time (E, 2, 2, 7). Lennox's action is to check the pressure (inches w.c.) at which the low pressure switch closes on a heat call, measure the operating pressure, and inspect the vent and combustion air inducer for correct operation and restriction. It resumes normal operation after the fault is cleared. Related codes: E223 is the low pressure switch failed open (it did not close on the heat call), E224 is it failed closed, E225/E226 are the high pressure switch equivalents, and E228 is a combustion air inducer calibration failure (check the vent system and pressure switch wiring). E229 is information only: the furnace is lighting on high fire because the low pressure switch did not close.

Likely causes

  • A restricted or blocked vent or combustion air intake (debris, nests, snow or ice at the termination, crushed or sagging pipe)
  • A combustion air inducer that is weak, dirty, or not running correctly
  • A cracked, disconnected, or water-filled pressure switch hose, or a loose switch wire
  • On condensing models, a clogged condensate trap or drain backing water into the inducer or hose (a common field cause, not a Lennox-listed one)
  • A low pressure switch that is failing and no longer holds closed at the specified pressure

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Read the code: watch the seven-segment LED through the sight glass. Recent codes can be recalled with the diagnostic button as described in your furnace's manual.
  2. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
  3. Go outside and check the furnace's vent and combustion air intake terminations. Clear snow, ice, leaves, or nests from the openings. Do not stand on a slick roof.
  4. If your furnace is condensing (white PVC pipes), look at the condensate drain and trap. If water is backed up or the drain line is clogged, turn power off first and clear it; a technician can help if you aren't sure.
  5. Turn power off at the furnace switch or breaker and look for a pressure switch hose that is cracked, kinked, or popped off its nipple. Reseat it only if it is plainly loose.
  6. Restore power and call for heat. If E227 returns, stop resetting. Measuring operating pressure with a manometer and testing the switch is technician work. Never jumper or bypass a pressure switch.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • E227 returns after the vent terminations and condensate drain are clear.
  • Lennox's next step needs a manometer to check the closing pressure and operating pressure in inches w.c.
  • The inducer is noisy, slow, or not running, or the switch or hose is damaged.
  • You see water in the inducer housing, or signs of a blocked flue.
  • If you smell gas or a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
