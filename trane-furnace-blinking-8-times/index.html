Code: 8 flashes🔥 Trane Gas Furnace

Trane Furnace Blinking 8 Times: Low Flame Sense Signal (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-26 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

On Trane and American Standard furnaces with an integrated furnace control (IFC), a red LED blinking 8 times then pausing means a low flame sense signal. The board can still see the burner flame through the flame sensor, but the signal is weaker than it should be. Trane's modulating control guide describes it as flame current that is low but still strong enough to allow operation, so the furnace may keep heating for now. The usual cause is a dirty, coated or badly positioned flame sensor, or a poor ground or connection in the flame sense circuit. Left alone it often turns into failed ignitions and lockouts.

What the code means

Trane's installer's guide 18-CH49D1-1 lists integrated furnace control error flash codes, and 8 flashes there means "Low flame sense signal". The 2023 IFC kit guide 18-CH66D1-5B-EN (for *UX/*UY/*DX/*DY R-series furnaces) uses the same code, "Low flame sense". Trane's modulating control guide 18-CH50D1-2 (board CNT05457) lists 8 flashes as Low Flame Sense and explains it in its alert table as LO FLAME SNS, Low Flame Signal: "Flame current is low, but still strong enough to allow operation." The same guide gives the control's flame detection limits: a minimum of 1 µA DC to ensure flame detection and a maximum of 0.1 µA DC for non-detection. Trane's service facts for the *UH1/*DH1 condensing furnaces define the 8 flash fault as flame sense current less than 1 micro-amp DC, and give technicians a short check: disconnect power, connect a meter set to the DC micro-amp scale in series with the flame sensor, restore power and call for heat. If the reading is less than 1.0 micro-amp, check that the flame sensor is located correctly in the flame (fix the location if not, otherwise replace the flame sensor); if the reading is 1.0 micro-amp or more, the sensor is good and the wiring and connections are checked. Related codes on the same chart include 2 flashes (system lockout, retries or recycles exceeded), 5 flashes (flame sensed when no flame should be present), 7 flashes (gas valve circuit error) and 9 flashes (check ignitor circuit and Line N to 24VAC common voltage on 18-CH49D1-1; open inducer limit switch on the newer boards). Numbering is not the same on every Trane furnace, and newer models with a number display show E-codes instead, so match the chart on your own furnace door before acting on this page.

Likely causes

  • A dirty or oxidized flame sensor rod (a thin coating builds up over time and weakens the signal)
  • A flame sensor that is bent, loose or not sitting in the burner flame (Trane's service chart checks sensor location first)
  • A loose, corroded or damaged flame sensor wire or terminal between the sensor and the control board
  • A poor ground path back to the furnace chassis, which the flame signal depends on
  • A weak or uneven burner flame, for example from low gas pressure or dirty burners (technician check)
  • A failed flame sensor that still reads low after cleaning and correct positioning

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Count the red LED through the sight glass in the lower door: 8 flashes and a pause is this code on an IFC that uses the chart above. Slow flashing is normal with no call for heat; fast flashing is normal during a call for heat.
  2. Note whether the furnace is still heating. Trane says the flame current is low but still enough to run, so heat may continue, but treat it as an early warning and get the flame sensor looked at soon.
  3. If you are comfortable with basic furnace maintenance, turn the furnace power switch off and shut the gas to the furnace, remove the single-screw flame sensor rod from the burner area, and gently clean the rod with a fine abrasive pad (not sandpaper that leaves grit). Reinstall it in the same position, restore gas and power, and watch a heat cycle. This is general practice, not a Trane instruction.
  4. Check that the flame sensor wire is pushed firmly onto the sensor and that nothing looks burnt, cracked or corroded. Do not pull the wire hard.
  5. If 8 flashes comes back, or you are not comfortable working near the burners, call a technician. Write down the furnace model number from the rating label inside the door and the flash count before you call. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • 8 flashes returns after the flame sensor has been cleaned, or the furnace starts locking out (2 flashes).
  • Measuring flame current with a meter in series with the flame sensor on the DC micro-amp scale during a call for heat; less than 1.0 micro-amp DC is the fault level in Trane's service facts.
  • Correcting the flame sensor location or replacing the sensor; if current reads 1.0 micro-amp or more, checking the sensor wiring, connections and ground.
  • Checking burner flame and gas pressure if the signal stays weak with a good sensor.
  • Never bypass or jumper a safety control. If a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.
