P0533: A/C Refrigerant Pressure Sensor A Circuit High (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0533 means the computer is seeing a high or implausible voltage from the A/C refrigerant pressure sensor or its wiring. Common results are an A/C that won't work or works erratically and (per RepairPal) cooling fans that may run all the time. Causes include a failed sensor, damaged wiring or connector, incorrect refrigerant pressure, or less often a module fault.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0533 means

The A/C refrigerant pressure sensor tells the vehicle's computers how much pressure is in the air conditioning system, so they can switch the A/C compressor on and off and run the system properly. OBD-Codes.com explains that it is usually a three-wire pressure transducer with a 5-volt reference wire, a signal wire and a ground, and that the computer compares the signal voltage with the 5-volt reference to calculate system pressure. Depending on the vehicle, the sensor is monitored by the body control module (BCM) or the electronic climate control module, and the engine computer turns on the check engine light. P0533 is the 'circuit high' code: OBD-Codes.com says it sets when a module sees a high electrical value from pressure sensor A or its circuit, while RepairPal describes it the same way it describes P0532, as the engine computer (PCM) detecting an irrational signal from the sensor. Related codes are P0530 (circuit), P0531 (range/performance), P0532 (circuit low) and P0534 (refrigerant charge loss).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Usually safe to drive, but get it diagnosed in a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P0533 'Moderate' severity. OBD-Codes.com says HVAC codes are generally fairly low severity, but because pressurized refrigerant is involved and a refrigerant leak could be behind the code, it may be more pressing. Expect the A/C to work poorly or not at all. Never open or loosen A/C lines yourself: the refrigerant is under pressure and is hazardous. If the engine temperature gauge climbs or the cooling fans stop working, pull over safely and get it checked.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • A/C may not operate, or the compressor doesn't engage when needed
  • Vent air temperature inaccurate, erratic or fluctuating (per OBD-Codes.com)
  • Limited or abnormal heating and A/C operation (per OBD-Codes.com)
  • Cooling fans may stay on all the time (listed by RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Defective or damaged A/C refrigerant pressure sensor
  2. Refrigerant leaking at the pressure sensor (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Incorrect refrigerant pressure or level (OBD-Codes.com lists 'low or incorrect')
  4. Damaged wiring (open circuit or short to power or ground), damaged connector or poor connections
  5. Climate control module or body control module problem (per OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Engine computer (PCM) failure (listed by RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read and write down all stored codes and freeze frame data, then check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, as OBD-Codes.com recommends.
  2. Find the pressure sensor (usually on an A/C line or near a line fitting) and look at it and its wiring. OBD-Codes.com notes it has seen these sensors and harnesses damaged by moving parts under the hood when the A/C line wasn't properly secured.
  3. Check the connector for corrosion, bent or loose pins, and make sure it is fully latched. Use proper test leads so you don't damage the pins.
  4. If your scan tool can show live A/C refrigerant pressure, compare the reading with the manufacturer's expected values while the A/C runs, as OBD-Codes.com suggests.
  5. Don't open the A/C system, loosen fittings or add refrigerant to 'test' it. OBD-Codes.com says this code can usually be diagnosed without opening the refrigerant system.

Typical fixes

  • Repair damaged, chafed, open or shorted sensor wiring, and secure a loose A/C line
  • Clean or repair a corroded or loose connector
  • Replace the A/C refrigerant pressure sensor
  • Find and repair a refrigerant leak and recharge the system to the correct specification
  • Repair or replace a faulty climate control module, BCM or PCM, only after the sensor and wiring test good

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The wiring and connector look fine and the code comes back
  • Pressure testing with an A/C manifold gauge set is needed. OBD-Codes.com advises against it if you have no refrigerant experience, since refrigerant is hazardous and harmful to the environment
  • Testing the sensor itself needs the manufacturer's pin-by-pin values, which OBD-Codes.com says vary a lot between makes, temperatures and sensor types
  • A refrigerant leak is suspected; refrigerant must be recovered and recharged with proper equipment
  • The cooling fans run constantly or the engine runs hot

FAQs

Can I drive with P0533?

Usually yes. RepairPal rates it 'Moderate' and OBD-Codes.com calls HVAC codes fairly low severity, though a refrigerant leak could be involved. Expect weak or no A/C and get it diagnosed in a reasonable time. If the engine starts running hot, stop and have it checked.

Will adding refrigerant fix P0533?

Not reliably. A 'circuit high' code usually points at the sensor or its wiring, and the sources list refrigerant only as an 'incorrect' pressure or level, not specifically low. Adding refrigerant without gauges can overcharge the system, so check the sensor and wiring first and leave charging to someone with proper equipment.

Why are my cooling fans running all the time with P0533?

RepairPal lists cooling fans that stay on all the time as a P0533 symptom. Mention it to the shop along with the code, since it helps confirm the A/C pressure signal is the problem.

What's the difference between P0533 and P0532?

P0533 means the sensor signal is reading too high, and P0532 means it is reading too low. Both point to the same A/C refrigerant pressure sensor circuit, and the sources list nearly the same causes for both.
