P0244: Turbocharger/Supercharger Wastegate Solenoid A Range/Performance (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0244 means the engine computer sees a range or performance problem with wastegate (boost control) solenoid A, the valve it uses to limit turbo or supercharger boost. OBD-Codes.com says it can be an electrical fault or a mechanical or vacuum problem, and that bad electrical or vacuum connections are the most common cause. RepairPal lists a PCM failure first, while OBD-Codes.com calls a bad PCM highly unlikely.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0244 means

On a turbocharged or supercharged engine, the powertrain control module (PCM) controls boost pressure through the wastegate/boost pressure control solenoid. OBD-Codes.com says the PCM sets P0244 when there is an issue with the total boost range or engine-related performance that can't be attributed to other circuit failures, and that unlike P0243 this code can be either an electrical circuit fault or an engine mechanical issue. It notes the code applies to vehicles including certain Ford, Mercedes and Subaru models, and that troubleshooting varies by manufacturer, wastegate type and wire colors. RepairPal titles P0244 simply "Turbocharger/Supercharger Wastegate Solenoid A", the same wording it uses for P0243, and says the PCM sets it when it cannot control the solenoid. The sources disagree on the likely cause: RepairPal lists a PCM failure first, while OBD-Codes.com says a failed PCM is highly unlikely and that the most common failure is electrical or vacuum connections. Related codes for the same solenoid are P0243 (circuit), P0245 (low) and P0246 (high).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon and drive gently until you do. RepairPal rates P0244 Urgent, meaning immediate action is needed, and says the computer may disable the turbo or supercharger to prevent further damage. OBD-Codes.com lists lack of power as a symptom.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • An instrument cluster message warning of the problem (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Lack of power (OBD-Codes.com) or lack of boost from the turbo or supercharger (RepairPal)
  • The computer may disable the turbo or supercharger to protect it (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Loose, corroded or damaged electrical or vacuum connections at the solenoid, which OBD-Codes.com calls the most common failure
  2. Missing or low vacuum to a vacuum-operated wastegate solenoid from a blocked or leaking line (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. An open in the control (ground) or power supply circuit between the solenoid and the PCM (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists a wiring issue)
  4. A short to ground or short to power in the solenoid's power supply circuit (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. A faulty wastegate/boost pressure control solenoid (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  6. A failed PCM (RepairPal lists it first; OBD-Codes.com says this is highly unlikely)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle first. OBD-Codes.com says the problem may be a known issue with a known fix.
  2. Find the wastegate/boost control solenoid and inspect its wiring, connector and any vacuum lines for rubbing, bare wires, burn spots, melted plastic, cracks or disconnected hoses. Pull the connector apart and look for burned or green, corroded terminals.
  3. Clean corroded terminals with electrical contact cleaner and a plastic brush, let them dry, and apply electrical grease. Then clear the code and see if it returns.
  4. If it comes back, unplug the solenoid and measure its resistance across the two terminals with a multimeter. It should be neither open nor shorted; check the spec in your service manual. OBD-Codes.com says to replace an open or shorted solenoid.
  5. With the ignition on, check for 12 volts on the solenoid's power supply wire. OBD-Codes.com says no voltage with the key on, or voltage with the key off, points to wiring from the PCM or relay, or possibly the PCM.
  6. If power is good, OBD-Codes.com describes using a test light connected to battery positive on the solenoid's ground wire while a scan tool cycles the solenoid. It should light each time; wiggle the harness to look for a flicker that shows an intermittent connection.
  7. Make sure all mechanical linkages are attached and working, and if the solenoid is vacuum operated, confirm there is enough vacuum at it. If not, find the blockage or leak.

Typical fixes

  • Clean or repair corroded, loose or damaged electrical connectors at the solenoid (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair a leaking, blocked or disconnected vacuum line or a loose mechanical linkage (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair an open or shorted power or control wire between the PCM or relay and the solenoid (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Replace the wastegate/boost control solenoid if it tests open or shorted, or if all tests pass and the code keeps returning (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Replace or reprogram the PCM only after the solenoid, wiring and vacuum supply are ruled out; OBD-Codes.com notes a replacement PCM must be programmed to the vehicle (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The engine has very low power or goes into limp mode
  • You don't have a multimeter, test light, scan tool that can command the solenoid, or a service manual with the resistance spec
  • The solenoid, wiring and vacuum supply test good and the code keeps returning
  • A PCM replacement is suggested, since it must be programmed to the vehicle

FAQs

Can I drive with a P0244 code?

Only gently and not for long. RepairPal rates it Urgent and says the computer may shut off the turbo or supercharger to prevent damage, so expect low power until it's fixed.

What is the difference between P0243 and P0244?

OBD-Codes.com describes P0243 as an electrical circuit fault only, while P0244 is a range/performance code that can be electrical or mechanical, including vacuum problems at the solenoid. RepairPal uses the same title and description for both.

Is P0244 a bad PCM?

The sources disagree. RepairPal lists a PCM failure first, while OBD-Codes.com says a failed PCM is highly unlikely and should only be considered after the solenoid has been replaced. Check connections, vacuum, wiring and the solenoid before suspecting the computer.

How much does it cost to fix P0244?

Neither source gives a repair cost. Cleaning a connector or fixing a vacuum line is usually far cheaper than a solenoid or PCM, so get a diagnosis or written estimate before buying parts.
