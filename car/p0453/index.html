P0453: EVAP System Pressure Sensor High Input (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0453 means the engine computer is seeing a signal from the EVAP (fuel tank) pressure sensor that is higher than it should be. OBD-Codes.com points mostly to the sensor and its wiring (an open or short to voltage on the signal wire, a bad connector or lost ground), plus real high tank pressure from blocked purge hoses or an overfilled tank. RepairPal adds the fuel tank, sending unit, charcoal canister and a canister vent valve that won't open.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0453 means

The evaporative emission (EVAP) system catches fuel vapor from the fuel tank, stores it in a charcoal canister, and later draws it into the engine to be burned. The fuel tank pressure (FTP) sensor tells the engine computer how much pressure or vacuum is in the tank and EVAP system, and the computer uses it for its periodic leak tests; RepairPal calls it the main sensor the computer relies on for leak test data. OBD-Codes.com says P0453 sets when the sensor signal shows higher than normal pressure, above 4.5 volts, and says a normal reading with the key on and engine off is usually near 2.5 volts at atmospheric pressure. RepairPal defines it as the sensor reporting pressure change values above specification during the EVAP monitor test or normal driving, and says shops see it set when readings stay above spec for ten seconds after a cold start or during the EVAP test, on two successive cold starts. The sensor sits on top of the fuel tank or on or near the fuel pump and gauge module, and OBD-Codes.com notes it is part of the fuel pump assembly on some vehicles. The sources emphasize different causes: OBD-Codes.com focuses on the sensor circuit and real high pressure from blocked purge hoses or an overfilled tank, while RepairPal also lists the tank, sending unit, canister and vent valve and names the fuel cap, purge valve and vent valve as common misdiagnoses. Related codes include P0450, P0451 (sensor range/performance), P0452 (sensor low) and P0454 (sensor intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P0453 Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety or drivability right away but should still be checked. Both OBD-Codes.com and RepairPal say the check engine light is usually the only thing you will notice. It can keep the car from passing an emissions inspection. If you smell raw fuel, get it checked sooner, and never ignore a strong fuel smell.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine (service engine soon) light on (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Usually no other symptoms; both sources say the driver typically notices nothing else
  • In some cases a noticeable fuel vapor smell (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Bad fuel tank pressure sensor (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists sensor, wiring or computer together)
  2. Open on the sensor signal wire, or a short to voltage on it (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Loose or damaged sensor connector, or loss of ground to the sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Abnormally high pressure in the fuel tank from a blocked EVAP purge hose or an overfilled tank (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Defective fuel tank sending unit, or a defective or damaged fuel tank (RepairPal)
  6. Defective carbon canister, or a canister vent valve that won't open (RepairPal; RepairPal also lists the purge and vent valves as common misdiagnoses)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and write down the freeze frame data as a baseline (RepairPal).
  2. If you have a scan tool that shows live data, check the fuel tank pressure sensor reading with the key on and engine off. OBD-Codes.com says it is usually near 2.5 volts at atmospheric pressure (it can vary a little with altitude) and should never be above 4.5 volts.
  3. Avoid topping off the tank after the pump clicks off; OBD-Codes.com lists an overfilled tank as one cause of high tank pressure.
  4. Look at the wiring and connector at the sensor for water, corrosion, broken or chafed wires (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. Don't start by replacing the fuel cap or purge and vent valves; RepairPal lists all three as common misdiagnoses for P0453.

Typical fixes

  • Repair a shorted or open signal wire, a bad ground or a damaged connector (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal says to verify the 5-volt reference, ground and signal circuits)
  • Replace the fuel tank pressure sensor if it has good power and ground but still reads high (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Clear a blocked EVAP purge hose (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair a canister, vent valve, sending unit or fuel tank problem found during diagnosis (RepairPal)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com's test: if the reading is above 4.5 volts, unplug the sensor; if high voltage remains, look for a short to voltage in the harness; if it drops away, confirm ground and 5-volt reference, then replace the sensor
  • If the reading is normal (OBD-Codes.com says close to 2.7 volts with the gas cap off), the fault is likely intermittent and needs a wiggle test of the wiring with a meter
  • RepairPal suggests testing the sensor with a vacuum gauge while watching its scan tool data, and checking the computer only after everything else tests good
  • The sensor can be part of the fuel pump assembly in the tank on some vehicles (OBD-Codes.com), which usually means lowering the tank
  • The code comes back, or you smell raw fuel

FAQs

Is P0453 serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate. Both sources say the check engine light is usually the only symptom, with a fuel smell in some cases. It isn't usually an emergency, but it can fail an emissions inspection, so get it diagnosed within a reasonable time.

Can a loose gas cap cause P0453?

OBD-Codes.com doesn't list the gas cap as a cause of P0453, and RepairPal names the fuel cap as a common misdiagnosis. A high reading points more to the sensor, its wiring, or real pressure build-up from a blocked purge hose, overfilled tank or stuck-closed vent valve.

What is the difference between P0452 and P0453?

Both involve the EVAP fuel tank pressure sensor. P0452 means the signal is lower than expected and P0453 means it is higher than expected. OBD-Codes.com says P0453 sets when the signal goes above 4.5 volts.

How much does it cost to fix P0453?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from a wiring or connector fix to a pressure sensor that sits in the fuel pump assembly and needs the tank lowered, so get a written estimate. Emission parts often have longer warranties, so check whether yours is still covered.
