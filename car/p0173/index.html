P0173: Fuel Trim Malfunction Bank 2 (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0173 means the engine computer has pushed its fuel trim adjustments on bank 2 to their limit and still can't get the air-fuel mixture right. Vacuum or intake air leaks and a faulty mass air flow (MAF) sensor are the most common causes.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0173 means

Fuel trim is how the engine computer (PCM) keeps fine-tuning the amount of fuel it injects so the mixture stays close to the ideal 14.7 parts air to 1 part fuel. RepairPal explains that the computer uses short-term and long-term fuel trim to make these corrections, and sets P0173 when it has reached its operational limit with them, meaning it can't achieve the right balance of fuel and air. OBD-Codes.com says P0173 means a malfunction in the computer's control of the air-fuel ratio, where the fuel trims reached their limit of adding fuel. The OBD-Codes.com article isn't consistent about which way: one paragraph says the computer was compensating for a real or perceived rich condition, while its later explanation says the computer was adding fuel to make up for a lean condition, real or perceived. Adding fuel normally corrects a lean (too much air) condition, which matches the vacuum-leak and MAF causes both sources list. Bank 2 is the side of a V6 or V8 engine opposite cylinder number 1, so this code only applies to engines with two banks; P0170 is the same fault on bank 1 and may be set along with it. OBD-Codes.com notes this code shows up most on Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen and other European cars, often alongside misfire or other fuel trim codes, while American cars more often set P0171, P0172, P0174 or P0175 for similar problems. Searches also show it on BMW, Range Rover, Jaguar, Chevrolet, Hyundai, Ford and Honda. It is a generic OBD-II code, but the exact setting conditions vary by make.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

RepairPal rates P0173 Urgent, meaning it should be looked at right away. OBD-Codes.com doesn't give a rating, but lists symptoms that can include stalling right after starting, black smoke and misfires. Many cars show no obvious symptoms, but an air-fuel mixture problem can get worse and lead to other faults, so get it diagnosed soon. If the engine stalls or misfires badly, avoid driving it until it's checked.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (both sources)
  • Often no other noticeable symptoms (RepairPal)
  • Poor fuel economy (both sources)
  • Hesitation when accelerating (RepairPal), or hesitation or misfire at idle or under load (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine starts and then stalls (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Black smoke from the tailpipe (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Vacuum or intake air leaks, such as cracked hoses, leaking intake manifold gaskets or a torn air intake hose (both sources)
  2. Faulty mass air flow (MAF) sensor (both sources); OBD-Codes.com calls this the most common cause, especially on Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen, and warns that many aftermarket MAF sensors cause problems
  3. Damaged MAF sensor wiring or connections (RepairPal), including oil getting into the MAF or oxygen sensor connectors (OBD-Codes.com); OBD-Codes.com suggests checking ignition coils, cam and crank sensors and the oil sensor for leaks that let oil wick into the wiring harness
  4. Leaking turbocharger charge-air hoses, if equipped (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Faulty fuel pressure regulator (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Faulty oxygen sensor (OBD-Codes.com); RepairPal warns the oxygen sensor is often replaced when the real problem is a vacuum leak or MAF sensor
  7. Engine oil diluted with fuel (OBD-Codes.com)
  8. On Mercedes-Benz: leaking camshaft adjuster solenoids, or a cracked crankcase vent hose under the intake manifold, which OBD-Codes.com says is covered by a service recall on some models
  9. The sources differ on the list: RepairPal names only intake manifold vacuum leaks and the MAF sensor and its wiring, while OBD-Codes.com adds fuel pressure, oxygen sensors, turbo hoses, oil intrusion and the Mercedes-specific items

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze-frame data. Note any misfire codes or other fuel trim codes (P0170 to P0175), and fix those along with P0173. If P0170 is also set, both banks are affected, which points to something they share, such as the MAF sensor or a vacuum leak common to both sides.
  2. Check for recalls and technical service bulletins for your vehicle. On some Mercedes-Benz models, OBD-Codes.com says there's a recall for the crankcase vent hose under the intake manifold; check it for cracks and make sure its check valve flows only one way.
  3. Inspect all vacuum hoses, the intake air hose and, on turbo engines, the charge-air hoses for cracks, tears or loose connections (both sources point to air leaks).
  4. Check the MAF sensor connector for oil or corrosion. With a scan tool, compare the MAF reading in grams per second against the spec for your engine; OBD-Codes.com gives example figures for a 1.8-liter Mercedes of about 3.5 to 5 g/s at idle, 9 to 12 g/s at 2,500 rpm with no load, and 90 g/s or more at wide-open throttle on a road test.
  5. If the MAF and hoses check out, have the fuel pressure tested and check the regulator for leaks (OBD-Codes.com).
  6. Inspect the oxygen sensor connectors for oil intrusion before replacing an oxygen sensor; RepairPal says replacing the oxygen sensor when the real cause is a vacuum leak or MAF sensor is a common mistake.

Typical fixes

  • Repair vacuum or intake air leaks, including hoses and intake manifold gaskets (both sources)
  • Replace the MAF sensor if it reads out of spec, or repair its wiring and connector (both sources); OBD-Codes.com recommends a sensor that meets original-equipment specs
  • Repair leaking turbo charge-air hoses (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair fuel pressure or fuel pressure regulator problems found in testing (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Fix the source of any oil leak into the wiring and replace an oil-fouled oxygen sensor (OBD-Codes.com); on Mercedes-Benz, OBD-Codes.com says a new oxygen sensor may need an adaptation with a compatible scan tool

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • You can't find a vacuum leak by looking; a shop can use a smoke test to locate it
  • The fuel pressure needs testing, which means working on the pressurized fuel system
  • The code comes with misfire codes, stalling or black smoke
  • A Mercedes-Benz needs a brand-compatible scan tool for sensor adaptations or recall work

FAQs

Is P0173 serious?

RepairPal rates it Urgent. Many cars run normally with it, but it means the engine computer can't control the air-fuel mixture properly, and OBD-Codes.com lists stalling, misfires and black smoke as possible symptoms, so get it diagnosed soon.

What's the difference between P0173 and P0174?

Both involve fuel trim on bank 2. P0173 is a general fuel trim malfunction, set when the computer reaches its correction limit, while P0174 specifically means bank 2 is too lean. OBD-Codes.com says the setting conditions are very similar and that European cars tend to set P0170 and P0173 where American cars set P0171 to P0175.

What if I have P0170 and P0173 together?

That means the fuel trims on both banks hit their limit. OBD-Codes.com describes them as the same malfunction on bank 1 and bank 2. A fault shared by both sides, like the MAF sensor, a vacuum leak or fuel pressure, is a more likely cause than a single oxygen sensor.

How much does it cost to fix P0173?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It depends on the cause, from a cracked hose to a MAF sensor or fuel system repair, so get a diagnosis or a written estimate first.
