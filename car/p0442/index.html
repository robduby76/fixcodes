P0442: Evaporative Emission (EVAP) System Leak Detected (small leak) (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 3 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0442 means the EVAP system, which stores fuel vapor so it doesn't escape, has a small leak, as small as about 0.04 inch. Start with the gas cap.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

🚗 Can I keep driving?

Usually yes. OBD-Codes.com says the only thing you will notice is the check engine light, sometimes with a fuel odor, and RepairPal says most drivers notice nothing. Get it checked in a reasonable time, and sooner if you smell fuel. A flashing check engine light is a different problem (a misfire): stop driving soon.

What to check first

In order, cheapest and easiest first. Stop when the code stays away.

  1. Check the gas cap. It should be on the car, undamaged and clicked tight. OBD-Codes.com lists a loose or improperly fitted cap and a non-factory (non-conforming) cap among the likely causes, and RepairPal lists a defective or damaged cap first.
  2. Remove and reinstall the cap until it clicks, clear the code, and drive for a day to see if it returns (OBD-Codes.com). The Wisconsin inspection program says the light can also turn itself off after three consecutive passing checks.
  3. If the code returns, replace the cap. OBD-Codes.com recommends an original-manufacturer cap rather than an aftermarket one.
  4. Look at the fuel filler neck for distortion or damage (RepairPal).
  5. Inspect the EVAP hoses and tubes you can see for cuts or holes (OBD-Codes.com), and look for loose or worn clamps and hardened or cracked seals, which RepairPal lists as possible small-leak sources.
  6. If it still comes back, have the EVAP system tested. OBD-Codes.com's more advanced steps are an EVAP leak test with a scan tool, checking that the purge valve works and a smoke test, because the leak can be a hole only about 0.04 inch across.
  7. Don't replace the purge or vent valve on a guess. RepairPal lists both as common misdiagnoses for P0442.

Quick FAQ

How small is a 'small' EVAP leak?

OBD-Codes.com says the leak can be a hole as small as 0.04 inch in diameter (about 1 mm). That is why a quick look often finds nothing and a smoke test or scan-tool leak test is used.

Why does the code take so long to come back or go away?

RepairPal says the computer runs the EVAP leak test only under set conditions: the vehicle must have been sitting for at least four to eight hours so engine and outside air temperatures match, with between 15 and 85 percent fuel in the tank. After a fix the test may not run right away, and the Wisconsin inspection program says the light turns off after three consecutive passing checks.

Can the wrong gas cap cause P0442?

Yes. OBD-Codes.com lists a loose or improperly fitted cap and a non-conforming cap (not the factory or original type) as likely causes, and recommends an original-manufacturer cap rather than an aftermarket one if you need to replace it.

What P0442 means

The EVAP system routes fuel vapor from the tank through hoses to a charcoal canister. Later, a purge valve lets engine vacuum draw those vapors into the engine to be burned. The PCM tests this system for leaks. P0442 means it found a very small one, from a hole as small as 0.04 inch in diameter.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Usually OK to drive

Generally safe to keep driving: you likely won't notice drivability problems. If you smell fuel, find the leak promptly.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Usually no drivability problems
  • Sometimes a fuel odor

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Loose or improperly fitted gas cap
  2. Non-original (non-conforming) gas cap
  3. Small hole or leak in a vapor hose or tube
  4. Other small EVAP leak
  5. Faulty vent O-ring seal
  6. Defective vent valve
  7. Defective purge valve
  8. Defective leak detection pump
  9. Distorted or damaged fuel tank filler neck, a split carbon canister, a fuel tank sending-unit seal, a fuel tank pressure sensor, or loose clamps and hardened O-rings (listed by RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Remove and reinstall the gas cap until it clicks, clear the code, and drive for a day to see if it returns.
  2. Inspect the gas cap seal for cracks or damage.
  3. Look over the EVAP hoses you can see (near the tank, canister and engine) for cracks or holes.

Typical fixes

  • Reseat or replace the gas cap (OBD-Codes.com recommends an OEM cap)
  • Replace a cracked vapor hose
  • Replace a faulty purge or vent valve or leak detection pump

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The code returns after a new cap: a smoke test is the usual way to find small leaks
  • An advanced scan tool is needed to run the EVAP leak test or check the purge solenoid
  • Your state's emissions inspection is coming up

FAQs

Will the light go off by itself?

It can. The Wisconsin inspection program explains that if the OBD system checks a component three consecutive times and no longer sees the problem, it turns the light off, for example a few days after you tighten a loose gas cap.
