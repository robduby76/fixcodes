P0103: Mass Air Flow (MAF) Circuit High (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0103 means the engine computer is seeing a signal from the mass air flow (MAF) sensor that is higher than it should be. Common causes are damaged or poorly connected MAF wiring, loose or corroded terminals, a dirty or contaminated MAF sensor, intake air leaks, or a failed sensor.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0103 means

The MAF sensor sits in the intake tract just after the air filter and measures the air being drawn into the engine. The engine computer (PCM/ECM) uses that reading to decide how much fuel to inject and, per RepairPal, how much ignition timing to use. P0103 is a generic OBD-II code defined as 'Mass or Volume Air Flow (A) Circuit High'. Sources describe the trigger differently: RepairPal says it sets when the PCM detects a high voltage output from the MAF sensor, while OBD-Codes.com describes it as the sensor's frequency signal not performing within the expected range of the MAF value the PCM calculates (some sensors output a voltage, others a frequency). Some MAF sensors also contain the intake air temperature sensor. Related MAF codes are P0100 (circuit), P0101 (range/performance), P0102 (circuit low) and P0104 (intermittent). Sources also disagree on cleaning: OBD-Codes.com calls careful cleaning with MAF cleaner spray a good DIY step, while RepairPal says cleaning may only hold off problems for a while on a contaminated sensor and that the sensor should be replaced.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0103 'Urgent', even though it says that in many cases you may not notice any symptoms besides the check engine light. OBD-Codes.com lists rough running, black smoke, hard starting and stalling as possible symptoms. If the engine stalls, runs very rough or blows black smoke, avoid driving it (especially in traffic) until it's fixed, and don't ignore new or flashing warning lights.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Often no other noticeable symptoms (per RepairPal)
  • Poor fuel mileage in some cases (listed by RepairPal)
  • Rough running engine (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  • Black smoke from the tailpipe (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  • Hard starting, or stalling after it starts (listed by OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. MAF sensor unplugged, or damaged MAF wiring (open, shorted, frayed or poorly connected)
  2. Loose or corroded terminals in the MAF sensor circuit (listed by RepairPal)
  3. Dirty or contaminated MAF sensor, for example from an over-oiled reusable air filter (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Failed MAF sensor
  5. Intake air leaks or vacuum leaks downstream of the MAF sensor (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  6. On some vehicles, loss of vacuum to the MAP sensor or high resistance in the MAP sensor ground circuit (listed by OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes. OBD-Codes.com notes misfire or O2 sensor codes may also be present, so look at the whole picture.
  2. Check that the MAF connector is fully plugged in, and inspect the wiring and terminals for damage, corrosion, fraying, or routing too close to ignition coils, wires, relays or motors.
  3. Inspect the intake ducts and hoses for air and vacuum leaks.
  4. Replace a dirty air filter. If you use an oiled reusable filter, OBD-Codes.com warns that over-oiling can contaminate the MAF sensor.
  5. Look closely at the MAF sensor's wire or film for dirt or oil. OBD-Codes.com suggests careful cleaning with MAF cleaner spray (never touch the element); RepairPal says cleaning may only be a temporary fix.
  6. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle.

Typical fixes

  • Reconnect the MAF sensor or repair damaged MAF wiring
  • Clean or repair loose or corroded MAF terminals and connector
  • Clean or replace a contaminated MAF sensor (OBD-Codes.com recommends an original-equipment replacement)
  • Repair intake air or vacuum leaks
  • Replace the MAF sensor if it is confirmed faulty

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The code returns after the connector, wiring and intake check out
  • RepairPal suggests recording freeze frame data, recreating the conditions on a test drive, and comparing MAF grams-per-second with load, throttle position and RPM; readings that jump around or move the opposite way confirm the fault
  • RepairPal describes a barometric pressure check at idle on vehicles where the barometric sensor is part of the MAF sensor: a reading far from your actual altitude points to a faulty sensor
  • RepairPal warns the MAF sensor is often replaced when the wiring is bad or the connector is defective

FAQs

Can I drive with P0103?

Often there are no symptoms beyond the light, but RepairPal rates it 'Urgent', and OBD-Codes.com lists rough running, black smoke and stalling as possible symptoms. Get it diagnosed soon, and don't drive if the engine is stalling.

Does P0103 mean I need a new MAF sensor?

Not necessarily. RepairPal lists an unplugged sensor, damaged wiring and loose or corroded terminals as common causes, and says the sensor is often replaced when the wiring or connector is the real problem. Check the connector, wiring and intake first.

Will cleaning the MAF sensor fix P0103?

Sometimes. OBD-Codes.com recommends careful cleaning with MAF cleaner spray, but RepairPal says cleaning may only help temporarily on a contaminated sensor and recommends replacement.

What's the difference between P0102 and P0103?

P0102 is MAF circuit low (signal lower than expected) and P0103 is MAF circuit high (signal higher than expected). Both are often caused by wiring, connector or sensor problems.

Why does P0103 show up a lot on Chevy, Ford, Toyota and Subaru searches?

Those makes show up often in searches for this code, but neither source names a model-specific problem. P0103 is a generic code, so diagnose it the same way and check for service bulletins for your specific vehicle.
