P0776: Pressure Control Solenoid B Performance or Stuck Off (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0776 means the transmission or engine computer has found that pressure control solenoid B, one of the valves that sets fluid pressure inside an automatic transmission, isn't working as expected or is stuck off. Common causes are a failed solenoid, low, dirty or contaminated fluid, a clogged filter, and wiring or connector faults; less often the valve body, the transmission pump or the computer itself.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0776 means

An automatic transmission changes gears by applying clutches and bands with fluid pressure. The control module uses pressure control solenoids to set that pressure in each hydraulic circuit at the right moment. RepairPal says the transmission control module (TCM) sets P0776 when it detects that a pressure control solenoid is not performing as desired. OBD-Codes.com says the powertrain control module (PCM) sets it when pressure control solenoid B is not functioning properly or is stuck in the off position. Most automatic transmissions have at least three of these solenoids, labeled A, B and C; the B solenoid's code family is P0775, P0776, P0777, P0778 and P0779 (OBD-Codes.com). It is a generic OBD-II code that shows up on Ford, GM, Toyota, Nissan, Chrysler, Honda and other vehicles, but the exact test values and repair steps vary by transmission, so the vehicle's service information should come first.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0776 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe. OBD-Codes.com calls it moderate at first but says it can get worse quickly if it isn't fixed. The transmission may slip, overheat or drop into limp mode, so avoid hard acceleration, towing and long trips until it's diagnosed, and get off the road safely if it suddenly loses drive.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission may not shift properly (RepairPal); OBD-Codes.com describes slipping when shifting or catching in gear
  • Vehicle goes into limp mode (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission overheating (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Lower fuel economy (both sources)
  • Possibly misfire-like symptoms (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed pressure control solenoid (both sources)
  2. Low transmission fluid (RepairPal), or dirty or contaminated fluid (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Dirty or clogged transmission filter (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Wiring problem, or a corroded or damaged connector (both sources)
  5. Faulty transmission control module (RepairPal) or PCM (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Defective transmission pump, defective valve body or restricted hydraulic passages (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. The sources differ: RepairPal lists only the module, low fluid, the solenoid and wiring, while OBD-Codes.com adds fluid contamination, the filter, the pump, the valve body and blocked passages. They also disagree on severity, with RepairPal rating it Urgent and OBD-Codes.com calling it moderate at first

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data before clearing anything; other transmission codes can point to the real cause.
  2. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, year and transmission; OBD-Codes.com says this can save a lot of time.
  3. Check the transmission fluid level and condition using the procedure in your owner's or service manual, and look for burnt smell, dark color or debris.
  4. Check your records for when the fluid and filter were last changed (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. Inspect the wiring and connectors to the transmission solenoids, the pump (if electric) and the computer for damage, corrosion, loose connections or bent pins.
  6. Further testing needs a digital multimeter and your vehicle's specific resistance, voltage and pressure specs, which vary by transmission (OBD-Codes.com).

Typical fixes

  • Top up, or change the fluid and filter (both sources point to fluid)
  • Replace the faulty pressure control solenoid
  • Clean corroded connectors or repair damaged wiring
  • Flush the transmission to clear restricted passages (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair or replace the valve body or transmission pump if diagnosis points there (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Reflash or replace the transmission or engine control module (both sources list the module)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission is slipping, overheating or stuck in limp mode
  • The fluid is burnt or full of debris
  • The fluid level and wiring check out and the code comes back
  • Testing needs pressure gauges, a scan tool or removing the pan and valve body
  • OBD-Codes.com warns this code can be misdiagnosed as an engine misfire, a transmission pump problem, an internal transmission problem or a driveline problem, so a proper diagnosis matters before buying parts

FAQs

Is P0776 serious?

Treat it as serious. RepairPal rates it Urgent. OBD-Codes.com says it usually starts as moderate but can get worse quickly, with slipping, overheating and limp mode possible.

Can low transmission fluid cause P0776?

Yes. RepairPal lists low fluid as a common cause, and OBD-Codes.com lists dirty or contaminated fluid and a clogged filter, so checking the fluid is the first step.

Can I keep driving with P0776?

Keep it short and gentle and get it checked soon. A solenoid problem can make the transmission slip or overheat, which can turn a small repair into a bigger one.

How much does it cost to fix P0776?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from a fluid service or wiring repair to a new solenoid, valve body or control module, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
