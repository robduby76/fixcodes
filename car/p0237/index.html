P0237: Turbocharger/Supercharger Boost Sensor A Circuit Low (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0237 means the engine computer is seeing a low voltage signal from the boost pressure sensor, which reads as low boost. OBD-Codes.com says chafed or burnt wiring near the turbo is the most common cause, ahead of a bad sensor or turbo. RepairPal also lists an exhaust leak.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0237 means

On a turbocharged or supercharged engine, the powertrain control module (PCM) watches boost pressure with a pressure sensor (OBD-Codes.com calls it the manifold absolute pressure, or MAP, sensor). The PCM sends the sensor a 5 volt reference and gets back a voltage that rises with boost. The PCM controls boost with the boost control solenoid and uses the sensor to check that the boost is correct. OBD-Codes.com says P0237 sets when the PCM sees a low voltage signal, meaning low boost, while it is commanding high boost through boost control solenoid A. RepairPal describes it more simply as the PCM detecting an incorrect signal from the boost pressure sensor. Manufacturers word it differently: OBD-Codes.com lists GM as Turbocharger Boost Circuit Low Input Conditions and Dodge/Chrysler as MAP Sensor Signal Too Low. The sources' cause lists differ: OBD-Codes.com names the boost sensor, turbo, PCM and wiring, and says wiring near the turbo is the usual culprit, while RepairPal adds an exhaust leak and lists a brake booster pressure sensor failure, which OBD-Codes.com does not mention. P0236 is the same sensor's range/performance code and P0238 is its circuit high code.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it fixed before you keep driving it. RepairPal rates P0237 Urgent, meaning immediate action is needed. OBD-Codes.com says that because of the increased chance of catalytic converter damage and turbo overboost, it should be corrected before continuing to use the vehicle. Expect low power while the code is active.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Low engine power or decreased performance (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Reduced fuel economy (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Lack of boost from the turbo or supercharger (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Chafed or burnt wiring near the turbocharger causing shorts or opens; OBD-Codes.com says this is the most common cause (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  2. Spread or poor terminals at the boost sensor or boost control solenoid connectors (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. A faulty boost sensor A (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. A faulty turbocharger or supercharger (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  5. An exhaust leak (RepairPal)
  6. A brake booster pressure sensor failure (RepairPal only)
  7. A faulty PCM, which OBD-Codes.com calls the least likely cause (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Scan for other codes first. OBD-Codes.com says wastegate control or 5 volt reference codes can create the conditions for P0237, so fix those first.
  2. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle; there may be a known fix.
  3. Inspect the wiring near the turbo closely for chafing, melting or burns. Unplug the boost sensor and boost control solenoid connectors and look for spread terminals; OBD-Codes.com suggests silicone dielectric compound when reconnecting.
  4. With the key on and engine off, back-probe the sensor's reference wire with a digital multimeter. OBD-Codes.com says it should read 5 volts, and the signal wire should read between 0.2 and 0.5 volts. If not, it says to suspect the boost sensor.
  5. OBD-Codes.com then describes running the engine with the meter connected and applying vacuum to the wastegate vacuum motor with a hand vacuum pump; the sensor voltage should rise. Its procedure says to suspect the PCM if it rises and the turbo if it doesn't. Wastegate designs vary, so check your service manual before doing this test.

Typical fixes

  • Repair chafed, burnt, shorted or open wiring near the turbo (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Clean or repair spread connector terminals at the boost sensor and boost control solenoid (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace the boost pressure sensor if its signal tests wrong with good reference voltage (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair an exhaust leak (RepairPal)
  • Repair or replace the turbocharger only after wiring and the sensor test good (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Replace or reprogram the PCM only as a last step (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The engine has very low power or goes into limp mode
  • You hear new turbo whine, grinding or see blue smoke
  • Wiring near the turbo is burnt and needs heat-resistant repair
  • You don't have a wiring diagram or a multimeter
  • The code comes back after the wiring and sensor check out

FAQs

Can I drive with a P0237 code?

It's not a good idea. RepairPal rates it Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com says it should be corrected before continuing to use the vehicle because of the chance of catalytic converter damage and turbo overboost.

Is P0237 a bad boost sensor?

Sometimes, but not usually first. OBD-Codes.com says chafed or burnt wiring near the turbo causes it more often than anything else, so check the wiring and connectors and test the sensor's voltages before buying one.

What is the difference between P0236, P0237 and P0238?

All three are boost sensor A codes. P0236 is range/performance, P0237 is the circuit reading low and P0238 is the circuit reading high.

Why do people search P0237 on Cummins, Power Stroke and Chevy Cruze?

P0237 is a generic code for any turbocharged or supercharged vehicle, and those are common turbo engines. Neither source gives a model-specific cause, so check for TSBs for your vehicle and follow the wiring and sensor checks above.

How much does it cost to fix P0237?

Neither source gives a repair cost. A wiring or connector repair is usually much cheaper than a sensor or turbo, so get a diagnosis or written estimate before buying parts.
