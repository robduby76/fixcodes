P2172: Throttle Actuator Sudden High Airflow (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P2172 means the engine computer (PCM) has detected a sudden burst of high airflow in the electronic throttle system. It often comes with limp-home mode. OBD-Codes.com says it is usually an informational code set because of a problem elsewhere, with vacuum leaks the most likely cause, followed by MAP sensor faults and abnormal system voltage, so check for other codes before replacing the throttle body.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P2172 means

On a drive-by-wire engine there is no cable to the throttle. The accelerator pedal position (APP) sensor tells the PCM how much power the driver wants, and the PCM opens the throttle plate with a motor, per RepairPal. RepairPal says the PCM sets P2172 when it detects sudden and excessive airflow through the engine, and may put the engine in limp-home mode to prevent unwanted acceleration. OBD-Codes.com says the code sets when sudden high airflow is detected in the throttle actuator control system, that it is one of a group of airflow codes (P2172 to P2175), and that on certain Dodge and Chrysler vehicles it may be described as High Airflow/Vacuum Leak Detected. OBD-Codes.com lists Dodge, Jeep, Chrysler, Volvo, Fiat and Mitsubishi among the makes that use it. The sources lean different ways on the cause: OBD-Codes.com describes P2172 mostly as an alert that the PCM has gone into fail-safe mode because of a fault in another system, while RepairPal's list centers on the pedal sensor, throttle motor, MAF sensor, vacuum hoses, wiring and PCM. RepairPal's explanation for P2172 is word for word the same as its explanation for the closely related P2173 (High Airflow Detected).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

RepairPal puts P2172 in its urgent category, meaning action is needed right away because it can cause severe damage or put the people in the car at risk. OBD-Codes.com rates it moderate to severe depending on the specific malfunction. The engine computer may drop into a limp-home mode that limits power and throttle response, so allow extra room when merging or passing. If the car won't accelerate normally, won't start, or the automatic transmission won't shift, get it off the road safely and have it checked rather than driving on.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Limp-home mode with reduced engine power (RepairPal)
  • No or low throttle response (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decreased engine performance and increased fuel consumption (RepairPal)
  • Automatic transmission may not shift (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine may not start (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Other trouble codes stored at the same time (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Vacuum leak or leaking vacuum hose (both sources; OBD-Codes.com calls vacuum leaks the most likely cause)
  2. Manifold absolute pressure (MAP) sensor malfunction (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Abnormal system voltages (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Dirty or out-of-calibration mass airflow (MAF) sensor (RepairPal)
  5. Accelerator pedal position (APP) assembly failure (RepairPal)
  6. Throttle control motor failure (RepairPal; OBD-Codes.com calls replacing the throttle actuator a rare repair)
  7. Wiring issue (RepairPal)
  8. Less often: PCM failure (RepairPal) or PCM software that needs reprogramming under a service bulletin (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Scan for other codes first. OBD-Codes.com says P2172 is usually informational and points to a fault elsewhere; diagnose and fix those codes before anything else.
  2. Look up technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your year, make, model and engine. OBD-Codes.com mentions bulletin 18-006-15 for 2013-2015 Dodge Dart and Chrysler 200 cars, which covers this code and others and is fixed by reprogramming the PCM.
  3. With the engine idling, listen for hissing and inspect vacuum hoses and intake connections for cracks, splits or loose fittings.
  4. Visually inspect the throttle body, pedal sensor, MAF sensor and MAP sensor wiring and connectors for damage or corrosion.
  5. Check battery and charging voltage, since OBD-Codes.com lists abnormal system voltages as a cause.
  6. Clear the code and see if it comes back on a test drive.

Typical fixes

  • Repair the faults behind any other stored codes first
  • Fix vacuum leaks or replace cracked vacuum hoses
  • Repair a faulty MAP sensor or its circuit, or clean or replace the MAF sensor
  • Correct charging or battery voltage problems
  • Repair damaged wiring or connectors
  • Reprogram the PCM if a TSB calls for it
  • Replace the accelerator pedal assembly or throttle body only after the above check out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com's common-mistake warning: replacing the throttle control actuator or PCM when other malfunctions are setting this code
  • Once other codes are cleared, or if none are present and P2172 remains, OBD-Codes.com says to evaluate the PCM and throttle control actuator, starting with a visual inspection of all wiring and connections, and to check the MAP sensor circuitry
  • The sources disagree on emphasis: OBD-Codes.com treats P2172 mainly as a fail-safe alert caused by another system (vacuum leaks, MAP sensor, voltage), while RepairPal lists throttle, pedal and MAF components directly; severity also differs (RepairPal urgent, OBD-Codes.com moderate to severe)
  • RepairPal's general advice for this code: most trouble codes come from a sensor reporting an out-of-range value, and just replacing the sensor may not fix the underlying issue

FAQs

Can I drive with P2172?

Only carefully and not far. RepairPal rates it urgent and OBD-Codes.com says moderate to severe. Limp-home mode can leave you with little throttle response, so get it diagnosed soon.

Does P2172 mean my throttle body is bad?

Not usually. OBD-Codes.com says the code is mostly informational and is often caused by another problem such as a vacuum leak, a MAP sensor fault or abnormal voltage, and it calls replacing the throttle actuator a rare repair. Check for other codes first.

What is the difference between P2172 and P2173?

Both are throttle actuator airflow codes in the P2172 to P2175 group. Per OBD-Codes.com, P2172 sets on sudden high airflow and P2173 on high airflow. RepairPal gives both the same explanation and the same urgent rating.
