P0730: Incorrect Gear Ratio (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0730 means the computer sees that the transmission isn't in the gear it expects, because engine or input speed doesn't match output speed for the commanded gear. Unlike P0731 to P0736 it doesn't name one gear. Common causes include low or dirty transmission fluid, faulty shift solenoids, valve body problems, torque converter issues, or internal wear.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0730 means

An automatic transmission picks between several gear ratios, and the computer checks that the gear it commanded is really engaged by comparing speed signals. RepairPal says the input speed sensor tells the transmission control module (TCM) how fast the engine side is spinning and the output speed sensor reports the output shaft speed; P0730 is set when the powertrain control module (PCM) sees more than a specified RPM difference between them, during shifting or at a steady speed in one gear, which often implies slipping. OBD-Codes.com describes it a little differently: the ECM, PCM or TCM often compares engine speed with the transmission speed sensor to work out gear ratio and torque converter slip, and sets the code when the calculation doesn't match the desired value. OBD-Codes.com stresses that P0730 is generic and doesn't point to any particular gear. The sources also word the definition differently: both title it Incorrect Gear Ratio, but RepairPal's definition line calls it a Transmission Control System Malfunction. It is a generic OBD-II code for 1996 and newer vehicles, though repair steps vary by make and model. Related codes are P0729 and P0731 to P0736 for specific gears; OBD-Codes.com says to repair any other transmission codes first.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it diagnosed soon and drive gently until you do. RepairPal rates P0730 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe, and says it often means the transmission is slipping. Both sources list shifting problems and slipping, and RepairPal adds that in some cases the engine may die when coming to a stop after freeway driving. If the car slips badly, won't shift, or stalls at stops, avoid driving it in traffic and have the transmission checked.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (both sources)
  • Delayed shift or not shifting into the correct gear (OBD-Codes.com); RepairPal says the vehicle will not shift properly
  • Transmission slipping (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal says the code often implies slipping)
  • Lower fuel economy (both sources)
  • In some cases, engine dying when coming to a stop after freeway driving, or misfire-like symptoms (RepairPal)
  • In unusual cases, no noticeable symptoms (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Low or dirty transmission fluid (OBD-Codes.com); RepairPal lists dirty fluid restricting the hydraulic passages
  2. Faulty shift solenoids (both sources; OBD-Codes.com adds wiring)
  3. Blockage or a problem in the valve body (transmission main control) (both sources)
  4. Worn pump or clogged fluid filter (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Torque converter clutch, its solenoid, or internal blockage (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Mechanical failure inside the transmission (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. Faulty transmission control module (OBD-Codes.com)
  8. Defective engine coolant temperature sensor (RepairPal)
  9. The sources differ: OBD-Codes.com lists internal mechanical failure, the pump and the torque converter, while RepairPal lists the coolant temperature sensor and calls an internal transmission problem, an engine misfire problem or a driveline problem common misdiagnoses

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and write down the freeze frame data. If there are other transmission codes, such as a solenoid electrical code, OBD-Codes.com says to fix those first.
  2. Check the transmission fluid level and condition before anything else; OBD-Codes.com says wrong level or dirty fluid can cause shifting issues that affect multiple gears.
  3. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle; there may be a known fix.
  4. If you have a scan tool that shows live data, RepairPal suggests watching input and output speed signals at a steady freeway speed in top gear; a large, varying difference at steady speed and throttle points to slipping.

Typical fixes

  • Correct the fluid level, or service dirty fluid and a clogged filter
  • Repair any shift solenoid electrical or wiring fault first, then replace faulty shift solenoids if testing points to them
  • Repair the valve body (main control) if passages are blocked or pressure is wrong
  • Repair the torque converter or internal parts when a stall speed test or other diagnosis confirms mechanical failure
  • Replace the coolant temperature sensor if it's faulty (RepairPal lists it as a cause)
  • Replace or reprogram the TCM only after other causes are ruled out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The car slips, won't shift, or stalls when coming to a stop
  • Several incorrect-ratio codes are stored at once; OBD-Codes.com says a torque converter stall speed test (done to the service manual's procedure) may point to the converter or an internal problem
  • RepairPal says that more than about a 200 RPM difference between input and output speed at steady freeway speed and throttle means the transmission is slipping and may need internal repairs
  • OBD-Codes.com says this repair often needs an advanced scan tool, advanced transmission knowledge and the factory service manual

FAQs

Is P0730 serious?

Yes, treat it as serious. RepairPal rates it Urgent and says it often means the transmission is slipping. Drive gently and get it diagnosed soon to avoid further transmission damage.

What's the difference between P0730 and P0731?

P0731 points to first gear specifically. OBD-Codes.com says P0730 is generic and does not point to any particular gear ratio, so it can show up with a problem shifting into or driving in any gear.

Can low transmission fluid cause P0730?

It can. OBD-Codes.com lists low or dirty fluid as a cause and says to check level and condition first; RepairPal lists dirty fluid restricting the hydraulic passages.

Does P0730 mean I need a new transmission?

Not necessarily. Both sources list fixable causes such as fluid, shift solenoids or the valve body, and RepairPal lists an internal transmission problem as a common misdiagnosis. Get a proper diagnosis before agreeing to a rebuild.

How much does it cost to fix P0730?

Neither source gives a repair cost. The price ranges widely depending on whether it's fluid, a solenoid, the valve body, the torque converter or internal parts, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
