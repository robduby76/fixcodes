P2201: NOx Sensor Circuit Range/Performance Bank 1 (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P2201 means the engine computer has found a range or performance problem with the bank 1 NOx sensor or its circuit. NOx sensors measure nitrogen oxides in the exhaust and are mostly found on diesel engines with a DEF (diesel exhaust fluid) system, like the Ford 6.7L Power Stroke, Ram 6.7L Cummins, GM Duramax, Mercedes Sprinter, VW and Audi TDI. The usual causes are a failed or soot-fouled NOx sensor, damaged wiring or connectors near the hot exhaust, and less often the engine computer.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P2201 means

A NOx sensor sits in the exhaust and reports how much nitrogen oxide the engine is putting out. RepairPal explains that on diesels the computer uses multiple NOx sensors to check how well the DEF and reduction catalyst system is turning NOx into nitrogen, carbon dioxide and water, and sets P2201 if one of the NOx sensors fails to operate properly. OBD-Codes.com says P2201 is set when the computer sees an out-of-range electrical condition in the bank 1 NOx sensor or its circuit. Bank 1 is the side of the engine with cylinder 1; on inline engines there is only one bank, and your service manual tells you which of several NOx sensors the code points to. P2200 is the general circuit code, and P2202, P2203 and P2204 are the low, high and intermittent versions.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Usually not an emergency, but get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P2201 as Moderate, and OBD-Codes.com says emissions codes are usually low on the severity scale but any electrical fault deserves some urgency. The check engine light will likely fail an emissions inspection.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Higher emissions (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Inconsistent fuel economy, erratic idle or excessive smoke (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Often no noticeable change in how the engine drives

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. A failed or damaged NOx sensor (both sources)
  2. A dirty, soot-caked sensor pickup, common in diesel exhaust (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Damaged wiring, often burnt or melted near the exhaust (both sources)
  4. A connector problem (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. A failed engine computer, which is uncommon (both sources)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data. P2201 often shows up with other DEF, SCR or NOx codes, and the related codes help narrow down which part of the system is at fault.
  2. Let the exhaust cool completely before touching anything near it.
  3. Find the bank 1 NOx sensor using your service manual, since many diesels have more than one NOx sensor.
  4. Inspect the sensor and its harness for damage from rocks, curbs, snow or ice, and for wires that are burnt or melted where they run near the exhaust.
  5. Check the connector for corrosion, bent pins or a loose fit.
  6. OBD-Codes.com suggests cleaning a soot-caked sensor. Sensors can seize in the exhaust threads; if you heat the threads to free one, heat the bung, never the sensor itself, and leave this to a shop if you've never done it.
  7. Look up technical service bulletins and emissions warranty coverage for your VIN before buying a sensor, as NOx sensors can be expensive.

Typical fixes

  • Replace a failed NOx sensor (both sources)
  • Repair damaged wiring or a bad connector (both sources)
  • Clean a soot-fouled sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair or replace the engine computer only after everything else checks out (both sources)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The sensor is seized in the exhaust
  • Your truck or van shows DEF or emissions warnings, a speed-limit countdown, or reduced power
  • You need a scan tool that can read live NOx sensor data to confirm the fault
  • The vehicle may still be under emissions warranty

FAQs

Is P2201 serious?

Usually not an emergency. RepairPal rates it Moderate, and OBD-Codes.com puts most emissions codes low on the severity scale. But it's an electrical or sensor fault in the emissions system, so get it diagnosed within a reasonable time.

Does P2201 only happen on diesels?

Mostly. NOx sensors are used mainly on diesels with DEF systems. OBD-Codes.com lists Ford, Mercedes Benz, BMW, VW, Audi, Chevrolet, GMC, Dodge, Ram and Sprinter, and its forum threads include a 2014 Ram 2500 6.7L and a 2010 Audi Q7.

Can I clean a NOx sensor instead of replacing it?

OBD-Codes.com suggests cleaning a dirty sensor, since diesel soot can cake on it. RepairPal only lists sensor failure, the engine computer and wiring. If the code comes back after cleaning and the wiring checks out, the sensor likely needs replacing.

Do the sources agree on P2201?

Mostly. OBD-Codes.com describes it as an out-of-range electrical condition in the bank 1 NOx sensor or circuit, while RepairPal describes it as a NOx sensor failing to operate properly while monitoring the DEF reduction system and drops 'Bank 1' from the title. OBD-Codes.com adds a dirty sensor and connector problems as causes; RepairPal doesn't.

How much does it cost to fix P2201?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It ranges from a wiring or connector repair to a new NOx sensor, so check emissions warranty coverage and get a written estimate after diagnosis.
