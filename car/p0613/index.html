P0613: TCM Processor (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0613 means the transmission control module (TCM) has reported an internal processor problem. OBD-Codes.com says the TCM is most often built into the engine computer (PCM) and that this code is usually caused by a failed controller or a programming error, though power, ground and network wiring problems need to be ruled out first. RepairPal lists a failed TCM and wiring issues as the common causes.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0613 means

The transmission control module (TCM) runs the automatic transmission. OBD-Codes.com explains that on some vehicles the TCM is a separate module, but most often it is integrated into the powertrain control module (PCM). Each time the ignition is turned on, the controllers run self-tests and use the controller area network (CAN) to compare signals between modules. If the PCM finds a problem with TCM function, it stores P0613 and may turn on the check engine light, sometimes only after several failed cycles. RepairPal describes the code as the PCM detecting an incorrect signal related to the TCM. The code is generic to OBD-II vehicles (1996 and newer); OBD-Codes.com names Chrysler, Dodge, Jeep, Audi and Land Rover among the makes that use it, and search interest also shows people looking it up for Ram, VW, Toyota and Mercedes, so check technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked right away. RepairPal rates P0613 'Urgent', meaning immediate action is needed because it can cause severe damage or put people at risk. OBD-Codes.com says internal control module codes must always be taken seriously and that P0613 can cause delayed shifts, erratic shifting or a failure to shift. If the transmission isn't shifting properly, avoid highway driving until it's diagnosed.

Common symptoms

  • Transmission doesn't shift properly (both sources)
  • Erratic, delayed or harsh shifting, or failure to shift (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Speedometer or odometer that doesn't work or reads wrong (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Check engine light, often with other stored TCM or PCM codes (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. A failed TCM or PCM (both sources)
  2. A TCM or PCM programming error (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Open or shorted wiring or connectors in the CAN harness, or other wiring problems (both sources)
  4. A failed power supply to the TCM or PCM (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. A poor control module ground (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data and write them down. OBD-Codes.com says any TCM/PCM power supply codes need to be repaired before you diagnose P0613.
  2. Check the fuses and relays that feed the TCM and PCM. OBD-Codes.com says to test fuses with the circuit loaded.
  3. Inspect the controller wiring and harnesses, and check the chassis and engine ground connections for corrosion or looseness.
  4. Look the modules over for signs of water, heat or collision damage. OBD-Codes.com says a controller damaged by water should be considered defective.
  5. Clear the code and test drive. If it doesn't come back, it may be intermittent, which OBD-Codes.com says makes it harder to diagnose; the fault may need to get worse before it can be pinned down.
  6. Look up TSBs for your year, make, model and engine that mention P0613.

Typical fixes

  • Repair a blown fuse, failed relay or bad power supply to the module
  • Clean and repair poor module grounds
  • Repair damaged CAN or module wiring and connectors
  • Reprogram the TCM/PCM if a programming error is found
  • Replace and program the TCM or PCM if it has failed

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com says that, unlike most codes, P0613 is likely caused by a defective controller or a controller programming error, and that even experienced, well-equipped technicians can find it a challenge to diagnose. Rule out power and ground problems before condemning a module.
  • A replacement controller has to be programmed. OBD-Codes.com says some can be bought pre-programmed from aftermarket sources, while others need on-board programming that only a dealer or other qualified shop can do.
  • The sources differ in detail: OBD-Codes.com describes an internal TCM processor fault found by self-tests and CAN signal checks and lists five causes, while RepairPal's short description says the PCM detects an incorrect signal (its page says 'from the PCM', apparently meaning the TCM) and lists only TCM failure and wiring. RepairPal rates it Urgent; OBD-Codes.com doesn't give a rating but says to take it seriously.

FAQs

Can I drive with P0613?

It's not a good idea. RepairPal rates it 'Urgent', and OBD-Codes.com says it can cause delayed, erratic or failed shifts. Get it diagnosed soon and avoid highway driving if the transmission is acting up.

Does P0613 mean the transmission is bad?

No. It points to the transmission control module (often built into the PCM), not the transmission's internal parts. Both sources list a failed module and wiring as causes, and OBD-Codes.com adds programming errors, power supply and ground problems.

Can I just swap in a used TCM or PCM?

Not without programming. OBD-Codes.com says controller replacement requires reprogramming, and without the right equipment a replacement won't complete the repair.

What codes are related to P0613?

P0700 (transmission control system malfunction) and other internal control module codes such as P0606 and P0607 can appear with it, and OBD-Codes.com says TCM/PCM power supply codes should be fixed first.
