P0215: Engine Shutoff Solenoid Malfunction (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0215 means the engine computer has detected a problem in the engine shutoff solenoid circuit, the electrically controlled valve (mostly on diesels) that can cut fuel to the engine. OBD-Codes.com lists a bad solenoid, shutoff relay, wiring, sensors that feed the shutoff system, or the PCM as causes. RepairPal rates it 'Urgent' and ties it to the relay that powers the injection pump.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0215 means

OBD-Codes.com explains that the engine shutoff solenoid sits in the fuel delivery system and, when the powertrain control module (PCM) commands it through an engine cutoff relay, closes a valve that stops fuel reaching the engine and returns it to the tank. Depending on the vehicle, the PCM may trigger it after a collision or rollover, extreme overheating, or a severe loss of oil pressure. The PCM monitors the solenoid circuit whenever the key is on, and if the circuit voltage differs from what it expects it stores P0215 and may turn on the check engine light; some vehicles need more than one failed cycle before the light comes on. RepairPal describes the code more narrowly: the high pressure fuel pump (injection pump) is powered through a relay the engine computer switches on, and P0215 sets if that relay does not supply power to the pump when the key is on. The system is used most on diesel vehicles but not only on them, and search interest shows people look it up most for VW, Audi, Dodge Cummins, Peugeot and Citroen. Shutoff systems vary widely by maker, so use your vehicle's service information.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Fix it soon. RepairPal rates P0215 'Urgent', meaning immediate action is needed. OBD-Codes.com also classes it as severe, because the same faults that set it can leave the engine unable to start. Many vehicles show no symptoms at first, but the engine may not start or may stall, so get it diagnosed before you rely on the vehicle for a long trip.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Frequently no symptoms when the code is first stored (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine won't start (both sources)
  • Engine may stall (RepairPal)
  • Other fuel system codes or lean exhaust codes (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Defective engine shutoff (cutoff) solenoid (OBD-Codes.com)
  2. Faulty engine shutoff relay, or the injection pump relay (both sources)
  3. Open, shorted or corroded wiring in the shutoff circuit (both sources)
  4. Malfunctioning tilt angle sensor, on vehicles that have one (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Bad oil pressure sending unit or engine temperature sensor feeding the shutoff system (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Faulty PCM or a PCM programming error (both sources)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data and write them down. If there are oil pressure, engine over-temperature or tilt sensor codes, OBD-Codes.com says to fix those first.
  2. If the vehicle was recently in a collision or tipped at an extreme angle, OBD-Codes.com says clearing the code may be all that's needed.
  3. Clear the code and drive normally. If it does not return before the PCM's readiness monitors complete, the fault was likely intermittent or tied to a condition that has passed.
  4. Check the fuse and relay for the shutoff solenoid or injection pump (your owner's manual or fuse box lid shows which), and look over the wiring and connector at the solenoid for damage or corrosion.
  5. Look up technical service bulletins for your year, make and model, since shutoff systems differ a lot between makers.

Typical fixes

  • Clear the code if it was set by a collision or extreme tilt and the fault does not return
  • Repair the underlying oil pressure, overheating or tilt sensor fault if one is present
  • Replace a faulty engine shutoff or injection pump relay
  • Repair open, shorted or corroded wiring and connectors
  • Replace the engine shutoff solenoid if it fails the resistance test
  • Repair or reprogram the PCM if testing points to it

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com's test sequence: unplug the shutoff solenoid and measure its resistance and continuity against the manufacturer's spec, replacing it if out of range; if it is good, check for voltage and ground at the solenoid connector; if they are missing there, check the matching pins at the PCM connector. Missing at the PCM points to the PCM or its programming; present at the PCM but missing at the solenoid points to the relay or an open circuit.
  • The sources describe the code differently: OBD-Codes.com treats it as a shutoff solenoid circuit fault that can involve several sensors (oil pressure, temperature, tilt angle), while RepairPal describes it as the injection pump relay failing to power the pump with the key on, and lists only the relay, wiring and PCM as common causes.
  • Both sources agree on severity: RepairPal rates it 'Urgent' and OBD-Codes.com calls it severe because of the possible no-start.

FAQs

Can I drive with P0215?

Often the car still runs at first, since OBD-Codes.com says there are frequently no symptoms. But RepairPal rates it 'Urgent' and both sources say the engine may not start, and RepairPal says it may stall, so get it checked soon rather than risk being stranded.

What usually causes P0215?

A bad shutoff or injection pump relay, wiring problems, or a failed shutoff solenoid are the usual suspects. OBD-Codes.com adds that a faulty oil pressure sender, temperature sensor or tilt sensor that feeds the shutoff system, or the PCM, can also set it.

Is P0215 only a diesel code?

Mostly. OBD-Codes.com says engine shutoff solenoid systems are used most often on diesel vehicles but not exclusively, and RepairPal's description refers to the diesel injection pump.

Will P0215 clear itself after an accident?

OBD-Codes.com says that if the vehicle was in a collision or at an extreme tilt angle, clearing the code may be enough. If it comes back, there is a real fault in the shutoff circuit to diagnose.
