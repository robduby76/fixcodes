P0784: 4-5 Shift Malfunction (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0784 means the computer has found a problem with the shift from 4th to 5th gear in an automatic transmission. Common causes include a failed 4-5 shift solenoid, wiring faults, low transmission fluid, an internal transmission problem or, less often, a faulty TCM or PCM.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0784 means

In an electronically controlled automatic transmission, the transmission control module (TCM) or powertrain control module (PCM) switches shift solenoids that route hydraulic pressure to the clutches or bands that change gears. P0784 is a generic code, so it can appear on any 1996-and-newer vehicle with a 5-speed or higher automatic, and the two sources explain it differently. OBD-Codes.com describes it mainly as a problem with the 4-5 shift solenoid and its circuit: the solenoid may be on/off or duty-cycle controlled, its circuit may be switched on the power side or the ground side depending on the vehicle, and some vehicles test the circuit even when that gear isn't in use by pulsing the solenoid too briefly to shift and checking for the small voltage spike (inductive kick) it sends back. RepairPal describes it as a gear-holding problem instead: the TCM detects that the transmission is not holding the requested gear and tells the engine computer to set P0784. So one source points first at the solenoid's electrical circuit, and the other at whether the transmission actually engages and holds the gear. OBD-Codes.com calls it "4-5 Shift Malfunction" and RepairPal's title is "4-5 Shift". OBD-Codes.com notes it is nearly identical to P0781, P0782, P0783 and P0829, which cover other shifts.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Treat it as urgent. RepairPal rates P0784 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe. OBD-Codes.com doesn't give a severity rating, but says the fault may shut down the transmission controls and put it into a failsafe mode that allows only one default gear. A car stuck in one gear can be slow to accelerate or unable to keep up with traffic, so drive gently, avoid highways, and get it checked soon.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Vehicle won't shift from 4th to 5th gear (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission may not shift properly (RepairPal)
  • Transmission controls shut down into a failsafe mode with only one default gear (OBD-Codes.com says this is possible)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed 4-5 shift solenoid or transmission solenoid (both sources)
  2. Wiring problems: shorts to power or ground from pinched wires, or opens from disconnected or loose connections (both sources list wiring; OBD-Codes.com gives the detail)
  3. TCM or PCM internal failure (both sources)
  4. Low transmission fluid level (RepairPal)
  5. Internal transmission failure (RepairPal)
  6. The sources differ: OBD-Codes.com lists only electrical causes, while RepairPal adds low fluid and internal transmission failure, which fits its description of the transmission not holding the requested gear

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read and write down all stored codes and the freeze frame data. Other transmission codes stored with P0784 can point to the real cause.
  2. Check the transmission fluid level and condition with the method in your owner's manual. RepairPal lists low fluid as a cause, and its common repair for P0784 is a fluid and filter change.
  3. Look for transmission fluid leaks at the pan, cooler lines and cooler.
  4. Inspect the transmission wiring harness and connectors for pinched, disconnected or loose wires, as OBD-Codes.com suggests.
  5. Clear the code and test drive to see whether it comes back and whether the 4-5 shift happens normally.

Typical fixes

  • Top up or change the transmission fluid and filter, and repair any leaks (RepairPal's listed common repair is a fluid/filter change)
  • Repair shorted, open or loose wiring and connectors in the 4-5 solenoid circuit
  • Replace the 4-5 shift solenoid when testing confirms it has failed
  • Repair the transmission internally when diagnosis points to an internal failure
  • Repair or replace the TCM or PCM only after the solenoid and wiring check out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission is stuck in one gear, slipping, or has burnt-smelling fluid
  • OBD-Codes.com's diagnosis uses a wiring diagram and a digital volt-ohm meter to check the 4-5 solenoid's power and ground circuits for opens, excess resistance and shorts to power or ground, with the battery and transmission connector disconnected
  • To check the TCM or PCM, OBD-Codes.com says to use an advanced scan tool that can command the 4-5 shift solenoid on (some vehicles also need the transmission control relay activated), or monitor whether the module sends power or ground to the solenoid while driving
  • Before agreeing to major transmission work, ask the shop to rule out fluid level, wiring and the solenoid first

FAQs

Is P0784 serious?

Yes. RepairPal rates it Urgent. OBD-Codes.com says the transmission may drop into a failsafe mode with only one gear, which makes the car hard to drive normally.

Can I keep driving with P0784?

Only gently and for short trips until it's checked. If the car won't shift into fifth or is stuck in one gear, avoid highways and get it diagnosed soon.

What is the most common fix for P0784?

The sources don't rank causes. RepairPal's listed common repair is a transmission fluid and filter change, and OBD-Codes.com's checks focus on the 4-5 shift solenoid and its wiring.

How is P0784 different from P0781, P0782 and P0783?

They are the same kind of fault for different shifts: P0781 is the 1-2 shift, P0782 is 2-3, P0783 is 3-4 and P0784 is 4-5. OBD-Codes.com says they are nearly identical.

How much does it cost to fix P0784?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from fluid service or a wiring repair to a shift solenoid, internal transmission repair or control module, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
