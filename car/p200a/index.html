P200A: Intake Manifold Runner Performance (Bank 1) (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P200A means the engine computer has found that the intake manifold runner control (IMRC) system on Bank 1 is not working as commanded. Possible causes listed by OBD-Codes.com and RepairPal include a failed runner actuator, broken or worn linkage, broken or restricted vacuum lines, wiring or connector faults, a bad runner sensor, and less often the PCM itself.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P200A means

Many engines have flaps inside the intake manifold, called an intake manifold runner control (IMRC) system, that the powertrain control module (PCM) moves to change how air flows into the cylinders. OBD-Codes.com explains that the flaps on one bank are usually attached to a single pivot rod running through the intake ports and moved together by one actuator, with each bank having its own rod and actuator. The PCM commands a position, then watches engine sensor data to see whether the intended effect happened; if it finds the system cannot be controlled effectively, it stores P200A and may turn on the check engine light (OBD-Codes.com). RepairPal says the code is set when the intake manifold runner does not operate as requested. OBD-Codes.com describes P200A as a generic OBD-II code for 1996 and newer vehicles (it names Hyundai, Kia, Mercedes-Benz, Sprinter and Vauxhall), with repair steps that vary by year, make, model and engine. Bank 1 is the side of the engine with cylinder 1; inline engines have only Bank 1. Note on sources: the two sources describe the system's purpose differently. OBD-Codes.com says airflow is restricted at low RPM to reduce emissions and the flaps create a swirl at higher RPM to improve fuel atomization and performance, while RepairPal says the runner lets more air flow through the intake at higher RPM. RepairPal's title for P200A also leaves out the bank, while OBD-Codes.com names Bank 1. Designs vary by maker, so check your vehicle's service information.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it diagnosed soon. RepairPal rates P200A Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety or drivability right away but should still be checked within a reasonable time. OBD-Codes.com says it should not be classed as severe but should be fixed at the earliest convenient opportunity, and notes that stored runner control codes typically leave the intake port flaps wide open. Expect weaker performance and lower fuel economy until it is fixed. If the engine hesitates badly, stumbles, or other warning lights come on, have it checked sooner.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Reduced engine performance (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Lower fuel economy (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Hesitation when accelerating (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Stumble at idle (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Lean or rich exhaust codes stored along with P200A (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Defective IMRC actuator (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  2. Broken, collapsed or restricted IMRC vacuum lines, or a faulty IMRC vacuum supply actuator (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  3. Broken or worn IMRC linkage or bushings (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Open or shorted IMRC circuits or connectors (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Bad IMRC sensor or another engine sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. PCM failure or a programming error, which OBD-Codes.com says to suspect only after everything else is ruled out (RepairPal, OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data and write them down before clearing, in case the fault is intermittent (OBD-Codes.com).
  2. If mass airflow (MAF), manifold pressure (MAP) or intake air temperature (IAT) codes are also stored, fix those first (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) matching your vehicle, engine, codes and symptoms (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Visually inspect the runner control linkage, vacuum lines and hoses, wiring and connectors; repair worn or damaged hardware before going further (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. Clear the code and test drive to see whether it comes back (OBD-Codes.com).

Typical fixes

  • Repair or replace broken or worn runner linkage or bushings
  • Replace broken, collapsed or restricted vacuum lines
  • Repair open or shorted wiring or connectors in the runner control circuit
  • Replace a failed IMRC actuator, vacuum supply actuator, control solenoid or runner sensor if testing confirms it
  • Repair or reprogram the PCM only after all other causes are ruled out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • With key on and engine off, a hand-held vacuum pump can be used to operate the runner actuator and confirm the flaps open, per OBD-Codes.com
  • Checking runner sensor readings on a scan tool, then testing sensors and the runner control solenoid with a digital volt/ohmmeter against factory specs, needs vehicle-specific diagnostic information (OBD-Codes.com)
  • The code keeps returning after you fix the linkage, vacuum lines and wiring
  • The fault is intermittent; OBD-Codes.com warns it may need to get worse before it can be diagnosed accurately

FAQs

Is P200A serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate, and OBD-Codes.com says it should not be classed as severe but should be fixed at the earliest convenient opportunity. Expect reduced performance and fuel economy until it is repaired.

Can I keep driving with P200A?

Neither source says you must stop driving. The effects they list are reduced performance, lower fuel economy, hesitation and a stumble at idle. Get it checked soon, and sooner if the engine runs badly.

What's the difference between P200A and P2004?

P200A is a general performance fault: the Bank 1 runner system is not working as commanded. P2004 is more specific and means the Bank 1 runner control is stuck open (RepairPal, OBD-Codes.com).

How much does it cost to fix P200A?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from replacing a vacuum line or repairing a connector to a new actuator or intake manifold parts, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
