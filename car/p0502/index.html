P0502: Vehicle Speed Sensor "A" Circuit Low Input (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0502 means the engine computer is seeing a low voltage signal from the main vehicle speed sensor. An erratic speedometer, odd shifting and ABS or traction control lights are common, and the cause is often a sensor, wiring or connector problem.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0502 means

The vehicle speed sensor (VSS) tells the powertrain control module (PCM) how fast the car is moving. RepairPal explains the reading drives the speedometer and is usually shared with the transmission, ABS, traction control and power steering. OBD-Codes.com describes most speed sensors as electromagnetic sensors that read a toothed reluctor ring on an axle, transmission or transfer case output shaft, differential or driveshaft, and says the "A" sensor is the primary one, usually in the transmission. P0502 is the circuit low code: OBD-Codes.com says the PCM has detected a low voltage input signal from speed sensor A, which can come from an electrical or a mechanical problem. RepairPal's write-up uses more general wording, saying the code sets when the PCM sees an inaccurate or irrational reading. The sources also differ on severity (OBD-Codes.com says severe, RepairPal says Moderate), and OBD-Codes.com says the check engine light may come on rather than always.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it diagnosed soon and drive with care. The sources disagree on how serious it is: OBD-Codes.com classifies P0502 as severe because it can cause drivability and anti-lock brake (ABS) problems, and says ABS may stop working in some cases, while RepairPal rates it Moderate. Until it's fixed, leave extra stopping distance and don't rely on the speedometer.

Common symptoms

  • Erratic or inaccurate speedometer/odometer (both sources)
  • Irregular or improper transmission shifting (both sources)
  • Check engine (service engine soon), traction control or ABS warning light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Traction control turning on or off unexpectedly, if equipped (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Other transmission and ABS codes stored, and ABS possibly not working in some cases (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty vehicle speed sensor, or a faulty wheel speed sensor (both sources)
  2. Cut or damaged wiring or connectors, especially near the speed sensors (both sources)
  3. Excessive metal debris built up on the speed sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Damaged or worn teeth on the reluctor ring (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Faulty PCM, or a faulty ABS/brake control module (PCM per both sources; ABS modules per OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Think about recent work: OBD-Codes.com notes speed sensors can be damaged during regular maintenance, and wheel speed sensors and their wiring are often damaged during brake jobs. If the code showed up right after a repair, suspect the harness, connector or sensor first.
  2. Inspect the speed sensor wiring and connectors for cut, chafed or damaged wires.
  3. If you can reach the sensor, check it for a buildup of metal debris on its tip, which OBD-Codes.com lists as a cause, and look at the reluctor ring teeth while it's out.
  4. Search for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your make, model and year.
  5. Be careful removing a sensor from the transmission case, since OBD-Codes.com warns hot transmission fluid can come out.

Typical fixes

  • Repair damaged speed sensor wiring or connectors
  • Clean metal debris off the speed sensor
  • Replace a faulty vehicle speed or wheel speed sensor
  • Repair a damaged reluctor ring, or a faulty PCM or ABS module found by testing

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com's procedure: retrieve all codes and freeze frame data, check TSBs, then test drive watching vehicle speed and wheel speed on the scan tool's data stream to narrow down which sensor or circuit is reading wrong.
  • Resistance-test the suspect sensor with a digital multimeter against the manufacturer's specification, disconnecting the related control modules first to avoid damaging them.
  • With the drive axles safely raised, check each sensor's signal on an oscilloscope while running the drivetrain and look for glitches in the waveform.
  • Get it checked promptly if the ABS light is on, since anti-lock braking may be disabled until the speed signal is fixed.

FAQs

What's the difference between P0500, P0501 and P0502?

All three are Vehicle Speed Sensor "A" codes. P0500 is the general malfunction code, P0501 is range/performance (a reading that doesn't make sense), and P0502 is circuit low: per OBD-Codes.com, the PCM is seeing a low voltage input from the sensor.

Can a brake job cause P0502?

It can. OBD-Codes.com says wheel speed sensors and their wiring are often damaged during brake repairs, so if P0502 appeared right after brake work, check the sensors, harnesses and connectors near the wheels first.

Will P0502 go away on its own?

RepairPal says most codes clear on their own once the system stops detecting the problem, but if P0502 stays it should be diagnosed to avoid a more serious problem.
