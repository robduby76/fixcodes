P0462: Fuel Level Sensor A Circuit Low (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0462 means the engine computer (PCM) is seeing a fuel level sensor signal that is too low or doesn't make sense. The sensor (sending unit) sits in the fuel tank, often as part of the fuel pump module. Causes listed by the sources are corroded connections or a short to ground in the sensor wiring, a failed or internally shorted fuel level sensor, and less likely a failed PCM.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0462 means

The fuel level sensor (FLS) is usually mounted in the fuel tank, often in the top of the fuel pump module. It turns the fuel level into an electrical signal for the PCM, which uses it to know how much fuel is in the tank and to work out fuel economy, and typically shares that information with other modules over the vehicle's data network. OBD-Codes.com says P0462 sets when this signal doesn't match the normal operating voltages stored in the PCM, including a check at initial key on, and that it is typically caused by electrical problems in the sensor circuit. RepairPal defines P0462 as Fuel Level Sensor "A" Circuit Low and says the PCM sets it when it detects an irrational signal from the fuel level sensor. The sources differ on causes: OBD-Codes.com calls a failed or internally shorted sensor likely, a short to ground possible and a failed PCM unlikely, and says corroded fuel tank connections are the most common area of concern; RepairPal lists fuel pump failure (because the sensor may be part of the fuel pump), PCM failure and wiring issues, without ranking them. OBD-Codes.com says the code shows up more often on certain Chrysler, GM, Ford, Lincoln, Mercury, Honda/Acura, Kia, Mazda, Mercedes-Benz, Mitsubishi, Infiniti, Nissan and Subaru vehicles. Related codes include P0460 (circuit), P0461 (range/performance), P0463 (circuit high) and P0464 (circuit intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P0462 Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety or drivability right away, and OBD-Codes.com says it is usually not severe because the engine computer can compensate for an electrical failure. The catch is that the compensation usually leaves the fuel gauge stuck reading Empty or Full. Until it's fixed, track your mileage and refuel early, and don't rely on the gauge, the low fuel light or the distance-to-empty display. Running out of fuel in traffic or on the highway is dangerous.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Fuel gauge reads wrong, often stuck on Empty or Full all the time (OBD-Codes.com); RepairPal lists an inaccurate fuel gauge reading
  • Decrease in the distance-to-empty mileage shown on the dash (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decrease in perceived fuel economy (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Corroded or damaged connector terminals or wiring at the fuel tank, which OBD-Codes.com calls the most common area of concern (RepairPal lists wiring issues)
  2. Short to ground in the signal circuit to the fuel level sensor (OBD-Codes.com: possible)
  3. Failed or internally shorted fuel level sensor (OBD-Codes.com: likely)
  4. Fuel pump failure, since the sensor may be part of the fuel pump (RepairPal)
  5. Failed PCM (RepairPal; OBD-Codes.com: unlikely)
  6. On some Ford products, an aftermarket remote start system can set a false code; Ford has a technical service bulletin on it that also covers auxiliary fuel tanks (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Search for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle first; the maker may have a PCM reflash for this code (OBD-Codes.com).
  2. Find the fuel level sensor connector at the tank or fuel pump module and inspect it and the wiring for rubbing, bare wires, burn spots, melted plastic, and burned or green, corroded terminals (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. Clean corroded terminals with electrical contact cleaner and a plastic bristle brush, let them dry and apply electrical grease, then clear the code and see if it returns. If it doesn't, the connection was most likely the problem (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. On Ford trucks with auxiliary tanks, OBD-Codes.com says to refuel the main tanks with the ignition off. Meanwhile, track mileage and refuel early instead of trusting the gauge.

Typical fixes

  • Clean or repair corroded fuel tank connector terminals and damaged wiring (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Repair a short to ground in the sensor signal circuit, or the power or ground wire if the voltage test fails (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a fuel level sensor that fails the resistance test; RepairPal notes it may be part of the fuel pump, so the repair can mean replacing the pump module (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Apply a manufacturer PCM reflash if a TSB covers it (OBD-Codes.com)
  • PCM replacement only after everything else checks out; OBD-Codes.com says a PCM can't be ruled out until the sensor is replaced, and a replacement must be programmed to the vehicle

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Testing uses a digital voltmeter at the sensor connector with key on, engine off; OBD-Codes.com says it should read 12 or 5 volts depending on the manufacturer's specification
  • Sensor resistance is checked with an ohmmeter against the maker's specification; it should be neither zero nor infinite (OBD-Codes.com gives about 80 ohms at half a tank as an example)
  • Reaching the sensor may mean working at the top of the fuel tank or fuel pump module
  • You smell raw fuel, see damage to the tank or wiring, or a new PCM needs programming

FAQs

Is P0462 serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate, and OBD-Codes.com says it is usually not severe because the PCM can compensate. The main risk is a fuel gauge stuck on Empty or Full, so track your mileage and refuel early until it's fixed.

Does P0462 mean I need a new fuel pump?

Not necessarily. RepairPal lists fuel pump failure as a cause because the fuel level sensor may be part of the pump, but OBD-Codes.com says corroded connections at the tank are the most common area of concern, so inspect and clean those first. If the sensor itself has failed and is built into the pump module, the module may need replacing.

What is the difference between P0462 and P0463?

P0462 is a fuel level sensor circuit low signal, while P0463 is a circuit high signal. Both point to the fuel level sensor and its wiring.

How much does it cost to fix P0462?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from cleaning a corroded connector to replacing the fuel level sensor or pump module, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
