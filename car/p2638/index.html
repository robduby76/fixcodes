P2638: Torque Management Feedback Signal A Range/Performance (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P2638 means a control module saw that the torque management feedback signal between the engine computer and the transmission computer was out of its expected range. In Jeep's factory service manual for the Wrangler JK, the transmission computer asks the engine computer to briefly cut engine torque during each shift, and sets P2638 when the engine computer doesn't confirm it did so. Jeep lists a CAN bus (network) problem, the transmission computer or the engine computer as possible causes. RepairPal describes it more simply as the two modules failing to communicate.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P2638 means

Modern automatic transmissions shift more smoothly when the engine briefly reduces torque during the shift. Jeep's 2012 and 2014 Wrangler JK factory service manuals explain that the transmission control module (TCM) requests these torque reductions over the CAN bus, the network the modules use to talk to each other, and then checks the response from the powertrain control module (PCM). If the response isn't within tolerance, the TCM adds to a counter, and when the counter reaches a threshold the code sets. The manual says it's monitored while engine intervention is active for at least 20 milliseconds, with no engine torque errors and engine torque demand above zero. RepairPal defines P2638 as the TCM and PCM failing to communicate with each other. P2637 is the general version of this code, and P2639 and P2640 are the low and high signal versions.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P2638 as Urgent, its highest category. The code points at how the engine and transmission computers work together during shifts, so watch for harsh or odd shifting and avoid hard driving until it's diagnosed.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Shifting that may feel harsher than normal, since the torque reduction during shifts is what the code monitors (based on Jeep's description of the system; the sources don't list symptoms)
  • Other engine or network (U-code) trouble codes stored at the same time

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. A CAN bus (module network) wiring or communication problem (Jeep factory service manual; RepairPal)
  2. A faulty engine computer, the PCM (Jeep factory service manual)
  3. A faulty transmission computer, the TCM (Jeep factory service manual)
  4. Corroded, damaged or pushed-out terminals at the TCM, especially power and ground circuits (Jeep factory service manual)
  5. An engine fault that keeps the PCM from reducing torque, since Jeep says to diagnose any engine codes first

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read codes from every module, not just the engine. Write down all codes and the freeze frame or environmental data before clearing anything.
  2. Fix any engine codes first. Jeep's procedure sends you to the engine diagnostics before going further with P2638.
  3. Look for CAN bus or U-codes in other modules. Jeep says those point to a network problem that needs to be fixed first.
  4. Check the battery and the main grounds, since low voltage and poor grounds can cause module communication faults.
  5. Inspect the transmission computer's connectors for corrosion, damage or pushed-out pins, paying attention to power and ground circuits.
  6. Look up technical service bulletins for your VIN, since software updates for the engine and transmission computers are common on these vehicles.

Typical fixes

  • Repair the engine fault or CAN bus problem behind any other stored codes (Jeep factory service manual)
  • Repair corroded or damaged terminals at the TCM (Jeep factory service manual)
  • Replace and program the PCM, if the code remains after other faults are fixed (Jeep factory service manual)
  • Replace the TCM, if the code resets after the PCM is replaced and its wiring checks out (Jeep factory service manual)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Several modules show communication codes at once
  • Module replacement or programming is needed, which takes a dealer-level scan tool
  • You can't find an engine or network fault to explain it

FAQs

Is P2638 serious?

RepairPal rates it Urgent. It involves the engine and transmission computers working together during shifts, so get it diagnosed soon and go easy on the vehicle until then.

Why does P2638 show up on the Jeep Wrangler?

Jeep's Wrangler JK service manual explains it: the transmission computer asks the engine computer to cut torque during each shift and sets P2638 when that isn't confirmed. Jeep says to check for engine codes and network codes first, then the engine computer, then the transmission computer and its wiring.

Do I need a new computer to fix P2638?

Not necessarily. Jeep's procedure has you fix any engine or CAN bus faults first. Only if the code keeps coming back does it move on to replacing the engine computer, and then checking the transmission computer's terminals before replacing it.

Do the sources agree on P2638?

Not completely. RepairPal says P2638 sets when the transmission computer and the engine computer fail to communicate. Jeep's factory service manual is more specific: the modules can be talking fine, but the engine computer doesn't confirm a torque reduction the transmission computer asked for during a shift. OBD-Codes.com has no article for P2638.

How much does it cost to fix P2638?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from a wiring or connector repair to programming or replacing a module, so get a written estimate after diagnosis.
