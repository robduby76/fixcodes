P0894: Transmission Component Slipping (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0894 means the computer has measured slip inside the automatic transmission, usually by comparing input (or engine) speed with output speed. Common causes include low fluid, failed control solenoids, a slipping torque converter, a faulty input or output speed sensor or its wiring, or worn internal parts such as clutch discs.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0894 means

An automatic transmission uses clutches, bands and servos, applied by hydraulic pressure that solenoids control, to hold parts of its planetary gearsets. RepairPal explains that when one of these devices stops holding, often because hydraulic pressure is low, the transmission slips, and the PCM or TCM sees it as a difference between transmission input and output speeds. OBD-Codes.com describes the same check: the powertrain control module (PCM) uses the input and output speed sensor signals to judge whether the transmission is working without slippage, and stores P0894 and turns on the check engine light when slip exceeds the allowed limit. The sources differ on what is slipping. RepairPal says the definition varies between manufacturers: on some vehicles P0894 means an internal component is slipping, while on others it means excessive torque converter clutch slip, detected by comparing engine speed with transmission output speed. RepairPal's short cause section also describes a third, different use: some vehicles engage a neutral locked turbine to load the engine and shorten cold-start warm-up, and P0894 sets if it does not engage properly. OBD-Codes.com does not mention the torque converter or this warm-up function. It is a generic OBD-II code (1996 and newer); OBD-Codes.com lists Chevrolet, GMC, Mazda, Mercedes-Benz, Toyota and Lexus among makes that use it, and repair steps vary by vehicle.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it diagnosed soon and drive gently until you do. RepairPal rates P0894 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe, and OBD-Codes.com says it points to either a serious electrical problem or a catastrophic mechanical transmission failure that should be fixed as quickly as possible. RepairPal adds the car may go into limp mode. If the transmission slips, engages harshly, or the fluid smells burnt, avoid highway and heavy-traffic driving and have it checked before it gets worse.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (both sources)
  • Transmission slipping (OBD-Codes.com); RepairPal lists transmission performance problems
  • Delayed or harsh gear engagement (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Erratic shift pattern (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Strong burnt smell from the transmission fluid (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Poor fuel economy (RepairPal)
  • Stuck in limp mode (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Low transmission fluid level (RepairPal; OBD-Codes.com says to check fluid level and condition first)
  2. Failed control solenoid(s) (RepairPal)
  3. Torque converter failure (RepairPal)
  4. Internal transmission failure, such as worn clutch friction material (both sources; OBD-Codes.com calls it catastrophic and says most slippage codes are caused by it)
  5. Defective input or output speed sensor, or shorted sensor wiring (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Faulty PCM/TCM or a programming error (both sources)
  7. The sources differ: RepairPal adds low fluid, solenoids and the torque converter, while OBD-Codes.com adds the speed sensors and their wiring

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and write down the freeze frame data. RepairPal says a technician looks into any other stored codes first.
  2. Check the transmission fluid level and condition (both sources). If it's low, find and fix the leak and top up with the recommended fluid, then clear the code and see if it returns.
  3. If the fluid is very dark and smells burnt, OBD-Codes.com says it needs changing along with the filter; when the pan is off, a pan mostly free of debris suggests the clutches are intact, while lots of friction material in the pan and on the magnet means the transmission needs a rebuild.
  4. Look for broken wires and loose connectors, and check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, which may include a PCM/TCM software update (both sources).

Typical fixes

  • Repair fluid leaks and correct the fluid level
  • Change dark, burnt fluid and the filter
  • Repair speed sensor wiring or replace a faulty input or output speed sensor after testing
  • Replace failed control solenoid(s)
  • Repair or replace the torque converter, or rebuild the transmission, when diagnosis confirms mechanical failure
  • Update the PCM/TCM software per a TSB, or replace the module only after other causes are ruled out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission slips, engages harshly, or goes into limp mode
  • There is a lot of friction material in the pan, which OBD-Codes.com says calls for a rebuild by a qualified technician
  • OBD-Codes.com says testing the speed sensor reference voltage, signal and ground needs a scan tool, a digital multimeter and vehicle-specific diagnostic information, and an oscilloscope helps spot glitches in the sensor signal
  • The code is intermittent; OBD-Codes.com says the condition may need to worsen before it can be diagnosed accurately

FAQs

Is P0894 serious?

Yes. RepairPal rates it Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com says it indicates a serious electrical issue or catastrophic mechanical transmission failure. Drive gently and get it diagnosed soon.

Can low transmission fluid cause P0894?

Yes. RepairPal lists low fluid level as a common cause, and both sources say to check the fluid level and condition first.

Does P0894 mean I need a new transmission?

Not always. Fluid, solenoids, speed sensors, wiring or software can cause it. OBD-Codes.com does say most transmission slippage codes come from internal mechanical failure, so heavy debris in the pan is a bad sign. Get a proper diagnosis first.

Is P0894 about the torque converter?

On some vehicles. RepairPal says the definition varies by manufacturer and can mean either internal slip or excessive torque converter clutch slip, and its cause section also mentions a cold-start warm-up function. Check what your make uses it for.

How much does it cost to fix P0894?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It ranges from a fluid top-up to a transmission rebuild, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
