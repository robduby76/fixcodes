P2000: NOx Trap Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 1) (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-21 · Based on 4 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P2000 means the engine computer measured more NOx (nitrogen oxides) after the NOx trap than its programmed limit allows on Bank 1. It mostly appears on diesel vehicles with NOx traps or SCR systems. P2xxx codes are SAE-defined generic codes, but repair steps vary a lot by make, so check your manufacturer's information.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P2000 means

Diesel exhaust carries more oxygen, which makes normal catalytic converters less effective at reducing NOx. Many diesels therefore add a NOx trap (adsorber) as part of the aftertreatment system. The PCM compares the NOx sensors before and after the trap to work out its efficiency. When NOx after the trap is above a programmed limit, it stores P2000 and may turn on the check engine light. About the code number: in powertrain codes, a second digit of 0 or 2 means a generic SAE-defined code, while 1 means manufacturer-specific. So P2000 is a generic definition, not a manufacturer code, but diagnosis is vehicle-specific. In the 2026 carfaultcodes.co.uk review of UK data, P2000 appears as a top code in this exact meaning. If a scanner shows a different description for your vehicle, follow the manufacturer's service information.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Not usually an immediate safety issue, but it can come with excess smoke, reduced performance, higher engine temperature and worse fuel economy. Get it diagnosed. Mostly seen on diesels with NOx traps and SCR (selective catalytic reduction) systems.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light may be on
  • Excessive exhaust smoke
  • Reduced engine performance
  • Higher engine temperature
  • Worse fuel economy

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Defective or overloaded NOx trap or trap element
  2. Faulty diesel exhaust fluid (reductant) injection system
  3. Too little or poor-quality NOx reductant fluid
  4. EGR system not working
  5. Severe exhaust leak upstream of the NOx trap

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle.
  2. Read all stored codes and freeze-frame data. Fix any EGR codes first.
  3. Look over the wiring and connectors near hot exhaust parts and sharp heat shields for damage.
  4. Check the exhaust for leaks ahead of the NOx trap.
  5. Make sure the reductant/DEF reservoir is filled with fluid of the correct type and quality. Follow the manufacturer's recommendations.

Typical fixes

  • Repair exhaust leaks or damaged wiring
  • Correct the reductant fluid level or quality
  • Repair the reductant injection system
  • Repair the EGR system
  • Replace the NOx trap or trap element

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Diagnosis needs a scanner, a multimeter and vehicle-specific service data (for example, ALLDATA)
  • NOx sensor, SCR or NOx trap work on a diesel
  • The vehicle is under emissions warranty

FAQs

Is P2000 manufacturer-specific?

By the SAE structure, no: P2xxx powertrain codes are generic (manufacturer-specific codes use a 1 as the second character, like P1xxx). But the repair is vehicle-specific, so always check your manufacturer's information.

Does P2000 apply to gasoline cars?

OBD-Codes.com describes it in terms of NOx traps used mainly on diesel engines.

Why do sources describe P2000 differently?

RepairPal names it 'NOx Adsorber Efficiency Below Threshold', the same thing as a NOx trap. Its explanation brings in the DEF and diesel particulate filter, while OBD-Codes.com centers on the NOx trap and NOx sensors. We follow the NOx-trap definition both sources share. Check your vehicle's service information for its exact strategy.
