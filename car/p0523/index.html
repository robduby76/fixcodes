P0523: Engine Oil Pressure Sensor/Switch High (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0523 means the engine computer is reading a signal from the oil pressure sensor that's too high. OBD-Codes.com says it's most often electrical, like a faulty sensor or wiring, but it can also be a real high-pressure problem, a blocked oil passage or the wrong oil.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0523 means

The oil pressure sensor (or sender) measures the engine's mechanical oil pressure and reports it to the powertrain control module (PCM) as a voltage. OBD-Codes.com explains that the sensor works on a 5 volt circuit and P0523 generally sets when the signal reaches about 4.6 volts or higher, meaning the PCM sees too high a value. On some vehicles that reading drives an oil pressure gauge; on others there's only a warning light. OBD-Codes.com calls it a generic code for 1996-and-newer vehicles that appears more often on Chevrolet, Jeep, GMC, Dodge, Honda and Cadillac, and lists P0520, P0521, P0522 and P0524 as related codes. The two sources frame it a little differently. OBD-Codes.com says the cause may be mechanical or electrical but is most likely electrical, and includes real high oil pressure from an oil pump fault, a restricted oil passage or the wrong engine oil. RepairPal describes it only as the PCM detecting an incorrect signal from the sensor and lists the sensor, the PCM and wiring as the common problems, with no mechanical causes. RepairPal also says the low oil warning light may come on, while OBD-Codes.com lists an oil pressure gauge reading high.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

RepairPal rates P0523 Urgent, its highest level, meaning immediate action is needed. OBD-Codes.com doesn't give a severity rating and says the cause is most likely electrical, such as a bad sensor or wiring, but it can also be real high oil pressure from a pump fault, a blocked oil passage or the wrong oil. Because the oil pressure warning can't be trusted while this code is set, check the oil level right away and don't keep driving if the engine makes unusual noises or you can't confirm it has oil.

Common symptoms

  • Oil pressure warning light on (both sources; RepairPal calls it the low oil warning light)
  • Oil pressure gauge reading high (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Check engine light on

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty oil pressure sensor or sender (both sources; RepairPal's only listed repair is replacing it)
  2. Faulty wiring or connector in the sensor circuit (both sources)
  3. Actual high oil pressure from an oil pump fault (OBD-Codes.com only)
  4. Obstruction or restriction in an oil passage (OBD-Codes.com only)
  5. Wrong engine oil, or old, thick or sludged oil (OBD-Codes.com only)
  6. PCM failure (RepairPal only)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle before going deep into diagnosis, as OBD-Codes.com recommends.
  2. With the engine off and the car on level ground, check the oil level on the dipstick, and make sure the oil is the correct type and weight and isn't old, thick or sludged.
  3. Inspect the wiring and connector at the oil pressure sender for broken or frayed wires, burnt spots, loose or exposed wiring and corrosion. A repair manual shows where the sender is on your engine.
  4. If you have a digital volt-ohm meter, check that the sensor is getting its 5 volt supply, and test the sensor against the manufacturer's specifications (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. Check the wiring from the sensor to the PCM for breaks from chafing or pinching, and make sure the connectors are tight and corrosion free.

Typical fixes

  • Change the oil and filter with the correct oil if the oil is wrong, old or sludged (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair damaged wiring or a bad connector in the sensor circuit (both sources)
  • Replace the oil pressure sensor if it fails testing (both sources; OBD-Codes.com says this or a wiring fix is the most likely repair)
  • Repair an oil pump fault or a restricted oil passage if a mechanical gauge confirms real high pressure (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair or replace the PCM (RepairPal; rare, only after other causes are ruled out)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • You need the actual oil pressure tested. OBD-Codes.com says a technician will compare a mechanical oil pressure gauge reading with the value the PCM sees on an advanced scan tool, which quickly shows whether the sensor or wiring is at fault
  • The engine makes unusual noises, or the oil warning comes on and you can't confirm the oil level is good
  • A mechanical gauge shows real high oil pressure, pointing to an oil pump or oil passage problem inside the engine
  • The code returns after a new sensor and wiring repairs

FAQs

Is P0523 serious?

It can be. RepairPal rates it Urgent. OBD-Codes.com says it's most likely an electrical fault, such as a bad sensor or wiring, but it can be real high oil pressure. Either way, while it's set you can't trust the oil pressure warning, so check the oil level and get it diagnosed soon.

Is P0523 just a bad oil pressure sensor?

Often, but not always. Both sources list the sensor and wiring, and OBD-Codes.com says replacing the sensor or fixing wiring is the most likely repair. OBD-Codes.com also lists an oil pump fault, a blocked oil passage and the wrong oil, so a mechanical oil pressure test is the way to be sure before buying parts.

What's the difference between P0523 and P0524?

P0523 means the oil pressure sensor's signal is reading too high, which is usually a sensor or wiring problem. P0524 means the sensor is reporting oil pressure that's too low, which may be a real and serious lubrication problem.
