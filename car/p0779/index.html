P0779: Pressure Control Solenoid B Intermittent (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0779 means the transmission or engine computer has seen pressure control solenoid B, one of the valves that sets fluid pressure inside an automatic transmission, working only intermittently. Because the fault comes and goes, loose or corroded connectors and damaged wiring are worth checking first, along with the solenoid itself; low or contaminated fluid, a clogged filter, the valve body, the pump or the computer are also listed.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0779 means

An automatic transmission changes gears by applying clutches and bands with fluid pressure. The control module uses pressure control solenoids to set that pressure in each hydraulic circuit at the right moment. OBD-Codes.com says the powertrain control module (PCM) sets P0779 when pressure control solenoid B is operating intermittently. RepairPal defines P0779 as Pressure Control Solenoid "B" Intermittent, but its explanation only says the transmission control module (TCM) sets it when a pressure control solenoid is not performing as desired. P0779 belongs to the B solenoid family: P0775 (general malfunction), P0776 (performance or stuck off), P0777 (stuck on), P0778 (electrical) and P0779 (intermittent) (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal). It is a generic OBD-II code that OBD-Codes.com says shows up on Ford, Lincoln, Mercury, Jaguar, Chevrolet, Toyota, Nissan, Allison/Duramax, Dodge, Jeep, Honda, Acura and other vehicles, but the exact test values and repair steps vary by transmission, so the vehicle's service information should come first.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0779 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe. OBD-Codes.com calls it moderate at first but says it can get worse quickly if it isn't fixed. The transmission may slip, overheat, catch in gear or drop into limp mode, so avoid hard acceleration, towing and long trips until it's diagnosed, and get off the road safely if it suddenly loses drive.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission may not shift properly (RepairPal); OBD-Codes.com describes slipping when shifting or catching in gear
  • Vehicle goes into limp mode (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission overheating (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Lower fuel economy (both sources)
  • Possibly misfire-like symptoms (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Corroded or damaged connector, or faulty or damaged wiring (both sources)
  2. Failed pressure control solenoid (both sources)
  3. Low transmission fluid (RepairPal), or dirty or contaminated fluid (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Dirty or clogged transmission filter (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Faulty transmission control module (RepairPal) or PCM (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Defective transmission pump, defective valve body or restricted hydraulic passages (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. The sources differ: RepairPal lists only the module, low fluid, the solenoid and wiring, while OBD-Codes.com adds fluid contamination, the filter, the pump, the valve body and blocked passages. They also disagree on severity, with RepairPal rating it Urgent and OBD-Codes.com calling it moderate at first, and they name different computers (TCM versus PCM) as the one that sets the code. Neither source explains what makes the fault intermittent, so a wiggle test of the wiring and connector while watching for the code is a reasonable place to start

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data before clearing anything; other B solenoid codes (P0775 to P0779) or other transmission codes can point to the real cause.
  2. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, year and transmission; OBD-Codes.com says this can save a lot of time.
  3. Inspect the wiring and connectors to the transmission pressure control solenoids, the pump (if electric) and the computer for damage, corrosion, loose connections or damaged pins.
  4. Check the transmission fluid level and condition using the procedure in your owner's or service manual, and look for burnt smell, dark color or debris.
  5. Check your records for when the fluid and filter were last changed (OBD-Codes.com).
  6. Further testing needs a digital multimeter and your vehicle's specific voltage and resistance specs; OBD-Codes.com says wiring should normally read 0 ohms with the power off unless the service data says otherwise, and that resistance or no continuity means an open or shorted wire to repair.

Typical fixes

  • Clean corroded connectors or repair or replace damaged wiring
  • Replace the faulty pressure control solenoid
  • Top up, or change the fluid and filter (both sources point to fluid)
  • Flush the transmission to clear restricted passages (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair or replace the valve body or transmission pump if diagnosis points there (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Reflash or replace the transmission or engine control module (both sources list the module)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission is slipping, overheating or stuck in limp mode
  • The wiring and connectors look fine and the code keeps coming back
  • Testing needs the solenoid circuit specs, a scan tool or removing the pan and valve body
  • The fluid is burnt or full of debris
  • OBD-Codes.com warns this code can be misdiagnosed as an engine misfire, a transmission pump problem, an internal transmission problem or a driveline problem, so a proper diagnosis matters before buying parts

FAQs

Is P0779 serious?

Treat it as serious. RepairPal rates it Urgent. OBD-Codes.com says it usually starts as moderate but can get worse quickly, with slipping, overheating, catching in gear and limp mode possible.

What's the difference between P0778 and P0779?

Both point at pressure control solenoid B. P0778 means an electrical malfunction in the solenoid or its circuit, while P0779 means the solenoid is operating intermittently, so the fault comes and goes (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal).

Why does P0779 come and go?

An intermittent code means the problem isn't present all the time. Neither source names a single cause, but both list wiring problems, and OBD-Codes.com also lists corroded or damaged connectors, which often cause on-and-off faults, so inspecting the solenoid wiring and connector is a sensible first step.

How much does it cost to fix P0779?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from a connector or wiring repair to a new solenoid, valve body or control module, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
