P0463: Fuel Level Sensor A Circuit High (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0463 means the computer that monitors the fuel level sensor (the sending unit in the fuel tank) is seeing a signal voltage that is too high. Common causes listed by the sources are an open or shorted signal wire, a bad or rusty ground at the tank, a faulty fuel level sensor or float, and less often the instrument cluster or a control module.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0463 means

The fuel level sensor, or sender, sits inside the fuel tank and is usually built into the fuel pump module. A float on an arm moves along a resistor as the fuel level changes, which changes the voltage signal sent to the fuel pump module, instrument cluster or engine computer (PCM), depending on the vehicle. OBD-Codes.com describes P0463 as a generic OBD-II code that sets when the fuel level signal goes above about 5 volts (the exact value depends on the system) for a set amount of time. RepairPal calls the signal irrationally high, indicating the impossible condition of more fuel in the tank than it can hold. The sources differ on what the gauge does: OBD-Codes.com says it may fluctuate or read empty or full, while RepairPal says it may read full when it isn't. They also differ on where to look first: OBD-Codes.com says senders normally last the life of the fuel pump and starts with the wiring, ground and tank, while RepairPal lists a defective sensor first. Related codes include P0460 (circuit), P0461 (range/performance), P0462 (circuit low) and P0464 (circuit intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P0463 Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety or drivability right away. The real risk is that your fuel gauge can't be trusted: RepairPal warns the gauge may read full when it isn't, and the engine can die from running out of fuel. Until it's fixed, track your mileage and refuel early, and don't rely on the gauge or the low fuel light. Running out of fuel in traffic or on the highway is dangerous.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Fuel gauge fluctuates abnormally or reads empty or full (OBD-Codes.com); RepairPal says it may read full when the tank isn't
  • Low fuel light and warning chime may come on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine hesitation or stalling, or the engine dies and won't restart because it is out of fuel even though the gauge reads full (RepairPal)
  • EVAP codes or EVAP monitors not ready (RepairPal)
  • In some cases no other symptoms (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Signal circuit to the fuel sender open or shorted to battery voltage (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists defective sensor wiring or connections)
  2. Open ground circuit, or high resistance from rust or a missing ground strap on the fuel tank (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Faulty fuel level sensor, including an open in its resistor (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  4. Damaged fuel level sensor float (RepairPal)
  5. Damage to the fuel tank affecting the sender or its circuit (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Faulty instrument cluster, or less likely a failed PCM, BCM or fuel pump control module (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and write down the freeze frame data (RepairPal).
  2. Look under the car for damage to the fuel tank from an impact, and check the wiring harness and connector at the tank (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. Look for a missing ground strap or a rusty ground where the tank or sender grounds to the frame, and clean or repair it (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Compare the gauge with reality: RepairPal's technician fills the tank, and if a gauge that reads nearly full takes more than 1 to 2 gallons, the sensor reading is wrong. Meanwhile, track mileage and refuel early.

Typical fixes

  • Repair an open or short in the sender signal wiring or connector (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Clean or repair the fuel level sensor ground, or replace a missing ground strap (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a fuel level sensor or float that fails testing; it is often part of the fuel pump module and may need the tank lowered (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Repair or replace a faulty instrument cluster if testing points there (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Control module (PCM, BCM or fuel pump module) diagnosis only after everything else checks out; OBD-Codes.com calls a failure there highly unlikely

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Testing uses a voltmeter for a ground voltage drop test (OBD-Codes.com says ideally 0.1 volt or less, and close to 1 volt means a problem) and a wiring diagram
  • Instrument cluster problems are hard for a non-professional to test and may need a scan tool that talks to the cluster (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Reaching the sender often means removing or lowering the fuel tank (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • You smell raw fuel, the tank is damaged, or the engine stalls or won't restart

FAQs

Is P0463 serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate. The engine itself is usually fine, but the fuel gauge can be wrong, and RepairPal notes the engine can die from running out of fuel while the gauge reads full. Track your mileage and refuel early until it's fixed.

Does P0463 mean I need a new fuel pump?

Not necessarily. RepairPal lists replacing the fuel pump when the real cause is the fuel level sensor as a common misdiagnosis, and replacing the sensor when the cause is a poor connection or chafed wiring as another. OBD-Codes.com says senders normally last the life of the fuel pump and suggests checking the wiring, ground and tank first. The sender is often part of the pump module, though, so the repair may still mean replacing that module.

Why does my gas gauge read full or empty?

A high signal from the sender can push the gauge to an extreme. OBD-Codes.com says the gauge may read empty or full or fluctuate; RepairPal says it may read full when the tank isn't.

How much does it cost to fix P0463?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from cleaning a ground connection to a fuel level sensor or pump module that needs the tank lowered, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
