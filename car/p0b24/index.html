P0B24: Hybrid Battery "A" Voltage Unstable (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0B24 means the hybrid battery computer has found that one cell or group of cells in the high-voltage battery is out of step with the rest, with its voltage drifting away from the pack average. It's mostly searched by Ford hybrid owners. Ford's fix starts with charging the battery and letting the car rebalance its cells, then checks the battery computer's connectors, and only then moves to a new battery computer or a new high-voltage battery.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0B24 means

A hybrid's high-voltage battery is made of many cells wired in series, and they need to stay at about the same state of charge. The Battery Energy Control Module (BECM), the computer on the battery pack, watches every cell. RepairPal says P0B24 sets when the BECM decides one cell isn't contributing equally to the high-voltage system. Ford's 2020-2026 Escape hybrid service manual is more specific: the BECM sets P0B24 when one module's cell voltage strays from the average module cell voltage for 15 seconds. The BECM has built-in cell balancing circuits that drain extra charge from high cells, and Ford says balancing happens when the battery is at 15% charge or more and the car has been switched off for more than 24 hours.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0B24 as Urgent and says the high-voltage system may not operate. Ford's service manual says that when P0B24 sets, charging and discharging of the high-voltage battery is limited and electric motor power is reduced, so the car will feel weaker. The check engine light will also fail an emissions inspection.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on, with the wrench (powertrain malfunction) light on a Ford (Ford Escape manual)
  • Reduced power, because Ford limits electric motor output and how fast the high-voltage battery can charge and discharge (Ford Escape manual)
  • The high-voltage hybrid system may not operate (RepairPal)
  • Cell balancing codes such as P0DAD or P0DAE may also be stored, since Ford diagnoses them together with P0B24 (Ford Escape manual)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Cells in the high-voltage battery that have drifted out of balance or a low battery charge (Ford Escape manual lists low high-voltage battery voltage)
  2. Loose or not fully seated connectors, damaged wiring or terminals at the battery computer (Ford Escape manual)
  3. A faulty Battery Energy Control Module (Ford Escape manual)
  4. A failing high-voltage battery or a weak cell in it (Ford Escape manual; RepairPal describes one cell not contributing equally)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Safety first: hybrids carry high voltage (Ford says about 300 volts DC). Never unplug orange cables or open the high-voltage battery yourself.
  2. Read every stored code with a scan tool that can read the hybrid battery computer, and write them down. Ford's manual says to fix other battery codes first, such as P0A7D (low state of charge) or contactor and precharge codes.
  3. Drive the car normally for a while so the hybrid system can recharge the battery. Ford's first step is simply letting the car charge the battery by about 10% with the engine running, then clearing the code and checking whether it returns.
  4. Ford's manual also relies on the car sitting switched off for more than 24 hours so the battery computer can balance the cells. If the code keeps coming back after that, take it to a shop.

Typical fixes

  • Recharge the high-voltage battery with the car in Ready mode, clear the code and retest (Ford Escape manual)
  • Reseat all battery computer connectors, which means removing the high-voltage battery, so this is a shop job (Ford Escape manual)
  • Replace the Battery Energy Control Module if the connectors were already fully seated and no service bulletin applies (Ford Escape manual)
  • Let the battery computer balance the cells over repeated 24-hour key-off periods, and replace the high-voltage battery if the code still returns (Ford Escape manual)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Any work that means removing the high-voltage battery or touching orange cables
  • You need a scan tool that reads hybrid battery data (Ford IDS/FDRS or equivalent) to clear the code and watch the battery's state of charge
  • The car loses power, shows a red triangle warning, or won't go into Ready mode
  • Ask a dealer whether your hybrid battery warranty still applies before paying for a new battery or battery computer

FAQs

Is P0B24 serious?

Yes. RepairPal rates it Urgent and says the high-voltage system may not operate, and Ford's manual says the car limits electric motor power and battery charging while the code is set. Get it diagnosed soon.

Does P0B24 mean I need a new hybrid battery?

Not necessarily. Ford's procedure starts by recharging the battery, then reseating the battery computer's connectors, then letting the computer balance the cells over 24-hour key-off periods. A new high-voltage battery is the last step, only if the code keeps coming back after all of that.

Can P0B24 show up after replacing the battery computer?

Yes. Ford's manual says that after a new Battery Energy Control Module is installed, cell balancing codes can come back because the cells are still out of balance, and re-balancing them is needed to clear the problem.

Do the sources agree on P0B24?

Mostly, with differences in detail. RepairPal describes it as one cell not contributing equally to the high-voltage system, while Ford's manual defines it as one module's cell voltage straying from the average for 15 seconds. RepairPal says the high-voltage system may not operate, while Ford describes limited charging and reduced power. Ford's manual also names different warning lights in different places: the wrench light in the code description, and the red triangle stop-safely warning in its overview.

How much does it cost to fix P0B24?

None of the sources give a repair cost. It can range from simply recharging and rebalancing the battery to replacing the battery computer or the whole high-voltage battery, so get a proper diagnosis and ask about hybrid warranty coverage first.
