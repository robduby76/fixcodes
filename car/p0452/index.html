P0452: EVAP System Pressure Sensor Low Input (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0452 means the engine computer is seeing a signal from the EVAP (fuel tank) pressure sensor that is lower than it should be. The sources point to a faulty fuel tank pressure sensor or its wiring, cracked, plugged or pinched vapor lines, a leaky fuel pump module gasket, a loose fuel cap, and tank, canister or vent valve problems. They disagree on how often the fuel cap is to blame.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0452 means

The evaporative emission (EVAP) system catches fuel vapor from the fuel tank, stores it in a charcoal canister, and later draws it into the engine to be burned. The fuel tank pressure sensor tells the engine computer how much pressure or vacuum is in the tank and EVAP system, and RepairPal calls it the main sensor the computer relies on for its leak tests. OBD-Codes.com says P0452 sets when the computer decides system pressure is unusually low, which points to a problem in the system and not necessarily just the sensor. RepairPal defines it as the sensor reporting pressure values below specification during the EVAP monitor test or normal driving, and says shops see it set when readings stay below spec for ten seconds after a cold start, on two successive cold starts. Sensors work differently by make: OBD-Codes.com notes a Buick sensor reads about 0.1 volt with pressure in the tank and up to 5 volts with vacuum, while a Honda sensor's voltage rises as pressure builds. Manufacturer names include Ford 'FTP Sensor Circuit Low Conditions' and GM 'Fuel Tank Pressure Sensor Circuit Low Input Conditions' (OBD-Codes.com). The sources disagree on the fuel cap: OBD-Codes.com says over 75 percent of the time the cap simply wasn't clicked shut, while RepairPal lists the fuel cap as a common misdiagnosis for this code. Related codes include P0450, P0451 (sensor range/performance), P0453 (sensor high) and P0454 (sensor intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P0452 Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety or drivability right away but should still be checked. Both OBD-Codes.com and RepairPal say the check engine light is usually the only thing you will notice. It can keep the car from passing an emissions inspection. If you smell raw fuel, get it checked sooner, and never ignore a strong fuel smell.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine (service engine soon) light on (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Usually no other symptoms; both sources say the driver typically notices nothing else
  • In rare cases a noticeable fuel vapor smell (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed fuel tank pressure sensor (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists sensor, wiring or computer together)
  2. Open or short in the sensor harness, or a faulty electrical connection at the sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Loose fuel cap (OBD-Codes.com says this is behind it over 75 percent of the time; RepairPal lists the fuel cap as a common misdiagnosis, so the sources disagree)
  4. Cracked, broken, plugged or pinched vapor lines to the canister or tank (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Leaky gasket at the fuel pump module (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Defective fuel tank sending unit, or a defective or damaged fuel tank (RepairPal)
  7. Defective carbon canister, or in some cases a defective canister vent valve (RepairPal; RepairPal also lists the purge and vent valves as common misdiagnoses)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and write down the freeze frame data as a baseline (RepairPal). Other EVAP codes, such as P0455 or P0456, can point to a related leak.
  2. Check that the fuel cap is on and clicked shut, and look at its seal. OBD-Codes.com calls this the most common fix; RepairPal warns the cap is often not the real cause, so keep going if the code returns.
  3. Look for technical service bulletins or recalls for your car (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Check your emissions warranty. OBD-Codes.com notes many vehicles have long warranties on emission parts, such as 100,000 miles, so the repair may be covered.
  5. From under the car, look at the vapor hoses running from the top of the tank toward the frame rail for breaks or crimps (OBD-Codes.com), then clear the code and see if it returns.

Typical fixes

  • Tighten or replace the fuel cap if it was loose or its seal is bad (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a fuel tank pressure sensor that fails testing (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Repair open or shorted sensor wiring or a bad connector (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal says to verify the 5-volt reference, ground and signal circuits)
  • Replace cracked, plugged or pinched vapor lines, or the fuel pump module gasket (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair a canister, vent valve, sending unit or fuel tank problem found during diagnosis (RepairPal)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com says reaching the sensor usually means removing the fuel tank, a difficult and somewhat hazardous job better left to a technician with a lift
  • Replacing vapor lines on top of the tank also requires lowering the tank (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Diagnosis needs a scan tool to watch live tank pressure readings and test the circuit (RepairPal), or a dedicated EVAP tester (OBD-Codes.com)
  • The code comes back after the fuel cap has been checked, or you smell raw fuel

FAQs

Is P0452 serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate. Both sources say the check engine light is usually the only symptom, with a fuel smell in rare cases. It isn't usually an emergency, but it can fail an emissions inspection, so get it diagnosed within a reasonable time.

Can a loose gas cap cause P0452?

The sources disagree. OBD-Codes.com says over 75 percent of the time the cap wasn't clicked shut, which stops the tank from holding vacuum. RepairPal lists the fuel cap as a common misdiagnosis for P0452. Checking the cap is free, but if the code returns, the pressure sensor, its wiring and the vapor lines are next.

Where is the fuel tank pressure sensor?

On top of the fuel tank, in or near the fuel pump module, or on some cars in the purge line coming off the module (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal). OBD-Codes.com says the tank usually has to come down to reach it.

How much does it cost to fix P0452?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from a fuel cap to a pressure sensor that needs the fuel tank lowered, and OBD-Codes.com notes it may be covered by an emissions warranty, so check that and get a written estimate.
