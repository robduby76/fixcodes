P0251: Injection Pump Fuel Metering Control A Malfunction (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0251 is a diesel code that means the engine computer got an unexpected signal from the injection pump's fuel metering control, the part that sets how much fuel the pump delivers. OBD-Codes.com says a failed fuel rack position sensor or its wiring is the usual cause. RepairPal rates it 'Urgent' and also lists the pump actuator, the pump itself, wiring and the PCM.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0251 means

On diesel engines with an injection pump, the fuel metering control 'A' sits on or inside the pump. OBD-Codes.com describes it as a fuel rack position (FRP) sensor plus a fuel quantity actuator: the sensor turns the amount of fuel the actuator is delivering into a voltage signal, and the powertrain control module (PCM) uses it to decide how much fuel to give the engine. If that signal doesn't match what the PCM expects for current conditions, even for a moment, or looks wrong at key on, it stores P0251. RepairPal explains it in terms of pump pressure: the fuel pump control actuator controls the injection pump's output pressure, and the PCM sets P0251 when it detects an incorrect signal from that actuator. OBD-Codes.com says the code applies to diesel OBD-II engines generally but shows up more often on some Mercedes-Benz and VW vehicles, and searches for it center on Dodge Cummins 5.9, GM 6.5 diesel and VW TDI. Which part of the circuit counts as 'A' depends on the vehicle, so check its service information.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it checked soon. The sources disagree here: OBD-Codes.com calls P0251 not severe because it is usually an electrical fault the engine computer can compensate for, while RepairPal rates it 'Urgent' and says the engine may lose performance or stall. Since a stall can happen while driving, don't put off a diagnosis.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decrease in fuel economy (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decreased engine performance (RepairPal)
  • Engine may stall (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed fuel rack position sensor, rated the likely cause (OBD-Codes.com)
  2. Fuel injection pump actuator (fuel quantity actuator) failure (both sources)
  3. Open, short to voltage or short to ground in the sensor signal circuit, or lost power or ground at the sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Wiring issue (RepairPal)
  5. Fuel injection pump failure (RepairPal)
  6. Failed PCM, rated unlikely by OBD-Codes.com (both sources)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check for technical service bulletins for your year, make and model first, since the issue may be known with a known fix.
  2. Find the sensor connector on or beside the injection pump and look over the wiring and connector for rubbing, bare wires, burn marks or melted plastic.
  3. Unplug the connector and check the terminals for burning or green corrosion. OBD-Codes.com suggests cleaning them with electrical contact cleaner and a plastic brush, letting them dry and applying electrical grease.
  4. Clear the code with a scan tool and drive. If it doesn't come back, OBD-Codes.com says the connection was most likely the problem.
  5. If the engine stalls or loses power, have it diagnosed promptly rather than continuing to drive it.

Typical fixes

  • Clean or repair corroded or damaged connectors at the pump sensor
  • Repair open or shorted signal, power or ground wiring
  • Replace the fuel rack position sensor / fuel quantity actuator if circuits test good
  • Replace the fuel injection pump if it is at fault (RepairPal lists fuel pump replacement as the common repair)
  • Replace and program the PCM only after the other parts are ruled out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com's test: key off, unplug the sensor, then key on engine off and measure between the harness power and ground terminals; expect 12 V or 5 V depending on the manufacturer's spec, otherwise repair power or ground or suspect the PCM. Then measure the signal terminal to ground, which should read 5 V; if not, repair the signal wire or suspect the PCM.
  • If all circuit tests pass and the code keeps returning, OBD-Codes.com points to the FRP sensor / fuel quantity actuator, and says the PCM can't be ruled out until that part is replaced. A replacement PCM must be programmed to the vehicle.
  • The sources disagree on severity and focus: OBD-Codes.com calls it not severe and centers on the sensor circuit, while RepairPal rates it 'Urgent', lists stalling, and names the injection pump itself and fuel pump replacement as common repairs.

FAQs

Can I drive with P0251?

Possibly for a short time, since OBD-Codes.com says the PCM can usually compensate. But RepairPal rates it 'Urgent' and says the engine may stall, so get it diagnosed soon and avoid long trips until it is.

What usually causes P0251?

OBD-Codes.com says the most likely cause is a failed fuel rack position sensor, with wiring faults possible. RepairPal lists the injection pump actuator, the pump itself, wiring and the PCM.

Is P0251 a diesel-only code?

Yes. Both sources describe the diesel fuel injection pump, and OBD-Codes.com says it applies to diesel fueled OBD-II engines.

Does P0251 always mean a new injection pump?

No. OBD-Codes.com recommends checking service bulletins, connectors and wiring first, then testing the sensor circuit. Corroded or loose connections can set it, and clearing the code after cleaning them may be all that's needed.
