P0380: Glow Plug/Heater Circuit A (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 3 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0380 means the engine computer has found a fault in glow plug circuit "A" on a diesel engine. Glow plugs heat the combustion chambers so a cold diesel starts easily. The cause can be a bad glow plug, but both sources point out that the relay, glow plug control module, fuse or wiring are common culprits too.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0380 means

Diesel engines rely on compression heat to ignite fuel, so on a cold start glow plugs heat up red hot for a short time to raise cylinder temperature. OBD-Codes.com explains that the powertrain control module (PCM) uses the coolant temperature at key-on to decide whether to run them, and on some engines keeps them on for a while after starting to reduce white smoke and noise. It says P0380 sets when there is an open in a glow plug or its circuit. RepairPal describes the test differently: it says the code sets when the PCM sees less than 4 volts going to the glow plugs when they should be on, or more than 4 volts when they should be off. These descriptions don't conflict, but they show the code covers the whole circuit, not just the plugs. OBD-Codes.com also notes that GM describes this code slightly differently, as 'Glow Plug Performance Conditions', and that P0382 is the matching code for circuit "B". It says P0380 seems more common on Volkswagen, GMC, Chevrolet and Ford diesels, though any diesel can set it. This code only applies to diesel engines.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

P0380 usually isn't an immediate danger once the engine is running, but it shouldn't be ignored. OBD-Codes.com lists hard starting, especially in cold weather, and a 'wait to start' light that stays on longer than normal. RepairPal notes the car may be hard to start, will fail an emissions inspection with the check engine light on, and in rare cases shows no noticeable symptoms. The bigger risk is being stranded on a cold morning when the engine won't start. RepairPal's page also carries a general 'Urgent' severity note, so get it diagnosed soon, especially before cold weather. Stop and get help if you notice burning smells or melted wiring around the glow plug harness or relay.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Glow plug or 'wait to start' light stays on longer than normal, or stays on solid (per OBD-Codes.com)
  • Hard starting, especially in cold weather
  • Failed emissions inspection because the check engine light is on (per RepairPal)
  • In rare cases, no symptoms the driver notices (per RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty glow plug relay
  2. Faulty glow plug control module (control unit)
  3. One or more defective glow plugs
  4. Damaged glow plug wiring or connections (open, short to ground, corrosion, loose nuts)
  5. Blown fuse in the glow plug circuit (listed by OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze-frame data. If you also have P0382 or other glow plug codes, OBD-Codes.com suggests fixing them in the order they appear.
  2. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, particularly on GM trucks, as OBD-Codes.com recommends.
  3. Check the fuses for the glow plug circuit and replace any that are blown.
  4. Visually inspect glow plugs, wiring and connectors for corrosion, bent or loose pins, loose terminal nuts and burnt spots.
  5. With a multimeter, check each glow plug's resistance and that its connector gets power and ground, comparing against the manufacturer's specs.
  6. If you replace a glow plug, start it in the threads by hand first, like a spark plug, to avoid cross-threading.

Typical fixes

  • Replace a failed glow plug relay
  • Replace a failed glow plug control module
  • Replace glow plugs that fail resistance testing
  • Repair damaged or corroded glow plug wiring and connectors
  • Replace a blown fuse, then find out why it blew

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The glow plug relay or control module isn't easy to reach (RepairPal says the control unit itself must then be tested)
  • You need a scan tool that can command the glow plug system on to check output voltage
  • Glow plugs have to come out of an aluminum head and feel seized, since a snapped plug is an expensive repair
  • The code returns after replacing glow plugs, which RepairPal says is often a sign the real fault was the relay, control unit or harness

FAQs

Is it safe to drive with P0380?

Usually, once the engine is running, but expect hard starts in cold weather. Get it fixed before winter so you aren't stranded.

Should I just replace all the glow plugs?

Not before testing. RepairPal lists replacing glow plugs as a common misdiagnosis when the actual problem is the glow plug relay, the control unit or the wiring harness.

What's the difference between P0380 and P0382?

OBD-Codes.com says they are very similar: P0380 is glow plug circuit "A" and P0382 is circuit "B".

Can a gas engine set P0380?

Glow plugs are a diesel part, so this code is for diesel engines.
