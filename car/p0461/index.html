P0461: Fuel Level Sensor A Circuit Range/Performance (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0461 means the engine computer (PCM) sees a fuel level sensor signal that is out of its expected range or doesn't make sense, for example the fuel level changing faster than is normal. The sensor (sending unit) sits in the fuel tank, often as part of the fuel pump module. The sources list a failed sensor or a sensing arm stuck mechanically, corroded connections or wiring faults, refueling with the ignition on, and less likely a failed PCM.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0461 means

The fuel level sensor (FLS) is usually mounted in the fuel tank, often in the top of the fuel pump module. It turns the fuel level into an electrical signal for the PCM, which uses it to know how much fuel is in the tank and to work out fuel economy, and typically shares that information with other modules over the vehicle's data network. OBD-Codes.com says P0461 sets when this signal doesn't match the normal operating voltages stored in the PCM, even for a second, and that the PCM also checks the signal at initial key on. It says the code can come from a mechanical cause (an implausible fuel level reading, such as the level changing too rapidly, which refueling with the ignition on or the engine running can cause) or from an electrical fault in the sensor circuit. RepairPal defines P0461 as Fuel Level Sensor "A" Circuit Range/Performance and says the PCM sets it when it detects an irrational signal from the fuel level sensor. The sources differ on causes: OBD-Codes.com calls a failed sensor or mechanically stuck sensing arm likely, wiring opens and shorts possible and a failed PCM unlikely, and says corroded fuel tank connections are the most common area of concern; RepairPal lists fuel pump failure (because the sensor may be part of the fuel pump), PCM failure and wiring issues, without ranking them. They also differ on severity, as described above. OBD-Codes.com says the code shows up more often on certain Chrysler, GM, Ford, Lincoln, Mercury, Kia, Mazda, Mercedes-Benz, Infiniti, Nissan and Subaru vehicles. Related codes include P0460 (circuit), P0462 (circuit low), P0463 (circuit high) and P0464 (circuit intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P0461 Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety or drivability right away. OBD-Codes.com says severity depends on the failure: an electrical fault is not as severe because the engine computer can compensate, but a mechanical failure (such as a stuck sensing arm) is severe. Either way the fuel gauge may stay stuck on Empty or Full or read wrong. Until it's fixed, track your mileage and refuel early, and don't rely on the gauge, the low fuel light or the distance-to-empty display. Running out of fuel in traffic or on the highway is dangerous.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Fuel gauge reads wrong, sometimes stuck on Empty or Full all the time (OBD-Codes.com); RepairPal lists an inaccurate fuel gauge reading
  • Decrease in the distance-to-empty mileage shown on the dash (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decrease in perceived fuel economy (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed fuel level sensor or a sensing arm stuck mechanically (OBD-Codes.com: likely)
  2. Corroded or damaged connector terminals or wiring at the fuel tank, which OBD-Codes.com calls the most common area of concern (RepairPal lists wiring issues)
  3. Open, short to voltage or short to ground in the signal circuit to the fuel level sensor (OBD-Codes.com: possible)
  4. Refueling with the ignition on or the engine running, which can make the fuel level change faster than the PCM expects (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Fuel pump failure, since the sensor may be part of the fuel pump (RepairPal)
  6. Failed PCM (RepairPal; OBD-Codes.com: unlikely)
  7. On some Ford products, an aftermarket remote start system can set a false code; Ford has a technical service bulletin on it that also covers auxiliary fuel tanks (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Search for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle first; the maker may have a PCM reflash for this code (OBD-Codes.com).
  2. Always refuel with the ignition off; OBD-Codes.com says refueling with the ignition on or engine running can set this code.
  3. Find the fuel level sensor connector at the tank or fuel pump module and inspect it and the wiring for rubbing, bare wires, burn spots, melted plastic, and burned or green, corroded terminals (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Clean corroded terminals with electrical contact cleaner and a plastic bristle brush, let them dry and apply electrical grease, then clear the code and see if it returns. If it doesn't, the connection was most likely the problem (OBD-Codes.com). Meanwhile, track mileage and refuel early instead of trusting the gauge.

Typical fixes

  • Clean or repair corroded fuel tank connector terminals and damaged wiring (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Repair an open or short in the sensor signal circuit, or the power or ground wire if the voltage test fails (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a fuel level sensor that fails the resistance test or has a stuck sensing arm; RepairPal notes it may be part of the fuel pump, so the repair can mean replacing the pump module (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Apply a manufacturer PCM reflash if a TSB covers it (OBD-Codes.com)
  • PCM replacement only after everything else checks out; OBD-Codes.com says a PCM can't be ruled out until the sensor is replaced, and a replacement must be programmed to the vehicle

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Testing uses a digital voltmeter at the sensor connector with key on, engine off; OBD-Codes.com says it should read 12 or 5 volts depending on the manufacturer's specification
  • Sensor resistance is checked with an ohmmeter against the maker's specification; it should be neither zero nor infinite (OBD-Codes.com gives about 80 ohms at half a tank as an example)
  • Reaching the sensor may mean working at the top of the fuel tank or fuel pump module
  • You smell raw fuel, see damage to the tank or wiring, or a new PCM needs programming

FAQs

Is P0461 serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate. OBD-Codes.com says it depends: an electrical fault is less severe because the PCM can compensate, while a mechanical failure is severe. The main everyday risk is a wrong fuel gauge, so track your mileage and refuel early until it's fixed.

Can refueling cause P0461?

OBD-Codes.com says yes: refueling with the ignition on or the engine running can make the fuel level change faster than the PCM expects and set the code. Refuel with the ignition off, clear the code and see if it comes back.

What is the difference between P0461 and P0462?

P0461 is a range/performance fault, meaning the fuel level signal doesn't make sense, while P0462 is a circuit low signal. Both point to the fuel level sensor and its wiring.

How much does it cost to fix P0461?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from cleaning a corroded connector to replacing the fuel level sensor or pump module, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
