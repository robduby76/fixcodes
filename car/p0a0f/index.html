P0A0F: Engine Failed to Start (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 5 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0A0F means a hybrid's control system tried to use the gasoline engine and it didn't start or didn't put out the power expected. It's best known on the Toyota Prius. Causes range from simply running out of gas, to a loose vacuum line or a cold-weather intake problem covered by Toyota service bulletins, to rare serious failures like a seized engine or a damaged hybrid transaxle.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0A0F means

In a hybrid, the gasoline engine is started and stopped by the hybrid control computer rather than a normal starter. According to Toyota's Prius repair manual, P0A0F is stored by the hybrid (HV) control computer, which then puts the car in fail-safe mode, when the engine computer reports a problem that affects hybrid control. Toyota uses information codes to narrow it down: abnormal engine output, engine unable to start, the same two caused by running out of fuel, and (on the 2004 Prius manual) an engine that won't start while cranking because the engine or transaxle is seized or jammed. RepairPal's generic definition focuses on that last case: it says the code sets when the power management control module detects the engine or transmission is seized or otherwise fails to operate properly.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0A0F as Urgent and lists "vehicle will not operate" as the symptom. On Toyota hybrids the hybrid control computer goes into a fail-safe mode when it sets this code, so the car may run poorly, refuse to start the engine, or not drive at all. The check engine light will also fail an emissions inspection.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light and hybrid system warning on
  • Vehicle will not operate (RepairPal)
  • Engine won't start or runs with poor power, with P3190 (Poor Engine Power) or P3191 (Engine Does Not Start) also stored (Toyota bulletin T-SB-0041-15)
  • Code appears after the car sits in temperatures below 23°F (-5°C), together with P2111 (Toyota bulletin T-SB-0103-12)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Running out of fuel (Toyota repair manual information codes 533 and 534)
  2. An engine control or fuel injection (SFI) system fault reported by the engine computer (Toyota repair manual)
  3. Purge valve (VSV) or PCV vacuum line come loose from the throttle body on 2010-2013 Prius and 2012-2013 Prius V (Toyota bulletin T-SB-0041-15)
  4. Cold-soak condition with P2111 on 2010-2015 Prius, 2012-2015 Prius Plug-in and 2012-2016 Prius V with the 2ZR-FXE engine (Toyota bulletin T-SB-0103-12)
  5. Internal engine failure or a seized engine (RepairPal; Toyota information code 238)
  6. Hybrid transaxle failure, a damaged transmission input damper, or wiring to the hybrid computer (RepairPal; Toyota information code 238)
  7. A failed engine computer or power management control module (both sources)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Safety first: hybrids carry high voltage. Never unplug orange cables or open the hybrid battery or inverter yourself.
  2. Check the fuel level first. Toyota's manual has separate P0A0F sub-codes for running out of fuel, and a near-empty tank on a hybrid can set this code.
  3. Read every stored code, including engine codes, with a scan tool, and write down freeze frame data. Toyota's procedure starts by reading engine codes and fixing those first, since P0A0F is usually a result of an engine-side fault.
  4. Look for P3190, P3191, P0171 or P2111 alongside P0A0F, and check whether your model year is covered by Toyota bulletins T-SB-0041-15 or T-SB-0103-12.
  5. On 2010-2013 Prius and 2012-2013 Prius V, look at the vacuum line from the purge valve and the PCV hose at the throttle body to see if one has popped off (T-SB-0041-15).
  6. If the engine won't crank or turn at all, stop there. Toyota's code 238 procedure checks for a seized engine or transaxle, which needs a shop.

Typical fixes

  • Add fuel, clear the codes and confirm the car starts and runs normally (Toyota manual run-out-of-fuel sub-codes)
  • Repair the engine or fuel system fault behind the other engine codes stored (Toyota manual)
  • Reattach the vacuum line, replace the purge valve and reprogram the engine computer on covered Prius and Prius V (T-SB-0041-15)
  • Replace the intake manifold with the updated part and reprogram the engine computer on covered Prius models with the cold-soak condition (T-SB-0103-12)
  • Replace the engine computer only if no engine codes explain the problem (Toyota manual)
  • Repair a seized engine, or replace the hybrid transaxle or transmission input damper, if that's what diagnosis finds (RepairPal; Toyota manual code 238)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The engine won't crank, or the car won't go into READY mode
  • You need a scan tool that reads hybrid system codes and information codes (Toyota Techstream or equivalent)
  • Your car may be covered by a Toyota service bulletin or hybrid warranty, so ask a dealer first
  • Any work near high-voltage orange cables

FAQs

Is P0A0F serious?

It can be. RepairPal rates it Urgent and says the vehicle may not operate. Sometimes the cause is as simple as running out of gas or a loose vacuum line, but it can also point to a seized engine or failed hybrid transaxle, so don't ignore it.

Can running out of gas cause P0A0F?

Yes. Toyota's Prius repair manual has P0A0F sub-codes specifically for abnormal engine output and engine unable to start due to running out of fuel.

Is there a Toyota service bulletin for P0A0F?

Yes, two that we found. T-SB-0041-15 covers 2010-2013 Prius and 2012-2013 Prius V with a purge valve vacuum line that separates from the throttle body. T-SB-0103-12 covers P2111 with P0A0F after a cold soak below 23°F on 2010-2015 Prius, 2012-2015 Prius Plug-in and 2012-2016 Prius V, fixed with an updated intake manifold and engine computer update.

Do the sources agree on P0A0F?

Not fully. RepairPal describes it as the engine or transmission being seized or failing to operate, and lists transaxle failure, internal engine failure and the control module as causes. Toyota's manual shows that most P0A0F sub-codes are set because the engine computer reported an engine output or starting problem (including running out of fuel), and only one sub-code (238) covers a seized engine or transaxle. Toyota's bulletins add vacuum line and cold-weather causes that RepairPal doesn't mention.

How much does it cost to fix P0A0F?

None of the sources give a repair cost. It ranges from a tank of gas or reattaching a vacuum line to replacing an engine or hybrid transaxle, so get a diagnosis and written estimate, and check for bulletin or warranty coverage first.
