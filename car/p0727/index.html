P0727: Engine Speed Input Circuit No Signal (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0727 means the computer isn't getting any signal from the engine speed input sensor. The transmission uses that signal to pick and time gears, so without it the transmission may shift badly or go into limp mode. Both sources agree on the effect; they differ on which sensor supplies the signal and on whether mechanical transmission slippage is a likely cause.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0727 means

OBD-Codes.com says P0727 is stored when the powertrain control module (PCM) detects no input voltage signal from the engine speed sensor, which may also be called the transmission input speed sensor or input speed sensor. It describes a hall-effect sensor fitted through the transmission case near the front of the input shaft, reading a toothed reluctor ring on that shaft. The code sets, and the check engine light may come on, if the PCM gets no engine speed input signal voltage for a set time under certain conditions, and it can be caused by a mechanical or an electrical problem. The PCM or transmission control module (TCM) may enter limp-in mode. RepairPal says the engine speed sensor tells the TCM how fast the engine is spinning so the right gear can be engaged, and that most vehicles use the crankshaft position sensor for this information; it lists the crankshaft position sensor, not a transmission input sensor, as the common cause. That is a disagreement worth knowing: depending on the vehicle, the signal may come from a dedicated input speed sensor in the transmission or from the crankshaft position sensor, so check your vehicle's service information before buying a part. It is a generic OBD-II code (OBD-Codes.com names BMW, GMC, Chevy Duramax, Saturn, Audi, Jaguar, VW, Volvo and Kia as examples), but repair steps vary by make and model. Related codes are P0725 (circuit malfunction), P0726 (range/performance) and P0728 (intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it diagnosed soon and drive gently until you do. OBD-Codes.com says P0727 should be fixed with urgency because severe transmission damage or drivability problems may result, and RepairPal rates it Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe. If the transmission shifts harshly, slips, won't shift or drops into limp mode, avoid highway driving and have it checked.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com says it may come on)
  • Transmission fails to shift or shifts erratically (OBD-Codes.com); transmission may not shift properly (RepairPal)
  • Harsh shifts in limp-in mode (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission slipping or delayed engagement (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Erratic or dead speedometer/odometer, or an incorrect or dead tachometer (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Increased fuel consumption (RepairPal)
  • Other transmission input/output speed codes stored as well (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty engine/transmission input speed sensor, or the transmission output speed sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  2. Faulty crankshaft position sensor, which RepairPal says most vehicles use for this signal and lists as the common repair
  3. Open or shorted wiring or bad connectors in the sensor circuit (both sources)
  4. Damaged or worn reluctor ring, or heavy metal deposits on the sensor's magnetic tip (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Mechanical transmission failure causing transmission or clutch slippage (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Failed PCM or TCM (RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check the transmission fluid level and condition first. OBD-Codes.com says fluid that smells burnt, looks extremely black or has a heavy metallic look points to mechanical failure, and that a level more than a quart low means checking the transmission housing, lines and cooler for leaks.
  2. Inspect the sensor wiring and connectors for damage (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. Read and write down all stored codes and freeze frame data, clear the codes and test drive to see if P0727 comes back (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. If it returns, watch the engine input speed on a scanner while driving. OBD-Codes.com says input speed that differs greatly from engine RPM points to a bad input speed sensor or a damaged or worn reluctor ring.
  5. If you remove a transmission-mounted sensor, put a container underneath because hot transmission fluid may spill out (OBD-Codes.com), and check its magnetic tip for metal deposits.

Typical fixes

  • Repair damaged wiring or connectors (both sources list wiring)
  • Replace a faulty input speed sensor that tests out of spec (OBD-Codes.com), or the crankshaft position sensor on vehicles that use it (RepairPal)
  • Clean metal deposits off the sensor tip or repair a damaged reluctor ring (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair the transmission if it is slipping or has failed mechanically (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair or replace a failed PCM or TCM, only after the sensor and wiring check out (RepairPal lists both as causes)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission slips, won't shift or goes into limp mode
  • The fluid is burnt, black or metallic, which OBD-Codes.com says suggests mechanical transmission failure
  • Testing the sensor and its circuits needs a digital volt/ohmmeter, the factory specifications and ideally an oscilloscope (OBD-Codes.com)
  • You can't tell whether your vehicle uses a transmission input speed sensor or the crankshaft position sensor for this signal

FAQs

Is P0727 serious?

Yes. OBD-Codes.com says to fix it with urgency because severe transmission damage or drivability problems may result, and RepairPal rates it Urgent. It can make the transmission shift badly, slip or go into limp mode.

Which sensor causes P0727?

It depends on the vehicle. OBD-Codes.com describes a transmission input speed sensor near the front of the input shaft, while RepairPal says most vehicles use the crankshaft position sensor and lists it as the common repair. Check your vehicle's service information before buying a sensor.

What's the difference between P0726 and P0727?

Both involve the engine speed input signal. P0726 means a signal is present but out of range or doesn't make sense, while P0727 means the computer sees no signal at all from the sensor (OBD-Codes.com).

How much does it cost to fix P0727?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from a wiring repair or sensor to transmission work if it's slipping, so get a diagnosis or a written estimate before replacing parts.
