P0847: Transmission Fluid Pressure Sensor/Switch B Circuit Low (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0847 means the transmission computer is seeing a signal from transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch "B" that is lower than it should be. OBD-Codes.com says it is typically an electrical circuit problem, most likely a failed or internally shorted sensor, with a short to ground in the signal wire also possible. RepairPal also lists low transmission fluid and a failed TCM.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0847 means

An automatic transmission uses fluid pressure to apply its clutches and change gears. A transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch (TFPS) turns that pressure into an electrical signal so the powertrain control module (PCM), or a separate transmission control module (TCM), can tell the operating pressure or when a shift is happening. OBD-Codes.com says the TFPS is usually attached to the side of the valve body inside the transmission, though on some vehicles it screws into the side of the case. It says P0847 is set when the "B" input does not match the normal operating voltages stored in the PCM/TCM's memory, here on the low side, that it is typically an electrical circuit issue, and that it may also come from internal mechanical problems in the transmission. RepairPal describes it more broadly: the PCM or TCM sets P0847 when it detects an issue with the hydraulic clutch or the pressure switch. Both sources agree on the name "Circuit Low". It is a generic OBD-II code; OBD-Codes.com lists Chevrolet, Acura, Honda, Saturn, Jeep and Toyota among makes that use it. Which sensor is "B" depends on the vehicle, so check a repair manual for yours. Its sibling codes for the same sensor are P0845 (circuit), P0846 (circuit range/performance), P0848 (circuit high) and P0849 (circuit intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it diagnosed soon. RepairPal rates P0847 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe. OBD-Codes.com is milder: it says that because this is an electrical failure the PCM/TCM can compensate to some degree, though it may change how the transmission shifts or put it in limp mode (starting off in 2nd or 3rd gear). If shifting feels wrong or the car is stuck in one gear, drive gently and avoid highway and heavy-traffic driving until it's checked.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Change in shift quality, or the transmission doesn't shift properly (both sources)
  • Car starts off in 2nd or 3rd gear because the transmission is in limp mode (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Increased fuel consumption (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed or internally shorted transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch (both sources; OBD-Codes.com rates it the likely cause)
  2. Short to ground in the sensor signal circuit (OBD-Codes.com rates it possible; RepairPal lists a wiring issue)
  3. Corroded external connector terminals (OBD-Codes.com says this is the most common area of concern)
  4. Low transmission fluid level (RepairPal)
  5. Faulty PCM or TCM (both sources; OBD-Codes.com rates it unlikely and says a replacement must be programmed)
  6. The sources differ: RepairPal adds low fluid level, while OBD-Codes.com focuses on the electrical circuit and says internal transmission problems can't be ruled out until the sensor has been replaced

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and diagnose any other transmission or power-supply codes along with P0847.
  2. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle; a known issue may have a known fix.
  3. Check the transmission fluid level and condition.
  4. Find the pressure sensor/switch connector and inspect the wiring for scraping, rubbing, bare wires, burn spots or melted plastic. Unplug it and look for burned or green, corroded terminals; clean them with electrical contact cleaner and a plastic brush, let them dry and apply electrical grease.
  5. Clear the code and see if it comes back. If it doesn't, OBD-Codes.com says the connections were most likely the problem.

Typical fixes

  • Clean or repair corroded connectors and damaged wiring (including a short to ground)
  • Top up the transmission fluid to the correct level
  • Replace the transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch
  • Replace and program the PCM/TCM only after other causes are ruled out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission shifts badly, is stuck in limp mode or the code comes back after cleaning the connections
  • On many vehicles the sensor is mounted on the valve body inside the transmission, so replacing it means removing the transmission pan
  • OBD-Codes.com's circuit tests use a digital voltmeter and ohmmeter and need the manufacturer's specifications: reference voltage is 12 volts or 5 volts depending on the vehicle, and sensor resistance varies by model
  • A replacement PCM/TCM must be programmed or calibrated to the vehicle

FAQs

Is P0847 serious?

It can be. RepairPal rates it Urgent. OBD-Codes.com says the computer can often compensate because it is an electrical failure, but shifting may change or the transmission may go into limp mode. Get it checked soon.

Can I keep driving with P0847?

Only gently and for short trips until it's diagnosed. If the car is stuck in one gear or shifts badly, avoid highway driving and get it looked at.

What is the difference between P0847 and P0848?

Both are for transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch "B". P0847 means the signal is too low (OBD-Codes.com points to a short to ground or a failed sensor), while P0848 means it is too high (a short to power, an open ground or a failed sensor).

What is the difference between P0845, P0846, P0847, P0848 and P0849?

All are for transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch "B". OBD-Codes.com lists P0845 as circuit, P0846 as circuit range/performance, P0847 as circuit low, P0848 as circuit high and P0849 as circuit intermittent.

How much does it cost to fix P0847?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from cleaning a connector or adding fluid to replacing the sensor or the TCM, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
