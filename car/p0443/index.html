P0443: EVAP Purge Control Valve Circuit (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0443 means the engine computer has found a problem with the EVAP purge control valve (purge solenoid) circuit, the electrical circuit it uses to open the valve that lets fuel tank vapors into the engine. Common causes listed by the sources are a purge solenoid with an internal short or open, damaged wiring or a corroded connector, and less often a bad driver circuit inside the engine computer.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0443 means

The evaporative emission (EVAP) system stores fuel vapors from the tank in a charcoal canister and later lets the engine draw them in and burn them, through the purge valve. OBD-Codes.com explains that the purge solenoid gets switched battery voltage, and the engine computer (ECM/PCM) opens it by grounding the control wire. With the solenoid off, the computer should see high voltage on that wire, and with it on, close to zero. If it doesn't see the expected voltages, or senses an open circuit, P0443 sets. OBD-Codes.com stresses that this is a CIRCUIT code, not necessarily a bad valve. RepairPal defines it as an improper purge flow caused by a physically malfunctioning purge valve or a shorted or open purge valve circuit, and its summary says the computer sets it when the EVAP system has flow when it should not. So the sources differ: OBD-Codes.com treats P0443 as electrical, while RepairPal also counts a valve that is mechanically stuck open or closed, and its technician notes tie the code to the EVAP leak test. Related codes include P0444 (circuit open) and P0445 (circuit shorted); OBD-Codes.com notes that other EVAP codes often appear alongside it.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P0443 Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety or drivability right away, and both sources say there may be no drivability problems at all. It can still cause a failed emissions inspection, and if the purge valve is stuck open the engine may run rough, hesitate or stall. If you smell raw fuel, find the source before driving: fuel vapor near a hot engine or exhaust is a fire risk.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Often no drivability problems at all (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Lean condition, rough running, hesitation or stalling, mainly if the purge valve is stuck open (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • A whooshing sound of pressure when you remove the gas cap, pointing to a purge valve that isn't working or is stuck closed (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Noticeable fuel odor in some cases (RepairPal)
  • Other EVAP codes stored at the same time (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Bad purge solenoid with an internal short or open (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists an electrically shorted purge valve solenoid)
  2. Wiring harness chafed or rubbing another part, causing a short or open in the control circuit (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  3. Connector worn, broken or shorted from water getting in (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  4. Purge valve physically stuck open or closed (RepairPal; OBD-Codes.com says the code points to the circuit, not necessarily the valve)
  5. Bad purge solenoid driver circuit inside the engine computer (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and write down the freeze frame data (RepairPal). Note any other EVAP codes, since they change what to look at.
  2. Find the purge solenoid (usually in the engine bay on a vacuum line to the intake) and inspect its connector and wiring for damage, rubbing or water (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. If you have a scan tool that can command the purge solenoid, switch it on and listen or feel for a click; it should click once, or repeatedly on some models (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Don't just buy a gas cap or a purge valve: RepairPal lists the fuel cap, the purge valve and the vent valve as common misdiagnoses for this code.

Typical fixes

  • Replace the purge solenoid if it doesn't click when its control wire is grounded with battery voltage present (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair an open or short in the purge solenoid wiring, or a damaged or corroded connector (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Replace a purge valve that is physically stuck open or closed (RepairPal)
  • Replace or repair the engine computer only if the solenoid and wiring test good and the computer's driver circuit is proven bad (OBD-Codes.com)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Testing the control circuit means checking for battery voltage at the solenoid and grounding the control wire with a jumper; OBD-Codes.com says not to attempt removing the wire from the ECM connector if you're unsure how
  • RepairPal's technician notes say the EVAP leak test uses the fuel tank pressure sensor, so a stuck valve, a leak or a bad pressure sensor can be involved; a shop smoke or scan-tool test can sort that out
  • You smell raw fuel, or the engine stalls or runs badly

FAQs

Is P0443 serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate. Both sources say there may be no drivability problems, but a stuck-open purge valve can cause rough running or stalling, and the code can fail an emissions test. Get it diagnosed within a reasonable time.

Does P0443 mean I need a new purge valve?

Not necessarily. OBD-Codes.com says P0443 points to the purge control circuit, not necessarily the valve, so wiring and connectors are suspects too, and RepairPal lists replacing the purge valve as a common misdiagnosis. Test the solenoid and its circuit before buying parts.

Can a loose gas cap cause P0443?

Neither source lists the gas cap as a cause, and RepairPal names the fuel cap as a common misdiagnosis for this code. Checking that the cap is tight doesn't hurt, but it's unlikely to be the fix.

How much does it cost to fix P0443?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from repairing a wire or connector to replacing the purge solenoid, or rarely the engine computer, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
