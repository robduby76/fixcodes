P0410: Secondary Air Injection System Malfunction (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0410 means the engine computer (PCM) found a fault in the secondary air injection system, the pump that blows fresh air into the exhaust after a cold start. Common causes are failed or restricted check valves, water that has frozen or corroded the air pump, a blown air pump fuse, a bad air pump relay, faulty control solenoids or vacuum lines, and carbon-plugged air passages.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0410 means

Secondary air injection is an emissions system that pumps outside air into the exhaust for a short time after a cold start. The extra air helps burn off the rich exhaust a cold engine makes, cutting hydrocarbons and carbon monoxide. It typically has an air pump, hoses or metal pipes to route the air, a solenoid or valve to control the flow, and one-way check valves that stop exhaust from flowing back into the pump (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal). P0410 is a generic OBD-II code and the broad 'system malfunction' code for this system. RepairPal says it sets when the PCM doesn't see the front oxygen sensor voltage drop enough while the pump is supposed to be adding air. The two sources disagree on the usual cause. OBD-Codes.com says about 90% of the time the check valves have worn out and let water from the exhaust back into the pump, where it can freeze in cold, humid climates, so the pump can't turn and the fuse blows. RepairPal instead points to check valves that are restricted (especially on many GM vehicles), a bad pump or relay, and carbon buildup. Related codes include P0411 (incorrect flow) and P0412 through P0419 (switching valve and control circuits).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get this checked within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P0410 'Moderate', meaning it may not threaten safety right away but should be diagnosed soon. Many cars show nothing but the check engine light, but the car may fail an emissions inspection while the code is set. If the pump is making a loud noise or keeps blowing its fuse, have it looked at sooner.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Often no other symptoms (RepairPal)
  • Hesitation on acceleration in some cases (both sources)
  • Noise from a bad or stuck air pump (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Worn, failed or restricted one-way check valve(s) (both sources; OBD-Codes.com says worn valves that let water back in, RepairPal says restricted valves on many GM vehicles)
  2. Water collected in the air pump or vent solenoid, freezing or corroding the pump (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Blown air pump fuse, often from a frozen pump overloading the circuit (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Faulty air pump or air pump relay (RepairPal)
  5. Faulty air control solenoids or vacuum lines (RepairPal)
  6. System restricted or plugged with carbon (RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check the air pump fuse under the hood. OBD-Codes.com notes a frozen pump commonly blows it, so if it's blown, look for why before just replacing it.
  2. On a stone-cold start, listen under the hood. RepairPal says the pump should start after about 5 to 10 seconds and sounds like a vacuum cleaner. If you hear nothing, check the pump and its relay.
  3. If you hear the pump, inspect the check valves, control solenoid, rubber hoses and metal air pipes for cracks, disconnections or blockage (RepairPal).
  4. Look for water inside the pump or hoses, a sign the check valves are letting exhaust moisture back in (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. With a live-data scan tool on a cold start, watch the front oxygen sensor voltage. RepairPal says it should drop below about 0.125 volts as the pump kicks in; if it doesn't, the air isn't reaching the exhaust.
  6. Check whether a technical service bulletin applies to your vehicle; OBD-Codes.com notes a GM TSB for this code.

Typical fixes

  • Replace the air pump fuse (after finding why it blew)
  • Replace the air pump together with the one-way check valve(s), and clean out the hoses (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace the air pump inlet hose (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a failed air pump relay (RepairPal)
  • Replace a faulty air control solenoid or damaged vacuum lines (RepairPal)
  • Clear carbon-restricted passages or replace restricted check valves (RepairPal)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The pump runs but the oxygen sensors still don't respond, which points to restricted check valves or carbon-plugged passages that can be hard to reach
  • On GM V6 or V8 engines, RepairPal says the check valves must be replaced as a set because the PCM needs to see an even oxygen sensor drop on both banks, or the code keeps coming back
  • OBD-Codes.com warns that replacing only the air pump often lets the problem return, so the check valves and hoses need attention too
  • RepairPal warns the air pump is often replaced when the relay was the real problem, so get it tested first
  • OBD-Codes.com notes the repair may be covered by your vehicle's emissions warranty even if the bumper-to-bumper warranty has ended, so ask a dealer

FAQs

Is it safe to drive with P0410?

RepairPal rates it 'Moderate'. Many cars show no symptom other than the check engine light, but get it diagnosed within a reasonable time, and expect an emissions inspection failure while the light is on.

Why does P0410 often show up in winter?

OBD-Codes.com explains that worn check valves let water from the exhaust back into the air pump. In cold, humid weather that water can freeze, so the pump can't turn when the PCM switches it on, which overloads the circuit and blows the fuse.

Should I just replace the air pump for P0410?

Not by itself. OBD-Codes.com says the problem may come back unless you also replace the one-way check valve and clean the hoses, and RepairPal says the pump is often replaced when the relay was at fault.

How much does it cost to fix P0410?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from a fuse or relay to a new air pump and check valves, and OBD-Codes.com notes it may be covered by the emissions warranty, so check that and get a written estimate first.
