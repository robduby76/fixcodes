P0010: "A" Camshaft Position Actuator Circuit, Bank 1 (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0010 means the engine computer has found a problem in the electrical circuit that controls the variable valve timing actuator for the 'A' camshaft on bank 1. Common causes are a faulty oil control valve (the VVT solenoid), an open or short in its wiring, or, less often, a failed engine computer.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0010 means

Many modern engines use variable valve timing (VVT, also called VCT) to shift the camshaft for better power and efficiency. The engine computer (PCM/ECM) does this with an oil control valve (OCV), also called a solenoid valve, that directs engine oil pressure to a camshaft actuator. OBD-Codes.com says P0010 sets when the voltage in that actuator circuit falls below a set limit for a set amount of time, which varies by make, model and engine. RepairPal names the code '"A" Camshaft Position Actuator Circuit/Open' and describes the trigger more broadly: the camshaft does not respond the way the PCM requests. Bank 1 is the side of the engine with cylinder 1, and the 'A' camshaft is generally the intake, left or front camshaft. P0013 is the matching circuit code for camshaft 'B' (usually exhaust), P0020 is the bank 2 version, and P0011 and P0012 are timing over-advanced and over-retarded codes for the same camshaft.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it diagnosed promptly. RepairPal rates P0010 Urgent, meaning it can lead to significant problems and should be checked right away. OBD-Codes.com gives no severity rating and lists only the check engine light and rough running as typical symptoms, so the two sources disagree on how serious it usually is. Either way, cam timing that the engine computer can't control can hurt power and fuel economy, and RepairPal's only listed common repair is an oil and filter change, which is cheap to rule out first.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Rough running (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decreased engine performance (RepairPal)
  • Increased fuel consumption (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty oil control valve (OCV), the VVT solenoid valve (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists variable valve timing actuator failure)
  2. Open or short in the VVT/VCT circuit wiring (both sources)
  3. Open or short inside the oil control valve or solenoid itself (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Failed or damaged engine computer (PCM) (both sources)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze-frame data. If P0010 shows up with cam timing codes such as P0011 or P0012, or with cam or crank sensor codes, note them all before replacing parts.
  2. Check the engine oil level and condition. RepairPal lists an oil and filter change as the common repair for P0010, and VVT systems run on engine oil pressure.
  3. Carefully inspect the bank 1 VVT/VCT wiring and connectors, especially at the oil control valve, for damage, corrosion, oil in the connector or loose pins, and repair as needed (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. With the engine warm, test the oil control valve's operation, as OBD-Codes.com recommends, and replace or repair it if it fails.

Typical fixes

  • Repair damaged VVT/VCT circuit wiring or connectors (both sources)
  • Replace a faulty bank 1 oil control valve, VVT solenoid or camshaft actuator (both sources)
  • Change the engine oil and filter with the correct oil specification (RepairPal's listed common repair)
  • Replace or reprogram the engine computer only after the wiring and valve have been ruled out (both sources list PCM failure)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The wiring and oil control valve check out but the code keeps coming back
  • You need the solenoid circuit tested for voltage, resistance and the computer's control signal with a meter or scope
  • The engine is running badly, rattling or stalling
  • Several cam or crank codes are set together, which can point to timing chain or mechanical problems

FAQs

Can I drive with P0010?

Only gently and get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0010 Urgent, while OBD-Codes.com gives no rating and lists rough running as the main symptom besides the check engine light.

Is P0010 a bad VVT solenoid?

Often. OBD-Codes.com lists a faulty oil control valve first, but it is a circuit code, so check the wiring and connector at the valve before replacing parts.

Can low or dirty oil cause P0010?

RepairPal lists an oil and filter change as the common repair for P0010, since the VVT system uses engine oil pressure. OBD-Codes.com focuses on the electrical circuit and the valve instead, so check both.

What's the difference between P0010 and P0011?

Both are about the same 'A' camshaft on bank 1. P0010 is a problem in the actuator's control circuit, while P0011 means the cam timing is over-advanced or not performing as expected.

How much does it cost to fix P0010?

Neither source gives a repair cost for P0010. It depends on whether the fix is an oil change, a wiring repair, a new oil control valve or, rarely, the engine computer, so get it diagnosed first.
