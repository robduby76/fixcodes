P0610: Control Module Vehicle Options Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0610 means the engine computer (PCM) has flagged an internal problem with how it's set up for your vehicle. OBD-Codes.com describes it as the PCM failing to recognize the vehicle's equipment options, or finding options that don't match the vehicle identification number (VIN), while RepairPal describes the PCM's own self-check calculations coming out wrong. Both list a failed PCM and wiring problems as causes, and OBD-Codes.com adds programming errors and power or ground faults.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0610 means

The powertrain control module (PCM) is programmed with the equipment your particular vehicle has, such as its engine, transmission and other options. OBD-Codes.com explains that each time the ignition is turned on, the PCM runs self-tests and uses the controller area network (CAN) to compare signals between modules. If the PCM fails to recognize the vehicle's equipment options, or if certain option settings don't match the VIN, it stores P0610 and may turn on the check engine light, sometimes only after several failed cycles. RepairPal describes it more generally: the PCM regularly runs a self-diagnosis in which it performs normal mathematical calculations, and if the results aren't as expected it sets P0610. The code is generic to OBD-II vehicles (1996 and newer); OBD-Codes.com names Jeep, Mazda, Ford and Dodge among the makes that use it, and search interest also shows people looking it up for Chrysler models such as the Chrysler 200, so check technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked right away. RepairPal rates P0610 'Urgent', meaning immediate action is needed because it can cause severe damage or put people at risk, and lists 'engine may not start' and 'engine may stall' as symptoms. OBD-Codes.com says internal control module codes must always be taken seriously and that P0610 can cause drivability problems up to a no-start. If the engine stalls or the transmission shifts erratically, don't keep driving it; have it towed.

Common symptoms

  • Engine won't start (both sources)
  • Engine stalls (RepairPal)
  • Decreased engine performance or drivability problems (both sources)
  • Erratic automatic transmission shifting (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Lower fuel economy (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Check engine light, often with other stored codes (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. A failed PCM (both sources)
  2. A PCM programming error, such as option settings that don't match the vehicle (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Open or shorted wiring or connectors in the CAN harness, or other wiring problems (both sources)
  4. A failed power supply to the PCM (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. A poor control module ground (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data and write them down. OBD-Codes.com says any ECM/PCM power supply codes need to be repaired before you diagnose P0610.
  2. Think about recent work: if the PCM was just replaced, reflashed or swapped from another vehicle, its option setup may not match your vehicle, which points toward programming.
  3. Check the fuses and relays that feed the PCM. OBD-Codes.com says to test fuses with the circuit loaded.
  4. Inspect the PCM wiring and harnesses, and check the chassis and engine ground connections for corrosion or looseness.
  5. Look the PCM over for signs of water, heat or collision damage. OBD-Codes.com says a controller damaged by water should be considered defective.
  6. Clear the code and test drive. If it doesn't come back, it may be intermittent, which OBD-Codes.com says makes it harder to diagnose.
  7. Look up TSBs for your year, make, model and engine that mention P0610.

Typical fixes

  • Repair a blown fuse, failed relay or bad power supply to the PCM
  • Clean and repair poor module grounds
  • Repair damaged CAN or PCM wiring and connectors
  • Reprogram the PCM with the correct vehicle options if a programming error is found
  • Replace and program the PCM if it has failed

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com says that, unlike most codes, P0610 is likely caused by a defective controller or a controller programming error, and that even well-equipped technicians can find it a challenge to diagnose. Rule out power and ground problems before condemning the PCM.
  • A replacement PCM has to be programmed. OBD-Codes.com says some can be bought pre-programmed from aftermarket sources, while others need on-board programming that only a dealer or other qualified shop can do.
  • The sources describe the trigger differently: OBD-Codes.com ties P0610 to the PCM not recognizing the vehicle's options or finding options that don't match the VIN, while RepairPal describes a failed internal calculation check. RepairPal lists only PCM failure and wiring as causes; OBD-Codes.com also lists programming errors, power supply and ground faults.

FAQs

Can I drive with P0610?

It's not a good idea. RepairPal rates it 'Urgent' and says the engine may stall or not start, and OBD-Codes.com says internal control module codes must always be taken seriously. Get it diagnosed soon.

Does P0610 mean the PCM is bad?

Often the PCM or its programming is the problem, but not always. OBD-Codes.com says to check fuses, relays, power and grounds before declaring the controller defective.

Why did P0610 show up after a PCM swap?

OBD-Codes.com says P0610 is stored when the PCM doesn't recognize the vehicle's equipment options or the options don't match the VIN, so a used or replacement PCM that hasn't been programmed for your vehicle can set it. Have it programmed by a dealer or qualified shop.

What codes are related to P0610?

Other internal control module codes such as P0601, P0602, P0603, P0606 and P0611 can appear with it, and OBD-Codes.com says ECM/PCM power supply codes should be fixed first.
