P0501: Vehicle Speed Sensor "A" Range/Performance (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0501 means the engine computer is getting a vehicle speed reading that's outside the expected range or doesn't make sense. A wrong or dead speedometer, odd shifting and an ABS or traction control light often come with it.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0501 means

The vehicle speed sensor (VSS) tells the powertrain control module (PCM) how fast the car is moving. RepairPal explains the reading drives the speedometer and is usually shared with the transmission, ABS, traction control and power steering. OBD-Codes.com describes a typical VSS as an electromagnetic sensor in the transmission housing that reads a toothed reluctor ring on the output shaft. P0501 is the range/performance code: OBD-Codes.com says the speed reading is outside the expected range (too high or too low), and RepairPal says the PCM sees an inaccurate or irrational reading. Unlike P0500, which is a general malfunction, P0501 means a signal is there but doesn't add up. RepairPal adds that the sensor isn't always on the transmission: it may be at the rear differential, in or near a wheel hub, or part of the speedometer. The sources disagree on one point: OBD-Codes.com says P0501 may not turn on the check engine light, while RepairPal lists the check engine light as a symptom.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Usually drivable with care, but get it diagnosed soon. RepairPal rates P0501 Moderate. OBD-Codes.com warns the symptoms match P0500: you may lose anti-lock brakes, the speedometer may read wrong or not at all, and automatic shifting can become erratic. Drive gently, leave extra stopping distance and don't rely on the speedometer until it's fixed.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (RepairPal; OBD-Codes.com says P0501 may not always trigger it)
  • Speedometer or odometer reading wrong or not working (both sources)
  • Erratic or improper automatic transmission shifting (both sources)
  • ABS, brake or traction control warning light on, and possible loss of anti-lock braking (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal mentions ABS and traction control lights)
  • Reduced rev limiter (OBD-Codes.com)
  • In some cases no noticeable symptoms, or performance problems (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty vehicle speed sensor (both sources)
  2. Broken, frayed or damaged wiring or a bad connector to the sensor (both sources)
  3. PCM not configured for the tire size actually fitted (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Faulty speedometer or instrument cluster (RepairPal)
  5. Communication (CAN) bus problems (RepairPal)
  6. Worn speed sensor drive gear, or a transmission or differential fault (RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Search for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your make, model and year first. OBD-Codes.com notes some vehicles, including some Toyotas, have a known bulletin.
  2. Find where your sensor is mounted (transmission, rear axle or wheel hub, depending on the vehicle) and inspect the wiring and connector for chafing, bare, broken or melted wires.
  3. If you recently changed to a different tire or wheel size, mention it to the shop, since a mismatch with the PCM's setting is a listed cause of a reading that's out of range.
  4. If the wiring checks out, OBD-Codes.com suggests checking the voltage at the sensor using your vehicle's procedure before replacing it.

Typical fixes

  • Repair damaged VSS wiring or connectors
  • Replace a faulty vehicle speed sensor
  • Reprogram the PCM for the correct tire size where that's the cause
  • Repair a faulty speedometer/cluster, CAN bus fault, worn drive gear or driveline problem found by testing

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • RepairPal's shop procedure: note the freeze frame data, then test drive with a scan tool to recreate the conditions and watch the VSS reading for a smooth, steady signal. If it's missing, scope the sensor directly to separate a bad sensor from a wiring or communication problem.
  • RepairPal notes a defective speedometer or instrument cluster can load down the signal until it disappears or gets too weak, so a new sensor alone may not fix it.
  • RepairPal lists common misdiagnoses: a speedometer problem, an internal transmission problem, an ABS/traction control problem or a driveline problem.
  • Get it checked promptly if the ABS light is on, since anti-lock braking may be disabled until the speed signal is fixed.

FAQs

What's the difference between P0500 and P0501?

Both are Vehicle Speed Sensor "A" codes. P0500 is the general malfunction code, while P0501 is range/performance: per OBD-Codes.com the speed reading is outside the expected range, and per RepairPal it's inaccurate or irrational. OBD-Codes.com says the symptoms are basically the same.

Can bigger or smaller tires cause P0501?

OBD-Codes.com lists a PCM that isn't configured for the actual tire size on the vehicle as a cause, so it's worth checking if the code appeared after a tire or wheel change.

Will P0501 go away on its own?

RepairPal says most codes clear on their own once the system stops detecting the problem, but if P0501 stays it should be diagnosed to avoid a more serious problem.
