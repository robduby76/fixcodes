P0617: Starter Relay Circuit High (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0617 means the engine computer (PCM) has seen high voltage in the starter relay circuit, the circuit that sends battery power to the starter when you turn the key to start. Often the engine won't crank or start, or it fails to crank only now and then. Common causes are a bad starter relay, ignition switch, starter or starter solenoid, a blown fuse, or corroded or damaged wiring, connectors or battery cables; a failed PCM is less common.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0617 means

The starter relay lets battery power reach the starter when the ignition is in the start position. OBD-Codes.com explains that the circuit starts at the battery and runs through the ignition switch, fuses, starter solenoid, starter relay and safety devices such as the clutch position sensor or neutral safety switch, and that the PCM sets P0617 when it detects a high voltage situation in this circuit. Related codes are P0615 (general circuit malfunction) and P0616 (circuit low). RepairPal titles the code 'Starter Relay Circuit High' but describes it more generally: the PCM sees that the starter circuit isn't operating properly, and the engine won't crank. The code is generic to OBD-II vehicles (1996 and newer); OBD-Codes.com names Cadillac, Nissan, Chevrolet, Buick, Honda, Acura, Infiniti, Peugeot, Toyota, GMC, Mazda, Ford and Subaru among the makes that use it, and search interest shows people looking it up for Chevrolet, GMC, Toyota (including the Camry) and Honda, so check technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0617 'Urgent', meaning immediate action is needed. OBD-Codes.com says that in most cases the vehicle won't start, though severity can range from just a check engine light on a car that still starts and runs to a car that won't start at all. Don't rely on the car for important trips until it's fixed.

Common symptoms

  • Engine won't crank or start (both sources)
  • Sporadic no-crank, where it fails to crank only some of the time (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Check engine light on, sometimes with the car still starting and running normally (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. A failed starter relay (both sources)
  2. A failed starter, or a faulty starter solenoid (both sources list the starter; OBD-Codes.com adds the solenoid)
  3. Damaged or faulty wiring (both sources)
  4. A faulty ignition switch (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. A blown fuse or fusible link (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Corroded or damaged connectors or battery cables (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. A failed PCM (both sources)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and write them down, including any P0615 or P0616 that point to a general or low circuit fault.
  2. Make sure the battery is charged (about 12 volts) and that the battery terminals and cables are clean, tight and undamaged. OBD-Codes.com lists corroded or damaged battery cables among the causes.
  3. Check the fuse (and fusible link, if fitted) for the starter relay circuit using your vehicle's fuse chart.
  4. Inspect the wiring and connectors between the battery, ignition switch, PCM, starter relay, starter solenoid and starter for rubbing, bare wires, burn spots, corrosion or bent pins.
  5. Look up TSBs for your year, make, model and engine that mention P0617. OBD-Codes.com says this can save a lot of time.

Typical fixes

  • Replace a blown fuse or fusible link
  • Clean corroded connectors and repair or replace battery cables or terminals
  • Repair or replace damaged wiring
  • Replace the starter relay
  • Replace the ignition switch
  • Replace the starter solenoid or starter
  • Flash or replace the PCM (only after the rest of the circuit checks out)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com's voltage check: the battery should read about 12 volts and the starter should have battery voltage with the key in the start position. Voltage present with the starter not engaging points to a defective starter or solenoid; no voltage points to the ignition switch or wiring. Continuity tests should be done with power removed, and wiring should normally read 0 ohms unless the service data says otherwise; resistance or no continuity means an open or shorted wire.
  • OBD-Codes.com says a common mistake is replacing the starter or PCM when damaged wiring or another component is the real problem.
  • The sources differ on severity and detail: RepairPal rates P0617 Urgent and says the engine will not crank, while OBD-Codes.com says most vehicles with this code won't start but severity ranges from just a check engine light on a car that still starts and runs to a car that won't start at all. OBD-Codes.com specifically ties P0617 to high voltage in the circuit, while RepairPal's description only says the circuit isn't operating properly (the same wording it uses for P0615 and P0616). OBD-Codes.com also lists more causes (ignition switch, solenoid, fuses, connectors and battery cables) than RepairPal's four (PCM, starter, starter relay, wiring).

FAQs

Can I drive with P0617?

If the car starts, you can usually drive it, but it may not start next time. RepairPal rates the code 'Urgent', and OBD-Codes.com says that in most cases the vehicle won't start, so get it checked before you get stranded.

Does P0617 mean I need a new starter?

Not necessarily. Both sources list the starter relay and wiring as causes alongside the starter, and OBD-Codes.com warns that replacing the starter or PCM when wiring or another part is at fault is a common mistake.

What's the difference between P0615, P0616 and P0617?

OBD-Codes.com says all three relate to the starter relay circuit: P0615 is a general circuit malfunction, P0616 is set when the PCM sees low voltage in the circuit, and P0617 is set when it sees high voltage.

Can a battery problem cause P0617?

OBD-Codes.com lists corroded or damaged battery cables among the causes and says the battery should read about 12 volts during testing, so check the battery and its cables first. Neither source lists the battery itself as a cause.
