P0432: Main Catalyst Efficiency Below Threshold, Bank 2 (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0432 means the engine computer thinks the main catalytic converter on bank 2 is no longer cleaning the exhaust as well as it should. A worn-out converter is the usual cause, but an engine running too rich or too lean, a bad oxygen sensor or an exhaust leak can also set it.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0432 means

The catalytic converter turns harmful exhaust gases (hydrocarbons, carbon monoxide and nitrogen oxides) into water vapor, carbon dioxide and nitrogen. OBD-Codes.com explains that the engine computer watches converter efficiency with the oxygen sensor after the converter, which should switch much more slowly than the front sensor; if the rear sensor switches too quickly, there is no difference between what goes into and comes out of the converter, so the computer decides it isn't working. RepairPal describes the same check as the rear sensor reading starting to match the front sensor. Bank 2 is the side of a V6 or V8 engine that does not have cylinder 1, so P0432 only appears on engines with two cylinder banks. P0430 is the general bank 2 catalyst efficiency code, P0431 is the bank 2 warm-up (close-mounted) converter, and P0422 is the main converter on bank 1.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Usually drivable short-term, but get it checked soon. Sources differ on how serious it is: RepairPal rates P0432 Urgent and says the engine may hesitate, lose power or even stall, while OBD-Codes.com lists only higher tailpipe emissions and the check engine light. RepairPal also warns that converters usually fail because of something else, such as a misfire or an internal oil or coolant leak, so find that cause before it ruins a new converter. If the check engine light is flashing, the engine is misfiring and you should stop driving soon. We treat P0432 as fix soon, not as an emergency.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (both sources)
  • Higher tailpipe emissions, which can fail an emissions test (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Hesitation or loss of power (RepairPal)
  • Stalling (RepairPal)
  • A hissing sound while accelerating (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Worn-out, damaged or non-functional catalytic converter (both sources)
  2. Engine not running at the right air-fuel ratio (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. A bad oxygen sensor (RepairPal)
  4. An exhaust leak (RepairPal)
  5. Unrepaired misfires or internal oil or coolant leaks that damaged the converter (RepairPal, as the usual reason converters fail)
  6. A low-quality aftermarket replacement converter the car's computer won't accept (OBD-Codes.com, if the code returns after a converter replacement)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes. If misfire codes (P0300 to P0308) or fuel trim codes such as P0174 are also present, fix those first, since OBD-Codes.com says an engine running outside the right air-fuel ratio can set this code and RepairPal says misfires commonly destroy converters.
  2. Listen and look for exhaust leaks around the bank 2 manifold, oxygen sensors and converter. RepairPal lists an exhaust leak as a possible cause.
  3. Check the bank 2 oxygen sensors with a scan tool on a warm engine. The rear sensor should switch much more slowly than the front one; if it switches almost as fast, the converter is likely not storing oxygen. A lazy or dead sensor can also be the real problem.
  4. OBD-Codes.com notes that a tune-up or engine repair may clear the code if the engine was running too rich or too lean.
  5. Check your emissions warranty before paying for a converter. RepairPal cites a federally mandated 8 year, 80,000 mile warranty on catalytic converters, though the problem that caused the failure is usually not covered.

Typical fixes

  • Repair any misfire, fuel mixture problem, oil burning or coolant leak first (both sources)
  • Fix any exhaust leak (RepairPal)
  • Replace a faulty oxygen sensor if testing confirms it (RepairPal)
  • Replace the bank 2 main catalytic converter once other causes are ruled out (both sources). OBD-Codes.com warns that some makes, especially Toyota and Subaru, are fussy about converter quality, and Subarus need a CARB-certified converter to keep the light off

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The check engine light is flashing, or the engine hesitates, loses power or stalls
  • The code came back after a converter was replaced, which OBD-Codes.com says can mean the replacement converter isn't good enough for that car
  • The car may still be under its emissions warranty, so a dealer diagnosis could save you the cost of a converter
  • You need to pass an emissions or state inspection test

FAQs

Can I drive with P0432?

Usually for a short while, but sources disagree on how serious it is. OBD-Codes.com lists only higher emissions and the check engine light, while RepairPal rates it Urgent and says the engine may hesitate, lose power or stall. Get it diagnosed soon, and stop driving if the check engine light flashes.

What's the difference between P0430, P0431 and P0432?

All three mean catalyst efficiency on bank 2 is below the allowed threshold. P0432 names the main converter, P0431 names the smaller warm-up converter close to the engine, and P0430 is the general bank 2 catalyst code.

Why did P0432 come back after I replaced the catalytic converter?

OBD-Codes.com says some cars, especially Toyotas and Subarus, won't accept cheaper aftermarket converters, and Subarus need a CARB-certified converter. It's also possible the original cause, such as a misfire, a fuel mixture problem or a bad oxygen sensor, was never fixed.

Is the catalytic converter covered by warranty?

It may be. RepairPal cites a federally mandated 8 year, 80,000 mile warranty on catalytic converters, and notes the problem that caused the failure is usually not covered. Check your own warranty terms.

How much does it cost to fix P0432?

Neither source gives a repair cost for P0432. It can range from a tune-up, exhaust leak repair or oxygen sensor to a catalytic converter, so get it diagnosed first.
