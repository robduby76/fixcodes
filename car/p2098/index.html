P2098: Post Catalyst Fuel Trim System Too Lean (Bank 2) (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P2098 means the engine computer sees a lean condition (too much air, not enough fuel) from the oxygen sensor after the catalytic converter on Bank 2. Possible causes listed by OBD-Codes.com and RepairPal include exhaust or vacuum leaks, a faulty oxygen sensor, low fuel pressure, a faulty mass air flow (MAF) sensor, misfires, a plugged converter and wiring problems.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P2098 means

Each exhaust path with a catalytic converter has an oxygen sensor before the converter and one after it. The powertrain control module (PCM) uses these sensors to control the fuel mixture and to check how well the converter is working. OBD-Codes.com explains that P2098 is set when the PCM recognizes a lean condition through the oxygen sensor signals on Bank 2, and RepairPal says it is set when the PCM sees an excessive amount of oxygen left in the exhaust after the catalytic converter. Bank 2 is the side of the engine that does not contain cylinder 1, so this code applies to V6, V8 and other engines with two banks. OBD-Codes.com describes it as a generic OBD-II code for 1996 and newer vehicles, with troubleshooting steps that vary by make and model. P2096 is the matching too-lean code on Bank 1, and P2099 is the too-rich code on Bank 2.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it diagnosed soon. RepairPal rates P2098 Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety right away but should be checked within a reasonable time. OBD-Codes.com gives no rating but says to trace and fix the cause as soon as possible to avoid damaging other parts. If the engine misfires, knocks (pings), loses power, or the check engine light flashes, keep driving to a minimum, since a lean or misfiring engine can overheat and damage the catalytic converter.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine (malfunction indicator) light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Poor fuel economy or increased fuel consumption (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Rough idle (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Poor acceleration (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Misfire (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Catalytic converter glowing red hot (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Possible spark knock (detonation or pre-ignition) (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Other related trouble codes stored along with P2098 (OBD-Codes.com)
  • OBD-Codes.com notes that, depending on the cause, the check engine light may be the only thing you notice

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Exhaust leaks: rust holes, cracked exhaust manifolds, or damaged or missing gaskets (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  2. Faulty oxygen sensor (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  3. Wiring issues (RepairPal)
  4. Low fuel pressure from a clogged fuel filter, failing fuel pump, failed fuel pressure regulator, or clogged or leaking injectors (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Large vacuum leak letting unmetered air into the intake manifold (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Misfires from bad spark plugs or plug wires (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. Faulty mass air flow (MAF) sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  8. Plugged catalytic converter (OBD-Codes.com)
  9. Contaminated fuel (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and deal with any other codes first, such as misfire, MAF or oxygen sensor heater codes (OBD-Codes.com).
  2. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, since some models have a known problem part (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. Listen for vacuum leaks between the MAF sensor and the intake manifold; leaks often sound like a whistle (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Inspect the exhaust system for rust holes, cracks, loose parts and missing gaskets, and make sure the oxygen sensors are tight (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. Inspect the oxygen sensor wiring harness and connectors; OBD-Codes.com says Jeeps and some Chrysler products are known for poor connectors.
  6. If you suspect bad fuel, OBD-Codes.com suggests filling up with a higher grade.

Typical fixes

  • Repair exhaust leaks, such as cracked manifolds or missing gaskets
  • Repair vacuum leaks
  • Repair damaged oxygen sensor wiring or connectors
  • Fix the cause of any misfire, such as worn spark plugs or burnt plug wires
  • Replace a faulty MAF sensor if its connector checks out and a MAF code is also stored (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Restore fuel pressure by replacing a clogged filter, failing pump, failed regulator or faulty injectors
  • Replace the downstream oxygen sensor on Bank 2 (the side without cylinder 1), as OBD-Codes.com suggests once other causes are ruled out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com says an advanced scan tool that graphs oxygen sensor data in real time saves a lot of time
  • Fuel pressure testing is needed
  • You suspect a plugged catalytic converter, for example the engine won't rev under load
  • The code returns after you fix leaks and check the wiring

FAQs

Is P2098 serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate. OBD-Codes.com says to fix the cause as soon as possible to avoid damage to other parts. Limit driving if the engine misfires, knocks or the light flashes.

What's the difference between P2098 and P0174?

P0174 is a lean condition seen through the main fuel trim on Bank 2. P2098 is a lean condition seen after the catalytic converter on Bank 2. They can share causes such as vacuum leaks and low fuel pressure.

Should I just replace the oxygen sensor?

Not first. OBD-Codes.com points out that an air leak or another fault can make a good sensor give a bad reading, so check for exhaust and vacuum leaks, wiring problems and other codes before replacing the sensor.

How much does it cost to fix P2098?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from a gasket or wiring repair to an oxygen sensor, MAF sensor, fuel pump or catalytic converter, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
