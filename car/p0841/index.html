P0841: Transmission Fluid Pressure Sensor/Switch A Circuit Range/Performance (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0841 means the engine or transmission computer sees a signal from transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch "A" that doesn't match what it expects. OBD-Codes.com says the most likely cause is a failed pressure sensor/switch, with wiring and connector problems and internal transmission faults also possible; RepairPal also lists low transmission fluid and a failed transmission control module (TCM).

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0841 means

The transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch (TFPS) turns hydraulic pressure inside the automatic transmission into an electrical signal for the powertrain control module (PCM) or transmission control module (TCM). OBD-Codes.com says it is usually mounted on the side of the valve body inside the transmission, though on some vehicles it screws into the transmission case. The computer uses the signal to judge transmission operating pressure and when a shift is happening; RepairPal describes the switch as part of how the computer uses hydraulic fluid to engage the clutches for smoother shifts. OBD-Codes.com says P0841 sets when the "A" input doesn't match the normal operating voltages stored in the computer, and that it can also come from mechanical problems inside the transmission such as internal leaks, cracked or missing check balls in the valve body, low line pressure or a stuck valve. Which sensor is "A" depends on the vehicle, so check a repair manual for your model. OBD-Codes.com calls it a generic code that applies to OBD-II vehicles including Jeep, Dodge, Mazda, Nissan, Honda and GM. Note on sources: RepairPal titles the code "Transmission Fluid Pressure Sensor/Switch A Circuit" without the words range/performance, and says it sets when the computer detects a problem with the hydraulic clutch or the pressure switch. The two sources also differ on severity (RepairPal says Urgent; OBD-Codes.com says it depends and the computer can partly compensate). Related codes P0840 and P0842 through P0844 cover the same sensor's other circuit faults.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked promptly. RepairPal rates P0841 Urgent, meaning it can lead to serious damage and should be looked at right away. OBD-Codes.com is less absolute: it says severity depends on which circuit failed and that the engine or transmission computer can compensate to some degree, but the transmission may shift differently or go into limp mode (starting off in 2nd or 3rd gear). If the transmission slips, won't engage a gear, or shifts harshly, avoid driving it until it is diagnosed.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Change in shift quality, or the transmission not shifting properly (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  • Vehicle starts off in 2nd or 3rd gear (limp mode) (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Increased fuel consumption (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch; OBD-Codes.com rates this the likely cause (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  2. Open circuit, short to voltage or short to ground in the sensor signal wiring, or other wiring issues (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)
  3. Corroded or damaged connector terminals, especially outside the transmission case (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Low transmission fluid level (RepairPal)
  5. Internal mechanical transmission problems such as internal leaks, damaged check balls, low line pressure or a stuck valve (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Failed TCM or PCM; OBD-Codes.com rates the PCM unlikely and notes a replacement must be programmed (OBD-Codes.com, RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check the transmission fluid level and condition using the method in your owner's manual, since RepairPal lists low fluid as a cause.
  2. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle; the problem may be a known issue with a known fix (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. If power-related codes or more than one pressure sensor/switch code are stored, diagnose those first, since they may be causing P0841 (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Find the pressure sensor/switch connector and inspect the wiring for rubbing, bare wires, burn spots or melted plastic, and the terminals for burning or green corrosion. Clean corroded terminals with electrical contact cleaner, let them dry and apply electrical grease (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. Clear the code and drive to see if it returns; OBD-Codes.com says if it doesn't, the connection was most likely the problem.

Typical fixes

  • Top up or correct the transmission fluid level if it is low
  • Clean or repair corroded connectors and damaged wiring in the sensor circuit
  • Replace the transmission fluid pressure sensor/switch if testing shows it is out of spec
  • Repair internal transmission problems (valve body, check balls, line pressure) if the sensor and wiring check out
  • Replace and program the TCM or PCM only after the sensor, wiring and transmission have been ruled out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • With the sensor unplugged and key on, engine off, the harness signal terminal should show the manufacturer's reference voltage (OBD-Codes.com says either 12 or 5 volts depending on the vehicle); a wrong reading points to wiring or the computer (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Checking the sensor's resistance against factory specs with an ohmmeter while wiggling the connector needs the service information for your vehicle (OBD-Codes.com)
  • The sensor is often on the valve body inside the transmission, so reaching it may mean removing the transmission pan (OBD-Codes.com describes the location)
  • The code keeps coming back after a new sensor, which OBD-Codes.com says can mean an internal transmission fault or, less likely, a failed PCM/TCM that must be programmed to the vehicle

FAQs

Is P0841 serious?

RepairPal rates it Urgent. OBD-Codes.com says severity depends on the circuit and the computer can partly compensate, but shifting may change or the transmission may go into limp mode. Have it checked promptly, and don't keep driving if the transmission slips or won't shift.

Can low transmission fluid cause P0841?

RepairPal lists low transmission fluid level as one of the common causes, so checking the fluid is a sensible first step.

Is P0841 always a bad pressure sensor?

No. OBD-Codes.com rates a failed sensor the likely cause, but wiring, corroded connectors and internal transmission problems can also set it, and RepairPal lists low fluid and a failed TCM.

How much does it cost to fix P0841?

Neither source gives a repair cost. The fix can range from adding fluid or cleaning a connector to replacing the sensor or repairing the transmission, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
