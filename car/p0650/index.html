P0650: Malfunction Indicator Lamp (MIL) Control Circuit (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0650 means the engine computer (PCM) has found a fault in the circuit that turns the check engine light on and off. Common causes are a burned-out bulb or LED, wiring or connector problems, or a bad ground; a faulty instrument cluster or PCM is less common.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0650 means

The Malfunction Indicator Lamp (MIL) is the check engine or service engine soon light. Both sources say it should light up for a few seconds when you turn the key or start the engine, then go out. OBD-Codes.com says the PCM controls the lamp through its ground circuit and sets P0650 when it sees too high, too low or no voltage on that circuit. RepairPal says P0650 sets when the MIL control circuit fails the self-test that runs each time the key is turned to start, and that the light itself will illuminate. The sources differ on what you'll see: OBD-Codes.com says the light may never come on when it should, or may stay on all the time, while RepairPal lists the check engine light coming on. They also differ on causes: OBD-Codes.com puts a burned-out bulb or LED first, while RepairPal lists the MIL driver inside the PCM first and warns that the PCM is often replaced when the real problem is wiring or the instrument cluster circuit board. RepairPal rates it 'Urgent'; OBD-Codes.com gives no rating.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0650 'Urgent'; OBD-Codes.com gives no severity rating. RepairPal says that in many cases the driver notices nothing wrong, but a check engine light that can't work properly may not warn you about other engine or emissions problems, and RepairPal lists failing an emissions inspection as a symptom.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light never comes on, even for a few seconds at key-on (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  • Check engine light on all the time (both sources)
  • Failing an emissions inspection because of the light fault (listed by RepairPal)
  • Often nothing else noticeable while driving (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Burned-out check engine light bulb or LED (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  2. Open or shorted wiring in the MIL circuit, or a poor connection at the bulb, cluster or PCM (both sources)
  3. Bad MIL ground circuit (listed by RepairPal)
  4. Faulty instrument cluster circuit board (RepairPal, as a common misdiagnosis)
  5. Faulty MIL driver in the PCM, or a failed PCM (both sources)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Turn the ignition on and watch the check engine light. OBD-Codes.com says if it lights for a few seconds and goes out, or comes on and stays on, the bulb or LED is working.
  2. If it never lights, check whether the bulb is burned out or not seated properly, and whether other warning lights and gauges in the cluster work. You may need to remove the cluster.
  3. Check the PCM or MIL fuse if your vehicle has one.
  4. Inspect the wiring and connectors between the cluster and the PCM for chafed insulation, bent pins, corrosion or broken terminals.
  5. With an advanced scan tool, command the light on and off. RepairPal suggests comparing the 'MIL command' and 'MIL actual' data values, and also scanning the instrument cluster and body control module for codes.
  6. With a multimeter and a vehicle-specific wiring diagram, check the circuit at the lamp and PCM ends for opens or shorts to ground before suspecting the PCM.

Typical fixes

  • Replace a burned-out bulb or reseat a loose one
  • Clean or repair corroded or damaged connectors
  • Repair open or shorted wiring, or a bad ground
  • Replace a blown PCM or MIL fuse
  • Repair or replace a faulty instrument cluster
  • Replace and program the PCM only as a last resort, after everything else checks out (both sources warn against replacing it too soon)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • You need to pass an emissions inspection and the light doesn't work
  • Other gauges or warning lights in the cluster also don't work
  • The wiring and bulb check out and the PCM is suspected; OBD-Codes.com says a new PCM needs special equipment to program
  • You don't have a wiring diagram for your vehicle

FAQs

Is it safe to drive with P0650?

RepairPal rates it 'Urgent', though it says drivers often notice nothing wrong. The real risk is that a check engine light that doesn't work can't warn you about a new engine problem, so get it fixed soon.

Why would my check engine light not come on at all?

OBD-Codes.com says the light should glow for a few seconds at key-on. If it never does, the bulb or LED may be burned out, or there's a wiring, connector, fuse or PCM problem in the light's circuit.

Can P0650 make me fail an emissions test?

RepairPal lists failing an emissions inspection as a symptom of P0650.

How much does it cost to fix P0650?

Neither source gives a repair cost. A bulb or fuse is cheap; a cluster or PCM costs much more, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
