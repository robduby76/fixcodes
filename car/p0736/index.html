P0736: Reverse Incorrect Ratio (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0736 means the computer saw that reverse gear isn't producing the ratio it expects, or that the transmission isn't going into reverse. Common causes include low or dirty transmission fluid, internal mechanical wear, a blocked passage or failed valve body (main control), a faulty shift solenoid, wiring problems, or a faulty transmission or powertrain control module.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0736 means

An automatic transmission reverses its output direction to back the vehicle up. OBD-Codes.com says the ECM, PCM or transmission control module (TCM) often compares engine speed with the transmission speed sensor to work out the gear ratio and torque converter slip, and sets P0736 and turns on the check engine light when that calculation doesn't match the desired value while shifting into or driving in reverse. RepairPal describes it more simply: the TCM uses solenoids to control the fluid pressure that applies each gear, and sets P0736 when it detects that the transmission is not going into reverse. The sources disagree on how reverse is controlled: OBD-Codes.com says reverse is typically not controlled by a solenoid and is most often applied hydraulically, through the manual shift linkage, even on computer-controlled transmissions, so a no-reverse problem most likely points to a mechanical or pressure fault, while RepairPal's general description has the TCM's solenoids applying every gear. It is a generic OBD-II code for 1996 and newer vehicles, though repair steps vary by make and model. OBD-Codes.com lists similar incorrect ratio codes P0730 through P0735 and says any other transmission codes should be fixed first.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Have it checked soon and be careful when backing up until you do. RepairPal rates P0736 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe. OBD-Codes.com gives no severity rating but lists a delayed shift or no shift into reverse and a slipping transmission. If reverse engages late, slips or doesn't engage at all, don't rely on it in tight spots or on slopes, and have the transmission diagnosed promptly.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Delayed engagement, or not shifting into reverse (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal says the transmission may not shift properly)
  • Transmission slipping (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Worse fuel economy (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Low or dirty transmission fluid (OBD-Codes.com)
  2. Mechanical failure inside the transmission (both sources)
  3. Blockage inside the transmission main control, or a failed valve body (OBD-Codes.com describes a blockage; RepairPal lists valve body failure)
  4. Faulty shift solenoid (OBD-Codes.com), although the same source says reverse itself is usually hydraulic rather than solenoid-controlled
  5. Faulty transmission control module (both sources), or a faulty PCM (RepairPal)
  6. Wiring issue (RepairPal)
  7. The sources differ on emphasis: OBD-Codes.com puts fluid, pressure and mechanical problems first, while RepairPal's cause list adds the PCM and wiring and doesn't mention fluid level, though it names a fluid and filter change as the common repair

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check the transmission fluid level and condition first, following your owner's manual procedure. OBD-Codes.com says low or dirty fluid can cause shifting problems in several gears.
  2. Read all stored codes. If there are other transmission codes, such as a shift solenoid circuit code, OBD-Codes.com says to fix those first.
  3. If P0736 is the only incorrect ratio code, OBD-Codes.com suggests testing, in a safe open area, whether reverse actually engages and holds.
  4. Look over the transmission wiring and connectors for damage or corrosion, and check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle.

Typical fixes

  • Correct the fluid level, or change dirty fluid and filter if the manufacturer recommends it (RepairPal lists a fluid and filter change as the common repair)
  • Repair wiring or connector faults, or replace a faulty shift solenoid if testing points to it
  • Repair or replace the valve body (main control) if a passage is blocked or pressure is wrong
  • Repair or rebuild the transmission if testing confirms internal mechanical failure
  • Repair, reprogram or replace the TCM or PCM only after other causes are ruled out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Reverse engages late, slips or won't engage at all
  • OBD-Codes.com says that because reverse is usually fully hydraulic, a transmission that won't go into reverse most likely has a mechanical or pressure problem, which needs pressure testing and transmission knowledge
  • Diagnosis may need an advanced scan tool to see which gear is commanded and whether engine speed matches the calculated transmission output speed (OBD-Codes.com)
  • OBD-Codes.com says advanced knowledge of transmission operation and overhaul is often needed, along with the factory service manual

FAQs

Is P0736 serious?

RepairPal rates it Urgent. OBD-Codes.com gives no rating but lists slipping and a delayed or missing shift into reverse. A reverse gear that slips or doesn't engage is a safety problem when parking or on a slope, so get it diagnosed soon.

Can a shift solenoid cause P0736?

OBD-Codes.com lists a faulty shift solenoid as a possible cause, but it also says reverse is typically not solenoid-controlled and is fully hydraulic, so a no-reverse problem is more often mechanical or pressure related. It adds that an electrical solenoid fault should set its own solenoid code.

Does P0736 mean my transmission is shot?

Not necessarily. Both sources list internal failure as one cause, but OBD-Codes.com says to check fluid level and condition first, and the cause lists also include the valve body, wiring and the control module.

How much does it cost to fix P0736?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It ranges widely, from a fluid service or wiring repair to a valve body repair or transmission rebuild, so get a diagnosis and a written estimate first.
