P0603: Internal Control Module Keep Alive Memory (KAM) Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0603 means the engine computer (PCM) has found a problem with its keep alive memory, the learned settings it saves between drives. Common causes are corroded or loose battery connections, a fault in the PCM's constant power wire, charging or ignition system interference, water damage, or an internal PCM or software fault.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0603 means

OBD-Codes.com explains that the keep alive memory (KAM) is the part of the powertrain control module's (PCM) memory that stores learned values, such as fuel trims and shift adaptations, that change with driving and sensor inputs. It is kept alive by a constant battery feed, the keep alive power (KAPWR) circuit. Disconnecting the battery erases it and the PCM falls back on its built-in base settings until it relearns, which is not the same as limp mode. P0603 sets when the PCM detects an error in this memory. OBD-Codes.com calls it a generic code that applies to all 1996 and newer vehicles, though repair steps vary by model. The sources describe it differently: RepairPal's short explanation says P0603 sets when the PCM loses communication with one or more control modules, while OBD-Codes.com ties it to the PCM's own keep alive memory and its power supply. Related internal module codes are P0601 (memory checksum), P0602 (programming error), P0604 (RAM error) and P0605 (ROM error).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0603 Urgent, meaning immediate action is needed. OBD-Codes.com says the check engine light may be the only sign, but lists engine stalling and no-start conditions as possible symptoms. If the engine stalls or won't idle, don't rely on the vehicle, since stalling in traffic is a real safety risk.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine stalling (OBD-Codes.com)
  • No-start condition (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Other warning lights on the dash (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Module communication codes stored along with P0603 (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Corroded battery terminals or loose battery connections (OBD-Codes.com)
  2. An open wire or poor routing in the keep alive power (KAPWR) circuit to the PCM (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. A charging system fault, such as alternator interference (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. An ignition system fault leaking high voltage into nearby wiring, such as cracked plug wires or coils (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. A damaged PCM, including water intrusion or an internal fault (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. An internal PCM software fault, or an aftermarket chip or tuner (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. Lost communication between the PCM and other control modules (RepairPal)
  8. The sources differ: RepairPal gives only module communication loss as the trigger, while OBD-Codes.com lists power, ground, interference and PCM causes and says it can be hard to isolate

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Clean and tighten the battery terminals and cables, and check the main grounds, fuse box and PCM connectors (OBD-Codes.com).
  2. Test the battery and charging system. A weak battery or bad alternator output can upset the PCM's power supply.
  3. Inspect wiring that runs near ignition parts, and check spark plug wires and coils for cracks where voltage could leak (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. If you have a multimeter, check the PCM power supply circuits while gently wiggling the harness around the engine and fuse boxes to find an intermittent drop (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. Look for signs of water getting into the PCM or its connectors (OBD-Codes.com).
  6. If an aftermarket tuner or chip is installed, return the PCM to stock programming and see if the code comes back (OBD-Codes.com).
  7. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) and PCM software updates for your vehicle (OBD-Codes.com).

Typical fixes

  • Clean or replace corroded battery terminals and repair loose connections or grounds (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair an open or chafed KAPWR wire to the PCM (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair charging or ignition system faults causing electrical interference (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Reflash the PCM with updated manufacturer software (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace the PCM only after power, ground and wiring check out; a new PCM usually needs programming to the vehicle (OBD-Codes.com says it has seen both wiring and PCM faults cause this code)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The engine stalls or won't start
  • Power and ground checks pass and the code returns, which can point to the PCM
  • The PCM needs a software reflash or replacement and programming, which needs dealer-level tools
  • Several module communication codes are stored at once

FAQs

Is P0603 serious?

It can be. RepairPal rates it Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com lists stalling and no-start as possible symptoms. Some cars run normally with it, but have it diagnosed soon.

Can a dead or disconnected battery cause P0603?

It can be related. OBD-Codes.com says disconnecting the battery erases the keep alive memory, and it lists corroded or loose battery connections as a cause. Check the battery and its cables before suspecting the computer.

Do I need a new PCM for P0603?

Not necessarily. OBD-Codes.com says it has been caused by both wiring and PCM faults, and some cases are fixed with a software reflash. Check power, grounds and wiring first.

How much does it cost to fix P0603?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from cleaning a battery terminal to a reflash or PCM replacement, so get a diagnosis or a written estimate first.
