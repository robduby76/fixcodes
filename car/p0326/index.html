P0326: Knock Sensor 1 Circuit Range/Performance, Bank 1 (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0326 means the engine computer thinks the signal from knock sensor 1 (bank 1, or the only knock sensor) is out of its expected range. The sources describe the trigger differently: RepairPal says it sets when the computer gets no signal from the sensor, while OBD-Codes.com says it can set when the knock signal is abnormally high or the knock can't be stopped by retarding timing. Common causes are a faulty sensor, wiring or connector problems, and less often a failed engine computer.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0326 means

A knock sensor is a small sensor bolted to the engine block that listens for knock or ping, which is fuel lighting off too early in the cylinders. When it hears knock, the engine computer (PCM) retards (delays) the spark timing to stop it. OBD-Codes.com explains that the sensor is usually a two-wire part with a 5 volt reference from the computer and a signal wire back, and that most computers learn the engine's normal knock pattern over time. According to OBD-Codes.com, P0326 may set when the computer decides the knock or noise level is out of the ordinary or abnormally high, or when the knock is severe and retarding the timing doesn't get rid of it. It also points out that a knock sensor can't tell real pre-detonation apart from noise caused by a mechanical engine problem. RepairPal describes it differently, saying the code sets when the computer does not receive a signal from the knock sensor. It is a generic OBD-II code, so it applies to most 1996 and newer vehicles, though repair steps vary by make and model. Related knock sensor 1 codes are P0325, P0327, P0328 and P0329.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it checked soon, but it's usually not an emergency. RepairPal rates P0326 Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety right away but should be diagnosed within a reasonable time. OBD-Codes.com notes it can go with real engine knock or pinging, which can damage the engine if it keeps happening. If you hear knocking or pinging, ease off hard acceleration and heavy loads, make sure you're using the fuel octane your owner's manual calls for, and get it looked at promptly. If the temperature gauge climbs toward hot, pull over and shut the engine off.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (both sources; RepairPal says it may or may not light)
  • Audible knocking from the engine compartment (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Pinging from the engine under acceleration (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty knock sensor (both sources)
  2. Wiring problem: knock sensor circuit open, shorted to ground or shorted to voltage (both sources)
  3. Failed engine computer (PCM) (both sources)
  4. Damaged knock sensor connector, or moisture or corrosion in it (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Fuel with a lower octane than the engine calls for (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. A real mechanical engine knock that the sensor is correctly hearing (OBD-Codes.com says to fix that first)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Listen for knocking. OBD-Codes.com says that if you can hear engine knock, fix the mechanical source first and then recheck the code.
  2. Make sure you're using the fuel octane listed in your owner's manual. OBD-Codes.com says lower octane than specified can cause pinging and may set P0326.
  3. Unplug the knock sensor and look for water or corrosion in the connector, and on sensors with a seal make sure engine coolant isn't fouling it, as OBD-Codes.com suggests.
  4. If you have a multimeter and service specs, OBD-Codes.com's test is to check for 5 volts at the sensor connector with the key on and engine off, then check the sensor's resistance to engine ground against the factory spec.
  5. With a scan tool showing live data, OBD-Codes.com suggests watching the knock signal at idle (it shouldn't show knock) and tapping lightly on the engine block to see whether the signal responds. Also make sure the sensor wiring isn't routed next to ignition wires.
  6. Write down the freeze frame data so a shop knows the conditions when the code set.

Typical fixes

  • Fix any real mechanical knock or use the correct octane fuel, then clear and retest
  • Clean or repair a corroded, wet or damaged knock sensor connector
  • Repair open or shorted wiring in the knock sensor circuit
  • Replace the knock sensor if it fails the resistance or tap test (RepairPal lists knock sensor replacement as the common repair)
  • If the 5 volt reference is missing, check the other sensors that share that reference for a short before suspecting the engine computer, as OBD-Codes.com describes

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • You can hear knocking or pinging from the engine
  • The knock sensor is hard to reach; on some engines it sits under the intake manifold
  • You don't have a scan tool that shows live knock sensor data, or the factory resistance spec
  • The 5 volt reference is missing, which means tracing a shared circuit or testing the engine computer

FAQs

Is P0326 serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate. It usually isn't an emergency, but if there is real knocking or the sensor isn't doing its job, the engine can be damaged over time. Get it diagnosed soon.

Can bad gas cause P0326?

It can. OBD-Codes.com says using lower octane fuel than the engine is specified for can cause pinging and may set P0326, so check that before replacing parts.

Why do the sources describe P0326 differently?

RepairPal says it sets when the computer gets no signal from the knock sensor, while OBD-Codes.com says it can set when the knock signal is abnormally high or the knock can't be stopped by retarding timing. Exact setting conditions vary by manufacturer, so a factory service manual for your vehicle is the final word.

How much does it cost to fix P0326?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It depends on whether the fix is fuel, a connector, wiring, the sensor itself or a mechanical engine problem, and on how hard the sensor is to reach, so get a diagnosis and written estimate first.
