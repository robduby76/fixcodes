P0511: Idle Air Control Circuit (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0511 means the computer has found a problem in the idle air control circuit and can't hold the idle speed where it wants it. Common causes are a failed idle air control valve, damaged wiring, a vacuum leak, or carbon buildup in the throttle body or valve.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0511 means

At idle the throttle plate is nearly closed, so the engine needs a controlled extra air path to keep running. On many vehicles that path is an idle air control (IAC) valve on or near the throttle body, which the powertrain control module (PCM) opens and closes constantly to hold the target idle speed. OBD-Codes.com says P0511 sets when the PCM detects a problem with the IAC circuit and the engine RPM doesn't match the programmed limits, and that any other, more specific IAC codes should be diagnosed first. RepairPal describes it more simply: the PCM can't achieve the desired idle RPM. It is a generic OBD-II code that OBD-Codes.com says applies to 1996 and newer vehicles, including Dodge, Chrysler, Hyundai, Jeep and Mazda. P0508 (circuit low), P0509 (circuit high) and P0505, P0506 and P0507 are related idle control codes.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Both sources treat this as serious. OBD-Codes.com says P0511 should be treated as severe and addressed with urgency because the engine may stall or idle at an unexpectedly high RPM, and RepairPal rates it Urgent, its highest level. A car that stalls when you come to a stop can lose power assist for steering and brakes, so get it checked soon and be careful in traffic until then.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Engine idle speed higher than normal (both sources)
  • Engine stalls, especially at idle or when coming to a stop (both sources)
  • Unusual noises from the throttle body area (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Other idle air control codes stored (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Defective idle air control motor or valve (both sources)
  2. Open or shorted IAC control wiring or connectors (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists a wiring issue)
  3. Engine vacuum leak (OBD-Codes.com only)
  4. Carbon buildup in the throttle body or IAC valve (OBD-Codes.com only)
  5. Faulty PCM or a PCM programming error (both sources list the PCM)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data and write them down. OBD-Codes.com says to diagnose any other, more specific IAC codes first.
  2. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) and recalls for your vehicle, since there may be a known fix.
  3. Inspect the IAC wiring and connectors for damage, corrosion or burnt spots and repair anything you find.
  4. Check for vacuum leaks, including the PCV valve and hoses, vacuum hoses, brake booster hose and check valve, a stuck-open EGR valve or leaking EGR gasket, and the intake manifold gasket.
  5. Clean carbon from the throttle body and IAC passages if your vehicle's procedure allows it.
  6. Clear the code and drive normally to see whether it returns.

Typical fixes

  • Repair damaged IAC wiring or connectors
  • Repair any vacuum leak
  • Clean carbon buildup from the throttle body or IAC valve
  • Replace the idle air control valve if it fails the manufacturer's test
  • Repair or reprogram the PCM (rare, only after other causes are ruled out)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The engine stalls in traffic or when stopping
  • The idle stays very high and you need to test the IAC system (OBD-Codes.com describes removing the IAC valve to see whether the idle can be controlled, and testing the IAC motor with a multimeter against manufacturer specs)
  • You can't find a vacuum leak but the idle is still out of control
  • The code returns after wiring repairs, cleaning and a new valve

FAQs

Is P0511 serious?

Yes. OBD-Codes.com says to treat it as severe and address it with urgency, and RepairPal rates it Urgent. Stalling at stops is the main risk, so get it fixed soon.

What is the most common cause of P0511?

Both sources point to a failed idle air control valve, wiring problems and, less often, the PCM. OBD-Codes.com also lists vacuum leaks and carbon buildup in the throttle body or IAC valve.

How is P0511 different from P0508 and P0509?

All three point to the idle air control circuit. OBD-Codes.com says P0508 means a value lower than expected and P0509 a value higher than expected, while P0511 is the general IAC circuit code, and more specific IAC codes should be fixed first.

Do the sources describe P0511 the same way?

Not quite. OBD-Codes.com describes it as a problem in the IAC circuit with engine RPM outside programmed limits, while RepairPal describes it as the PCM being unable to reach the desired idle RPM. RepairPal's cause list is also shorter: it lists only the IAC valve, the PCM and wiring, and doesn't mention vacuum leaks or carbon buildup.

How much does it cost to fix P0511?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It depends on the cause, from a wiring repair or vacuum hose to a new idle air control valve, so get the cause diagnosed first.
