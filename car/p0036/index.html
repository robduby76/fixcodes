P0036: HO2S Heater Control Circuit (Bank 1, Sensor 2) (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0036 means the engine computer has found a fault in the heater circuit of the rear (downstream) oxygen sensor on Bank 1, the one after the catalytic converter. An open heater inside the sensor is a common cause, but broken power or ground wiring, a corroded exhaust ground strap or the computer can also set it.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0036 means

Bank 1 Sensor 2 is the oxygen sensor after the catalytic converter on the side of the engine with cylinder #1 (an inline engine has only Bank 1). OBD-Codes.com explains that heated oxygen sensors have a built-in heater that warms the sensor so it starts giving useful readings sooner. Depending on the vehicle the sensor has three or four wires: usually two carry the sensor signal to the engine computer (PCM/ECM) and the others feed the heater. Three-wire sensors are usually grounded through the exhaust system, while four-wire sensors have a separate ground wire. The heater may get its power or its ground from the PCM, or from another source the PCM controls. RepairPal puts it simply: when the PCM detects the heater wire is not operating, it sets P0036. The code refers only to the heater half of the sensor, not its oxygen reading. GM's wording, per OBD-Codes.com, is 'HO2S Heater Control Circuit Conditions'. P0037 and P0038 are the circuit-low and circuit-high versions for the same sensor, P0141 is the general heater circuit malfunction code for it, and P0030 is the same fault on the front (Sensor 1) sensor. OBD-Codes.com notes P0056 is basically identical but for Bank 2.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Usually drivable. RepairPal rates P0036 Moderate: not an immediate safety threat, but get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. OBD-Codes.com says you probably won't notice anything besides the check engine light, but a car with an active check engine light will usually fail an OBD2 emissions inspection.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • OBD-Codes.com says you will probably notice no other symptoms, since the heater only runs briefly after a cold start and this sensor sits after the catalytic converter, so it doesn't feed the air/fuel mixture; it is mainly used to check catalytic converter efficiency

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Open circuit inside the oxygen sensor's heater (OBD-Codes.com)
  2. Open power or ground wire to the oxygen sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Corroded or broken exhaust system ground strap, on sensors grounded through the exhaust (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Failed heater circuit wiring, or a failed engine computer (PCM/ECM) (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. RepairPal's page only says the PCM sets the code when it detects the heater wire is not operating and doesn't list specific causes

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes. If P0036 shows up with other heater codes such as P0030 or P0056, look for a shared fuse, relay, power or ground problem before replacing several sensors.
  2. Let the exhaust cool first; OBD-Codes.com warns it gets very hot.
  3. Visually inspect the Bank 1 Sensor 2 sensor (after the catalytic converter, cylinder #1 side) and its wiring for damage or loose connections.
  4. Unplug the sensor and, using a wiring diagram, measure the heater resistance across the heater pins with a multimeter on the ohms scale. OBD-Codes.com says the heater should show some resistance; an excessive or over-limit reading means the heater is open and the sensor needs replacing.
  5. Back-probe the heater ground wire at the connector and check resistance to a known good ground.
  6. Back-probe the heater power wire and check for voltage to ground at a cold start. OBD-Codes.com says no power at the connector during a cold start points to the power supply circuit or the PCM itself.

Typical fixes

  • Replace the Bank 1 Sensor 2 oxygen sensor when its heater tests open
  • Repair damaged or loose wiring, or an open power or ground wire, to the sensor
  • Clean or replace a corroded or broken exhaust ground strap
  • Diagnose the engine computer only after the sensor, wiring and power supply test good

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The code returns right after a new sensor is fitted, which points to wiring, the power supply or the computer
  • You need a wiring diagram to tell whether the PCM supplies the heater's power or its ground on your vehicle
  • The sensor is seized in the exhaust pipe

FAQs

Is P0036 the front or rear oxygen sensor?

The rear one. Sensor 2 on Bank 1 sits after the catalytic converter on the cylinder #1 side. OBD-Codes.com says it is mainly used to verify catalytic converter efficiency, which is why a heater fault here usually doesn't change how the engine runs.

What's the difference between P0036, P0037, P0038 and P0141?

All four point at the heater in the Bank 1 Sensor 2 oxygen sensor. P0036 is the heater control circuit fault, P0037 means the computer saw a low voltage on that circuit, P0038 means it saw a high voltage, and P0141 is the general heater circuit malfunction code. Which one sets depends on how the manufacturer monitors the heater.

Do the sources agree?

Yes, as far as they go. Both say the code is about the oxygen sensor's heater circuit. RepairPal rates it Moderate and only says the PCM sets it when the heater wire isn't operating, while OBD-Codes.com gives specific causes (an open heater, open power or ground wires, a corroded exhaust ground strap, wiring or the PCM). Neither source gives a repair cost.
