P0455: Evaporative Emission (EVAP) System Leak Detected (large leak / no purge flow) (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 3 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0455 means the EVAP system has a large leak or no purge flow. The most common fix is the gas cap: reseat it, clear the code, and see if it comes back.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

🚗 Can I keep driving?

Usually yes. OBD-Codes.com says you likely won't notice drivability problems, and RepairPal rates P0455 Moderate, meaning get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal says some drivers notice a fuel odor from escaping vapors, so if you smell fuel, get it checked promptly. A flashing check engine light is a different problem (a misfire): stop driving soon.

What to check first

In order, cheapest and easiest first. Stop when the code stays away.

  1. Check the gas cap. It should be on the car, undamaged and clicked tight. RepairPal lists a missing or loose cap as the most common reason for P0455, and OBD-Codes.com also lists a non-factory (non-conforming) cap.
  2. Remove and reinstall the cap until it clicks, clear the code, and drive for a day to see if it returns (OBD-Codes.com). The Wisconsin inspection program says the light can also turn itself off after three consecutive passing checks.
  3. If the code comes back, replace the gas cap (OBD-Codes.com). For the related P0442 it recommends an original-manufacturer cap rather than an aftermarket one.
  4. Look at the fuel filler neck for distortion or damage (RepairPal).
  5. Inspect the EVAP hoses you can see and reach for cuts, holes or loose connections. OBD-Codes.com says you may hear a vacuum noise or smell fuel near the leak.
  6. Where you can see them safely, look for damage at the charcoal canister and fuel tank. RepairPal lists a split or damaged canister, a bad tank sending unit seal and a damaged tank among the causes.
  7. If the cap and hoses are fine, get a shop to test the system. OBD-Codes.com says P0455 can mean a large leak or no purge flow, and RepairPal lists the purge valve and vent valve as common misdiagnoses, so don't replace them on a guess.

Quick FAQ

Will tightening or replacing the gas cap fix P0455?

Often, yes. OBD-Codes.com says the most common repair is to remove and reinstall the cap, clear the codes and drive for a day, and to replace the cap if the code returns. RepairPal lists a missing or loose cap as the most common reason. If a good cap doesn't fix it, the leak is somewhere else in the EVAP system.

What besides the gas cap can cause P0455?

RepairPal lists a damaged fuel filler neck, torn or punctured EVAP hoses, a defective fuel tank sending unit gasket or seal, a split or damaged carbon canister, a defective vent or purge valve, a damaged fuel tank and a defective fuel tank pressure sensor.

What does a 'large leak' mean in P0455?

RepairPal says P0455 appears when the EVAP system can't build enough vacuum, which is typically caused by an air leak. OBD-Codes.com describes it as a large leak or no purge flow. The EVAP system captures fuel vapors rather than liquid fuel, so this is a vapor leak, although RepairPal says some drivers notice a fuel odor.

What P0455 means

The EVAP system stores fuel vapor in a charcoal canister and later purges it into the engine to be burned. P0455 means the PCM detected a large leak, or a lack of purge flow, in that system. Manufacturers word it slightly differently. For example, Chrysler says 'EVAP Large Leak Detected' and Nissan says 'canister purge system – gross leak'. RepairPal explains that it appears when the EVAP system can't build enough vacuum, usually because of an air leak.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Usually OK to drive

Generally safe to keep driving: you likely won't notice drivability problems. If you smell fuel, get it checked promptly.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Usually no drivability problems
  • Possibly a fuel smell or a vacuum noise near a leak

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Loose, missing or improperly fitted gas cap
  2. Non-original (non-conforming) gas cap
  3. Other leak or damaged part in the EVAP system, such as a cut or disconnected hose
  4. Damaged fuel tank filler neck, a split carbon canister, a defective vent or purge valve, a fuel tank sending-unit seal, a fuel tank pressure sensor, or a damaged fuel tank (listed by RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Remove and reinstall the gas cap, clear the code, and drive for a day to see if it returns.
  2. If it returns, replace the gas cap.
  3. Inspect the EVAP hoses for cuts or holes. You may hear a vacuum noise or smell fuel near the leak.

Typical fixes

  • Reseat or replace the gas cap
  • Repair cut or disconnected EVAP hoses
  • Repair other damaged EVAP components found by testing

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • A new cap and visual inspection don't fix it: a smoke test will find the leak
  • Parts near the fuel tank need lifting the vehicle or lowering the tank

FAQs

Why do I have both P0455 and P0442?

Both are EVAP leak codes for different leak sizes. Diagnose the system as a whole, starting with the cap and hoses.
