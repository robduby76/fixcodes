P250F: Engine Oil Level Too Low (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P250F means the engine computer (PCM) has decided the engine oil level is too low, based on the oil level sensor in or on the oil pan. Both RepairPal and OBD-Codes.com say the most likely cause is simply low oil, but OBD-Codes.com also lists a bad oil level sensor, wiring or connector faults, a blown fuse, oil that is too full, and rarely the PCM.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P250F means

OBD-Codes.com explains that P250F belongs to the engine oil level sensor circuit (also called the oil level safety circuit), alongside related codes P250A through P250E. The circuit watches the oil level and oil pressure so the engine's internal parts get enough lubrication, and the oil level sensor is usually mounted on or inside the oil pan, with the exact location depending on the vehicle. When the PCM detects a too-low oil level, it sets P250F and turns on the check engine light, the service engine soon light, or both. RepairPal gives the same definition and adds that a message may also appear on the instrument cluster. The sources agree on the meaning; they differ in scope, since RepairPal names only low oil as the cause while OBD-Codes.com also lists sensor, wiring, fuse and PCM faults and, perhaps surprisingly, an oil level that is too high.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Stop and check your oil. RepairPal rates P250F urgent, meaning immediate action is needed, and OBD-Codes.com calls it severe because too little lubrication or oil pressure can damage internal engine parts very quickly. OBD-Codes.com also notes the engine computer may shut the engine down to protect it. Check the dipstick before driving further, and don't keep driving if the oil is low and you can't find out why.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light and/or service engine soon light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • A low oil message on the instrument cluster (RepairPal)
  • Low reading on the oil pressure gauge (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine may not start, since OBD-Codes.com says the PCM may shut the engine down to prevent damage

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Low engine oil level (both sources; OBD-Codes.com calls it the most likely cause)
  2. Defective oil level sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Dirty or clogged oil pressure sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Engine oil level too high (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Faulty or damaged wiring, or a corroded, damaged or loose connector (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Blown fuse or fusible link, if the circuit has one (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. Defective PCM (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Park on level ground and check the oil on the dipstick, then top up or correct the level as needed. OBD-Codes.com calls this the first critical step.
  2. If the oil was very low, find out why before you keep driving. OBD-Codes.com warns that just adding oil and driving on can bring the code back soon and cause extreme engine damage if there's a leak or the engine is burning oil.
  3. If the level is overfilled, have the excess drained, since OBD-Codes.com lists an oil level that is too high as a cause.
  4. Look up technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, then inspect the oil level sensor wiring and connectors for rubbing, bare wires, burn spots, corrosion and damaged pins (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. Check for a blown fuse or fusible link in the oil level circuit if your vehicle has one (OBD-Codes.com).

Typical fixes

  • Topping up the oil, or changing the oil and filter (OBD-Codes.com), and repairing any leak that caused the low level
  • Cleaning or replacing the engine oil level sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Cleaning corrosion from connectors and repairing or replacing faulty wiring (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replacing a blown fuse or fusible link, or repairing faulty ground straps (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Flashing or replacing the PCM, only after everything else is ruled out (OBD-Codes.com)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Voltage and continuity tests of the oil level sensor circuit with a digital multimeter, using the vehicle's own specs and troubleshooting charts (OBD-Codes.com says good wiring and connections should read 0 ohms with power removed)
  • A continuity test from the PCM to the frame to check ground straps and ground wires (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Confirming actual oil pressure with a mechanical oil pressure gauge (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Avoiding the common mistake OBD-Codes.com names: replacing the oil level sensor when wiring or connections are actually setting the code

FAQs

Can I drive with P250F?

Not until you've checked the oil. RepairPal rates P250F urgent and OBD-Codes.com calls it severe, because low lubrication can damage the engine very quickly. If the dipstick shows the oil is low, top it up and find the cause before driving any distance.

My oil level looks fine. Why do I still have P250F?

OBD-Codes.com lists other causes: a defective oil level sensor, wiring or connector problems, a blown fuse, a clogged oil pressure sensor, an overfilled engine, or rarely the PCM. Inspect the wiring first, since OBD-Codes.com says replacing the sensor when wiring is the real problem is a common mistake.

Can too much oil cause P250F?

OBD-Codes.com lists an oil level that is too high as a possible cause, though RepairPal only mentions low oil. If you recently overfilled the engine, have the extra drained.
