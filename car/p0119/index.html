P0119: Engine Coolant Temperature (ECT) Sensor 1 Circuit Intermittent (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0119 means the engine computer has seen an intermittent or erratic signal from the engine coolant temperature (ECT) sensor circuit. Common causes include a failing ECT sensor, loose, corroded or chafed wiring and connectors, and cooling-system problems such as low coolant, a faulty thermostat, or rusty coolant.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0119 means

The ECT sensor is threaded into a coolant passage (in the block, cylinder head, intake manifold or thermostat housing, depending on the vehicle) and measures coolant temperature. The engine computer (PCM) uses it for fuel delivery, idle speed, ignition timing and cooling fan control, and RepairPal notes it also helps protect the engine from overheating. OBD-Codes.com explains it is usually a thermistor on a 5-volt reference circuit whose resistance drops as it heats up; a two-wire sensor feeds only the PCM, while a three-wire sensor also feeds the dash gauge. Both sources agree P0119 sets when the PCM sees an intermittent or erratic signal rather than one that is simply stuck high or low. They differ on causes: OBD-Codes.com lists low coolant and a faulty thermostat alongside the sensor and wiring, while RepairPal lists rusty or corroded coolant and a plugged or restricted cooling system. Exact set conditions vary by manufacturer. Related ECT codes include P0115 (circuit), P0116 (range/performance), P0117 (circuit low) and P0118 (circuit high).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0119 'Urgent', and OBD-Codes.com says it should be addressed urgently because the coolant temperature signal affects how the engine runs. RepairPal also says many cars show no symptoms besides the check engine light. If the temperature gauge reads hot, steam appears, or the cooling fans aren't running when the engine is hot, stop driving, because overheating can damage the engine. Only open the cooling system when the engine is cool.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • In many cases no other noticeable symptoms (per RepairPal)
  • Hard starting or poor fuel economy (listed by RepairPal)
  • Rough idle on a cold start, hesitation or stumble on acceleration, or a rich exhaust smell (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  • Possible overheating, or an electric cooling fan that runs constantly or not at all (listed by OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failing ECT sensor
  2. Open, shorted, loose, corroded or chafed wiring or connector pins in the ECT circuit
  3. Low engine coolant level (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Faulty thermostat (listed by OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Rusty or corroded coolant, or a plugged or restricted cooling system (listed by RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data. OBD-Codes.com says to diagnose other ECT codes (P0115 to P0118) and engine over-temperature codes first.
  2. With the engine cool, check the coolant level and condition. If it is down by more than a few quarts, look for a leak; if it is rusty, RepairPal notes that can cause this code.
  3. Inspect the ECT sensor connector and wiring for corrosion, loose or broken pins, and wiring routed near hot exhaust manifolds, which OBD-Codes.com says can short to ground intermittently.
  4. Clear the code and drive normally. Because the fault is intermittent, it may take a while to come back.
  5. With a scan tool, watch the coolant temperature reading while the engine warms up. It should climb smoothly; sudden jumps or dropouts point to the sensor, connector or wiring.

Typical fixes

  • Repair loose, corroded or chafed ECT wiring or connector pins
  • Replace the ECT sensor if its reading doesn't match actual temperature or its resistance is out of spec
  • Top up coolant and fix any leaks
  • Replace a faulty thermostat
  • Flush the cooling system and refill with the correct coolant if it is rusty or restricted

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The code keeps returning after the sensor has been replaced. OBD-Codes.com describes comparing the scan tool reading with an infrared thermometer reading of actual engine temperature and checking sensor resistance against the manufacturer's specs with a multimeter.
  • RepairPal recommends duplicating the set conditions on a test drive while watching the ECT reading, comparing it with the intake air temperature reading, and confirming a steady 5-volt reference and good ground at the connector with the correct wiring diagram.
  • RepairPal warns the ECT sensor is often replaced when the real cause is rusty coolant, a poor connection or chafed wiring, or a cooling fan or cooling-system problem
  • The engine is actually overheating, or the cooling fans aren't working

FAQs

Can I drive with P0119?

Carefully, and not for long. RepairPal rates it 'Urgent' and OBD-Codes.com says to address it urgently, though RepairPal adds that many cars show no symptoms besides the check engine light. Watch the temperature gauge and stop driving if the engine overheats.

What is the difference between P0119 and P0115?

P0115 is a general ECT circuit fault, such as a voltage that is abnormally high or low. P0119 specifically means the signal is intermittent or erratic, which often points to a loose connection, chafed wiring or a sensor that is starting to fail.

I replaced the coolant temperature sensor and P0119 came back. Why?

Both sources list causes besides the sensor. Check the connector pins and wiring (especially near the exhaust), the coolant level and the thermostat per OBD-Codes.com, and coolant condition and cooling-system restrictions per RepairPal.

Why does P0119 show up a lot on Chevy, GMC and Ford searches?

Those makes show up often in searches for this code, but neither source names a model-specific problem. P0119 is a generic code, so diagnose it the same way and check for service bulletins for your specific vehicle.
