P0054: HO2S Heater Resistance (Bank 1, Sensor 2) (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0054 means the engine computer has measured more resistance than it allows in the heater circuit of the downstream (rear) oxygen sensor on Bank 1, the sensor after the catalytic converter on the side of the engine with cylinder #1. A failed sensor heater, damaged wiring or connectors, a blown fuse or fusible link, or a bad engine control relay are the usual causes.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0054 means

Bank 1 is the side of the engine that contains cylinder #1, and Sensor 2 is the downstream oxygen sensor, the one after the catalytic converter. On four-cylinder engines there is only one bank. OBD-Codes.com explains that a heated oxygen sensor has a separate heater circuit that typically carries battery voltage (12.6 volts minimum), sometimes through an in-line fuse or relay. When the engine is cold, the engine computer (PCM) sends battery voltage to the heater so the sensor warms up quickly, then shuts it off once the engine reaches normal operating temperature. If the PCM detects more resistance in the heater circuit than its programmed limits allow, it stores P0054 and may turn on the check engine light; some vehicles need the fault on more than one drive before the light comes on. RepairPal describes the trigger more generally, saying the PCM sets P0054 when it detects that the heater wire is not operating. P0054 is the Sensor 2 version of P0053; P0036, P0037 and P0038 are the heater control circuit codes for the same sensor, and P0141 is the general heater circuit malfunction code.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Usually drivable, but don't ignore it. The sources disagree on how serious it is: RepairPal rates P0054 Moderate (not an immediate safety threat, but get it diagnosed within a reasonable time), while OBD-Codes.com says it should be considered severe because the downstream oxygen sensor heater is inoperable. A car with an active check engine light will likely fail an OBD2 emissions inspection. Exhaust parts get very hot, so let the car cool fully before touching the oxygen sensor.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com; some vehicles need several drive cycles with the fault first)
  • Reduced fuel economy (both sources)
  • Black smoke from the exhaust during a cold start, from running rich (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Slow or delayed starting when cold, from running lean (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decreased engine performance (RepairPal)
  • Other related trouble codes stored at the same time (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty Bank 1 Sensor 2 oxygen sensor (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists oxygen sensor failure)
  2. Burnt, broken or disconnected wiring or connectors (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists a wiring issue)
  3. Blown fuse or burnt fusible link in the heater circuit (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Defective engine control relay (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Engine computer (PCM) failure (RepairPal; OBD-Codes.com does not list it for this code)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, since the fault may be a known issue with a known fix (OBD-Codes.com).
  2. Find the right sensor: Bank 1 is the side with cylinder #1 and Sensor 2 is the one after the catalytic converter. Let a recently run exhaust cool before touching it.
  3. Visually inspect the oxygen sensor wiring and connectors, especially where the harness runs near hot exhaust pipes and manifolds or past sharp edges such as exhaust heat shields (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Test the related fuses and fusible links with a multimeter while the heater circuit is switched on, because OBD-Codes.com notes a fuse can look fine unloaded and fail under load. A blown fuse suggests the heater circuit has shorted to ground.
  5. Record all stored codes and freeze frame data, clear the codes and test drive to see if P0054 comes straight back.
  6. If it does, with a cold engine watch the oxygen sensor heater data on a scan tool. OBD-Codes.com says heater voltage should be virtually the same as battery voltage while the engine is below operating temperature; a difference points to a resistance problem. You can also measure the sensor heater resistance with an ohmmeter (with the related modules disconnected) and compare it to your vehicle's specification.

Typical fixes

  • Repair burnt, chafed or disconnected wiring and connectors, and keep the harness away from hot exhaust parts (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a blown fuse or burnt fusible link, after finding what shorted it (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a defective engine control relay (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace the Bank 1 Sensor 2 oxygen sensor if its heater has failed; RepairPal lists this as the common repair
  • Look at the engine computer only after the sensor, wiring, fuses and relay test good (RepairPal lists PCM failure as a possible cause)
  • After the repair, drive until the readiness monitors complete to confirm the fix (OBD-Codes.com)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • P0054 comes back after a new sensor or new exhaust work, as in OBD-Codes.com forum threads on a 2008 Buick LaCrosse and a 2007 Chevy Avalanche
  • Fuses keep blowing, which points to a short you need a wiring diagram to trace
  • You don't have a multimeter, the heater resistance spec and wiring information for your vehicle
  • The old sensor is seized or breaks off in the exhaust pipe

FAQs

Is P0054 serious?

The sources disagree. RepairPal rates it Moderate, meaning it isn't an immediate safety threat but should be diagnosed within a reasonable time. OBD-Codes.com says it should be considered severe because the downstream sensor heater isn't working. Either way, it can hurt fuel economy and will likely fail an emissions inspection while the light is on.

What's the difference between P0053 and P0054?

Both mean the PCM measured too much resistance in an oxygen sensor heater circuit on Bank 1. P0053 is the upstream sensor (before the catalytic converter) and P0054 is the downstream sensor (after it).

What exactly triggers P0054?

The sources word it differently. OBD-Codes.com says the PCM sets P0054 when it detects more resistance in the heater circuit than its programmed limits allow. RepairPal says the PCM sets it when it detects the heater wire is not operating, the same wording it uses for the heater control circuit codes.

Is the engine computer a likely cause?

The sources differ. RepairPal lists PCM failure as one of the common problems behind P0054, while OBD-Codes.com's cause list covers only the sensor, wiring, fuses and relay. Rule those out first.

How much does it cost to fix P0054?

Neither source gives a price for this code. The usual repair is replacing one downstream oxygen sensor or fixing its wiring, fuse or relay, so get a quote for that once the circuit has been checked.
