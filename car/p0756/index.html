P0756: Shift Solenoid B Performance or Stuck Off (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0756 means the computer has detected that shift solenoid B, one of the electrically controlled valves that route transmission fluid so an automatic transmission can change gears, isn't performing as expected or is stuck off. Both sources list a faulty shift solenoid, low transmission fluid and wiring problems as causes; RepairPal adds a failed transmission control module, and OBD-Codes.com adds dirty fluid, a clogged filter, a defective valve body, restricted passages, internal transmission failure and a faulty PCM.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0756 means

Automatic transmissions shift by sending fluid pressure to the right clutches and bands at the right time, and the shift solenoids open and close valves in the valve body to do that. OBD-Codes.com (John Ingalls, former service manager and Air Force mechanic) describes P0756 as a generic code for a detected performance problem or a stuck-off condition in the shift solenoid B circuit, seen by the powertrain control module (PCM), on vehicles including Chrysler, Ford, Dodge, Hyundai, Kia, Ram, Lexus, Toyota, Mazda, Honda and VW. It says most automatic transmissions have at least three shift solenoids (A, B and C), that the B-solenoid codes are P0755 through P0759, and that an overdrive or other transmission warning lamp may also come on. RepairPal defines P0756 as Shift Solenoid "B" Performance/Stuck Off, but its explanation says the transmission control module (TCM) sets the code when it detects that a pressure control solenoid isn't performing as desired, wording it uses on other shift solenoid codes too. OBD-Codes.com doesn't tie solenoid B to a particular gear and says which codes set depends on the vehicle, transmission and number of gears, so check your vehicle's service information for what solenoid B controls and which module (PCM or TCM) runs it.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon, and don't keep driving hard on it. RepairPal rates P0756 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe. OBD-Codes.com says the severity normally starts as moderate but can become severe rapidly if it isn't fixed, and lists transmission slipping, overheating and limp mode among the symptoms. If the transmission slips, overheats, catches in gear or drops into limp mode, limit driving and have it diagnosed. An open code can also fail an OBD2 emissions inspection.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com); an overdrive or transmission warning lamp may also light (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission may not shift properly (RepairPal)
  • Transmission slipping, overheating or catching in gear (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Vehicle enters limp mode (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decreased fuel economy or increased fuel consumption (both sources)
  • Possible misfire-like symptoms (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty shift solenoid (both sources)
  2. Low transmission fluid level (both sources)
  3. Faulty or damaged wiring, or a corroded or damaged connector (both sources)
  4. Transmission control module failure (RepairPal) or faulty PCM (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Dirty or contaminated fluid, or a dirty or clogged transmission filter (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Defective valve body or restricted hydraulic passages (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. Internal transmission failure (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Write down every code stored with P0756 and the freeze frame data; other transmission codes, such as A or C solenoid codes or other B-solenoid codes (P0755 to P0759), help narrow the problem.
  2. Look up technical service bulletins for your year, model and transmission, and check when the fluid and filter were last changed, as OBD-Codes.com recommends.
  3. Check the transmission fluid level and look for contamination; both sources list low fluid as a cause.
  4. Inspect the wiring and connectors to the transmission solenoids, transmission pump and PCM for scraping, rubbing, bare wires, burn spots, corrosion and damaged pins, and check the shift linkage for security and binding where your transmission has one (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. With power removed, OBD-Codes.com says wiring continuity should read 0 ohms unless your service data says otherwise; resistance or no continuity points to an open or shorted wire. Voltage specs vary by vehicle, so follow your vehicle's troubleshooting chart.

Typical fixes

  • Top up or change the transmission fluid and filter (OBD-Codes.com; both sources list low fluid)
  • Clean corroded connectors or repair damaged wiring (both sources list wiring)
  • Repair or replace the faulty shift solenoid (both sources)
  • OBD-Codes.com also lists repairing or replacing the valve body, a transmission flush to clean passages, repairing or replacing the transmission, and flashing or replacing the PCM
  • RepairPal lists TCM failure as a cause, so a module fault is possible once the fluid, wiring and solenoid check out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission slips, overheats, catches in gear or goes into limp mode; RepairPal rates P0756 Urgent
  • OBD-Codes.com says the advanced steps are very vehicle specific and need a digital multimeter and the vehicle's technical references
  • Valve body work, internal transmission repair, or flashing or replacing the PCM or TCM are shop jobs

FAQs

Is P0756 serious?

Yes, it should be fixed soon. RepairPal rates it Urgent. OBD-Codes.com says it usually starts as moderate but can become severe rapidly if not corrected, with slipping, overheating and limp mode possible.

Can I drive with P0756?

Neither source gives a safe distance. Because RepairPal rates it Urgent and OBD-Codes.com says it can worsen quickly, keep driving to a minimum and stop if the transmission slips, overheats or goes into limp mode.

What's the difference between P0756 and P0755 or P0758?

All three point to shift solenoid B. OBD-Codes.com describes P0755 as a general malfunction in the solenoid B circuit, P0758 as an electrical malfunction, and P0756 as a performance problem or stuck-off condition, so P0756 is more often about the solenoid not doing its job than about an open or shorted wire.

Can low transmission fluid cause P0756?

Yes, both sources list low fluid, and OBD-Codes.com also lists dirty fluid and a clogged filter, so checking the fluid is a sensible first step.

Why do the sources disagree?

RepairPal's explanation talks about the TCM and a pressure control solenoid, while OBD-Codes.com describes a performance or stuck-off problem in the shift solenoid B circuit seen by the PCM. OBD-Codes.com lists many more causes, including valve body, filter and internal transmission problems, and rates severity as moderate at first rather than Urgent. Neither source gives a repair cost.
