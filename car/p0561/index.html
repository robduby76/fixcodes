P0561: System Voltage Unstable (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0561 means the engine computer (PCM) saw the vehicle's system voltage behaving abnormally, which OBD-Codes.com and RepairPal title System Voltage Unstable. OBD-Codes.com calls a faulty alternator the most common cause; a weak or failing battery, high resistance in the battery or alternator wiring and, rarely, a failed PCM are the other things to check.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0561 means

The charging system (alternator and voltage regulator) keeps the electrical system at the right voltage while the engine runs. On many vehicles the PCM controls the alternator's voltage regulator through a power or ground circuit, and it watches its own ignition feed circuit to see whether charging is working, per OBD-Codes.com. Both sources title P0561 System Voltage Unstable, but neither describes a separate test for unstable voltage. OBD-Codes.com's description says a code sets when the voltage is too high, too low, or not present when it should be (the same wording it uses for P0560), and RepairPal says P0561 sets when battery voltage drops below 10 volts (the same wording it uses for P0560 and P0562). The exact trigger varies by vehicle. OBD-Codes.com says a healthy system reads about 13.2 to 14.7 volts at the battery with a load on, and treats readings well below 12 volts or above 15.5 volts as a charging system problem. P0560 (general malfunction), P0562 (low) and P0563 (high) are the related system voltage codes.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked soon and don't plan long trips until it is. RepairPal rates P0561 Urgent, meaning it can cause serious damage or leave you stranded. OBD-Codes.com gives no severity rating, but lists stalling, a no-start and a transmission that may not shift among the possible symptoms. If the red battery light is on too, have the battery and charging system tested right away. An open code can also fail an OBD2 emissions inspection.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Red battery or charging light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission may not shift (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine may not start, or may stall (both sources)
  • Some electrical accessories, such as the radio, may not work (RepairPal)
  • Lower fuel economy (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty alternator (OBD-Codes.com calls it the most common cause; RepairPal lists alternator failure first)
  2. Low battery voltage, a failing battery or a battery that was recently disconnected (OBD-Codes.com's repair steps; RepairPal lists battery failure)
  3. High resistance in the cable between the alternator and the battery (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. High resistance or an open circuit between the alternator and the control module (OBD-Codes.com); RepairPal lists a wiring issue
  5. Less often, a failed PCM (OBD-Codes.com calls this unlikely; RepairPal also lists PCM failure)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle; OBD-Codes.com says this can save time.
  2. Check the alternator belt, which OBD-Codes.com calls the most neglected part of the charging system, and make sure the battery terminals and cables are clean and tight. A loose connection can make voltage jump around.
  3. With the engine running, headlights on and the blower fan on high, measure voltage at the battery with a multimeter. OBD-Codes.com says it should be between 13.2 and 14.7 volts; well below 12 volts or above 15.5 volts points to the charging system, mainly the alternator.
  4. If you don't have a meter, most auto parts stores will test the battery, starter and charging system for little or no cost, per OBD-Codes.com.
  5. If voltage is normal, clear the code and see if it returns. If it doesn't, it was likely an intermittent or history code (for example after the battery was disconnected).
  6. If it returns, inspect the PCM connectors and wiring for corrosion, rubbing, melted plastic or burn marks, and clean the terminals.

Typical fixes

  • Replace the alternator if testing shows it isn't charging correctly; RepairPal lists alternator replacement as a common repair
  • Charge or replace the battery if testing confirms it is weak or has failed; RepairPal lists battery replacement as a common repair
  • Repair or replace high-resistance battery and alternator cables
  • Repair wiring or connectors between the alternator and the PCM
  • Replace the PCM only after all other tests pass; it must be programmed to the vehicle (OBD-Codes.com)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Battery voltage with the engine running is well below 12 volts or above about 15.5 volts and you aren't comfortable replacing the alternator
  • The voltage looks normal but the code keeps returning; OBD-Codes.com's next steps test the PCM's ignition feed and ground circuits with the harness unplugged, plus a wiggle test for intermittent connections, and need a wiring diagram
  • The car stalls, won't start or won't shift
  • A PCM replacement is suspected, since it must be programmed to the vehicle

FAQs

Is P0561 serious?

RepairPal rates it Urgent. OBD-Codes.com gives no rating but lists stalling, a no-start and shifting problems as possible symptoms, so treat it as something to fix soon before a charging problem leaves you stranded.

Is P0561 the alternator or the battery?

OBD-Codes.com calls a faulty alternator the most common cause, and its repair steps also mention low battery voltage or a disconnected battery. RepairPal lists both alternator and battery replacement as common repairs. A charging system test (voltage at the battery with the engine running and a load on) tells you which one it is.

How is P0561 different from P0560, P0562 and P0563?

By title, P0560 is a general System Voltage Malfunction, P0561 is System Voltage Unstable, P0562 is System Voltage Low and P0563 is System Voltage High. In practice both sources describe P0560 and P0561 with nearly the same text, and the diagnosis is the same charging system test.

Why do the sources disagree?

RepairPal rates P0561 Urgent while it rates P0560 and P0563 only Moderate, and it says the code sets when battery voltage drops below 10 volts. OBD-Codes.com says a code sets when voltage is too high, too low or not present. Neither source's description specifically explains the 'unstable' part of the title. RepairPal lists battery failure as a cause, which OBD-Codes.com's cause list doesn't (though its repair steps mention low battery voltage), and only OBD-Codes.com lists a red battery light, shifting problems and lower fuel economy as symptoms. Both agree the alternator is the top suspect.
