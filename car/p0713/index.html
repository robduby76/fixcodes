P0713: Transmission Fluid Temperature Sensor A Circuit High (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0713 means the signal from the transmission fluid temperature sensor is too high. OBD-Codes.com says this usually points to an open circuit, such as a broken wire or unplugged connector. A failed sensor or damaged wiring is the most common cause, but the sources also list the engine or transmission computer and problems inside the transmission.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0713 means

The transmission fluid temperature (TFT) sensor tells the computer how hot the transmission fluid is, and the computer uses that to set shift points, line pressure and torque converter clutch operation. OBD-Codes.com says the sensor is often in the transmission oil pan, gets a reference voltage (usually 5 volts) from the computer, and is a negative temperature coefficient thermistor, so its signal voltage goes down as the fluid gets hotter and up as it gets colder. A signal that is stuck high therefore looks to the computer like very cold fluid. OBD-Codes.com says P0713 sets when the computer sees a high TFT sensor signal, which usually means an open circuit. RepairPal describes it more generally, saying it sets when the computer detects an incorrect signal from the sensor. The sources differ slightly on the name: RepairPal calls it Circuit High, while OBD-Codes.com says Circuit High Input. The related sensor A codes are P0710 (circuit), P0711 (range/performance), P0712 (circuit low) and P0714 (intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it diagnosed soon and drive gently until you do. RepairPal rates P0713 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe. OBD-Codes.com calls it moderate to severe, says it can sometimes point to a transmission problem, and advises addressing it as soon as possible. If the transmission is stuck in limp mode or shifting harshly or late, keep trips short and slow and have it checked.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Transmission not shifting properly, or harsh or delayed shifts (both sources)
  • Transmission stuck in limp mode (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Torque converter clutch not working properly (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission temperature warning light on the dash (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty transmission fluid temperature sensor (both sources)
  2. Wiring problems, such as an open (broken or disconnected) sensor circuit (both sources)
  3. Engine computer (PCM) failure (both sources)
  4. Transmission computer (TCM) failure (RepairPal)
  5. A problem inside the transmission, especially if other transmission codes are also set (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check the transmission fluid level and condition using the method in your owner's or service manual.
  2. Inspect the sensor wiring and connectors for loose or unplugged connectors, broken or chafed wires and corrosion, repair anything you find, clear the code and see if it returns (OBD-Codes.com). If the code appeared right after a fluid change or other work under the car, check that connector first.
  3. Check for technical service bulletins for your vehicle (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. OBD-Codes.com describes a quick circuit check with a scan tool: watch the transmission temperature reading, unplug the sensor (the reading should drop to a very low value), then jumper the connector terminals. If the reading jumps to a very high temperature, the wiring and computer can see the input and the sensor itself is the likely fault.
  5. OBD-Codes.com also describes a resistance test: unplug the sensor, measure across its terminals with a multimeter, and start the engine. Resistance should drop smoothly as things warm up; if it doesn't, the sensor is faulty. Use your vehicle's specs where available.
  6. To find an open circuit, OBD-Codes.com says to check for the 5-volt reference at the sensor connector with the ignition on, then check continuity of the reference and ground wires between the sensor and the computer with the ignition off. A reading of OL (out of limits) means an open wire that needs repair.

Typical fixes

  • Reconnect or repair open, broken or corroded wiring and connectors (both sources)
  • Replace the transmission fluid temperature sensor if it fails testing (both sources); on some transmissions it sits inside the pan or is part of an internal harness, so check the service manual
  • Repair an open in the 5-volt reference or ground circuit between the sensor and computer, or the computer's own ground (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair or replace the engine or transmission computer only after the sensor and wiring check out (both sources)
  • Have the transmission inspected if the circuit is good and other transmission codes are present (OBD-Codes.com)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission is in limp mode or shifting harshly
  • Other transmission codes are set along with P0713
  • The sensor is inside the transmission pan or built into an internal harness, so replacing it means draining fluid and dropping the pan
  • A new sensor doesn't clear the code, which can point to wiring, the computer or the transmission itself

FAQs

Can I drive with P0713?

Only gently and not for long. RepairPal rates it Urgent and OBD-Codes.com calls it moderate to severe. The transmission may shift harshly or sit in limp mode until it's fixed.

Does P0713 mean my transmission is too hot?

No. A high signal from this sensor reads as very cold fluid, because the sensor's voltage rises as temperature drops. OBD-Codes.com says it usually means an open circuit, so check the wiring and connector first.

What's the difference between P0712 and P0713?

Both are about transmission fluid temperature sensor A. P0712 means the signal is too low, which OBD-Codes.com says usually means a short. P0713 means the signal is too high, which usually means an open circuit.

How much does it cost to fix P0713?

Neither source gives a repair cost for P0713. Reconnecting a plug or repairing a wire costs far less than a sensor that is inside the pan or part of an internal harness, so get it properly diagnosed first.
