P0A80: Replace Hybrid Battery Pack (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0A80 means the hybrid battery management system has found that one or more modules or cells in the high-voltage hybrid battery pack are weak or out of balance with the rest. The usual cause is a worn-out hybrid battery, but OBD-Codes.com also lists loose or corroded busbar connections, failed battery sensors and battery cooling fan problems.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0A80 means

A hybrid battery pack is made of many battery modules wired together. A battery control module (OBD-Codes.com calls it the hybrid vehicle battery management system, or HVBMS) watches the voltage, resistance and temperature of each part of the pack and reports to the engine computer (PCM). P0A80 sets when it finds that one or more modules can no longer do their share of the work, which OBD-Codes.com describes as a weak cell failure. The code applies to many hybrids, including Toyota (Prius, Camry), Lexus, Ford, Hyundai and GM models, per OBD-Codes.com. The sources differ on causes: RepairPal lists only high-voltage battery assembly failure, while OBD-Codes.com also lists sensor failures, loose or corroded busbar connectors or cables, and battery fan problems, and suggests that on vehicles under about 100,000 miles a loose or corroded connection is the more likely culprit. OBD-Codes.com also notes the code by itself does not shut off the hybrid charging system, though the fault behind it may.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Treat this as urgent. RepairPal rates P0A80 Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com calls it a serious malfunction in a major hybrid component that should be addressed as an urgent matter. The hybrid system may stop working, power can drop, and the car may not run normally, so get it diagnosed promptly. The hybrid battery carries dangerous high voltage: do not open the battery pack or touch its orange cables yourself.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on, often with other hybrid battery codes (OBD-Codes.com)
  • High-voltage (hybrid) system may not operate (RepairPal)
  • Electric propulsion system shuts off (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Lower fuel economy (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Reduced overall performance (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. A failed or worn-out high-voltage battery assembly, module or cell (RepairPal, OBD-Codes.com)
  2. High resistance in individual cells, or voltage or temperature differences between cells (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. A failed battery management sensor (voltage or temperature) (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Hybrid battery cooling fans not working properly (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Loose, broken or corroded busbar connectors or cables (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. The sources differ: RepairPal lists only battery assembly failure, while OBD-Codes.com says a connection problem is more likely on vehicles under about 100,000 miles

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and write them down with any freeze frame data. Other hybrid battery codes stored with P0A80 help narrow it down (OBD-Codes.com).
  2. Check that the hybrid battery cooling fan and its air intake aren't blocked, without opening the battery pack. OBD-Codes.com lists a poorly working battery fan as a cause.
  3. Check for technical service bulletins and any hybrid battery warranty coverage on your vehicle with the dealer before paying for repairs.
  4. Do not open the battery pack, test cells or touch orange high-voltage cables. OBD-Codes.com says the pack should only be serviced by qualified personnel.

Typical fixes

  • Clean or repair loose, broken or corroded busbar connectors and cables (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a faulty battery voltage or temperature sensor that fails manufacturer specifications (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair a battery cooling fan that is not working properly (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace the high-voltage battery assembly (RepairPal). OBD-Codes.com says individual cells or modules can be replaced and packs can be remanufactured, but a factory replacement pack is the most reliable fix, with a used pack as a lower-cost option

FAQs

Is P0A80 serious?

Yes. RepairPal rates it Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com calls it a serious malfunction that should be addressed urgently.

Can I drive with P0A80?

Limit driving and get it diagnosed soon. The hybrid system may stop working or performance may drop, so don't count on the car for long trips until it's checked.

Does P0A80 always mean a new hybrid battery?

Not always. RepairPal points to battery assembly failure, but OBD-Codes.com says loose or corroded connections, sensors and battery fans can also cause it, especially on vehicles under about 100,000 miles.

How much does it cost to fix P0A80?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from cleaning a connection to replacing the whole battery pack, so get a diagnosis, check your hybrid battery warranty, and get a written estimate first.
