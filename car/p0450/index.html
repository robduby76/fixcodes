P0450: EVAP System Pressure Sensor Malfunction (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0450 means the engine computer has found a problem in the EVAP fuel tank pressure sensor circuit. OBD-Codes.com lists a bad pressure sensor, a loose or bad fuel cap, a clogged tank pressure relief valve, damaged EVAP hoses and a cracked charcoal canister. RepairPal describes it as the computer getting an inaccurate signal from the pressure sensor.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0450 means

The evaporative emission (EVAP) system catches fuel vapor from the fuel tank, stores it in a charcoal canister, and later draws it into the engine to be burned. The EVAP (fuel tank) pressure sensor tells the engine computer how much pressure or vacuum is in the tank so the rest of the system can act on it. OBD-Codes.com says P0450 is stored when the computer detects a malfunction in the EVAP pressure sensor circuit, or when EVAP or tank pressure stays outside a programmed range for a set time under certain conditions; RepairPal says it sets when the computer detects an inaccurate signal from the sensor. OBD-Codes.com notes the sensor is almost always in the fuel tank, built into the fuel pump and fuel level sending unit housing, and that both electrical and mechanical problems, including abnormally low or high EVAP pressure, can set the code. The sources differ in detail: OBD-Codes.com gives a full list of causes including the fuel cap, hoses and canister, while RepairPal's P0450 summary names only the pressure sensor signal. Note that the lower part of RepairPal's P0450 page repeats its P0452 (sensor low input) text, so its detailed causes there are for P0452, not P0450. Related codes include P0451 (sensor range/performance), P0452 (sensor low), P0453 (sensor high) and P0454 (sensor intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P0450 Moderate, meaning it may not threaten safety or drivability right away but should still be checked. OBD-Codes.com says it will not seriously affect drivability and should not be classed as severe. It can keep the car from passing an emissions inspection.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine (malfunction indicator) light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • In most cases no other symptoms (OBD-Codes.com)
  • A slight decrease in fuel efficiency (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Bad EVAP / fuel tank pressure sensor (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal describes an inaccurate sensor signal)
  2. Loose, defective or missing fuel cap (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Clogged fuel tank pressure relief valve (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Broken, collapsed, burned or otherwise damaged EVAP hoses or lines (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Cracked or broken charcoal canister (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Remove the fuel cap, check its seal, and screw it back on properly (OBD-Codes.com).
  2. Look over the EVAP hoses and lines, especially where they run near sharp edges or the hot exhaust (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. Read all stored codes and write down the freeze frame data before clearing anything (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. After clearing, drive normally; OBD-Codes.com says EVAP codes almost always need several drive cycles before they come back or readiness is set.
  5. If your scan tool shows live data, watch the EVAP pressure sensor reading; OBD-Codes.com says if pressure is back within spec after fixing the cap, that may have solved it.

Typical fixes

  • Tighten or replace a loose or bad fuel cap (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace the EVAP pressure sensor if it fails the manufacturer's test (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair open or shorted sensor circuits (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace damaged EVAP hoses, a clogged tank pressure relief valve or a cracked charcoal canister if found (OBD-Codes.com)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com suggests testing the pressure sensor before doing a smoke test, since this is a pressure sensor code
  • If the sensor meets spec, unplug the related modules and test each circuit with a digital volt/ohmmeter for opens or shorts (OBD-Codes.com)
  • The sensor is usually on top of the tank and may be part of the fuel pump module (OBD-Codes.com), which can mean lowering the tank
  • A smoke test may be needed to find hose or canister damage (OBD-Codes.com)
  • The code keeps coming back after repairs

FAQs

Is P0450 serious?

RepairPal rates it Moderate. OBD-Codes.com says it won't seriously affect drivability and usually causes no symptoms besides the check engine light, sometimes a slight drop in fuel economy. It isn't usually an emergency, but it can fail an emissions inspection, so get it diagnosed within a reasonable time.

Can a loose gas cap cause P0450?

OBD-Codes.com lists a loose, defective or missing fuel cap as a possible cause and suggests checking and re-tightening it first. If the code comes back after a few drive cycles, the pressure sensor and its wiring are the next things to check.

What is the difference between P0450 and P0452, P0453 or P0454?

All of them involve the EVAP fuel tank pressure sensor. P0450 is the general sensor malfunction code, while P0452 means the signal is too low, P0453 means it is too high, and P0454 means it is intermittent.

How much does it cost to fix P0450?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It can range from a new fuel cap to a pressure sensor built into the fuel pump module that needs the tank lowered, so get a written estimate. Emission parts often have longer warranties, so check whether yours is still covered.
