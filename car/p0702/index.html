P0702: Transmission Control System Electrical (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0702 means the engine or transmission computer has found an electrical problem somewhere in the automatic transmission's control system. OBD-Codes.com says it is usually stored along with other transmission codes, and those should be diagnosed and fixed first.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0702 means

Automatic transmissions on OBD-II vehicles are run electronically: a control module reads engine and transmission sensors and operates shift, pressure-control and torque converter clutch solenoids. OBD-Codes.com explains that most vehicles build the transmission controller into the powertrain control module (PCM), while some use a separate transmission control module (TCM) that shares data with the PCM over the CAN network. It says P0702 is a generic code (it lists Ford, Honda, Mazda, Mercedes and VW among examples) that sets when the PCM detects a malfunction in the electrical portion of the transmission control system, that only automatic-transmission vehicles should show it, and that it is usually stored only when other electrical transmission control codes are also present. The sources describe it differently: RepairPal says the PCM sets P0702 when the TCM cannot select and engage the correct gear, while OBD-Codes.com frames it as an electrical fault (sensors, circuits or the controller). Both use the same name, Transmission Control System Electrical. Related codes are P0700 (transmission control system MIL request), P0701 (transmission control system range/performance), and the range sensor codes P0705 to P0709.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it fixed right away. OBD-Codes.com says a P0702 should be addressed immediately and that the limp-in mode it can trigger is not meant for long-term use. RepairPal rates P0702 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage to the vehicle. Avoid long drives and highway speeds until it's diagnosed.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Harsher than normal shifts if the transmission goes into limp-in mode, which greatly raises pump pressure (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Erratic shift patterns (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission not shifting at all (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decreased fuel efficiency (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Other transmission-related codes stored at the same time (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed transmission sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  2. Open or shorted circuits in the transmission control system, including the internal transmission harness, which OBD-Codes.com says is often damaged by sharp edges and heat inside the case (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Defective transmission controller or a PCM programming error (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. RepairPal's description: the TCM cannot select and engage the correct gear (RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read and write down all stored codes and freeze frame data before clearing anything. OBD-Codes.com says to diagnose and repair any other transmission codes first, starting with the one stored first, before working on P0702.
  2. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, since the problem may be a known issue with a known fix.
  3. Visually inspect the transmission control wiring and connectors for damage, corrosion or loose pins.
  4. Test the battery and check the battery cables and terminal ends, as OBD-Codes.com recommends, because low or unstable voltage can upset the electronics.
  5. Clear the codes and test drive. If the code comes back right away, go back to the freeze frame data and the other stored codes.
  6. Use a service manual, scan tool and digital volt/ohmmeter for circuit testing, since wiring and procedures vary by make and model.

Typical fixes

  • Repair the faults behind any other stored transmission codes
  • Repair open or shorted wiring or connectors in the transmission control system, including the internal harness
  • Replace a failed transmission sensor
  • Reprogram or replace the PCM or TCM only after wiring, sensors and other codes check out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission won't shift, is stuck in limp mode, or shifts very harshly
  • Several transmission codes are stored and you don't have a scan tool or wiring diagrams
  • The fault is in the internal harness, which means removing the transmission pan or more
  • A module needs reprogramming or replacement

FAQs

Is P0702 serious?

Yes. RepairPal rates it Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com says it should be addressed immediately because limp-in mode isn't meant for long-term use.

Why do I have other transmission codes with P0702?

OBD-Codes.com says P0702 is usually only stored when other electrical transmission control codes are present, and recommends diagnosing and repairing those first.

Can a weak battery cause P0702?

OBD-Codes.com includes testing the battery, cables and terminal ends early in its diagnosis, so it's worth ruling out before replacing parts. It doesn't say a battery alone sets the code.

How much does it cost to fix P0702?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It depends on whether the fix is a wiring repair, a sensor or a module, so get a diagnosis or written estimate first.
