P0234: Turbocharger/Supercharger Overboost Condition (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0234 means the engine computer (PCM) saw more turbocharger or supercharger boost pressure than it allows. On turbo engines the most common cause is a wastegate that sticks closed or isn't getting the right pressure signal, often from a bent actuator rod or a loose or clogged boost-control hose. A faulty boost pressure sensor or wiring can also set it.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0234 means

A turbocharger or supercharger pushes extra air into the engine (forced induction), so a small engine can make more power. On a turbo, a wastegate valve opens to send exhaust around the turbine once boost reaches the target, which keeps boost from climbing further. The PCM controls how far the wastegate opens with a solenoid, stepper motor or pulse modulator, using readings from the boost, MAP, barometric, temperature and knock sensors. P0234 sets when the boost pressure reading goes above the PCM's limit. RepairPal adds that knock sensors reporting pinging can also set it on some engines. "A" refers to the first (or only) turbocharger or supercharger. The opposite code, P0299, means boost is lower than expected.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it fixed soon and drive gently until then. RepairPal rates P0234 Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com says it should be corrected as soon as possible, because boost pressure above the engine's limits can damage the engine or transmission. Avoid hard acceleration and towing, and stop driving if the engine knocks loudly or the temperature gauge climbs. The engine computer may also put the car in limp mode, which limits power. An open code can also fail an OBD2 emissions inspection.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (both sources)
  • Loss of power, or no turbo boost if the PCM goes into limp mode (both sources)
  • Engine pinging or knocking, especially on acceleration (both sources)
  • Engine hesitation or stalling (RepairPal)
  • Misfiring (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine or transmission running hot, with harsh shifting (OBD-Codes.com)
  • In many cases, no other symptoms (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Wastegate stuck closed, so the turbo keeps spinning faster (OBD-Codes.com calls this the most likely cause when no other codes are stored)
  2. Defective boost actuator (wastegate actuator) (both sources)
  3. Bent rod between the wastegate actuator and the turbo (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Disconnected, cracked or clogged hose to the wastegate or boost controller (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Faulty boost controller (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Defective boost pressure sensor, or corroded wiring or connectors to it (RepairPal)
  7. Defective knock sensors or their wiring (RepairPal)
  8. Rough-running or misfiring engine (RepairPal)
  9. On Dodge trucks with the Cummins diesel, a MAP sensor that faults intermittently at idle (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes. OBD-Codes.com notes that boost-control parts have their own electrical codes, which point to the cause; with no other codes, the problem is most likely mechanical.
  2. Inspect the wastegate actuator rod at the turbo and make sure it isn't bent.
  3. Check every hose to the wastegate actuator and boost controller for cracks, loose ends or blockages.
  4. Check the boost pressure sensor connector and wiring for corrosion or damage.
  5. If you own a hand vacuum pump, OBD-Codes.com suggests applying vacuum to the wastegate actuator and checking that the rod moves at the pressure listed in your service manual. A rod that doesn't move, or an actuator that won't hold vacuum, points to the actuator or a stuck wastegate.

Typical fixes

  • Repair or replace bent, cracked, loose or clogged wastegate and boost-control hoses
  • Repair a bent wastegate actuator rod
  • Replace a wastegate actuator that is out of spec or won't hold vacuum
  • Repair a wastegate stuck inside the turbo (usually means removing the turbo)
  • Replace a faulty boost controller
  • Repair boost sensor wiring or connectors, or replace the boost pressure sensor once tested bad
  • Repair knock sensor or misfire problems if testing points there

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The engine knocks under load or runs hot; both are signs of possible engine damage
  • The wastegate appears stuck inside the turbo, since that usually means removing the turbo
  • You're about to replace the boost sensor. RepairPal says it's often replaced when the real problem is a bad wastegate, a poor connection or a bad knock sensor

FAQs

Is P0234 serious?

Yes. RepairPal rates it Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com says too much boost can damage the engine or transmission. Get it diagnosed soon and avoid hard acceleration until then.

Can I drive with P0234?

Short, gentle trips to a shop are usually fine if the engine runs normally. Don't tow or drive hard, and stop if you hear knocking or the engine starts to overheat. The car may be in limp mode with reduced power.

What's the difference between P0234 and P0299?

P0234 means boost pressure is too high (overboost). P0299 means boost is lower than the PCM expected (underboost).

Should I replace the boost pressure sensor?

Not before testing. RepairPal says the sensor is often replaced when the real cause is a bad wastegate, a poor connection or a bad knock sensor, and OBD-Codes.com points to the wastegate first.

Why do the sources disagree?

OBD-Codes.com focuses on the mechanical wastegate side (stuck wastegate, bent rod, hoses, boost controller) and lists overheating and harsh shifting as symptoms. RepairPal focuses on the electrical side (boost sensor and wiring, knock sensors, misfires) and says many cars show no symptoms. Both rate it as something to fix soon.
