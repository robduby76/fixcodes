P0709: Transmission Range Sensor Circuit Intermittent (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0709 means the signal from the transmission range sensor, the switch that reports which gear the shifter is in, keeps dropping out or comes and goes. OBD-Codes.com says intermittent signal problems like this are most often caused by wiring, followed by a faulty range sensor; a bad computer is rare.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0709 means

The transmission range sensor (TRS), also called the inhibitor switch, PRNDL input or neutral safety switch, tells the transmission control module (TCM) and powertrain control module (PCM) whether the transmission is in park, reverse, neutral, drive or a lower gear. RepairPal says it is attached directly to the transmission's manual valve. OBD-Codes.com explains that the sensor receives a 12-volt signal from the ignition switch and sends a 12-volt signal back to the PCM/TCM on the circuit for the selected gear position; P0709 sets when that signal is not present all the time or is intermittently missing. It is a generic OBD-II code, though repair steps vary by make and model. The sources word it differently: RepairPal names the code "Circuit Intermittent" and says the PCM sets it when the sensor input is erratic or irrational (the same general wording it uses for the other range sensor codes), while OBD-Codes.com describes an intermittently missing signal but its page heading reuses the "Circuit High" title of P0708, which looks like an error on that page. On the warning light, OBD-Codes.com calls P0709 a type "B" code that turns on the check engine light only after the fault is seen on two consecutive key cycles; RepairPal does not list the check engine light among its symptoms. Related codes are P0705 (circuit malfunction), P0706 (range/performance), P0707 (circuit low) and P0708 (circuit high).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it fixed right away. OBD-Codes.com says the transmission is forced into limp-home (safe) mode, that this can cause severe internal transmission damage, and advises avoiding using the vehicle until it's repaired. RepairPal rates P0709 Urgent. Because the range sensor usually includes the park/neutral safety switch, set the parking brake and keep your foot on the brake whenever you start the engine until it's fixed.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on after the fault is detected on two key cycles in a row (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Obvious lack of power, most noticeable pulling away from a stop, because the transmission starts in third gear in limp-home mode (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission may not shift properly (RepairPal)
  • Increased fuel consumption (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty or damaged range sensor wiring or connector; OBD-Codes.com says wiring is the most common cause of intermittent signal problems (both sources)
  2. Faulty transmission range sensor (both sources)
  3. Rarely, PCM or TCM failure (both sources; OBD-Codes.com calls it rare)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes before clearing anything, since P0709 can appear alongside other transmission codes.
  2. Set the parking brake and block the wheels before testing anything near the shifter or transmission.
  3. Closely inspect the range sensor wiring harness and the internal contacts of the sensor connector for damage, corrosion or looseness, as OBD-Codes.com recommends doing first and thoroughly.
  4. If you find and repair a problem, clear the codes and test drive to see if it returns.
  5. OBD-Codes.com's wiggle test: if the code returns, set a digital multimeter to volts, back-probe the appropriate circuits at the PCM/TCM and wiggle the harness at and leading to the range sensor. If you can't see any intermittent voltage loss, suspect a faulty range sensor. A scan tool with live data is the most accurate way to diagnose it.
  6. Follow your vehicle's service manual for sensor location, wiring diagram and adjustment procedure, since these vary by make.

Typical fixes

  • Repair damaged or loose range sensor wiring or connector terminals
  • Replace a faulty transmission range sensor
  • Replace or reprogram the PCM or TCM only after wiring and the sensor check out

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission is stuck in limp mode or won't go into gear
  • The engine starts with the shifter out of park or neutral
  • You can't reproduce the dropout with a wiggle test but the code keeps coming back
  • You don't have a scan tool that shows live range sensor data

FAQs

Is P0709 serious?

Yes. OBD-Codes.com says limp mode can cause severe internal transmission damage and advises avoiding using the vehicle until it's repaired. RepairPal rates it Urgent.

What's the difference between P0708 and P0709?

OBD-Codes.com says P0708 is set when the range sensor reports two different gear positions at the same time for more than 30 seconds, while P0709 is set when the sensor's signal is intermittently missing. That's why wiring problems are the top suspect for P0709.

Why does my car start off in third gear with P0709?

OBD-Codes.com explains that after the second key cycle with the fault, the computer forces the transmission into limp-home mode, which starts off in third gear and causes an obvious loss of power from a stop.

How much does it cost to fix P0709?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It depends on whether the fix is a wiring repair, a new range sensor or a module, so get a diagnosis or a written estimate first.
