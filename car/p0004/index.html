P0004: Fuel Volume Regulator Control Circuit High (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0004 means the engine computer sees a higher-than-expected electrical reading in the control circuit of the fuel volume regulator on the high-pressure fuel pump. The usual causes are a failed regulator solenoid or a wiring problem such as a short.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0004 means

Vehicles with high-pressure fuel systems (common rail diesels and, according to OBD-Codes.com, gasoline direct injection engines) use a high-pressure pump with a fuel volume regulator that controls fuel pressure at the rail. RepairPal describes the regulator as the part that lets the fuel system release excess fuel back to the tank. OBD-Codes.com says P0004 refers to an electrical reading in the regulator circuit that is above expected parameters; RepairPal says the code sets when the engine computer (PCM) determines the regulator is not sending back the proper voltage signal. OBD-Codes.com says P0004 is not a very common code and is seen more often on diesel and GDI engines. It is a generic OBD2 code, so exact repair steps vary by make and model. Related codes are P0001 (circuit open), P0002 (range/performance) and P0003 (circuit low).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

RepairPal rates P0004 urgent, meaning it should be dealt with right away. OBD-Codes.com gives no rating, but lists a no-start and limp mode (reduced power) among the symptoms, and RepairPal says the engine may stall. Get it diagnosed soon and avoid relying on the vehicle until it is fixed.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Decreased engine performance or lack of power (both sources)
  • Limp mode (reduced power) (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine may not start (OBD-Codes.com) or may stall (RepairPal)
  • Increased fuel consumption (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed fuel volume regulator solenoid (both sources)
  2. Wiring or connector problem in the regulator circuit, such as a short or corrosion (both sources)
  3. Engine computer (PCM) problem, less likely (RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your year, make and model, which can point straight to a known fix (OBD-Codes.com).
  2. Visually inspect the wiring and connectors at the fuel volume regulator for broken or chafed wires, corrosion or loose connections, and repair what you find (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. Do not apply battery voltage directly to the regulator wires; OBD-Codes.com warns this can damage the system.
  4. Check for other stored codes, especially P0001, P0002, P0003 or fuel rail pressure codes such as P0087 to P0093, which help narrow down the fault.

Typical fixes

  • Repair shorted or corroded regulator wiring or connectors (both sources)
  • Replace the fuel volume regulator if it fails testing (both sources); on many engines it is part of or mounted on the high-pressure pump, so check the service manual for your vehicle
  • RepairPal lists fuel pump replacement as the common repair, which fits engines where the regulator is built into the high-pressure pump
  • Suspect the PCM only after the regulator and its circuit check out (RepairPal)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Following the factory service manual's troubleshooting steps for the specific engine (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Checking the regulator's two wires back to the PCM for shorts to voltage, opens and resistance using the factory wiring diagram
  • Comparing commanded and actual fuel rail pressure with a scan tool
  • PCM testing or replacement if everything else checks out

FAQs

Can I keep driving with P0004?

It is not a good idea. RepairPal rates P0004 urgent and says the engine may stall, and OBD-Codes.com lists limp mode, lack of power and a no-start as possible symptoms. Get it diagnosed soon.

What does 'circuit high' mean?

It means the engine computer is seeing a higher electrical reading (voltage) in the fuel volume regulator's control circuit than it expects. A wiring short or a failed regulator solenoid are the usual reasons.

How is P0004 different from P0001, P0002 and P0003?

All four are fuel volume regulator control circuit codes. P0001 points to an open circuit, P0002 to a range or performance problem, P0003 to a circuit reading low and P0004 to a circuit reading high.

Do the sources agree on the details?

Mostly. Both name a failed regulator and wiring as causes. OBD-Codes.com describes P0004 as an electrical reading above expected parameters, while RepairPal says the PCM has decided the regulator is not sending back the proper voltage signal. OBD-Codes.com's page heading also shows 'Range/Performance' (the P0002 wording) above the correct 'Circuit High' definition. RepairPal lists the PCM as a cause, adds stalling and higher fuel use as symptoms, and names fuel pump replacement as the common repair, which OBD-Codes.com does not mention. RepairPal rates it urgent; OBD-Codes.com gives no severity rating. Neither source gives a repair cost.
