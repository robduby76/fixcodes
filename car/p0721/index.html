P0721: Output Speed Sensor Circuit Range/Performance (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0721 means the engine or transmission computer sees a signal from the transmission's output shaft speed sensor that is out of range or doesn't make sense. The computer uses that signal to time shifts and confirm the right gear is engaged, and on some vehicles to drive the speedometer. Both sources list a failed output speed sensor, wiring and the control module; OBD-Codes.com adds fluid, filter, valve body and shift solenoid causes.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0721 means

The output shaft speed sensor reads how fast the transmission's output shaft is turning. OBD-Codes.com says the powertrain control module (PCM) uses it to control the shift solenoids, which change gears at the right time, and that on some vehicles it also drives the speedometer. RepairPal says the sensor tells the transmission control module (TCM) how fast the output shaft spins so it can verify the correct gear is engaged. OBD-Codes.com says P0721 is set by the PCM when the output speed sensor is out of range or not functioning properly. RepairPal says the code is set by the PCM or TCM when the TCM receives an inaccurate or irrational signal, and that this indicates an issue inside the transmission. That is the main disagreement: OBD-Codes.com treats it as a sensor or circuit range problem, while RepairPal reads it as a sign of a problem inside the transmission. It is a generic OBD-II code (OBD-Codes.com lists VW, BMW, Mercedes, Chevrolet, GMC, Allison, Duramax, Dodge, Ram, Ford, Honda, Hyundai and Audi among others), though repair steps vary by make, model and transmission. Related output speed sensor codes are P0720 (circuit malfunction), P0722 (no signal) and P0723 (intermittent).

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it diagnosed soon and drive gently until you do. RepairPal rates P0721 Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe. OBD-Codes.com says it normally starts as moderate but can become severe quickly if it isn't fixed, and lists limp mode, a transmission that won't shift or shifts roughly, and an erratic speedometer. If the transmission won't shift, slips or drops into limp mode, avoid highway driving and have it checked.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Transmission does not shift or shifts roughly (OBD-Codes.com), or may not shift properly (RepairPal)
  • Lower fuel economy (both sources)
  • Limp mode (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Speedometer showing an incorrect or erratic reading (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Possible misfire-like symptoms (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty output speed sensor (both sources)
  2. Damaged wiring (both sources) or a corroded or damaged connector (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Faulty PCM (both sources); RepairPal also lists a faulty TCM
  4. Dirty or contaminated transmission fluid, a clogged transmission filter or restricted hydraulic passages (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Faulty shift solenoid or transmission valve body (OBD-Codes.com)
  6. Faulty engine coolant temperature sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. The sources differ in emphasis: OBD-Codes.com gives a long list including fluid and hydraulic causes, while RepairPal lists only the sensor, wiring and control modules but says an irrational signal points to a problem inside the transmission

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Check for technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle, year and transmission; OBD-Codes.com says this can point you to a known fix.
  2. Check the transmission fluid level and condition, and find out when the fluid and filter were last changed (OBD-Codes.com).
  3. Inspect the wiring and connectors to the output speed sensor, transmission solenoids, transmission pump and PCM for rubbing, bare wires, burn spots, corrosion or damaged pins (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Depending on the vehicle, check the transmission linkage for security and free movement (OBD-Codes.com).
  5. With power removed, check the sensor wiring for continuity; OBD-Codes.com says wiring and connections should normally read 0 ohms unless the service data says otherwise.

Typical fixes

  • Change the transmission fluid and filter, or flush the transmission to clean the passages (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a faulty output speed sensor (both sources list it)
  • Clean corroded connectors or repair or replace damaged wiring (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Repair or replace a faulty shift solenoid or valve body (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Reflash or replace the PCM if everything else checks out (OBD-Codes.com); RepairPal also lists TCM failure as a cause

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The transmission won't shift, slips, or goes into limp mode
  • Advanced testing is vehicle specific and needs a digital multimeter and the factory technical data for your vehicle (OBD-Codes.com)
  • RepairPal says an irrational signal from this sensor indicates an issue inside the transmission, which needs proper diagnosis before parts are replaced
  • OBD-Codes.com warns it is commonly misdiagnosed as an engine misfire, an internal transmission problem or a driveline problem

FAQs

Is P0721 serious?

Treat it as serious. RepairPal rates it Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com says it starts as moderate but can become severe quickly if not fixed. It can stop the transmission from shifting properly or put the car in limp mode.

What's the difference between P0721 and P0722?

P0721 means the output speed sensor signal is out of range or doesn't make sense, while P0722 means the computer is not seeing a signal from that sensor at all (OBD-Codes.com). Both are in the same family as P0720 and P0723.

Can P0721 make my speedometer act up?

Yes. OBD-Codes.com says the output speed sensor may control the speedometer on some vehicles and lists an incorrect or erratic speedometer reading as a symptom.

How much does it cost to fix P0721?

Neither source gives a repair cost. The price depends on whether it's wiring, the sensor, fluid, or a deeper transmission problem, so get a diagnosis or a written estimate before replacing parts.
