P0217: Engine Coolant Over Temperature (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0217 means the engine computer saw the coolant temperature climb into an overheat range. Common causes include low coolant from a leak, a thermostat stuck closed, cooling fans that don't run, a failing water pump, a clogged radiator, or a leaking head gasket.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0217 means

Your engine's computer watches coolant temperature through a sensor in the cooling system. When the coolant gets hotter than the manufacturer's limit, it stores P0217 and turns on the check engine light, and some cars also light an overheat warning. RepairPal sums it up simply: if the engine overheats, P0217 will be stored. OBD-Codes.com says an overheat condition is usually anything above about 240 to 250 degrees F, though the exact limit varies by manufacturer, and that the warning is meant to come on before engine damage occurs. It is a generic OBD-II code, so it applies to most 1996 and newer vehicles, though the repair steps vary by make and model. OBD-Codes.com adds that if you have P0217 but the engine isn't actually overheating, the coolant temperature sender may be faulty.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Stop driving as soon as it's safe. P0217 means the engine has overheated. RepairPal rates it Urgent, meaning it can cause severe damage or be unsafe, and OBD-Codes.com says shutting the engine down as soon as possible should be your first priority. Let the engine cool completely before opening the hood or touching the radiator cap, since hot coolant is under pressure and can cause serious burns. Never add cold water to a hot, empty cooling system.

Common symptoms

  • Engine overheating (RepairPal; OBD-Codes.com describes it as the overheat condition that sets the code)
  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Loss of power (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Coolant pushed out of the reservoir or radiator, or low coolant (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Sweet, acrid smell of leaking coolant (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Air conditioning stops working; OBD-Codes.com says some cars shut the A/C off during an overheat to reduce engine load

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Low coolant, usually from a leak in the cooling system (both sources)
  2. Thermostat stuck closed or faulty (both sources)
  3. Cooling fan(s) not working (both sources)
  4. Faulty water pump, such as a broken impeller (both sources)
  5. Head gasket leaking combustion gases into the cooling system (both sources)
  6. Restriction or debris in the radiator, A/C condenser, or cooling system (OBD-Codes.com)
  7. Towing an overloaded trailer (OBD-Codes.com)
  8. A faulty coolant temperature sender, if the code is set but the engine isn't really overheating (OBD-Codes.com only; RepairPal doesn't list a sensor cause)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Let the engine cool fully, then check the coolant level. OBD-Codes.com stresses checking it only on a cold engine.
  2. If the coolant is low, look for leaks at hoses, the radiator, the water pump and the heater core area. OBD-Codes.com notes an overheated engine can push coolant out of the radiator cap, so a missing leak isn't proof there isn't a problem.
  3. With the engine cool, look for leaves, bugs or debris blocking the radiator and A/C condenser, and make sure the plastic air-guide panels around the radiator are in place.
  4. Check whether the cooling fans run when the engine is warm or the A/C is on.
  5. Read all stored codes; a coolant temperature sensor code alongside P0217 points toward the sensor or its wiring.

Typical fixes

  • Repair the coolant leak and refill and bleed the cooling system
  • Replace the thermostat (OBD-Codes.com recommends replacing it after any overheat, since overheating can damage it)
  • Repair or replace cooling fans, fan relays or wiring
  • Replace a failed water pump
  • Clean or replace a clogged radiator
  • Replace the head gasket if a combustion gas leak test confirms it (RepairPal lists head gasket replacement as a common repair)
  • Replace the coolant temperature sender only if the engine isn't truly overheating and testing confirms it's faulty

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The temperature gauge or warning light comes on again after topping off coolant
  • Coolant keeps disappearing with no visible leak, or there's white exhaust smoke, which can point to a head gasket
  • You need a combustion gas leak test (block test) on the cooling system, as OBD-Codes.com describes
  • The radiator needs to come out for inspection, or the water pump is suspected

FAQs

Is P0217 serious?

Yes. RepairPal rates it Urgent, and OBD-Codes.com says shutting the engine down as soon as possible should be your first priority. Driving an overheating engine can cause severe damage such as a blown head gasket.

Can I drive with P0217?

Not while the engine is overheating. Pull over when it's safe, turn the engine off, and let it cool before checking coolant. Driving on can turn a cheap fix like a thermostat or hose into major engine damage.

What if my engine isn't overheating but I have P0217?

OBD-Codes.com says a faulty coolant temperature sender can set the code without a real overheat. Have the sensor and its readings checked against the actual engine temperature.

How much does it cost to fix P0217?

Neither source gives a repair cost. It depends entirely on the cause, from topping off coolant or a thermostat to a water pump or head gasket job, so get a diagnosis and written estimate first.
