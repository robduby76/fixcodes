P0052: HO2S Heater Control Circuit High (Bank 2, Sensor 1) (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0052 means the engine computer has found a high-side fault in the heater circuit of the front (upstream) oxygen or air/fuel ratio sensor on Bank 2, the side of the engine without cylinder #1. A failed heater inside the sensor is the most common fix, but a shorted heater circuit, broken or frayed wiring, a heater fuse or relay, or (rarely) the engine computer can also set it.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0052 means

Bank 2 Sensor 1 is the oxygen sensor before the catalytic converter on the side of the engine that does not contain cylinder #1, so this code only appears on V-type and flat engines with two banks. On many vehicles this sensor is an air/fuel (A/F) ratio sensor, which is why OBD-Codes.com's generic title reads 'Oxygen (A/F) Sensor Heater Control Circuit High'. The sensor only reads accurately once it is hot, so it has a built-in electric heater. RepairPal says the PCM sets P0052 when it detects that the heater wire is not operating. OBD-Codes.com describes it as the heater circuit reading higher than normal, and says that on some Toyota vehicles the code is triggered at a threshold of 10 A; its article mixes 'resistance' and amps when describing this, so check your service manual for the exact set condition. Nissan's wording, per OBD-Codes.com, is 'Heated oxygen sensor (HO2S) 1 Bank 2, heater voltage high'. P0050 is the general heater circuit code for the same sensor, P0051 is the circuit-low version, and P0031/P0032 are the Bank 1 equivalents.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Usually drivable. RepairPal rates P0052 Moderate: not an immediate safety threat, but get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. OBD-Codes.com says you most likely won't notice anything beyond the check engine light, but a car with an active check engine light will likely fail an OBD2 emissions inspection.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on; OBD-Codes.com says this is most likely the only thing you'll notice
  • Decreased engine performance (RepairPal)
  • Increased fuel consumption (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Failed heater inside the oxygen (A/F) sensor (both sources; RepairPal calls it oxygen sensor failure)
  2. A short in the heater circuit in the sensor (OBD-Codes.com)
  3. Broken or frayed wiring or connectors leading to the sensor and/or heater relay (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal lists a wiring issue)
  4. Engine computer (PCM/ECM) failure (both sources)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes. Owners in OBD-Codes.com forum threads (for example a 2004 Dodge Dakota, 2006 Jeep Wrangler and 2010 Jeep Liberty) report P0052 together with P0032, P0038 and P0058, the 'heater control circuit high' codes for all four sensors. When several sensors set the same code at once, check what they share, such as a heater fuse, relay, feed or ground, before replacing sensors.
  2. Find the right sensor: Bank 2 is the side of the engine without cylinder #1, and Sensor 1 is the one before the catalytic converter. Inspect its wiring and connector for melted, chafed or broken wires, and check the heater fuse and relay if your vehicle has them.
  3. Unplug the sensor and, with the key on, check for 12 volts on the heater feed at the harness connector (OBD-Codes.com).
  4. Check the heater ground circuit for continuity, and measure the resistance and voltage of the wiring against your vehicle's specifications.
  5. Measure the heater resistance across the sensor's heater terminals and compare it with the service manual value; an out-of-spec heater means the sensor needs replacing.

Typical fixes

  • Replace the Bank 2 Sensor 1 oxygen (A/F) sensor; RepairPal lists this as the common repair
  • Repair shorted, broken or frayed wiring and connectors in the heater circuit
  • Replace a failed heater fuse or relay, after finding what caused it
  • Diagnose the engine computer only after the sensor and wiring test good

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The code returns right after a new sensor is fitted, which points to wiring, the heater feed or the computer
  • Several heater-high codes (P0032, P0038, P0052, P0058) set at once and a shared fuse, feed or ground isn't obviously at fault
  • The sensor is seized in the exhaust pipe or hard to reach behind the engine

FAQs

What's the difference between P0032 and P0052?

They describe the same fault on different sides of the engine. P0032 is the Bank 1 Sensor 1 heater circuit high (the side with cylinder #1), and P0052 is the Bank 2 Sensor 1 heater circuit high. OBD-Codes.com notes P0052 is very similar to P0031, P0032 and P0051.

Which sensor do I replace for P0052?

The front (upstream) sensor on Bank 2, before the catalytic converter. OBD-Codes.com's page ends by calling the common fix 'replace the air/fuel (O2 oxygen) sensor #2 on bank 1', which contradicts its own description of the code; the code definition (Bank 2 Sensor 1) is the one to follow, and RepairPal simply lists oxygen sensor replacement.

Do the sources agree on the symptoms?

Not entirely. OBD-Codes.com says you'll most likely notice only the check engine light. RepairPal lists decreased engine performance and increased fuel consumption. Neither treats it as a safety problem.

Should I buy an OEM or aftermarket sensor?

OBD-Codes.com recommends an original-equipment (dealer) sensor, saying aftermarket sensors are more often less reliable. It also says the part may fall under the federal emissions warranty, so ask your dealer.
