P0431: Warm Up Catalyst Efficiency Below Threshold, Bank 2 (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0431 means the engine computer thinks the bank 2 warm-up catalytic converter (the small converter close to the engine that heats up first) is no longer cleaning the exhaust as well as it should. A worn-out or damaged converter is the usual cause, but a faulty oxygen sensor or an exhaust leak can also set it.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0431 means

Many engines have a small warm-up catalytic converter mounted close to the exhaust manifold so it reaches working temperature quickly after a cold start, plus a larger main converter further down the exhaust. RepairPal explains that the engine computer compares the front and rear oxygen sensors to judge converter efficiency: a working converter stores oxygen, so the rear sensor reading stays fairly steady, and if the rear sensor starts to match the front sensor, the computer sets P0431. OBD-Codes.com describes the same thing as the downstream oxygen sensor on bank 2 detecting that the converter is not working as efficiently as specified. Bank 2 is the side of a V6 or V8 engine that does not have cylinder 1, so P0431 only appears on engines with two cylinder banks. P0430 is the more common general catalyst efficiency code for bank 2, P0432 is the main catalyst on bank 2, and P0421 is the warm-up catalyst on bank 1.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Usually drivable short-term, but don't ignore it. Sources differ on how serious it is: RepairPal rates P0431 Urgent, while OBD-Codes.com says you will likely not notice any drivability problems, though a rough or hard idle when cold is possible. RepairPal also notes some cars lose power. The bigger worry is what damaged the converter: RepairPal says catalytic converters usually fail because of something else, such as an engine misfire or an internal oil or coolant leak, so get that found and fixed before it ruins a new converter. If the check engine light is flashing, the engine is misfiring and you should stop driving soon. We treat P0431 as fix soon, not as an emergency.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Often no noticeable drivability problems (both sources)
  • Rough or hard idle when the engine is cold (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Noticeable lack of engine power in some cases (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Worn-out or defective warm-up catalytic converter (both sources)
  2. Engine misfires that have damaged the catalytic converter (RepairPal)
  3. An oxygen sensor that is not reading properly (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. An exhaust leak (OBD-Codes.com)
  5. Internal oil or coolant leaks that contaminate the converter (RepairPal, as a common reason converters fail)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes. If misfire codes (P0300 to P0308) or fuel trim codes are also present, fix those first, because RepairPal says an unrepaired misfire is a common reason converters fail.
  2. Inspect the exhaust for leaks, especially near the bank 2 manifold and oxygen sensors. OBD-Codes.com lists this as the first step.
  3. Test the bank 2 oxygen sensors, especially the rear (downstream) sensor after the converter. OBD-Codes.com suggests measuring its voltage and testing every oxygen sensor while you're at it. With a scan tool on a warm engine, a rear sensor that switches up and down almost as fast as the front sensor suggests the converter is no longer storing oxygen.
  4. Look for signs the engine is burning oil or coolant, such as blue or white exhaust smoke or falling fluid levels.
  5. Check your emissions warranty before paying for a converter. RepairPal cites a federally mandated 8 year, 80,000 mile warranty on catalytic converters, while OBD-Codes.com says many manufacturers give a five year, unlimited mileage warranty on emissions parts. Terms vary, so check your owner's warranty booklet or ask a dealer.

Typical fixes

  • Repair any misfire, oil burning or coolant leak first (RepairPal)
  • Fix any exhaust leak (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace a faulty oxygen sensor if testing confirms it (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Replace the bank 2 warm-up catalytic converter once other causes are ruled out (both sources)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • RepairPal lists common misdiagnoses: replacing oxygen sensors when the warm-up converter is really damaged, and replacing the converter when the real problem is a misfire or internal engine damage raising emissions
  • The car may still be under its emissions warranty, so a dealer diagnosis could save you the cost of a converter
  • The check engine light is flashing, or the engine has lost power
  • You need to pass an emissions or state inspection test

FAQs

Can I drive with P0431?

Usually for a short while. OBD-Codes.com says you will likely not notice drivability problems, apart from a possible rough idle when cold, but RepairPal rates it Urgent and notes some cars lose power. Get it diagnosed soon, and stop driving if the check engine light flashes.

What's the difference between P0430 and P0431?

Both mean bank 2 catalyst efficiency is below the allowed threshold. P0431 specifically names the warm-up catalyst, the small converter close to the engine that heats up first after a cold start, while P0430 is the general bank 2 catalyst code. P0432 names the main catalyst on bank 2.

Does P0431 always mean a new catalytic converter?

No. OBD-Codes.com lists a faulty oxygen sensor and an exhaust leak as possible causes, and RepairPal warns that converters are often replaced when a misfire or internal engine problem is the real cause. Rule those out first.

Is the catalytic converter covered by warranty?

It may be. RepairPal cites a federally mandated 8 year, 80,000 mile warranty on catalytic converters, and notes the problem that caused the failure is usually not covered. OBD-Codes.com mentions five year, unlimited mileage coverage from many manufacturers. Check your own warranty terms.

How much does it cost to fix P0431?

Neither source gives a repair cost for P0431. It ranges from an exhaust leak repair or oxygen sensor to a catalytic converter, so get it diagnosed first.
