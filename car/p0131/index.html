P0131: O2 Sensor Circuit Low Voltage, Bank 1 Sensor 1 (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0131 means the signal from the front (upstream) oxygen sensor on bank 1 has stayed at a low voltage for too long. A worn-out or failed sensor is the most common cause, but wiring faults, exhaust leaks ahead of the sensor, and vacuum or intake leaks can also cause it.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0131 means

Bank 1 Sensor 1 is the upstream oxygen sensor, in front of the catalytic converter, on the side of the engine that contains cylinder 1 (on a four-cylinder engine there is only bank 1). A conventional oxygen sensor's voltage swings between low (lean, extra oxygen in the exhaust) and high (rich), and the engine computer uses those swings to adjust the fuel mixture. When the voltage stays low, the computer sees what looks like a constantly lean exhaust. The two sources describe the trigger differently: OBD-Codes.com says P0131 sets when the sensor's voltage stays low for longer than 2 minutes, while RepairPal says it sets when the voltage stays below 400 millivolts for more than twenty seconds, or when an air-fuel ratio sensor stays in a lean-biased mode too long, and notes the exact criteria vary by make and model. On many newer cars the Sensor 1 position uses a wide-band air-fuel ratio sensor instead of a conventional oxygen sensor, and RepairPal says the same code applies to it. P0137 is the low-voltage code for the downstream sensor (Bank 1 Sensor 2), and P0132 is the high-voltage code for this same sensor.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Usually drivable for now, but get it checked soon. RepairPal rates P0131 Moderate: not an immediate threat to safety or the car, but it should be diagnosed within a reasonable time. OBD-Codes.com says there may be no symptoms beyond the check engine light, while RepairPal lists rough running, stalling and worse fuel economy, so take it more seriously if the engine isn't running right. The front oxygen sensor is what the engine uses to set its fuel mixture, and a fault here can mean higher emissions and a failed emissions test.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Often no other noticeable symptoms (OBD-Codes.com; RepairPal says this happens only in some unusual cases)
  • Rough idle or rough running (RepairPal)
  • Worse fuel economy (RepairPal)
  • Engine stalling (RepairPal)
  • Black smoke or bad-smelling exhaust (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Faulty oxygen sensor or air-fuel ratio sensor (both sources)
  2. Wiring or circuit problem: OBD-Codes.com lists high resistance or an open in the signal circuit, and a short to voltage on the signal circuit; RepairPal lists defective sensor wiring or circuit problems
  3. Faulty sensor heater circuit (RepairPal)
  4. Exhaust leak upstream of the sensor (RepairPal)
  5. Intake air or vacuum leak (RepairPal)
  6. Low fuel pressure (RepairPal)
  7. Faulty engine coolant temperature sensor (RepairPal)
  8. Engine computer needs a software update, or a failed engine computer (RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze-frame data. If P0131 shows up with lean codes such as P0171, or with other oxygen sensor codes, sort those out together rather than replacing the sensor straight away.
  2. Find the Bank 1 Sensor 1 sensor (before the catalytic converter, on the cylinder 1 side) and inspect its wiring and connector for melted insulation, chafing against the exhaust, corrosion or a loose plug. Wiggle the harness with the engine running and watch for changes.
  3. Listen and look for exhaust leaks ahead of the sensor, such as a cracked manifold or leaking gasket; outside air drawn in there can make the sensor read lean.
  4. Check for vacuum and intake leaks: cracked hoses, a loose intake duct after the airflow sensor, or a hissing sound at idle.
  5. With a scan tool showing live data, watch the sensor reading on a warmed-up engine. A conventional sensor should switch up and down repeatedly; one that sits low no matter what you do with the throttle points to the sensor, its circuit, or a real lean condition.

Typical fixes

  • Replace the faulty Bank 1 Sensor 1 oxygen or air-fuel ratio sensor (both sources)
  • Repair a short, open or high resistance in the sensor's signal circuit (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Fix an exhaust leak ahead of the sensor, or an intake or vacuum leak (RepairPal lists these as causes)
  • Address low fuel pressure or a faulty coolant temperature sensor if testing finds them (RepairPal)
  • Have the engine computer's software updated, if the manufacturer has released an update for this problem (RepairPal)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The code comes back after a new sensor is fitted, which suggests wiring, a leak, fuel pressure or the computer
  • You need fuel pressure testing, a smoke test for vacuum leaks, or a scope to check how fast the sensor switches
  • The car has a wide-band air-fuel ratio sensor, which RepairPal says needs different tests from a conventional oxygen sensor
  • The engine stalls or runs badly, since RepairPal lists stalling and rough running among the symptoms

FAQs

Can I drive with P0131?

Usually, for a short while. RepairPal rates it Moderate, and OBD-Codes.com says there may be no symptoms other than the check engine light. If the engine idles rough, stalls or smells rich, get it looked at sooner.

Does P0131 always mean a bad oxygen sensor?

No. A failed sensor is the most common cause, but both sources list wiring problems, and RepairPal also lists exhaust leaks ahead of the sensor, vacuum leaks, low fuel pressure and a faulty coolant temperature sensor. Check those before buying a sensor.

What's the difference between P0131 and P0137?

Both are low-voltage oxygen sensor codes on bank 1. P0131 is the upstream sensor before the catalytic converter (Sensor 1), and P0137 is the downstream sensor after it (Sensor 2).

How much does it cost to fix P0131?

Neither source gives a repair cost for P0131. It depends on whether the fix is a new sensor, a wiring repair, or fixing a leak or fuel pressure problem, so get it diagnosed first.
