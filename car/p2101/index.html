P2101: Throttle Actuator Control Motor Range/Performance (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P2101 means the engine computer (PCM) sees that the electronic throttle is not moving the way it is being told to. RepairPal says the PCM sets it when the throttle is not opening in proportion to the accelerator pedal signal, and may put the engine into limp-home mode. OBD-Codes.com says it is usually an electrical problem in the throttle actuator circuit, but a mechanically stuck throttle blade can also cause it.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P2101 means

On a drive-by-wire engine, the accelerator pedal position (APP) sensor tells the PCM how much power the driver wants, and the PCM opens the throttle plate with an electric motor, per RepairPal. RepairPal says that if the PCM detects the throttle is not opening correctly in proportion to the APP signal, it sets P2101 and may limit engine power (limp-home mode) to prevent unwanted acceleration. OBD-Codes.com describes P2101 as a generic code for engines with electric throttle actuators that shows up more often on certain Ford and Nissan vehicles, and says it is typically set by electrical problems in the throttle actuator circuit, though mechanical issues such as a stuck throttle blade can also set it. The sources lean different ways on causes: OBD-Codes.com points first at wiring, connectors and relays, with the actuator a close second and the PCM unlikely, while RepairPal also lists a failed accelerator pedal position assembly.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

RepairPal puts P2101 in its urgent category, meaning action is needed right away because it can cause severe damage or put the people in the car at risk. OBD-Codes.com says severity is usually severe, because the engine computer cannot fully compensate and often holds the engine at a fixed idle (around 1,000 to 1,200 rpm) with little or no ability to accelerate. If the car won't speed up normally or drops into limp-home mode, get it off the road safely and have it checked rather than driving on.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine stuck at a fixed idle speed, often around 1,000 to 1,200 rpm (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Engine won't accelerate (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Limp-home mode with reduced engine power (RepairPal)
  • Decreased engine performance (RepairPal)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. Open or short in the wiring or connectors to the throttle actuator, including relays and relay connections (OBD-Codes.com says likely; RepairPal lists wiring issues)
  2. Failed throttle actuator or throttle control motor, electrically or mechanically, such as a stuck throttle blade (both sources)
  3. Failed accelerator pedal position (APP) assembly (RepairPal)
  4. Less often: failed PCM (both sources; OBD-Codes.com says unlikely)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Look up technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your year, make, model and engine, as OBD-Codes.com recommends as the first step.
  2. Find the throttle body and inspect its connector and wiring for rubbing, bare wires, burn spots or melted plastic.
  3. Unplug the connector and check the terminals for burning or green corrosion. OBD-Codes.com suggests electrical contact cleaner and a plastic bristle brush, then dielectric grease once dry.
  4. With the engine off, check whether the throttle blade is stuck or packed with carbon.
  5. Clear the code and see if it returns. OBD-Codes.com says if it stays away, the connections were most likely the problem.

Typical fixes

  • Repair damaged wiring, connectors or relay connections in the throttle actuator circuit
  • Replace the throttle body or throttle actuator if it tests open or shorted or is mechanically stuck
  • Replace the accelerator pedal position assembly if it is found faulty
  • Replace or reprogram the PCM only after other checks pass (a replacement PCM must be programmed to the vehicle, per OBD-Codes.com)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com's test sequence: with the harness unplugged, measure actuator motor resistance against the vehicle's spec (open or zero ohms means replace the actuator), confirm 12 V on the power supply circuit while commanding the actuator with a scan tool, then use a test light on the ground circuit while actuating it and wiggle the harness to catch an intermittent connection
  • If every test passes and P2101 keeps returning, OBD-Codes.com says the throttle actuator is most likely at fault, though a failed PCM can't be ruled out until the actuator has been replaced
  • The sources differ on emphasis: OBD-Codes.com ranks wiring and connectors as the most common cause with the actuator a close second, while RepairPal lists the APP assembly, PCM, throttle control motor and wiring without ranking them; severity is urgent (RepairPal) and usually severe (OBD-Codes.com)
  • RepairPal's general advice for this code: most trouble codes come from a sensor reporting an out-of-range value, and just replacing the sensor may not fix the underlying issue

FAQs

Can I drive with P2101?

Not safely for long. RepairPal rates it urgent and OBD-Codes.com says usually severe. The engine may sit at a fixed idle or limp-home mode with little ability to accelerate, so get it diagnosed soon.

Does P2101 mean I need a new throttle body?

Not always. OBD-Codes.com says connectors, wiring and relays are the most common problem area, with the actuator a close second. Inspect and clean the connections and see if the code returns before buying parts.

What's the difference between P2101 and P2100?

Both involve throttle actuator A's control motor circuit. P2100 is the circuit open code, while P2101 means the circuit or throttle is not performing in the expected range, per OBD-Codes.com and RepairPal's code lists.
