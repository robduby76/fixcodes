P2422: EVAP Vent Valve Stuck Closed (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 4 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P2422 means the engine computer thinks the EVAP vent valve, which lets fresh air into the fuel vapor system, is stuck closed. RepairPal lists a failed vent valve, wiring problems and the engine computer as the common causes. Honda's service information and bulletins add a very common one on Hondas: dirt clogging the canister vent or drain tube.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P2422 means

The EVAP system stores fuel vapor from the tank in a charcoal canister and later draws it into the engine to be burned. The vent valve (Honda calls it the EVAP canister vent shut valve) is normally open so fresh air can enter the system, and it closes only when the engine computer seals the system to test it for leaks. Honda's Civic service manual explains that when purging starts, the fuel tank pressure sensor should read near atmospheric pressure. If it instead sees too much vacuum, the vent path is blocked or the valve is stuck closed, and P2422 is set. On the Civic it takes two drive cycles in a row before the light comes on, and the light goes off by itself after three trips without the fault. RepairPal describes the trigger differently, saying the code sets when the computer sees the system venting when it should not. That wording matches "stuck open" (P2421) more than "stuck closed", so for this code the factory description of excessive vacuum is the clearer one.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Drive with caution, fix soon

Not an emergency, but get it diagnosed within a reasonable time. RepairPal rates P2422 as Moderate. The vent valve is part of the fuel vapor (EVAP) system, so the car usually keeps running, but the check engine light will keep you from passing an emissions inspection in most areas.

Common symptoms

  • Check engine light on
  • Usually no drivability symptoms, because the vent valve is part of the fuel vapor system
  • Failed emissions or state inspection while the light is on
  • On some Hondas, P1454 (fuel tank pressure sensor range) may be stored with it, since the computer can't always tell the two faults apart at engine start (Honda Civic manual)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. A clogged EVAP canister vent or drain tube, often from dirt and dust (Honda Civic manual; Honda bulletins 20-056 and 20-002)
  2. A failed EVAP vent valve that is stuck closed (RepairPal, Honda Civic manual)
  3. Damaged, corroded or loose wiring to the vent valve (RepairPal)
  4. A failed engine computer, which is rare (RepairPal)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data. Fix any fuel tank pressure sensor codes first, since the Honda test depends on that sensor reading correctly.
  2. Check for technical service bulletins for your vehicle. Honda has bulletins for P2422 on the 2016 to 2022 Pilot, 2019 to 2022 Passport and 2017 to 2022 Ridgeline.
  3. Find the charcoal canister and vent valve (usually near the fuel tank) and inspect the vent or drain tube. Honda's bulletin says to disconnect the rubber drain tube and look for dust or dirt by inspecting and tapping it.
  4. Inspect the vent valve's connector and wiring for damage, corrosion and looseness.
  5. After a repair, clear the code and drive normally. The test runs once per drive cycle, so confirm the light stays off over a few days of driving.

Typical fixes

  • Clean or replace a clogged vent or drain tube. On affected Hondas, the bulletin fix is to replace the canister assembly and install a canister drain kit with a filter box (Honda bulletins 20-056 and 20-002)
  • Replace the EVAP vent valve (RepairPal, Honda Civic manual)
  • Repair damaged wiring or connectors (RepairPal)
  • Repair or replace the engine computer only after everything else checks out (RepairPal)

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • You need a scan tool that can command the vent valve or read fuel tank pressure
  • The canister sits in a hard-to-reach spot (Honda's Ridgeline bulletin has AWD trucks lowering the rear differential to reach it)
  • The code returns after you clear a clogged tube or replace the valve
  • You need the light off for an inspection deadline

FAQs

Is P2422 serious?

Not usually for driving. RepairPal rates it Moderate. The car typically runs normally, but the EVAP system isn't working as designed and the check engine light can fail an emissions inspection, so get it diagnosed soon.

Why is P2422 so common on Honda Pilots and Ridgelines?

Honda's bulletins say dirt can clog the EVAP canister vent tube on the 2016 to 2022 Pilot, 2019 to 2022 Passport and 2017 to 2022 Ridgeline. The fix in those bulletins is to replace the canister and add a drain kit with a filter box. Check with a Honda dealer whether the bulletin applies to your vehicle.

What's the difference between P2421 and P2422?

P2421 means the vent valve is stuck open and P2422 means it's stuck closed. Honda's manual detects stuck closed when the fuel tank pressure sensor sees too much vacuum after purging starts.

How much does it cost to fix P2422?

None of these sources give a repair cost. It depends on whether it's a clogged tube, a vent valve or a whole canister, so get a written estimate after diagnosis.
