P0611: Fuel Injector Control Module Performance (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 2 cited source(s)

⚡ Quick answer

P0611 means the engine computer has found a problem with the part of the system that drives the fuel injectors. OBD-Codes.com describes it as an internal performance fault in the portion of the PCM that controls the injectors (usually built into the PCM), while RepairPal describes a separate fuel injector control module (FICM) sending an incorrect signal that the PCM picks up. Both list a failed module, wiring and power or ground problems as causes.

🚨 Safety first

A flashing check engine light means a severe misfire: slow down and stop driving soon. A red brake or oil pressure warning needs immediate attention. Pull over safely and don't keep driving.

What P0611 means

Fuel injectors are fired by a driver circuit, sometimes called the fuel injector controller or fuel injector control module (FICM). OBD-Codes.com explains that this controller is most often integrated into the powertrain control module (PCM), but on some vehicles it is a separate module. Each time the ignition is turned on, the PCM runs self-tests and uses the controller area network (CAN) to compare signals between modules; if it finds that fuel injector function can't be controlled effectively, it stores P0611 and may turn on the check engine light, sometimes only after several failed cycles. RepairPal describes the same code from the separate-module side: the FICM controls the signal to the injectors, and the PCM sets P0611 when it detects an incorrect signal from the FICM. The code is generic to OBD-II vehicles (1996 and newer); OBD-Codes.com names Ford Powerstroke diesels, Hyundai and Toyota among the makes that use it, and search interest also shows people looking it up for Mercedes and Alfa Romeo, so check technical service bulletins (TSBs) for your vehicle.

Severity: can I keep driving?

Stop and fix promptly

Get it checked right away. RepairPal rates P0611 'Urgent', meaning immediate action is needed because it can cause severe damage or put people at risk, and lists 'engine may not start' and 'engine may stall' as symptoms. OBD-Codes.com says internal control module codes must always be taken seriously and that P0611 can cause drivability problems up to a no-start. If the engine stalls or runs badly, don't keep driving it; have it towed.

Common symptoms

  • Engine won't start (both sources)
  • Engine stalls (RepairPal)
  • Drivability problems or reduced engine performance (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Lower fuel economy (OBD-Codes.com)
  • Check engine light, often with other stored codes (OBD-Codes.com)

Likely causes

Roughly most to least common, based on the sources below. Your vehicle may differ.

  1. A failed PCM (both sources)
  2. A failed fuel injection control module (FICM) on vehicles that have a separate one (RepairPal)
  3. A PCM programming error (OBD-Codes.com)
  4. Open or shorted wiring or connectors in the CAN harness, or other wiring problems (both sources)
  5. A failed power supply to the control module, or a poor module ground (OBD-Codes.com)

Diagnostic steps you can do

⚠ Before you start

Work on a cool engine, parked on level ground with the parking brake set. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

  1. Read all stored codes and freeze frame data and write them down. OBD-Codes.com says any ECM/PCM power supply codes need to be repaired before you diagnose P0611.
  2. Check the fuses and relays that feed the PCM (and the FICM if your vehicle has one). OBD-Codes.com says to test fuses with the circuit loaded.
  3. Inspect the module wiring and harnesses, and check the chassis and engine ground connections for corrosion or looseness.
  4. Look the modules over for signs of water, heat or collision damage. OBD-Codes.com says a controller damaged by water should be considered defective.
  5. Clear the code and test drive. If it doesn't come back, it may be intermittent, which OBD-Codes.com says makes it harder to diagnose.
  6. Look up TSBs for your year, make, model and engine that mention P0611.

Typical fixes

  • Repair a blown fuse, failed relay or bad power supply to the module
  • Clean and repair poor module grounds
  • Repair damaged CAN or module wiring and connectors
  • Reprogram the PCM if a programming error is found
  • Replace and program the FICM or PCM if it has failed

Repair costs vary widely by vehicle, region, parts brand and shop, so we don't quote prices. Get a written estimate after diagnosis.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • OBD-Codes.com says that, unlike most codes, P0611 is likely caused by a defective controller or a controller programming error, and that even well-equipped technicians can find it a challenge to diagnose. Rule out power and ground problems before condemning a module.
  • A replacement controller has to be programmed. OBD-Codes.com says some can be bought pre-programmed from aftermarket sources, while others need on-board programming that only a dealer or other qualified shop can do.
  • The sources describe the code differently: OBD-Codes.com treats it as an internal fault in the PCM's injector-control section (noting a separate controller exists on some vehicles), while RepairPal treats it as the PCM receiving an incorrect signal from a separate FICM and lists FICM failure first. Which applies depends on whether your vehicle has a separate injector control module.

FAQs

Can I drive with P0611?

It's not a good idea. RepairPal rates it 'Urgent' and says the engine may stall or not start, and OBD-Codes.com says internal control module codes must always be taken seriously. Get it diagnosed soon.

Does P0611 mean the FICM is bad?

Often the module is the problem, but not always. RepairPal lists FICM failure first, then PCM failure and wiring, and OBD-Codes.com says to check fuses, relays, power and grounds before declaring any controller defective.

Can I just swap in a used PCM or FICM?

Not without programming. OBD-Codes.com says controller replacement requires reprogramming, and without the right equipment a replacement won't complete the repair.

What codes are related to P0611?

Other internal control module codes such as P0606 and P0607 can appear with it, and OBD-Codes.com says ECM/PCM power supply codes should be fixed first.
