16x25x2🌬 2-inch HVAC filter

16x25x2 Air Filter: Actual Size, MERV Guide & Replacement

Reviewed 2026-10-22 · 8 sources

⚡ Quick answer

A 16x25x2 filter is the nominal size; it actually measures about 15.5 x 24.5 x 1.75 inches (15 1/2 x 24 1/2 x 1 3/4) per Filterbuy. Match all three numbers, especially the 2-inch depth, pick MERV 8–13 for your system, and check it monthly.

Nominal vs actual size

Nominal (order this)16 x 25 x 2 in
Actual15.5 x 24.5 x 1.75 in

A 2-inch-deep version of the very common 16x25 size, used in furnace and air-handler racks built for a thicker filter. Filterbuy's 16x25x2 product page lists the actual size as 15.5 x 24.5 x 1.75, and its brand comparison shows Filtrete and Nordic Pure 16x25x2 filters at the same 15.5 x 24.5 x 1.75, so switching between those brands is a direct swap. The depth is what matters most here: a 16x25x1 (about 0.75 inch deep) leaves a gap in a 2-inch rack, and a 16x25x4 won't fit at all. Filters are deliberately cut smaller than their nominal label so they slide into the slot; length and width typically run about 1/4 to 1/2 inch under nominal. Actual sizes vary slightly by brand, so compare against the small print on your old frame.

How to measure

  1. Turn the system off at the thermostat (and the service switch) before pulling the filter.
  2. Read the frame first: the large print is the nominal size (what you order); small print, if present, is the actual size.
  3. If there is no label, measure length, width and depth edge to edge with a tape measure.
  4. Round each measurement up to the nearest whole inch to get the nominal size (e.g. 19.5 x 24.5 x 0.75 → 20x25x1).
  5. No old filter? Measure the filter slot or cabinet opening the same way.
  6. Note the airflow arrow: it should point toward the furnace or blower.

⚠ When higher MERV can restrict airflow

EPA notes that higher-MERV filters tend to restrict airflow, and recommends using the highest rating your system fan and filter slot can accommodate. Most systems handle MERV 13 if the filter is replaced frequently. Thin 1-inch slots are the most restrictive; deeper 4-inch media filters generally create less static pressure. Weak airflow from vents, a system that runs longer, or a filter that bows are signs to step down a grade or ask an HVAC technician.

How often to replace

ENERGY STAR: check your filter every month and change it when it looks dirty, at least every three months. Pets, dust, renovations and heavy heating/cooling seasons can mean more frequent changes.

FAQs

What is the actual size of a 16x25x2 air filter?

16x25x2 is the nominal (ordering) size. Filterbuy lists its 16x25x2 at an actual 15.5 x 24.5 x 1.75 inches (15 1/2 x 24 1/2 x 1 3/4), and its brand comparison shows Filtrete and Nordic Pure 16x25x2 filters at the same dimensions. Still check the small print on your old frame if you're switching to another brand.

My old filter says 15 1/2 x 24 1/2 x 1 3/4. Is that a 16x25x2?

Yes. Round each measurement up to the nearest whole inch: 15 1/2 becomes 16, 24 1/2 becomes 25 and 1 3/4 becomes 2, which gives the nominal size 16x25x2.

Can I use a 16x25x1 in a 16x25x2 slot?

Not well. A 16x25x1 is only about 0.75 inch deep, so in a 2-inch rack it leaves a gap that lets air bypass the filter. Depth must match the slot, and stacking two 1-inch filters isn't a substitute for one 2-inch filter.

Is a 16x25x2 better than a 16x25x1?

Only if your system has a 2-inch slot. A 2-inch filter has more media depth (about 1.75 inches versus 0.75), so it holds more dust between changes, but you have to use the depth your filter rack was built for.

How often should I replace a 16x25x2 filter?

ENERGY STAR says to check your filter monthly and change it when it is dirty, at least every three months. Filterbuy says most homes change a 16x25x2 every 60 to 90 days; pets, dust or construction may mean more frequent changes.

Is MERV 13 safe for my system?

EPA says most furnaces and HVAC systems can accommodate a MERV 13 filter without equipment problems if it is replaced frequently, but higher-MERV filters do tend to restrict airflow. If you notice weak airflow or are unsure, ask an HVAC technician.

Which way does the arrow go?

Per ENERGY STAR, the airflow arrow on the filter frame should point toward the furnace or blower unit.
