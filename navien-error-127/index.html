Code: E127 / 127🚿 Navien Tankless Water Heater

Navien Error Code 127 (E127): Air Pressure Sensor (APS) Fault

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

E127 on a Navien means the control board isn't getting a usable signal from the air pressure sensor (APS), the part that tells the board how much combustion air is moving through the unit. Navien's NPN manual describes it as "Air volume Open/Short/Default value/No answer," meaning the sensor circuit reads as broken, shorted, stuck at its default value, or silent. There's no owner fix in Navien's chart: try one reset, check that the vent and intake aren't blocked, and if E127 comes back, call Navien Technical Support or a licensed technician to check the sensor, its wiring and tubing, the fan and the board.

What the code means

Navien's NPN User's Information Manual (NPN-160U/180U/199U and NPN-120E/160E/180E/199E) lists E127 as "Abnormal operation: Air volume Open/Short/Default value/No answer," with the action "Contact Technical Support at 1-800-519-8794." Navien's NFB-C commercial boiler manual lists the same code number as "Abnormal operation: APS," which confirms that the "air volume" signal comes from the air pressure sensor. The APS is one of the safety devices Navien lists for the NPN, and the NFB-C manual shows both a target APS voltage and the current APS voltage in its information menu, so the board expects a specific voltage from it. In plain terms, E127 is a sensor-signal fault: the reading is missing, impossible (open or shorted), or never moved from its starting value. That's different from E110, which Navien lists as abnormal air pressure on the NPN (the sensor works but reports too little airflow, usually a blocked vent), and from E157, abnormal air volume control, which is also its own code in the NPN chart.

Likely causes

  • A failed air pressure sensor (APS) that reads open or shorted
  • A loose, unplugged or damaged wire or connector between the APS and the main control board (PCB)
  • A cracked, kinked, disconnected or water-filled pressure tube going to the APS, which can leave the sensor stuck at its default reading (a technician check; Navien's manuals don't spell out the tubing as a cause)
  • A combustion fan problem. Navien's manuals don't list this for E127, but the APS reads the air the fan moves, and one NPN-160U owner on JustAnswer reported Navien Tech Support sending both an air pressure switch and a new fan assembly for code 127.
  • Less often, a faulty main control board (PCB) that can't read the sensor

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Reset once. Navien's NPN manual says to press the Reset button on the front panel; on outdoor models, press the OK button once so "RST" shows on the display.
  2. Check outside that the vent and air intake terminations aren't blocked by snow, ice, leaves or a nest. That's Navien's step for the related E110 code, and it's safe to look without taking anything apart.
  3. If E127 comes back after a reset, stop resetting. Navien's chart gives no owner fix for E127 and says to contact Technical Support at 1-800-519-8794.
  4. Have the model number, serial number and error code ready when you call, and ask whether the unit is still under warranty before paying for parts.
  5. If you smell gas or a CO alarm goes off, leave the house and call your gas company or 911 from outside.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Reading the current APS voltage against the target value in the unit's information or diagnostic menu, where the model offers one (the NFB-C manual lists both)
  • Checking the APS connector and harness to the main board for loose pins, corrosion or damage
  • Inspecting the APS pressure tube for cracks, kinks, water or a loose fitting
  • Checking fan operation and RPM, since the APS reads the airflow the fan creates
  • Replacing the air pressure sensor (the NPN parts list shows it as a separate part) and, if the error continues, the fan or main control board
  • Confirming the vent, intake and condensate drain are clear and correctly installed before returning the unit to service
