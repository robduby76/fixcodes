Code: 156❄️ Trane HVAC System

Trane / American Standard Error 156: High Superheat Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Err 156 (156.00 or 156.01) on a Trane or American Standard communicating system means the control measured high superheat, which usually points to the system being low on refrigerant or a restriction in the liquid line. Trane's guide says the system is still allowed to run on 156.00, so this is a warning rather than a shutdown, but it shouldn't be ignored. A sensor that's out of calibration or an indoor expansion valve (EEV) problem can also set it. Checking charge and restrictions needs a technician with gauges.

What the code means

Superheat is how many degrees the refrigerant vapor is heated above its boiling point by the time it leaves the coil; too much superheat means the coil is being starved of refrigerant. Trane's System Alert Guide lists two versions. ERR.156.00, High Indoor Superheat Error, means superheat above 30 degrees was detected by the indoor unit for 5 consecutive cooling cycles, and system operation is allowed. ERR.156.01, High Superheat Error, means superheat above 50 degrees was detected by the outdoor unit for more than 20 minutes in cooling or heating mode. Trane's alert code table for its communicating controls (CL2 codes) shows 156.00 as a Major alert displayed as SYSTEM1 ERR with the same 30-degree, 5-cycle rule, and 156.01 as a Normal alert displayed as LOW CHARGE, triggered by superheat of 35 degrees or more for more than 60 minutes. The two Trane documents give different thresholds for 156.01, so the exact numbers depend on your equipment and control version. Related alerts: 155 is an EEV motor fault, and 164 covers the indoor EEV stuck at its minimum or open position.

Likely causes

  • System low on refrigerant charge, usually from a leak (Trane lists this first for both 156.00 and 156.01)
  • A restricted liquid line or a clogged filter drier
  • Indoor EEV (electronic expansion valve) motor not seated properly on the valve, or the wrong EEV for the unit
  • Indoor coil temperature or gas temperature sensor out of range
  • Outdoor suction temperature sensor out of calibration, mounted in the wrong place, loose or uninsulated (for 156.01)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels.

  1. Write down the exact alert (156.00 or 156.01) and whether the system was cooling or heating; the technician will want both.
  2. Check how the system is performing. If it still cools or heats normally, 156.00 lets it keep running, but schedule service rather than clearing the alert and forgetting it.
  3. Replace a dirty air filter and make sure registers and returns are open. This won't fix a charge problem, but it removes airflow as a variable before the technician arrives.
  4. Look at the refrigerant lines at the outdoor unit. Note any oily spots, frost or ice on the lines or coil, or a hissing sound, and tell the technician.
  5. If the alert returns or the house isn't reaching temperature, call for service. A system that is low on charge loses capacity and can overheat the compressor over time, and refrigerant work needs a licensed technician.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Any Err 156 that comes back, since the usual causes (low charge, a restriction) don't fix themselves.
  • The system runs but can't keep up, or you see ice on the lines or coil.
  • The display reads LOW CHARGE.
  • A technician should measure superheat and subcooling against the unit's Service Facts, leak-check the system if charge is low, check the liquid line and filter drier for a restriction, confirm the indoor EEV motor is seated and is the correct valve, compare the coil and gas temperature sensor resistance to the chart in the literature, and check the outdoor suction sensor's calibration, location, mounting and insulation.
