Carrier Furnace Code 23: Pressure Switch Did Not Open (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-15 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Carrier furnace code 23 means the furnace control saw a pressure switch already closed when a heat call started, when it should still be open. The usual suspects are obstructed pressure tubing or a switch that is stuck closed. The control keeps flashing 23 until the switch opens. Testing it involves live 24-volt checks, so call a licensed technician if the code stays.

What the code means

Carrier's troubleshooting guides define status code 23 as "PRESSURE SWITCH DID NOT OPEN". On the 58MVP / 58MVB / 58UVB variable-speed guide (58-06T), it means the low-heat or high-heat pressure switch (LPS or HPS) is already made when a call for heat starts. The control flashes code 23 until the switch opens, and only then does the heating cycle begin. The 2-stage guide (58-3T) uses the same idea for the low-heat pressure switch (LPS): it is made at the start of a heat call, and the control flashes 23 until it opens. Carrier's service label tells the technician to check for obstructed pressure tubing and a pressure switch stuck closed. This is different from codes 31 and 32, where a pressure switch fails to close or reopens during a run. With 23, the problem is that the switch is closed when it should not be, before the inducer has started.

Likely causes

  • Obstructed or kinked pressure tubing (Carrier lists this first)
  • A pressure switch stuck closed (Carrier's checkout replaces the pressure switch assembly if it is wired correctly)
  • Pressure switch wiring that does not match the wiring diagram (the 58-06T procedure checks for a miswired or incorrectly wired LPS or HPS)
  • On the variable-speed 58-06T guide, an inducer motor that is already running, or a faulty furnace control (the guide's last steps replace the inducer assembly or the control)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Note the exact code first. On Carrier boards, 23 is 2 short flashes and 3 long flashes in the guide's counting rule (first digit short flashes, second digit long flashes). Don't confuse it with a status word such as CL or CFn.
  2. Turn the thermostat off and switch the furnace off at its service switch or breaker before looking at anything.
  3. With the power off, look at the small rubber pressure tubing running from the inducer housing to the pressure switch. Check that it is attached at both ends, not kinked, cracked or collapsed, and that no water, spider webs or debris are blocking it.
  4. Restore power and call for heat once. If 23 comes back right away, stop. Further testing means live 24-volt measurements across the switch, which is not homeowner work.
  5. Never bypass or jumper a pressure switch. It is a safety device that proves the venting is working.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Code 23 comes back after you have checked the tubing. Carrier's checkout needs a meter on the switch wiring and the inducer.
  • The pressure switch assembly needs replacing, or the wiring has to be corrected against the wiring diagram.
  • The inducer motor runs when it should be off, or the control board may be at fault.
  • You smell gas, see scorching or soot, or the furnace has repeated vent problems. Leave and call your gas utility or a technician.
  • Do not jumper the pressure switch to get heat. That removes a venting safety.
