Smeg Washing Machine E01 Error Code: Door Not Shut Properly

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

E01 on a Smeg washing machine means the machine thinks the door isn't shut properly. Smeg's fix is to shut the door firmly until you hear it click; if the code keeps coming back, Smeg says to turn the machine off, unplug it and call an authorised service agent. In practice the usual culprits are laundry caught in the door seal, a worn or bent door hook, or a door lock (interlock) that has stopped working.

What the code means

Smeg's washing machine user manual (for example the LBW50CIT, in its "Automatic fault warnings and what to do" section) lists E01 as "the door of your machine is not shut properly". Machines without a numeric display show it as a combination of flashing wash-progress lights (Wash, Rinse, Spin, End) rather than as text. Smeg's action is to shut the door properly until you hear a click, and if the machine still shows the fault, to turn it off, unplug it and contact the nearest authorised service agent. The same manual's operating section says to check that no laundry is trapped between the rubber seal and the door and to push the door gently until it clicks, because the programme won't start unless the door is fully closed. Smeg UK's help page puts E01 in the group of codes that need an engineer visit once the simple check hasn't cleared it. Neighbouring codes help narrow things down: E02 is low water pressure or water level, E03 is a pump or pump filter problem, and E04 is too much water in the machine. If your display shows OPEN instead, Smeg UK says that also just means the door needs closing properly.

Likely causes

  • Door not pushed fully shut, so it never clicks home (Smeg's first check)
  • A sock, sleeve or other laundry caught between the door and the rubber seal
  • Door hook on the door bent, worn or loose so it doesn't engage the lock
  • Faulty door lock (interlock) that no longer reports the door as closed
  • Loose connection in the door lock wiring or a control board fault (an engineer's job, per Smeg)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance (or switch off its breaker) and shut off the water supply valves before removing panels or disconnecting hoses. Keep towels handy for residual water.

  1. Open the door and check the rubber seal all the way round for clothing caught in it, then push the door gently until you hear it click.
  2. Turn the machine off, unplug it for a minute or two, plug it back in and start the programme again. This clears a one-off E01 that came from the door not being fully shut.
  3. With the machine unplugged, look at the hook on the door. If it's bent, cracked or loose, or the door has dropped on its hinges so the hook misses the slot, that part needs replacing or the door needs realigning.
  4. Never force the door open while the machine is running; Smeg says it unlocks shortly after the cycle ends.
  5. If E01 comes back with the door properly shut and nothing in the seal, stop there: Smeg's instruction is to turn it off, unplug it and call an authorised service agent.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The door clicks shut and nothing is caught in the seal, but E01 still comes back.
  • The door lock clicks but the machine never starts, or the door won't lock at all.
  • You'd need to remove the front panel or door seal to reach the lock, which means working near mains-voltage wiring.
  • The machine is under warranty: Smeg UK lists 0330 094 7779 for in-warranty repairs, and it also has a find-an-engineer service for out-of-warranty machines.
