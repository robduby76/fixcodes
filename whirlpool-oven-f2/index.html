Code: F2♨️ Whirlpool Oven

Whirlpool Oven F2 Error Code: Temperature Sensor or Keypad, Depending on the Control (Meaning, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

A plain F2 on a Whirlpool range or wall oven (no E number after it) usually points to the oven temperature sensor circuit. Whirlpool's own help pages say there may be a problem with the oven temperature sensor, the control, or the associated wiring, and on those controls F2 also covers an oven that ran too hot. On some older Whirlpool controls, though, F2 is the keypad group (shown with an E code like F2 E0 or F2 E1). Start with Whirlpool's one-minute breaker reset; if F2 comes back, test the oven sensor first.

What the code means

Whirlpool's Product Help pages for F2 on both ranges and wall ovens give the same answer: there may be a problem with the oven temperature sensor, the control, or the associated wiring, and the first step is to turn off the circuit breaker for one minute, turn it back on, and watch for a minute to make sure the code doesn't return. On Whirlpool controls that use a single F2 code this way, a Whirlpool diagnostic and temperature calibration tech sheet (model YKEBS278DW3, as quoted by MyPros+) defines F2 as the oven temperature sensor open or shorted, or the oven temperature too high: over 575°F (301°C) in bake or over 950°F (510°C) in self-clean. Its repair step is to check the sensor connection, measure the sensor at about 1,080 ohms at 70°F (adding about 2 ohms per degree), replace the sensor if the reading is wrong, and if the sensor is fine, inspect the control for welded relay contacts that keep the oven heating. Other Whirlpool electronic oven controls split things differently. On tech sheet 8302072, for example, F2 is the keypad group (E0 key held too long or shorted, E1 keypad tail not connected, E5/E6 Cancel key circuit), and the sensor and over-temperature faults are F3 E0 to F3 E3 instead. Newer Whirlpool ovens that show F2 E0, F2 E1 or F2 E2 are pointing at the keypad; see our F2 E1 page for those. So check whether your display shows an E number after F2, and go by the tech sheet tucked behind your range or under the control panel.

Likely causes

  • An oven temperature sensor (the thin probe at the back of the oven cavity) that has failed open or shorted
  • A loose, corroded or heat-damaged sensor connector or harness. Whirlpool's tech sheets say connector corrosion is the most common cause of control trouble.
  • The oven actually ran too hot (over 575°F in bake or 950°F in self-clean), for example because a relay on the control welded closed and kept the element on
  • A faulty electronic oven control
  • On some older Whirlpool controls, a keypad problem instead: a stuck or shorted key or a disconnected keypad ribbon (these usually show an E code with the F2)
  • A one-time glitch that a breaker reset clears

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before opening panels.

  1. Write down exactly what the display shows. A plain F2 usually means the sensor circuit; F2 with an E number (F2 E0, F2 E1, F2 E2) means the keypad.
  2. Do Whirlpool's reset: turn off the range or oven breaker(s) for one minute, turn them back on, and watch the display for one minute. If F2 stays away, run a normal bake and check the oven heats to temperature.
  3. If F2 returns, or the oven seemed to overheat, leave the breaker off. Whirlpool's tech sheet links this code to welded relays that can keep the oven heating.
  4. With power off and the oven cool, look at the sensor probe at the back of the oven. Make sure it isn't bent, burned or touching the oven wall.
  5. If you're comfortable with a multimeter: remove the sensor's two mounting screws, gently pull it forward, unplug its connector and measure across the two pins. Whirlpool lists about 1,080 ohms at 70°F, rising about 2 ohms per degree. An open reading or near zero means the sensor is bad.
  6. If the sensor reads correctly, check its connector and harness for loose, burned or corroded pins, then reconnect firmly.
  7. Replace the sensor with the part for your model number, restore power and run a bake test.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • F2 comes back after the reset and a good-testing (or new) sensor. The control may have a welded relay and need replacing.
  • The oven kept heating with the code showing or got far hotter than set. Keep the breaker off until it's checked.
  • The sensor connector or wiring is burned behind the oven or inside the control panel.
  • You aren't comfortable pulling the range out, removing panels or testing a 240-volt appliance.
  • The oven is under warranty. Whirlpool's help pages list 1-866-698-2538 for service.
