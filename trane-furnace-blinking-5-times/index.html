Code: 5 flashes🔥 Trane Gas Furnace

Trane Furnace Blinking 5 Times: Flame Sensed When No Flame Should Be Present (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-14 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

A red LED blinking 5 times then pausing on a Trane (or American Standard) furnace control board means the control senses a flame when there should not be one. The board treats that as a safety fault. Trane's help center describes it as flame sensed with the gas valve not energized, or flame sensed with no call for heat. It is a job for a technician, not a DIY repair.

What the code means

Trane's integrated furnace control (IFC) flash code chart in installer's guide 18-CH49D1-1 lists 5 flashes as "Flame sensed when no flame should be present". Trane's help center page on ignition controller board diagnostics words it as flame sensed and the gas valve not energized, or flame sensed and no call for heat. In plain terms, the control is seeing a flame signal at a moment when the burners should be off. That can be real flame, for example a gas valve that is not closing fully, or it can be a false signal from a dirty or shorted flame sensor or its wiring. Because a flame that should not be there is a safety problem, this is not a code to ignore or work around. Note that other Trane boards number the codes differently: on the control described in Trane's help center, 6 flashes is the flame rollout switch and 7 is weak flame, while the older IFC chart uses 6 for reversed power or poor grounding and 8 for low flame sense. Check the label on your own furnace door for the chart that matches your board.

Likely causes

  • A gas valve that is leaking by or not closing completely, so burners keep a flame after the call for heat ends (needs a technician)
  • A flame sensor with heavy carbon or oxide buildup, or one that is shorted to ground, sending a false flame signal
  • Damaged or pinched flame sensor wiring, or a loose or corroded connection
  • A fault on the control board's flame-sensing circuit
  • Moisture or condensation in the burner area affecting the flame sense signal

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Count the red LED through the sight glass in the lower door: 5 flashes and a pause is this code. Trane's chart lists slow flashing as normal with no call for heat and fast flashing as normal during a call for heat.
  2. If you can see flame at the burners while the thermostat is not calling for heat, or you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside. Do not try to fix it yourself.
  3. Turn the thermostat to off, wait a few minutes, then turn the furnace power off at the switch for about 30 seconds and back on to see whether the code clears. A one-time false reading may not return.
  4. If the code returns, stop and call a heating technician. Cleaning a flame sensor is a common service step, but it involves removing a part from a gas appliance, so Trane's guides leave it to qualified installers.
  5. Write down your model number from the rating label inside the door before you call.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • 5 flashes returns after a power reset. Do not keep resetting a furnace that is flagging a flame it should not have.
  • You see flame with no call for heat, or hear the burners or gas valve acting strangely, or smell gas.
  • Testing the gas valve, flame sensor circuit, wiring and control board with a meter. That is live-voltage work on a gas appliance.
  • Never bypass or jumper any safety control. If a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.
