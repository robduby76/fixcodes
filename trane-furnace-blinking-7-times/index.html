Code: 7 flashes🔥 Trane Gas Furnace

Trane Furnace Blinking 7 Times: Gas Valve Circuit Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-20 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

On Trane and American Standard furnaces with an integrated furnace control (IFC), a red LED blinking 7 times then pausing means a gas valve circuit error. Trane's newer board literature spells it out: the control senses 24 volts at the gas valve when it should not be there, or it tried to open the gas valve and did not sense 24 volts. This is a safety fault in the circuit that opens the gas, so it is a technician job, not a reset-and-forget code.

What the code means

Trane's installer's guide 18-CH49D1-1 lists integrated furnace control error flash codes, and 7 flashes there means "Gas valve circuit error". The 2023 IFC kit guide 18-CH66D1-5B-EN (for *UX/*UY/*DX/*DY R-series furnaces) uses the same wording. Trane's modulating control guide 18-CH50D1-2 (board CNT05457) calls 7 flashes an "External gas valve circuit error" and explains it in its alert table as Gas Valve Fault, ERR 93: either the control senses 24V present at the gas valve when it should not be, or the control tried to turn on the gas valve but 24V was not sensed. A solid red LED on that board is a different fault, an internal gas valve error. In other words, the board watches the 24 volt wire that powers the gas valve and the signal did not match what it commanded. That points to the gas valve wiring, the gas valve, or the control board, not to a dirty filter or a venting problem. Trane's service facts for the *UH1/*DH1 condensing furnaces give technicians a short check for this code: turn the thermostat off and measure for 24 VAC between the red lead at the gas valve connection and chassis ground. Voltage there with no call for heat means the wiring must be corrected; no voltage means the IFC is replaced; then operation is checked. Related codes on the same chart include 5 flashes (flame sensed when no flame should be present), 6 flashes (115 volt AC power reversed, poor grounding or low voltage), 8 flashes (low flame sense signal) and 9 flashes (check ignitor circuit and Line N to 24VAC common voltage on 18-CH49D1-1; open inducer limit switch on the newer boards). Numbering is not the same on every Trane furnace, and newer models with a number display show E-codes instead, so match the chart on your own furnace door before acting on this page.

Likely causes

  • A loose, pinched, chafed or miswired wire between the control board and the gas valve, often after recent service or a part replacement
  • A replacement gas valve or control board wired differently from the original
  • A shorted wire that puts 24 volts on the gas valve lead when the board has not called for gas
  • A failed gas valve that does not draw or pass the signal the board expects
  • A failed gas valve relay or sensing circuit on the integrated furnace control board (confirmed only after the wiring is checked)

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Count the red LED through the sight glass in the lower door: 7 flashes and a pause is this code on an IFC that uses the chart above. Slow flashing is normal with no call for heat; fast flashing is normal during a call for heat. A solid red LED is a different fault.
  2. Turn the thermostat to off. Trane's own check for this code starts with the thermostat off, and you should leave it off until a technician has looked at the furnace.
  3. If you want to see whether it was a one-time glitch, turn the furnace power switch off for about 30 seconds and back on once. Do not keep cycling power to force heat; the board stopped because it could not trust the gas valve circuit.
  4. If the code showed up right after installation, a repair or a new part, call whoever did the work. A miswired gas valve does not develop by itself.
  5. Do not unplug, jumper or test the gas valve wiring yourself. Write down the furnace model number from the rating label inside the door and the flash count before you call. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • 7 flashes comes back after a single power reset, or the burners do not light.
  • Measuring 24 VAC between the gas valve red lead and chassis ground with the thermostat off, and correcting the wiring if voltage is present (Trane service facts check).
  • Inspecting the gas valve harness and connector, testing the gas valve, and replacing the integrated furnace control if the wiring checks good.
  • Any gas valve or control board replacement, followed by a full check of the ignition sequence and gas pressure.
  • Never bypass or jumper a safety control or the gas valve circuit. If a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.
