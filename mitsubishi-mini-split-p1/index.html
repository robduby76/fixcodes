Mitsubishi Mini Split Error Code P1: Intake (Room Temperature) Sensor Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

P1 on a Mitsubishi mini split means the indoor unit's room temperature thermistor, called TH1 or the intake sensor, is reading as shorted or open. The unit pauses for 3 minutes and tries again; if the reading is still bad it stops and shows P1. The usual causes are a loose or unplugged sensor connector on the indoor controller board, a broken sensor wire, a failed thermistor, or less often a faulty indoor controller board.

What the code means

Mitsubishi's Mr. Slim (P-series) service manuals list P1 as "Intake sensor error" in the indoor unit check-code table, and the troubleshooting table calls it an abnormality of the room temperature thermistor (TH1). The board checks this sensor all the time in cooling, drying and heating. A reading of 90 °C (194 °F) or more counts as a short, and -40 °C (-40 °F) or less counts as an open circuit. When either is detected the unit goes into 3-minute resume prevention mode, and P1 is set only if it doesn't return to normal after those 3 minutes. On the wireless remote self-check, P1 is 1 blink or beep. On some outdoor boards (for example the PUY-A NHA7/NKA7 series) the same fault shows as LED1 4 blinks and LED2 1 blink. P1 is the room air sensor; P2 and P9 are the indoor pipe sensors (TH2 liquid and TH5 condenser/evaporator).

Likely causes

  • The room temperature thermistor (TH1) has failed, so its resistance no longer matches Mitsubishi's characteristic table.
  • The TH1 connector is loose or not fully seated on the indoor controller board (CN20 on the PLA-A BA4 cassette manual).
  • A broken thermistor wire or poor contact in the wiring, which can show up only when the lead is bent or pulled.
  • A defective indoor controller board.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

The indoor controller board is reached with the unit's covers off and can carry line voltage. Switch off power at the breaker or disconnect before touching connectors or measuring thermistor resistance, and leave board work to a qualified HVAC technician.

  1. Turn the system off at the breaker or disconnect, wait a minute, then turn it back on. Mitsubishi's countermeasure ends with turning the power off and on again after the check, and a one-off glitch may clear.
  2. If P1 comes straight back, note the exact indoor model number from the unit's rating label. The sensor and board checks need the front panel and electrical cover off, which exposes live parts.
  3. Compare the room temperature shown on a wired remote controller (if you have one) with a thermometer next to the indoor unit. A reading that's way off, or stuck, points toward the sensor or board.
  4. Call a Mitsubishi dealer or HVAC technician for the connector, resistance and board checks below.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • With power off, check the TH1 resistance against Mitsubishi's table: about 15.0 kΩ at 0 °C (32 °F), 9.6 kΩ at 10 °C (50 °F), 6.3 kΩ at 20 °C (68 °F), 4.3 kΩ at 30 °C (86 °F) and 3.0 kΩ at 40 °C (104 °F). The thermistor is R0 = 15 kΩ ± 3%, about 5.4 kΩ at 25 °C (77 °F).
  • While measuring, gently pull and bend the lead wire. Mitsubishi notes this can reveal a broken wire or contact failure that a still reading misses.
  • Check the connector on the indoor controller board (CN20 for TH1 on the PLA-A BA4 manual; the Mr. Slim chart lists CN20, CN21, CN29 and CN44 for the indoor thermistors). Reseat it, turn the power on again and check whether the unit restarts.
  • Check the room temperature display on the remote controller. If it differs abnormally from the actual room temperature and the thermistor and wiring are good, replace the indoor controller board.
  • Turn the power off and on again to operate after the check.
