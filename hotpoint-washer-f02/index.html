Code: F02🧺 Hotpoint Washer

Hotpoint Washing Machine F02 Error Code: Motor Circuit Fault (Drum Won't Turn or Turns On and Off)

Reviewed 2026-10-26 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

F02 on a Hotpoint washing machine is a motor circuit fault. Hotpoint says the drum turns only now and then, or not at all, during the wash. The control board isn't getting a proper speed signal back from the motor, which usually means the motor is jammed, a wire or connector has worked loose, or the motor's speed sensor (tacho) has failed. You can check the drum turns freely by hand, but the electrical checks and any motor or board replacement are engineer work.

What the code means

Hotpoint UK lists F02 as "Motor circuit fault": intermittent or no drum rotation during the wash cycle. Hotpoint's advice is to call 0344 822 4224 to book an engineer. Hotpoint and Indesit machines in the UK share the same control electronics, and Indesit's WIL10 technical documentation (the French service file for this control platform) gives the technical meaning: the motor is locked, or its tachometer (the small speed-sensing coil on the motor) is short-circuited or open. Its service steps are to check whether the motor is jammed, check the contacts of the J9 connector on the control board, measure the tacho coil between pins 1 and 2 of the J9 wiring connector (it should read between 115 and 170 ohms; if it's shorted or open, check the wiring between those pins), check continuity between pins 6 and 7 of J9 on machines with a three-phase motor, then replace the motor, and finally replace the board. F02 is different from F01, a control board fault where some or all functions can't be selected, from F03, a temperature sensor fault, and from F05, a drain blockage or pump problem. The exact connector numbers can differ between Hotpoint model ranges, so an engineer will work from your model's own wiring diagram.

Likely causes

  • The drum or motor is physically jammed, for example by an item caught between the drum and tub, so the motor can't turn (WIL10 service file: motor locked)
  • A loose, corroded or burnt contact at the motor connector or the J9 connector on the control board
  • A failed tacho (speed sensor) coil on the motor, reading short-circuited or open instead of 115 to 170 ohms
  • Damaged wiring between the motor and the control board, often where the harness flexes near the tub
  • A failed motor, or less often a faulty motor drive on the control board

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the machine before any check. Don't remove the back panel or test the motor and board connectors yourself unless you're trained; the board carries mains voltage. Never open the door with hot water in the drum.

  1. Switch the machine off and unplug it at the wall for at least 2 minutes, then plug it back in and try a rinse and spin. If F02 clears and stays away, it may have been a one-off glitch.
  2. With the machine unplugged and empty, turn the drum by hand. It should spin smoothly and quietly. If it's stiff, grinding or stuck, something may be caught between the drum and tub, or the bearings may be worn; don't keep running the machine.
  3. Make sure the load isn't too big or bunched into one heavy lump, which can make the drum struggle to turn.
  4. If the door is locked with water in the drum, don't force it. Leave the machine unplugged and drain it through the pump filter at the bottom front, with towels and a shallow tray ready.
  5. If F02 comes back, book a Hotpoint engineer on 0344 822 4224. The tacho and wiring checks are done with the back panel off and a multimeter, at the motor and board connectors.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • F02 keeps returning after a power reset, or the drum won't turn at all: Hotpoint's advice is to book an engineer.
  • The engineer checks the motor isn't jammed, checks the board connector contacts, measures the tacho coil (115 to 170 ohms on the WIL10 platform) and the motor wiring, then replaces the motor or, if those are fine, the control board.
  • You hear grinding or the drum feels rough when turned by hand: that points to a mechanical problem such as bearings or a trapped item, which needs the drum area opened up.
  • The machine is still under warranty: use Hotpoint service first.
