Code: 9 flashes🔥 Trane Gas Furnace

Trane Furnace Blinking 9 Times: Ignitor Circuit or Grounding Problem (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-11-27 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

On many Trane and American Standard furnaces with an integrated furnace control (IFC), a red LED blinking 9 times then pausing points to the hot surface ignitor circuit. Trane's IFC chart says to check the ignitor circuit and the voltage between line neutral (N) and 24 VAC common, which should be 2 volts or less, and flags a possible grounding problem. Trane's service facts for *UH1/*DH1 furnaces define the 9 flash fault as the ignitor circuit open or shorted, or the ignitor relay stuck closed. One exception matters: the newer IFC boards used in Trane's *UX/*UY/*DX/*DY R-series upgrade kits use 9 flashes for an open inducer limit switch instead, so check the chart on your own furnace door.

What the code means

Trane's installer's guide 18-CH49D1-1 (SiNi igniter assembly IGN00117) prints the integrated furnace control error flash codes, and 9 flashes there reads: "Check Ignitor Circuit and Line 'N' to 24VAC 'Common' voltage (≤ 2 volts) [possible grounding problem]." The same guide warns that the IFC is polarity sensitive (the hot leg of the 115 VAC supply must go to the black field lead) and that the cabinet needs an unbroken ground. Trane's Service Facts UH1-DH1-SF-1L for the *UH1/*DH1 single-stage condensing furnaces has a 9 Flash Fault LED troubleshooting chart that defines it as an Igniter Relay Fault (the control board has sensed that the igniter relay has stuck closed) or an Ignitor Fault (the control board has sensed that the ignitor circuit is open or shorted). The chart's checks: make a call for heat and see whether the igniter glows; if it does not, check for 120 VAC between IGN-H and IGN-N on the control; if voltage is present, disconnect power, unplug the igniter and measure its resistance, which should read 37 to 70 ohms at about 75°F (outside that range, replace the ignitor); a good igniter with good harness and connectors points to replacing the IFC. On the newer IFC used in Trane's 2023 kit guide 18-CH66D1-5B-EN (KIT16582-16585 for *UY/*DY/*UX/*DX R-series furnaces), 9 flashes means "Open Inducer Limit switch", a different fault on the inducer housing. Related codes on the same charts include 6 flashes (115 VAC power reversed, poor grounding or low system voltage; the newer board adds ignitor triac fault), 2 flashes (system lockout) and 8 flashes (low flame sense). Numbering is not the same on every Trane furnace, and models with a number display show E-codes instead, so match the chart on your own furnace door before acting on this page.

Likely causes

  • A cracked, burnt-out or open hot surface ignitor (it stops glowing, or reads outside 37-70 ohms at about 75°F on the *UH1/*DH1 chart)
  • A damaged ignitor harness or a loose connector between the ignitor and the control board
  • A poor ground: Trane's IFC chart checks that line neutral to 24 VAC common reads 2 volts or less and calls out a possible grounding problem
  • No 120 VAC output at the board's IGN-H and IGN-N terminals during a call for heat, or an ignitor relay stuck closed, which points to the integrated furnace control
  • On the newer R-series kit boards (18-CH66D1-5B-EN): an open inducer limit switch on the inducer housing, a different fault with the same flash count

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Count the red LED through the sight glass in the lower door: 9 flashes and a pause. Slow flashing is normal with no call for heat; fast flashing is normal during a call for heat. Read the flash-code chart on the inside of your furnace door, because the newer R-series boards use 9 flashes for an open inducer limit switch.
  2. Turn the thermostat up and watch a start through the sight glass: the inducer should start, then the ignitor should glow orange before the gas valve clicks. If the ignitor never glows, tell your technician; that is the first branch of Trane's chart.
  3. Turn the power off at the furnace switch, wait a minute and turn it back on. On power-up, newer Trane boards flash the last 4 stored faults (newest first), which is useful to note before calling.
  4. Do not touch the ignitor: Trane warns it gets extremely hot, and silicon carbide and silicon nitride ignitors crack easily if handled. Ignitor replacement and voltage checks are technician work unless you are trained.
  5. Write down the furnace model number from the rating label inside the door and the flash count before you call. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • The ignitor does not glow on a call for heat, or 9 flashes keeps coming back after a power cycle.
  • Checking for 120 VAC between IGN-H and IGN-N on the control during ignition, then measuring the unplugged ignitor (37-70 ohms at about 75°F on Trane's *UH1/*DH1 chart) and inspecting the harness and connectors.
  • Measuring line neutral to 24 VAC common (2 volts or less per Trane's IFC chart), and checking furnace grounding and line polarity (hot leg to the black field lead).
  • Replacing the ignitor with the correct part for the model, or replacing the IFC when the ignitor and wiring test good.
  • On R-series kit boards, finding why the inducer limit switch is open. Never bypass or jumper a safety control. If a CO alarm sounds, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.
