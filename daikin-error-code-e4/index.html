Code: E4🔧 Daikin Mini Split

Daikin Error Code E4: Low Pressure Trip (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 3 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

E4 on a Daikin air conditioner or heat pump means the outdoor unit saw refrigerant suction (low-side) pressure drop too low, so it shut down to protect the compressor. Daikin's usual suspects are a refrigerant shortage or leak, a closed shutoff valve after installation, a clogged refrigerant line or stuck expansion valve, or a faulty low pressure sensor or outdoor board. Clean the filters and reset the power once; if E4 comes back, call a licensed HVAC technician.

What the code means

Daikin's SkyAir inverter service manual SiUS25-501 titles E4 "Outdoor Unit: Actuation of Low Pressure Switch," though on these units it's detected by the low pressure sensor: the error is set when suction pressure drops below a specific value. Before that, the unit tries to protect itself by limiting compressor speed as low pressure falls, then going into low pressure standby when the sensor reads under about 10 psi. Daikin says that when low pressure standby happens 3 times within 60 minutes, in cooling or in heating, the unit outputs E4. The outdoor board's own monitor mode lists E4 as "Abnormal suction pressure." Daikin's Simple Self-Diagnosis code list uses the same meaning, "Actuation of low pressure switch (LPS)," across its split, SkyAir and VRV equipment. E4 isn't in the error table for Daikin's Entra wall-mount mini splits, so check your model's manual if you see E4 on a wall-mount unit.

Likely causes

  • An abnormal drop in low pressure from a refrigerant shortage ("out of gas"), usually a leak somewhere in the system. This is the first cause in Daikin's flowchart.
  • Shutoff (stop) valves left closed at the outdoor unit after installation, which Daikin lists both as a supposed cause and as an installation mistake.
  • Refrigerant system clogging, or an electronic expansion valve stuck fully closed.
  • Wiring and piping connected to the wrong unit (wrong connection) on systems with more than one line set.
  • On a new install, the remote controller's operating mode was changed before the check run; Daikin says to set every indoor unit to cooling for the check run.
  • A defective low pressure sensor, a defective connector contact, or a defective outdoor unit PC board.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off the power at the breaker or outdoor disconnect before opening any panel; the outdoor inverter board can hold a dangerous charge after power is removed. Leak repair, refrigerant recovery and recharging must be done by a certified technician (in the US, EPA Section 608 certification is required). Don't keep resetting a unit that trips on low pressure, since running short of refrigerant can damage the compressor.

  1. Turn the system off and check the indoor unit's air filters. Clean them if they're dusty, and make sure supply and return grilles aren't blocked, since poor indoor airflow lowers suction pressure.
  2. Look for obvious problems at the outdoor unit and the line set: crushed or kinked copper lines, oily spots at fittings (a sign of a refrigerant leak), or ice on the lines.
  3. Turn the power off at the breaker or disconnect, wait a few minutes, then restore power and restart once.
  4. If E4 shows up right after a new installation, call the installer: closed shutoff valves and a check run done in the wrong mode are installation fixes Daikin calls out by name.
  5. If E4 comes back after a reset, stop running the system and call a licensed HVAC technician. Low pressure usually means a refrigerant problem that needs a certified tech.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Following Daikin's SiUS25-501 flowchart: confirming the stop valves are fully open on both the gas and liquid side, and opening them if not.
  • Confirming whether low pressure really dropped, and if so checking for low charge or a leak, refrigerant system clogging, wrong wiring or piping connections, and an electronic expansion valve stuck fully closed.
  • If low pressure is actually normal, measuring the sensor voltage (VL) at X45A pins 2 and 3 on the outdoor PC board (A1P) and comparing it to Daikin's pressure sensor pressure/voltage table. If the voltage matches the pressure, replacing the low pressure sensor; if not, replacing the outdoor PC board.
  • On a new install, confirming the additional refrigerant charge was finished and recalculating the required charge from the piping length, then running the check operation with all indoor units set to cooling.
  • Checking connector contact at the low pressure sensor, per Daikin's self-diagnosis code list.
