Code: PF🌀 Kenmore Dryer

Kenmore Dryer PF Error Code: Power Failure During the Cycle (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

PF on a Kenmore dryer means Power Failure: the power dropped out while the dryer was running, so the cycle stopped. In most cases nothing is broken. Press Start to continue the cycle, or press Stop (Pause/Cancel on some models) to clear the display. If PF shows up often, Sears says to check the dryer's power cord and the outlet.

What the code means

Sears PartsDirect lists PF the same way for Kenmore 110-series electronic-control dryers, Kenmore Elite dryers and Kenmore Elite Oasis dryers: a power failure occurred while the dryer was running. Its fix is to restart the cycle (press Start to continue, or Stop to clear the display), and if the code appears frequently, check the power cord and the electrical outlet. A Whirlpool dryer tech sheet (8541833) for the same family of electronic controls describes it the same way: "PF" flashes to show that a power failure occurred while the dryer was running, and you press Start to continue or Pause/Cancel to clear the display. So PF is the control telling you what happened to its power, not a heater, motor or sensor fault. Kenmore 110-series dryers are built by Whirlpool; you'll find the model number on the label inside the door opening.

Likely causes

  • A power outage, flicker or brownout while the dryer was running
  • A tripped breaker or blown household fuse. Whirlpool's tech sheet lists a blown fuse or tripped breaker among the first things to check before servicing
  • A loose, damaged or worn power cord, the part Sears names for frequent PF codes
  • A loose plug or a worn outlet that interrupts power during the cycle, which Sears also says to check
  • Loose or corroded connections where the cord attaches at the dryer's terminal block

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Unplug the appliance or switch off its breaker before opening panels.

  1. Press Start to continue the cycle, or press Stop (Pause/Cancel) to clear PF from the display.
  2. Check whether the house had an outage or flicker. A one-off PF after an outage needs no repair.
  3. Check the dryer's breaker or fuses. An electric dryer runs on 240 volts through a double breaker or two fuses, so make sure it's fully on, or switch it fully off and back on.
  4. Unplug the dryer and make sure the plug seats firmly in the outlet. Don't use an extension cord.
  5. With the dryer unplugged, look over the power cord for cuts, crushed spots or scorching, and check that the cord's strain relief at the back of the dryer is tight.
  6. If PF keeps coming back with no outage in the house, have the cord, terminal block and outlet checked (see below).

🛠 When to call a pro

  • PF keeps appearing during cycles even though the house power is steady. Sears says to check the power cord and outlet when it's frequent, and the cord may need replacing.
  • The breaker keeps tripping or a fuse keeps blowing. Call an electrician.
  • The outlet, plug or cord looks burned or feels hot. Stop using the dryer and have an electrician check the circuit.
  • Replacing the cord or checking the terminal block screws behind the rear access cover, if you aren't comfortable working with 240-volt wiring. Unplug the dryer first.
  • PF appears along with other codes or the controls act erratically. Whirlpool's tech sheet notes that corroded connectors are the most common cause of control failure, which is a job for a technician.
