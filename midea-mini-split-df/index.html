Code: dF🔧 Midea Mini Split

Midea Mini Split dF Code: Outdoor Unit Defrosting (Meaning, What's Normal, When to Call)

Reviewed 2026-10-24 · Based on 4 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

dF on a Midea mini split isn't a fault. It means the outdoor unit is defrosting its coil during heating mode, and Midea's manuals say this is not a malfunction. While it's on, the heat and fans pause for a few minutes, and steam from the outdoor unit is normal. Let it finish; only call for service if dF never clears, keeps coming back every few minutes, or shows up with a real error code.

What the code means

In heating mode the outdoor coil runs colder than the outside air, so in cool, damp weather frost builds up on it and heating capacity drops. Midea's installation and operation manual explains that the system then runs a defrost operation, the indoor unit shows "dF" on its display, and the indoor fan stops so it doesn't blow cold air, adding "This is not a malfunction." Midea's own owner's manual for its Ukrainian residential inverter splits lists dF as defrosting of the outdoor unit, only in heating mode, and notes the outdoor unit may give off steam while it happens. Midea's Oasis Plus service manual describes the logic: the unit decides to defrost from the outdoor coil temperature (T3), outdoor air temperature (T4) and compressor run time; the compressor keeps running while the indoor and outdoor fans stop, and defrost ends when the outdoor coil has warmed up or after 15 minutes at most. On Midea inverter outdoor boards with a digital display, a technician will also see "dF" (or dF alternating with the running frequency) on the outdoor PCB during defrost. Midea's VRF manuals show it as dfx, where x is the defrost step.

Likely causes

  • Normal operation: heating in cold, humid weather (roughly freezing to just above freezing is when frost builds fastest), so the outdoor coil frosts over and the unit melts it off.
  • Frequent defrosts in very cold or wet weather. Midea's own troubleshooting table lists "frosting and defrosting frequently" as a reason heating seems weak, with the fix being simply to wait for milder conditions.
  • Poor airflow over the outdoor coil from snow, leaves, a dirty coil or a unit sitting too low in snow, which makes it frost up sooner. Midea's troubleshooting table also lists dirty condenser fins and a dirty air filter as causes of poor performance.
  • If dF lasts far longer than 15 minutes or repeats nonstop, the defrost isn't finishing, which points to a real problem such as a faulty outdoor coil (T3) or outdoor air (T4) sensor, a reversing (four-way) valve issue, low refrigerant or an outdoor fan problem. Those normally also bring up a separate error code.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

dF is a normal status, so there's nothing to repair while it's showing. The outdoor fan can restart on its own when defrost ends, so keep hands and tools away from the fan grille and switch off the breaker or outdoor disconnect before clearing debris near it. Don't use sharp tools, heat guns or open flames to remove ice. Many newer Midea units use R32 refrigerant, which is mildly flammable, and any sensor, valve or refrigerant work belongs to a licensed HVAC technician.

  1. Wait. Leave the unit in heating mode and let defrost finish on its own; Midea's control ends it once the outdoor coil warms up, and no later than 15 minutes. The indoor fan restarting and warm air returning means it's done.
  2. Don't turn the unit off and on to make dF go away. Interrupting defrost just leaves the ice on the coil, and the unit will defrost again soon after.
  3. Steam or a hiss and water running off the outdoor unit during or right after dF are normal; that's the melted frost.
  4. Keep at least a foot or two of clear space around the outdoor unit. Brush snow off the top and sides with a broom, clear leaves from the coil, and make sure melt water can drain away from under the unit instead of refreezing there.
  5. Clean or replace the indoor air filters. A clogged filter cuts heating airflow and makes the whole system work harder.
  6. If the outdoor coil is still a solid block of ice after dF ends, or dF keeps coming back every few minutes all day, call a technician rather than chipping at the ice; you can puncture the coil.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • dF stays on much longer than 15 minutes, never clears, or the outdoor coil stays iced after defrost. A technician checks the outdoor coil (T3) and outdoor air (T4) temperature sensors, since Midea's control uses them to start and end defrost.
  • The coil doesn't clear during defrost. The technician checks that the four-way reversing valve is switching and that its coil is getting power from the outdoor board.
  • The unit frosts up again almost immediately. The technician checks the refrigerant charge and the outdoor fan, since a low charge or a weak fan makes the coil run colder and frost faster.
  • A real error code (an E, P or F code) shows up alongside poor heating. Look up that code; dF on its own is a status, not a fault.
