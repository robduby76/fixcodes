Code: 91❄️ Trane HVAC System

Trane / American Standard Error 91: Communication Error (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-25 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Err 91 on a Trane or American Standard communicating system (ComfortLink II, AccuLink, or the matching American Standard controls) is a communication alarm: one part of the system has stopped hearing from another. The two digits after the dot tell you which link failed. 91.00 is the blower motor, 91.01 the inducer motor board, 91.02 the thermostat-to-outdoor-unit data line, 91.03 a missing system clock, 91.05 and 91.06 miswired or shorted bus wiring, and 91.99 a thermostat that didn't start up cleanly. Trane rates every 91 alert as critical. Start by writing down the full code, power-cycling the thermostat and system, and checking for an added communicating air cleaner; anything past that is voltage testing for a technician.

What the code means

Trane's communicating systems pass data between the comfort control (thermostat), the indoor unit and the outdoor unit over a low-voltage bus, and inside some furnaces between the main control board, the blower motor and the inducer drive board. Trane's System Alert Guide (August 2020) and its ComfortLink II alert code table list a family of 91 alarms, all rated Critical. ERR.091.00 Blower Communication Loss: loss of communication between the serial communicating blower motor and the control board (CLII / AccuLink units only); on 3-stage and modulating furnaces a corrupted signal between the furnace control board and the inducer drive board can also cause it. ERR.091.01 Inducer Communication Loss: loss of communication between the furnace control board and the draft inducer motor control board. ERR.091.02 System Communication Error: loss of heat/cool demand; the comfort control sends demand every minute, and the fault is reported after 3 missed messages. ERR.091.03 No System Clock: the system clock, set by the Bit Master on the indoor control board (air handler or furnace) or relay panel, is not reporting. ERR.091.05 Communication Mis-Wire Error: the R and D wires are reversed at the comfort control or indoor unit. ERR.091.06 Communication Error, Low Bus Voltage: bus voltage stuck low. ERR.091.99: the HVAC communication bus did not initialize correctly, usually after a thermostat reboot that didn't finish starting up.

Likely causes

  • 91.00: no line voltage to the blower motor or inducer motor control board (115 VAC on a furnace, 230 VAC on an air handler), or a bad low-voltage connection between the control board and the blower motor. Trane also lists a failed control board or blower motor, decided by a 24-volt go/no-go test.
  • 91.00 on 3-stage and modulating furnaces: the inducer drive board corrupting the signal. Trane's test is to unplug the 4-pin connector at E9 on the furnace board, cycle power and see whether the blower runs.
  • 91.01: a bad connection or harness between the furnace control board and the draft inducer motor control board, or a failed inducer drive board. On a call for heat the green COM LED on the inducer board should flash.
  • 91.02: the data (D) line to the thermostat broken or disconnected, D and the common (B) line reversed at the outdoor unit, or the D line shorted to ground on the way to the outdoor unit. A communicating air cleaner on the D wire can also be the cause.
  • 91.03: the thermostat isn't communicating with the indoor unit or relay panel. Trane's reference is about 12 VDC for good communication and 16 VDC for lost communication.
  • 91.05: R and D wires swapped at the thermostat or indoor unit.
  • 91.06: a communicating air cleaner with a faulty control board, or D and B wires reversed at the thermostat, outdoor unit or indoor unit.
  • 91.99: a thermostat reboot that didn't fully initialize its internal components.

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels.

  1. Write down the full alert, including the digits after the dot (91.00, 91.02 and so on). The fix depends entirely on which one it is.
  2. For 91.99, do what Trane says: power the thermostat down for 5 seconds, then power it back up. Turning off the breaker for the indoor unit for about a minute and then back on is a reasonable next step for the other 91 codes too.
  3. If you have a communicating air cleaner (one wired into the thermostat's communication wires), note it for the technician. Trane's own test for 91.02 and 91.06 is to disconnect the air cleaner or its D data wire and see whether the alert clears.
  4. If someone recently replaced the thermostat or rewired anything, mention it. Swapped R and D wires (91.05) or D and B wires (91.06) are installation mistakes Trane names directly.
  5. Walk the visible thermostat wire run to the outdoor unit and look for cut, chewed or crushed cable, which can ground the data line (91.02). Don't splice it yourself.
  6. Leave the furnace and outdoor unit panels closed. The blower and inducer checks in 91.00 and 91.01 involve line voltage and live board testing.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Any 91 alert that returns after a power cycle, since Trane rates all of them as critical.
  • 91.00 or 91.01 on a furnace: a technician checks line voltage to the blower or inducer board, measures about 12 VDC between the 12V and GND pins on the control board and at terminals 1 and 4 of the blower's low-voltage plug, and runs Trane's 24 VAC go/no-go test on pins 3 and 4 of the blower harness. If the motor runs, the control board is replaced; if it doesn't and the harness tests good, the motor is.
  • 91.02, 91.03 or 91.06: a technician measures DC voltage between D and B at the thermostat and at the indoor and outdoor units, and checks the Bit Master LED on the indoor control board (off, dim or flickering points to a reversed or grounded D line).
  • A shorted or damaged wire run to the outdoor unit that needs replacing.
  • A faulty air cleaner board, which Trane says to replace per its service bulletin.
