Carrier Furnace Code 34: Ignition Proving Failure (Meaning, Causes, Fixes)

Reviewed 2026-10-23 · Based on 2 manufacturer & service sources

⚡ Quick answer

Status code 34 on a Carrier furnace (3 short flashes, then 4 long flashes) means "ignition proving failure": the control didn't sense flame during the trial for ignition, or lost the flame signal while the burners were running. It retries the ignition sequence 3 more times, and if all fail it goes to code 14, ignition lockout. The usual causes Carrier lists are a dirty (oxidized) flame sensor, gas turned off or low gas pressure, a failed hot surface igniter, a bad gas valve, or a poor ground.

What the code means

Carrier status codes are two digits: the first digit is the number of short flashes and the second the number of long flashes, so code 34 is 3 short flashes followed by 4 long flashes. Carrier's variable-speed (troubleshooting guide 58-06T) and 2-stage (guide 58-3T) furnace guides define it as an ignition-proving fault: flame was not sensed during the trial for ignition period. The control repeats the ignition sequence 3 more times before going to status code 14 (ignition lockout), which auto-resets after 3 hours. The code can also mean the flame signal was lost during steady-state operation; in that case the blower comes on for a delay before the furnace tries again (the selected blower off-delay on variable-speed models, a 90-second recycle delay on 2-stage models). Carrier's checklist covers the gas supply (manual valve or gas valve turned off, low inlet gas pressure, a defective gas valve), the hot surface igniter, oxide build-up on the flame sensor, flame current (0.5 microamps DC minimum, 4.0 to 6.0 typical), and control ground continuity, including that the green ground wire must be connected to the furnace sheet metal and the flame sensor must not be grounded.

Likely causes

  • Oxide build-up on the flame sensor, so the control can't read the flame (Carrier lists 0.5 microamps DC minimum, 4.0 to 6.0 typical)
  • The manual gas shut-off valve, or the switch on the gas valve, turned off
  • Low inlet gas pressure or low manifold pressure
  • A defective hot surface igniter, or a broken igniter harness
  • A defective or slow-opening gas valve (Carrier says gas should flow at full pressure within 4 seconds), or a bad connection in the gas valve wires
  • Poor control ground continuity, such as the green ground wire not connected to the furnace sheet metal, or a flame sensor that is grounded or out of position
  • Inadequate flame carryover or rough ignition, or air leaking around the igniter bracket
  • A failed furnace control board, once the igniter, gas valve, flame sensor and gas supply all check out

Safe DIY checks

⚠ Before you start

Turn off power at the furnace/HVAC service switch and the breaker before opening panels. Shut the gas valve before any work near the burners, and never disconnect or alter the gas line. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility.

  1. Confirm the code: look through the sight glass on the blower door and count 3 short flashes followed by 4 long flashes. Write it down before you cut power, because the code clears when power is turned off.
  2. Make sure the gas is on: check the manual gas shut-off valve on the pipe to the furnace (handle in line with the pipe) and the on/off switch or knob on the gas valve. If other gas appliances aren't working either, call your gas utility.
  3. Let the furnace finish its retries. If it lights on a later try and keeps running, note it, since a code 34 that keeps coming back still points to a cause from Carrier's list.
  4. Replace a dirty furnace filter, and make sure the blower door is seated firmly so the door switch stays closed.
  5. If you're comfortable doing it with the power and gas off, Carrier's guides list cleaning the flame sensor rod with fine sandpaper or fine steel wool to remove oxide build-up. Don't bend it out of position. If you're not sure which part is the sensor, leave it for a pro.
  6. If the furnace goes into lockout (code 14), Carrier's control auto-resets after 3 hours. Repeated lockouts need a technician.
  7. If you smell gas at any point, don't try to relight or reset anything: leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

🛠 When to call a pro

  • Running Carrier's component test to see whether the hot surface igniter glows orange/white hot by the end of the 15-second warm-up, then checking for 115 VAC at the igniter and continuity through the harness and igniter.
  • Checking for 24 VAC at the gas valve on a call for heat, continuity of the gas valve wires back to the control, and whether the valve opens and lets gas flow.
  • Measuring flame current with a DC microammeter in series with the flame sensor wire; below 0.5 microamps means cleaning, repositioning or replacing the sensor electrode.
  • Checking inlet and manifold gas pressure, firing rate, flame carryover, and air leakage around the igniter bracket.
  • Checking the control's ground continuity and that the flame sensor isn't grounded.
  • If the burners pulsate, checking the vent and combustion-air piping, burner orifices and gas valve regulation as Carrier's guides direct.
  • Replacing the furnace control only after the igniter, gas valve, flame sensor and gas supply all check out.
